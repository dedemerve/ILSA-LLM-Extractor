# Phase 3.14 — Cross-Cycle Candidate Review and Scope Correction

**Date:** 2026-10-15  
**Status:** 28 heuristic flags reviewed; independent search exposed a corpus-coverage false negative; broader completeness audit remains open.  
**Locked source corpus:** `analysis/02_prediction_taxonomy/study_classification.csv` (1,266 records; SHA-256 in the review manifests).  
**Review files:** `analysis/03_data_feasibility/audit/cross_cycle_candidate_review_v2.csv`, `analysis/03_data_feasibility/audit/external_forecast_candidate_review_v2.csv`.

## Executive finding

The original 28 `cross_cycle_prediction_not_fully_verified` entries were generated by a broad heuristic. The rule also flags multi-cycle descriptive work, reviews, measurement/linking studies, off-scope datasets, student or school outcomes, and derived constructs. Source-aware review therefore finds that **28 is not a count of genuine ILSA country-score forecasts**.

More importantly, screening only those 28 is insufficient to establish that the 1,266-record corpus contains no genuine country-level future-cycle forecast. A publisher full-text search found a qualifying historical PISA forecast candidate that is **absent from the 1,266 records**: Becker and Coyle (2026), DOI `10.3389/feduc.2026.1734988`. The article fits country-specific linear/quadratic trends using PISA waves through 2018, forecasts 2022 country means, and calculates forecast deviations against measured 2022 scores. The model choice is described as a comparison of linear and quadratic fit using an F test of their R-squared values; the paper then calculates observed-minus-forecast 2022 deviations. See the [publisher full text](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2026.1734988/full), especially Methods, “Trends and forecasts.”

This is a direct counterexample to any unqualified claim that *the literature* has no country-level held-out PISA forecast. It does not prove the 1,266 corpus contains this record: exact DOI/title matching shows it is absent. It also does not prove the paper meets the corpus's original inclusion criteria, which must be checked against the documented search strategy. Until those criteria are recovered and applied, the correct conclusion is **corpus coverage/false-negative risk; do not claim an exhaustive zero across the literature**.

## Review of the 28 flagged records

The row-level audit preserves all source taxonomy fields and adds disposition, rationale, evidence basis, and source link. It distinguishes evidence inspected at full text or publisher page from a decision based on structured extraction. The source taxonomy remains unchanged.

Key records:

- **PISA 2015 → PISA 2018 science ML paper** (DOI `10.1007/s00521-023-08901-6`): full text describes model fitting/tuning using 2015 records and application to 2018 students; it also aggregates student predictions into country means. This is cross-cycle student-level transfer with country summaries, not a direct forecast of country score time series. Availability of target-cycle predictors before the cycle is not established. It should not be described as a verified pre-cycle country-mean forecast.
- **TIMSS quality-assurance neural-network paper** (DOI `10.1186/s40536-025-00262-x`): full text says the NN was trained jointly using observed target-cycle student scores in the target outcomes (e.g., TIMSS 2011 G4 and 2015 G8) and notes a leave-one-out design was not implemented. It is retrospective QC screening, not a genuinely held-out target-cycle forecast.
- **Historical equity trend, dataset interpolation, and 2100 Flynn-effect papers** are related time-trend or extrapolation work, but their targets are respectively an equity indicator, missing annual panel cells, and a derived IQ-like index—not a later PISA/TIMSS/PIRLS country mean evaluated as an untouched future cycle.
- Many remaining flags are irrelevant to the strict outcome definition (reviews, non-ILSA datasets, within-assessment process prediction, single-cycle student/school targets, or scale-linking/descriptive work). The detailed row-by-row reasons and evidence strength are in the CSV.

## What is and is not established

1. **Established:** The classifier has 28 rows in its broad cross-cycle status, and the row-level review makes those 28 inspectable.
2. **Established:** The 2026 Becker–Coyle study is absent from the 1,266-row classifier table and, according to its publisher full text, forecasts PISA 2022 country means from PISA data through 2018 with target-cycle forecast deviations.
3. **Not established:** That the 1,266-record corpus has complete coverage of all eligible PISA/TIMSS/PIRLS country-score forecasts. Finding one absent qualifying study proves a coverage risk, not the total number of omissions.
4. **Not established:** A corpus-wide count of genuinely prospective country-level ILSA forecasts after source verification. The 28-row review cannot detect every false negative because the automated trigger is not a validated recall screen.
5. **Not established:** That the Becker–Coyle paper belongs in the original 1,266 corpus. The original database search dates, source set, language/document-type criteria, and exclusion log must be checked before changing corpus membership or method-frequency denominators.

## Implications for the project

- Keep existing RF/XGBoost frequency counts as **general predictive ILSA corpus counts**, not counts for genuine forecasting studies.
- The found country-level PISA forecast uses conventional **linear/quadratic trend regression**, not RF or XGBoost. It is a relevant baseline precedent; its discovery weakens the rationale that the literature's most frequent algorithm labels specifically motivate country-level forecasts.
- Do not change the locked 1,266-record method-frequency output in place. First reconstruct corpus search scope and decide whether the external study qualifies for a separately versioned corpus update.
- Replace the prior unqualified “zero confirmed in the literature” sentence with a scope-limited statement: “No qualifying country-level future-cycle forecast was confirmed in the 28 heuristic flags after review; an independently found 2026 PISA study outside the coded corpus does use a pre-2022 historical trend forecast, so overall literature completeness is unresolved.”

## Required next audit

1. Recover search databases, query strings, last-search date, publication-year window, language and document-type criteria, deduplication rules, and exclusion log for the 1,266 records.
2. Re-run a targeted independent search for terms combining ILSA program names with country mean/national score, forecasting/trend extrapolation, later-cycle prediction, and held-out evaluation. Search both ML and conventional statistical forecasting because the current algorithm taxonomy is ML-heavy.
3. Screen records outside the 28 flags, especially traditional-statistics and non-predictive task-code rows, for country-level PISA/TIMSS/PIRLS cycle forecasts.
4. Retrieve and inspect the external candidate's full paper and supplement (the publisher full text is available), record the exact inputs, forecast origin, model-selection data, and target comparisons, and assess inclusion against the original corpus criteria.
5. If eligible, create a new corpus release and recompute all method-frequency denominators; if excluded, document the specific exclusion reason and retain it in the supplemental candidate log.
6. Have a second reviewer independently code borderline candidates, reconcile disagreements, and report agreement before strengthening a “no examples” claim.
