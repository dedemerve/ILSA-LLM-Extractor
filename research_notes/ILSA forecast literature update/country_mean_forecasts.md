# Published ILSA Country-Mean Forecast Literature

## What published studies actually forecast future ILSA country/economy means?

### Takeaway

A focused search identified a small, uneven literature. The strongest direct matches are (1) a 2025 PISA mathematics paper that projects country-level means one assessment cycle ahead (PISA 2025) from PISA 2009–2022, and (2) a 2025 TIMSS quality-assurance paper that predicts later Grade 8 country means from earlier TIMSS cycle data, but its purpose and validation design are anomaly screening rather than a clean, prospective forecasting benchmark. No comparable published future-cycle mean-forecasting study was located for PIRLS, ICCS, ICILS, or PIAAC in this search. This is a bounded search result, not proof that no such work exists.

### Cited Findings

| Study | Program/domain and target | Model / horizon | Evaluation / classification | Evidence weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kaplan, Harra, Stampka & Jude (2025), *Stacking Models of Growth* | PISA mathematics country-level proficiency, analyzed separately for girls and boys; data 2009–2022; illustrative one-cycle-ahead PISA 2025 forecast. | Bayesian growth-curve framework: linear and latent-basis growth variants; country pace-of-progress models and Bayesian stacking of predictive distributions. One PISA cycle (~3 years; 2022 to 2025). | LOO expected log predictive density is used to compare/stack growth-rate models; authors show within-sample and one-cycle-ahead prediction plots. The paper was published before PISA 2025 results and does not validate the 2025 projection against its subsequently released observed value. The main prediction is sex-specific country trajectory/proficiency; do not assume that the plotted output is an overall country mean benchmark directly comparable to ordinary all-student score forecasts. Peer-reviewed original methods/application article. [Cambridge article](https://doi.org/10.1017/psy.2025.2) | High relevance to forecasting methodology; moderate direct comparability to pooled country means/domains. Single program/domain. |
| Braun, von Davier & Chen (2025), *Rethinking TIMSS quality assurance* | TIMSS Grade 8 mathematics and science country means. Method 1 predicts G8 outcomes from G4 contextual/background data from the preceding cycle (approximately the same cohort); Method 2 predicts G8 in one administration from G8 in the previous administration (different cohorts). Data 2011, 2015, 2019. | Two-stage feed-forward neural network (four hidden/output layers, followed by country-level regression correction); one 4-year TIMSS administration interval. | Reports country residual distributions and whether most results were “reasonably well predicted”; residual tail screening, not a standard rolling-origin forecast evaluation with MAE/RMSE against held-out future cycles. Authors explicitly say an ideal leave-one-country-out validation was not conducted because of computational cost. The target is a later-cycle observed country mean, but the design is retrospective and aims to identify unusual data/results, not establish operational forecast skill. Peer-reviewed original methodology article. [Springer article](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40536-025-00262-x) | High relevance to ILSA country means and cross-cycle transfer; limited forecast-skill comparability due validation/objective. |
| Daşbaşı (2025), *Dynamic modeling of PISA achievement scores* | Türkiye-only PISA mathematics, science, reading country means; purported short-term scenario extrapolations to 2025–2027. | ANN using nine annual household-survey problem indicators; system of ODEs fit to aggregate indicators and scores. | Models use five actual PISA cycle outcomes (2009, 2012, 2015, 2018, 2022); paper reports RMSE/SSE/R² and within-data fit. It also linearly interpolates sparse cycle data to annual values. It expressly calls future values mathematical, scenario-based extrapolations; no prospective validation against PISA 2025 was reported in the paper. The paper itself cautions that PISA is cross-sectional, interpolation is synthetic, and high R² may reflect low-variance aggregates. Peer-reviewed Scientific Reports article, but very small effective outcome series and fit/extrapolation metrics do not establish forecast accuracy. [Nature / Scientific Reports](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-32456-2) | Relevant as a forecast-like example, but low weight for comparative forecast-performance claims. |

Published in *Personality and Individual Differences*. | OECD adult-skills cohort regression described in *Adult Skills and Productivity: New Evidence from PIAAC 2023* (2025) | Reconstructs cohort-level PIAAC adult skills for earlier periods/additional countries by regressing PIAAC on same-cohort PISA performance and mean years of schooling, then aggregates to country human-capital stocks. | Cross-sectional/cohort regression and backcasting/extrapolation to periods/countries without PIAAC observations; not a forecast of future PIAAC cycle scores. | OECD notes PIAAC has only two country observations/cycles and insufficient coverage for conventional cross-country time-series regressions. This is a data-extension/imputation strategy for historical or missing outcomes, not prospective cycle forecasting. [OECD report, box C.1](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/06/adult-skills-and-productivity_5e9a03df/12ac6e8c-en.pdf) | Authoritative grey-literature evidence on data scarcity; exclude as direct forecast. |

- The Kaplan et al. article states that it uses 53 countries with complete PISA mathematics outcomes from 2009–2022, and forecasts one period to 2025 using estimated pace-of-progress and stacked growth models. It reports improved predictive performance for stacking over single member models using Kullback–Leibler divergence / predictive evaluation, but this is not the same as post-release 2025 forecast error. [Cambridge article](https://doi.org/10.1017/psy.2025.2)
- The TIMSS article explicitly distinguishes same-cohort Grade 4→Grade 8 prediction from different-cohort Grade 8→Grade 8 next-administration prediction. It reports predictions of 2015/2019 Grade 8 means in math and science; the two-stage NN/regression approach appears to fit most countries reasonably, while the different-cohort approach generally performs less well than the same-cohort approach. [Springer article](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40536-025-00262-x)
- As of this search date (2026-10-25), the actual PISA 2025 results have since been released by OECD (8 September 2026), so Kaplan et al.’s PISA 2025 projection can now in principle be scored against observed data. The paper itself predates those results and did not publish that validation. Any retrospective score must align the paper’s country coverage, sex-specific target, domain, and PISA 2025 trend/link uncertainty before comparison. [OECD PISA 2025 Results, Volume I](https://doi.org/10.1787/73451bc5-en)
- OECD assessment trend statistics use linked scales and account for sampling/measurement uncertainty; these uncertainties matter when scoring small forecast errors against observed cross-cycle mean changes. [OECD PISA 2025 Results, Volume I](https://doi.org/10.1787/73451bc5-en)

### Inferences

- A defensible literature comparison should label forecasts by target and design: **prospective future-cycle mean forecast** (Kaplan’s PISA 2025 projection, as published before results); **retrospective next-administration prediction / anomaly screen** (TIMSS NN); **within-cycle or same-cohort transfer** (TIMSS G4→G8); **ex-post trajectory fit or scenario extrapolation** (Türkiye PISA ODE/ANN); **proficiency-rate forecast** (not identified in this focused search); and **derived/backcast country skill estimates** (PIAAC cohort regressions). These are not interchangeable forecast tasks.
- Evidence found is too sparse and heterogeneous to rank time-series models against Random Forest/XGBoost for country-level mean forecasts. The direct forecast literature does not provide a multi-program, common-test-window head-to-head comparison of RF, XGBoost, and conventional time-series models.
- Published direct evidence is concentrated in PISA and TIMSS. Absence of retrieved direct forecast papers for PIRLS, ICCS, ICILS, and PIAAC signals a search gap, not verified nonexistence or corpus exclusion.

### Gaps

- No published direct future-cycle country-mean forecasts were located for PIRLS, ICCS, ICILS, or PIAAC in this bounded search. Existing trend descriptions and assessment-cycle comparisons are not forecasts.
- No paper located directly compares RF/XGBoost with time-series models on the same ILSA country/domain/cycle holdouts and consistent loss metrics.
- The search did not score Kaplan et al.’s PISA 2025 projected values against the now-released 2025 results; a fair re-evaluation requires extracting the paper’s country-level projected values and target definition, then matching OECD observed data and uncertainty conventions.
- No direct ILSA forecast study was identified for country proficiency-rate outcomes. (This is a search gap, not evidence such studies do not exist.)
- Search boundaries: web search on 2026-10-25, primarily English-language query variants; primary publisher/OECD/IEA pages prioritized. Search was focused on published work naming PISA, TIMSS, PIRLS, ICCS, ICILS, or PIAAC and forecasting/projection/prediction of future country/economy achievement means. Non-English indexes and paywalled bibliographic databases were not systematically searched. Search-result counts are not reproducible PRISMA counts; no inference about whether a paper is present in the project’s coded corpus is made.

## Search strategy and screening boundaries

### Search Strategy

```text
RESEARCH QUESTION: Among published work using PISA, TIMSS, PIRLS, ICCS, ICILS, or PIAAC, which studies forecast future-cycle country/economy mean achievement scores, and which merely predict within-cycle outcomes, proficiency rates, reconstruct past values, or fit/extrapolate trends?
========================================
Sources searched:      Web search; publisher primary pages (Cambridge Core, Springer Nature, Scientific Reports/Nature); OECD/IEA institutional sources; ILSA-Gateway bibliography record; Mendeley Data repository record
Search terms:          "PISA country mean score forecasting future cycle model"; "TIMSS country mean achievement score projections forecasting"; "PIRLS country mean score prediction forecast future cycles"; "ICILS country mean score forecast achievement trend projection"; "forecasting PISA country-level scores future cycle"; "PISA forecasting mean scores country"; "TIMSS forecasting country-level achievement score"; "PIRLS mean score forecast country projection model"; "PIAAC country score future forecast adult skills prediction model"; "ICCS forecasting country average civic knowledge scores future"
Date range:            Published literature available through 2026-10-25; no lower date cut-off
Inclusion criteria:    Published primary studies or authoritative reports that use ILSA data and predict/project country/economy-level mean achievement in an ILSA domain; adjacent work retained only when clearly labeled as not a direct match
Exclusion criteria:    Student-level classification/achievement prediction without future country-mean target; trend descriptions without prediction; proficiency-rate-only outcomes; non-ILSA assessments; derivative summaries without primary-source support; ex-post fits treated as direct forecast evidence
Results:               Web result pool not counted as a deduplicated systematic-review corpus; 5 directly inspected primary/authoritative sources included in evidence table, with 2 adjacent/non-direct sources retained for classification; no PRISMA flow count claimed
```

## What does this evidence imply for cross-program and model-family comparisons?

### Takeaway

The searched published evidence does not support a general claim that conventional time-series methods outperform (or underperform) RF/XGBoost for forecasting ILSA country means. At present, the usable direct evidence is sparse and non-comparable: one PISA growth/stacking projection, one TIMSS model-based next-cycle QC prediction, and Türkiye-only projection papers with substantial fit-versus-forecast limitations. The project’s own common-holdout benchmark can therefore address a real evidence gap, provided it uses consistent forecast origins, horizons, and scoring across models and does not treat unlike published prediction tasks as head-to-head benchmarks.

### Cited Findings

- The PISA Bayesian stacking paper reports that stacking outperformed member models in LOO predictive comparison for country-level pace-of-progress distributions (KL divergence), but its one-cycle forecast is for PISA mathematics and should not be presented as evidence that time-series models beat RF/XGBoost. [Cambridge article](https://doi.org/10.1017/psy.2025.2)
- The TIMSS NN paper shows a concrete cross-cycle method for predicting Grade 8 country means from 2011/2015/2019 data in math and science, while noting generally less symmetric residuals / weaker fit for different-cohort G8→G8 prediction than same-cohort G4→G8. It does not report a standardized MAE/RMSE comparison against RF, XGBoost, ARIMA, or baseline persistence in a held-out forecasting design. [Springer article](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40536-025-00262-x)
- Türkiye PISA continuous-time/ANN work evaluates 2009–2022 cycle observations and interpolated annual values, but clarifies that the interpolated values are synthetic and future outputs mathematical extrapolations. Its reported ANN/ODE fit metrics are therefore not directly comparable with rolling-origin errors from future-cycle prediction. [Nature article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-32456-2)
- The OECD reports PIAAC country time coverage is sparse (two cycles/observations in the cited 2025 analysis); cohort regression methods use same-cohort student assessment data to estimate otherwise unavailable adult-skill values. This reinforces that PIAAC reconstruction should not be counted as direct future cycle prediction. [OECD report](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/06/adult-skills-and-productivity_5e9a03df/12ac6e8c-en.pdf)
- PISA 2025 results became available after the Kaplan et al. paper. This makes an external validation possible in principle, but only after extracting the paper’s forecast values/estimand and aligning to the official OECD 2025 estimates and link/sampling uncertainty. [OECD PISA 2025 results](https://doi.org/10.1787/73451bc5-en)

### Inferences

- For RQ1, compare each algorithm only within shared program × domain × grade/cycle test windows. Report pooled or cross-program summaries secondarily, with program and horizon stratification; distinguish scale units, and use forecast-origin (rolling-origin) evaluation where feasible.
- Include simple persistence / last-observation and linear-trend baselines before claiming complex time-series or ML model advantage. Evaluate point error (MAE/RMSE) and, where probabilistic intervals exist, proper interval/distribution scores; account for sampling/link uncertainty of the observed country means.
- Use the published studies as context/method precedents, not numerical external benchmarks, unless raw forecast predictions and matching realized target values can be recovered.
- For RQ2, a knowledge-base integration claim requires an ablation under the same forecast-origin split: identical base time-series model, with versus without KB-derived features/priors/weights; prevent KB variables from encoding future outcomes or assessment-cycle leakage. Publish fold-level errors and uncertainty, not only an aggregate percentage improvement.

### Gaps

- No common validation cohort or unified benchmark across PISA/TIMSS/PIRLS/ICCS/ICILS/PIAAC was found in the inspected sources.
- No directly comparable published RF/XGBoost-versus-time-series study on country-level ILSA mean scores was located.
- No evidence in these sources establishes that a structured literature-derived knowledge base improves the forecast of future country means; the relevant project ablation is an empirical, project-specific result and must be validated independently.
- The retrieved Yıldırım & Yapıcı Mendeley repository advertises GRU/LSTM/ARIMA/Prophet PISA reading forecasts for Türkiye (including a 2025 horizon), but the source currently inspected establishes only a data/software artifact and not peer-reviewed publication or external forecast validation. [Mendeley Data record](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/yy9wwkg2ww/1)
