# Literature-derived knowledge integration in ILSA forecasting

## What counts as knowledge-base integration, and what does the published evidence show?

### Takeaway

I found **no primary study directly integrating an ILSA-corpus-derived structured knowledge base into a time-series model to forecast future country-level ILSA mean achievement**. Primary forecasting literature does show that explicit expert rules/predictions can be fused with a neural forecaster, and that learned temporal/structural memory can improve continual multivariate forecasting in non-education domains; neither establishes benefit for ILSA country-cycle series. The repository's M1 is a narrower, testable literature-informed feature-prior intervention, not a retrieval-augmented or symbolic knowledge-base system.

### Cited Findings

- **Search record / boundaries.** On 2026-10-25, searched the web search engine with: `ILSA country achievement time series forecasting machine learning country mean scores`; `PISA country-level achievement score forecasting time series knowledge base`; `TIMSS country mean achievement forecasting machine learning`; `knowledge guided time series forecasting structured knowledge base feature selection`; `"Knowledge Forcing" LSTM forecasting paper 2023 rules dataset`; `"Knowledge Informed Time Series Forecasting" KDD 2025 experiments`; `"TIMSS quality assurance" neural network country-level mean score forecast 2015 2019`; `"ILSA" "forecast" country achievement scores machine learning prediction future cycle`; `Chattha Malik Dengel Ahmed Knowledge Forcing 2023 DOI 14259`; `"Knowledge Forcing" "4 real-world datasets" results dataset`; `"Literature-informed" time series forecasting knowledge weights feature selection`; and `"Addressing data dependency in neural networks" KENN time series forecasting 2025`. Inclusion: primary paper or official assessment documentation that states integration mechanism and/or ILSA outcome/evaluation; one peer-reviewed forecasting review included only for method recommendations. Exclusion: secondary summaries, search snippets unsupported by accessible original records, studies without forecasting or relevant knowledge mechanism. Search engine did not return auditable total-result/dedup counts; this is a targeted evidence scan, not an exhaustive systematic review.

- **Direct method evidence — Knowledge Forcing (KF-LSTM).** Chattha et al. (ICANN 2023 proceedings) combine an explicit knowledge-driven system (KDS) with an LSTM encoder-decoder: KDS forecasts enter the decoder through a residual connection alongside sequential history. Authors report evaluation on four real-world datasets in multi-horizon forecasting and claim average 20% improvement over previous state of the art. This is actual explicit knowledge-output fusion, unlike merely adding ordinary predictors; the available abstract does not identify dataset-specific effects, uncertainty, or ILSA relevance. Evidence tier: primary peer-reviewed conference paper; direct methodological analogue, indirect domain evidence. [DFKI full paper](https://www.dfki.de/fileadmin/user_upload/import/14319_Knowledge_forcing_ICANN23.pdf); [DFKI publication record](https://www.dfki.de/en/web/research/projects-and-publications/publication/14319).

- **Direct method evidence — KENN.** Chattha et al. (Machine Learning, 2025) propose a residual fusion scheme combining a knowledge-driven system and a DNN for time-series forecasting. The abstract reports constrained-data/inaccurate-knowledge experiments, including a claim that KENN using 10% of the data beats a DNN trained on all data, and comparison with state-of-the-art methods on five real-world datasets. This is primary peer-reviewed journal evidence for explicit knowledge-system plus neural forecast fusion, but it is not evidence that literature-derived ILSA predictor importance weights improve country-level achievement forecasts. [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10994-024-06714-9); [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03903).

- **Direct method evidence — KI-TSF.** Pan & Song (KDD 2025 paper) model time-varying inter-series dependencies as dynamic graphs and use structural-consistency regularization plus representation-matched memory replay of prior regimes. In 12-horizon continual-learning experiments on Traffic-CL, Solar-CL, HAR-CL, and synthetic data, the table compares KI-TSF with conventional and deep-learning baselines on average performance and average forgetting. This is structured *learned temporal/dependency knowledge* retained from prior data regimes, not an external research-corpus knowledge base. It is an architectural analogue only; paper reports no ILSA data. Evidence tier: primary conference paper, but the accessible paper is a short 3-page version and reports only four benchmarks. [KDD-hosted paper PDF](https://kdd2025.kdd.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/paper_5.pdf).

- **Direct ILSA forecasting-adjacent evidence — TIMSS validation study.** Braun, von Davier & Chen (2025) use neural networks to map prior Grade 4 TIMSS student/background information to later Grade 8 country means (2011→2015 and 2015→2019), then regress country-level residuals on selected changes in background-response distributions. Their second stage selects variables using residual correlations at p≤.05, groups them into SES/positive-attitude/negative-attitude blocks, and uses each block's first principal component to adjust the prediction. A second scenario predicts a later Grade 8 cycle from previous Grade 8 data. This is country-level cross-cycle prediction/validation and a useful caution about cohort/context shifts, but it does not use a literature-derived KB or a time-series forecaster; authors note that a leave-one-out strategy would be preferable but computationally costly. The study's purpose is quality assurance and outlier detection, so it cannot be treated as a benchmark establishing general forecasting performance. Evidence tier: primary peer-reviewed ILSA paper. [Full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40536-025-00262-x).

- **Method recommendations (secondary evidence, not proof of ILSA gains).** Meisenbacher et al.'s review of automated forecasting pipelines distinguishes exogenous variables from feature-selection methods and notes that domain-specific exogenous features can encode domain knowledge; it describes filter, wrapper, embedded and hybrid selection, and recommends reducing candidates with a filter then validating with a model/pipeline-specific wrapper. This supports treating corpus-derived predictor evidence as candidate feature priors requiring out-of-sample testing; it does not validate any particular knowledge weight or causal claim. Evidence tier: peer-reviewed review, indirect methodological guidance. [Review DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/widm.1475).

- **Repository implementation inspected (local, not an external literature finding).** `scripts/run_loco_forecasting.py` defines M0 as unweighted Ridge, M1 as Ridge that scales mapped `lag_` features by √(literature weight), and M2 as persistence. `_feat_weight()` maps a lag feature through `_ILSA_TO_CANONICAL` into the fixed corpus-derived weight dictionary; unmapped variables receive 1.0. `outputs/stage5/predictor_weights_v2.csv` contains the predictor canonical name, feature name, evidence-summary columns and normalized/raw weights. Thus M1 tests a *fixed literature-informed coefficient/feature prior* on available lagged covariates. It does not retrieve evidence at prediction time, build an explicit fact/relationship graph used by the forecast, input knowledge-system forecasts as a residual branch, or fine-tune a pretrained time-series model. Under a broad operational definition it is one structured-knowledge-to-model pathway; under the narrower definition of a structured KB being queried/encoded into forecast logic, it is not a full KB integration. The project should name this precisely as “literature-derived feature-weight prior” unless it implements and evaluates an explicit KB representation.

- **Forecasting terminology boundary.** Generic fine-tuning (e.g., LoRA) changes model parameters using task data. It qualifies as KB integration only if the intervention explicitly injects KB facts/relations/rules or corpus-derived priors into that tuning objective or model inputs and the comparator isolates that knowledge contribution. Fine-tuning on historical PISA cycle data alone is data adaptation, not evidence of KB benefit. This is a definitional distinction applied to the project's intervention; the KF-LSTM/KENN papers illustrate explicit KDS fusion mechanisms.

- **Assessment-statistics boundary.** OECD documentation states PISA is not longitudinal, and that precise mean-score/trend interpretation uses comparable scales, multiple cycles, and uncertainty from sampling and plausible values; where comparing changes, trends based on three or more measurements reduce uncertainty relative to one-cycle differences. This matters because a country-cycle aggregate panel contains sparse, uneven waves and sampling/imputation uncertainty; naive model MAE alone omits outcome-estimation uncertainty. [OECD PISA FAQ](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/programmes/pisa/pisa-frequently-asked-questions-faqs.html); [OECD PISA data analysis guidance](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/programmes/pisa/how-to-prepare-and-analyse-the-pisa-database.html).

- **Evidence-strength snapshot:** KF-LSTM and KENN are direct primary evidence that explicit KDS outputs can be fused with time-series neural models in other domains; KI-TSF is primary evidence on dynamic dependency/temporal memory in non-ILSA continual forecasting; Braun et al. are primary ILSA evidence for country-level cross-cycle score validation but not KB integration. The claims in their abstracts (e.g., 20%, 42.2%) are author-reported summary effects across heterogeneous datasets and should not be transferred numerically to ILSA. No independent replication or direct ILSA KB experiment was located in this scan.

### Inferences

- The defensible RQ2 novelty is the **domain transfer gap**: whether evidence-derived predictor priors or richer explicit knowledge can help sparse country-cycle achievement forecasts, tested against matched baseline forecasts. Existing generic forecasting studies establish feasibility of mechanisms, not expected direction or magnitude for ILSA.
- A minimal valid claim for the current M0/M1 experiment is: “We tested whether corpus-derived fixed weights on eligible lagged predictors change held-out forecast accuracy.” If weights are only applied to prior achievement/cross-program lag features, do not claim the full ILSA predictor knowledge base was integrated.
- For a genuine structured-KB experiment, freeze a versioned corpus KB before each forecast origin; record each fact/construct, source studies, study/effect counts, direction, uncertainty, program/domain match, and provenance. Define integration arms separately: (A) current fixed prior weights; (B) KB-derived eligible exogenous/context covariates or explicit relations/rules; (C) optional KDS forecast/residual-fusion arm. Compare each with identical folds, training inputs apart from the intended knowledge intervention, and tuning budget. Do not collapse those mechanisms into one “KB” treatment.
- Evaluation should be expanding-window/rolling-origin by target cycle with country-level pairing, equal weighting across target cycles where appropriate, and leakage-safe construction of KB weights and predictor availability at each origin. Report MAE/RMSE plus paired country-cluster uncertainty intervals and performance versus persistence/linear trend; conduct ablations (no KB, weights-only, added context covariates, rule/graph fusion) and sensitivity to KB coverage/weighting. These are methodological recommendations, not findings of the cited studies.
- RQ2 as phrased asks “to what extent” across programs/domains. An M0/M1 paired score over a small panel may estimate a benchmark-specific observed difference, but not establish a general effect unless the panel has adequate independent target cycles, leakage controls, complete coverage, and uncertainty analysis. Any cross-program transfer must distinguish domain/grade/scale differences and missingness from model benefit.

### Gaps

- No direct primary evidence found for corpus-derived, structured ILSA research knowledge integrated with a forecasting model for future country-level mean scores across PISA/TIMSS/PIRLS/ICCS/ICILS. Search coverage was web-indexed English material plus project-local code; subscription-only databases and non-English sources were not exhaustively searched.
- Search did not locate independent replications of KF-LSTM or KENN, nor an ILSA transfer study of these mechanisms. The author-reported aggregate improvements should therefore be treated as preliminary cross-domain evidence, not expected ILSA effect sizes.
- Need inspect the full model/evaluation artifacts to establish which canonical predictors actually entered each M0/M1 fold, their coverage, whether fixed weights were determined without future-cycle leakage, and whether the newest `rq_evidence_v5` outputs use the intended same-fold comparison. The static weight file and code establish the declared mechanism, not that every weight affects every fitted forecast.
- PISA country means and trend estimates have nontrivial sampling/imputation uncertainty; the current note does not audit whether the project's target series includes official standard errors or plausible-value/replicate-weight uncertainty for every program-cycle. Official PISA guidance explicitly requires appropriate plausible-value and variance handling when recomputing estimates; a forecast study using published country means should retain their reported uncertainty and comparability limitations.
- ILSA cycles are sparse and irregular across programs and domains. Literature found here does not establish that a complex neural or knowledge-fusion model will beat persistence/linear trend under the project's small-number-of-waves regime.
