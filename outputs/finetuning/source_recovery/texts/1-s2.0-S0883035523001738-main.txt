A B S T R A C T  
School belonging is an important positive feeling for students within schools. Guided by the 
ecological system theory (Bronfenbrenner, 1994) and teacher-emotion-student-outcome concep­
tual framework (Frenzel et al., 2021), this study investigates the mechanism underlying teachers’ 
enthusiasm and students’ school belonging with the mediation role of four teaching practices (i.e., 
teacher’s support, teacher-directed instruction, teacher feedback, and adaptive instruction) in a 
Chinese school context. Data from 12,057 Chinese students in PISA 2018 were analyzed using 
multilevel mediation models. Results show that (1) teacher enthusiasm plays an important role in 
predicting students’ school belonging, where the mediating effects of the four teaching practices 
account for 33.14 % of the total effects; (2) teacher feedback is the most influential mediator; (3) 
mediation effect sizes vary to a moderate extent. Finally, discussion and future directions are 
provided.   
1. Introduction 
School belonging refers to students feeling personally accepted, respected, included, and supported by others in the school social 
environment (Goodenow & Grady, 1993). It has been found to positively influence student’s academic performance (Anderman, 
2002), wellbeings (Arslan et al., 2020), resilience (Sanders & Munford, 2016), learning motivation, and classroom engagement (Kiefer 
et al., 2015) while reducing student misbehaviors, such as absence, dropout rates (Korpershoek et al., 2020), and adolescent pregnancy 
(Resnick et al., 1997). Hence, school belonging is attracting the interest of educational researchers and practitioners. 
Understanding the factors that contribute to students’ school belonging is also critical, where the ecological system can provide a 
comprehensive lens (Bronfenbrenner, 1994, see Fig. 1), because school belonging involve not only individual-level characteristics but 
also interaction between individual students and their microsystem (e.g., teachers), mesosystem (e.g., the school’s social culture), 
exosystem (e.g., family-school interaction), macrosystem (e.g., social norms and cultural values), and chronosystem (e.g., change in 
the society; Allen & Kern, 2017; Allen et al., 2018). In a review, Slaten et al. (2016) conclude that there are at least four individual-level 
factors influencing school belonging, that is, ethnicity, gender, mental health, and academic motivation. Within the microsystem, this 
study delves into teacher enthusiasm, a positive emotion and behavior style that teachers expressed during their interaction with 
students (OECD, 2019b) and draws on part of the teacher-emotion-student-outcome conceptual framework, which encompasses three 
paths: (1) direct transmission effects between teacher and student emotions; (2) the mediation effects of teachers’ teaching practices; 
(3) recursive effects from student outcomes on teacher emotions (Frenzel et al., 2021). First, both teacher enthusiasm and students’

school belonging pertain to positive emotions in schools. Teachers are an important source for students to gain support, acceptance, 
inclusion, and respect (e.g., Wallace et al., 2012; Crouch et al., 2014). A student who perceives a higher level of teacher enthusiasm can 
form a stronger sense of school belonging because she/he appraises teachers’ enthusiastic behavior and expression as a sign of 
welcome, support, and inclusion in schools. Students find the school environment more enjoyable if teachers are enthusiastic (Frenzel 
et al., 2009; Klem & Connell, 2004). That is, a direct transmission effect might exist between teacher enthusiasm and students’ school 
belonging. Moreover, teacher enthusiasm plays an important role in the quality of teaching practices (e.g., Kunter, 2013), and there is 
also growing evidence in favor of the importance of teaching practices in cultivating students’ school belonging (Anderman, 2003; 
Allen et al., 2018, 2021; Alesech & Nayar, 2021). In a meta-analysis conducted by Allen et al. (2018), among the ten factors from 
individual-level to meso‑level associated with school belonging, teacher support at the microsystem stands out. Therefore, there can be 
a mediation effects of teaching practices between teacher enthusiasm and students’ school belonging. 
At the macrosystem, Cortina et al. (2017) found that culture is a significant factor influencing school belonging, and east Asian 
students experience a lower sense of school belonging. Previous research on the school belonging of Chinese students has found some 
special patterns, trajectories, and effects on academic achievement and psychological development (e.g., Liu & Lu, 2011; Li & Jiang, 
2018; Zhang et al., 2021). Moreover, as teacher-student interaction plays a critical role in school (van de Pol et al., 2010), it is 
important to investigate how the positive emotion from the teacher side can be associated with the school belonging from the student 
side through various teaching practices. 
However, there is limited research connecting these pieces of information, especially when it comes to the mechanism underlying 
teacher enthusiasm, teaching practices, and students’ school belonging. Hence, following the teacher-emotion-student-outcome 
conceptual framework, this study aims to bridge the gap by examining the direct effect of teacher enthusiasm on school belonging 
and the mediation role of teaching practices between them and shedding light on school belonging mechanism of an under-researched 
community, namely Chinese students, using the Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA) 2018 data. 
2. Literature review 
2.1. School belonging and teacher enthusiasm 
The importance of belonging is rooted in human evolution and personal development (DeWall, 2011). Since school is one of the 
most crucial places where students acquire knowledge and learn to interact with society, school belonging becomes central to students’

Numerous studies have linked school belonging to students’ academic performance and non-academic development. It is found to 
have an important influence on students’ grades and competence (Pittman & Richmond, 2007), academic motivation (Gillen-O’Neel & 
Fuligni, 2013), emotions (Fong Lam et al., 2015), and stress (Abdollahi et al., 2020). School belonging is also positively related to 
numerous psychological variables such as psychological adjustment and functioning (Pittman & Richmond, 2007; Gummadam et al., 
2016), and emotional well-being (Arslan, 2018). Meanwhile, it can decrease many mental illnesses, e.g., emotional distress (Arslan, 
2018) and depressive symptoms (Newman et al., 2007). 
In the classical definition proposed by Goodenow and Grady (1993), a sense of belonging to school involves four components: 
acceptance, respect, inclusion and support in the school environment. At the micro-level of the ecological system, teachers serve as a 
critical source for those components for students (Anderman, 2003; Bowen et al., 1998; Crouch et al., 2014; Slaten et al., 2016; 
Wallace et al., 2012). Teacher enthusiasm, which refers to a lively and motivating teaching style that is reflected in a range of teacher 
behaviors, such as facial expressions and the frequent use of humor, can play an important role in forming an enjoyable and engaging 
learning environment (Keller et al., 2016; Kunter et al., 2013; OECD, 2019b) and increasing students’ school belonging. 
The connection between teacher enthusiasm and students’ school belonging can be conceived from two paths through a direct 
transmission and the mediation effect of teaching practices (Frenzel et al., 2021). First, students can feel more belonged to school 
because they sense the acceptance and support from teachers’ enthusiastic behaviors through their interactions in schools. Research 
has shown teacher enthusiasm can help create a conductive and welcoming atmosphere for students (Keller et al., 2016) and influence 
a series of positive outcomes from student end, including students’ interest, intrinsic motivation, vitality, and study enjoyment 
(Patrick et al., 2000; Kim & Schallert, 2014), which increases the plausibility of teacher enthusiasm contributing to students’ school 
belongings, as it is also a positive emotion in school. Second, enthusiastic teachers tend to exhibit higher occupational well-being 
(Kunter et al., 2011), lower emotional exhaustion (Keller et al., 2016), and thus better teaching practices (Kunter et al., 2013) and 
it has been proven that teaching practices are important predictors of students’ school belonging (Anderman, 2003; Allen et al., 2018, 
2021; Alesech & Nayar, 2021). Therefore, teacher enthusiasm can enhance student’s school belonging through various teaching 
practices. This study concentrates on four teaching practices considered in PISA because of their salient function in students’ learning 
and feeling towards school (OECD, 2019b), which will be detailed in the following section. 
2.2. The mediating role of teaching practices 
Teaching practices cover a wide range of activities typically initiated by teachers during the teaching process. Some teaching 
practices have been shown effective, such as giving students opportunities to respond (Partin et al., 2010), providing academic and 
behavioral feedback for students (Hattie & Timperley, 2007), and teachers’ observed emotional support (Gest & Rodkin, 2011). 
Various teaching behaviors are regarded as the mediators in teacher-emotion-student-outcome conceptual framework (Frenzel et al., 
2021). PISA 2018 includes four teaching practices: teacher support, teacher-directed instruction, perceived teacher feedback, and 
adaptivity of instruction (OECD, 2019a). 
First, teacher support manifests itself by, for example, encouraging students, setting goals and rules, treating students fairly, and 
giving them the opportunity to make their own choices (OECD, 2019b). PISA focuses on the direct support from teachers perceived by 
students. Teacher support has been found to be positively related to student’s academic scores, learning motivation, and self-efficacy 
beliefs (Sakiz et al., 2012; Tennant et al., 2015; Pitzer& Skinner, 2017) and negatively linked to school problems and misbehaviors 
(Tennant et al., 2015). There is no surprise it is also a strong positive predictor of school belonging (Allen et al., 2018; Demanet & Van 
Houtte, 2012), as support constitutes the definition of belonging (Goodenow & Grady, 1993), and teachers represent a major support 
students can receive from schools. Meanwhile, the importance of teacher enthusiasm has also been found in creating a supportive 
environment and producing learning support of quality (Keller et al., 2016; Kunter et al., 2013). This research situates teacher support 
as a mediator between teacher enthusiasm and school belonging. 
Second, teacher-directed instruction refers to students’ exposure to teaching practices that are wholly initiated by teachers for 
structuring student learning (Cairns & Areepattamannil, 2022). It has been found to be positively related to student’s academic 
performance and affective outcomes (Stockard et al., 2018), and helps foster positive teacher-student relationships (e.g., Ghaith, 
2003). School belonging involves a sense of connectedness and identification in school, which includes both cognitive and affective 
components (Hurtado & Carter, 1997). Anderman (2003) found that setting a clear goal to students can improve their sense of 
belonging to school. Research has also illustrated that the association between teacher enthusiasm and various instruction behaviors 
and expression, including verbal and non-verbal languages (Frenzel et al., 2021; Keller et al., 2016). Thus, it will be meaningful to 
investigate how teacher enthusiasm is transmitted into school belonging through the mediator of teacher-directed instruction. 
Third, feedback is a powerful tool in teaching and learning, and effective feedback from teachers comes after effective instruction, 
where correctness, form, timing, and students’ perception matter (Hattie & Timperley, 2007). In PISA, teacher feedback involves 
feedback provided by teachers on students’ performance, strengths, and weaknesses (OECD, 2019b). It is found that students from 
collectivist cultures (e.g., China) prefer indirect and group-based feedback when compared with students from individualistic cultures 
(De Luque & Sommer, 2000). Moreover, teacher feedback has been proven to be related to students’ academic performance (Baliram & 
Ellis, 2019) and teacher-student relationship (Skipper & Douglas, 2015). In a special education setting, teacher feedback is found to 
facilitate students’ acceptance and belonging (Alesech & Nayar, 2021). However, its relationship with school belonging to a more 
general student population remain unclear. In an example made by Frenzel et al. (2021), teachers’ feedback can contain some sorts of 
signals of teachers’ enthusiasm and influence students, e.g., a comment perceived warm and enthusiastic can lead to a positive emotion 
for students. In other words, teachers’ enthusiasm can be expressed in their feedback to students, which in turn enhances their school

Fourth, students come to school with diverse needs, interests, and motivations and have different levels of knowledge, skills, and 
abilities. Adaptivity of instruction refers to how teachers tailor their teaching in accordance with students’ level and learning content 
and has been accepted as the cornerstone of effective teaching and adopted in many teaching frameworks (Danielson, 2011). It can be 
explained by a cycle with three components: (1) student stimulus (e.g., student misconception), (b) teacher reflection and metacog­
nition (e.g., how to adjust teaching practices to address students’ misconception), and (c) teacher action (e.g., questioning and 
encouraging (Gallagher et al., 2022; Parsons et al., 2018). Adaptivity of instruction has been receiving increasing attention recently 
owing to the advancement of artificial intelligence technologies (Holstein et al., 2020), which makes large-scale personalized in­
structions possible. Existing literature found that adaptivity of instruction has a positive influence on student’s academic achievement 
(e.g., literacy, mathematics, science, and social studies) and nonacademic outcomes (e.g., engagement; K¨onig et al., 2020; Parsons 
et al., 2018). Adaptive instruction is a way in which teachers show care and support to students’ learning. On one hand, it requires 
teachers’ intensive engagement (Gallagher et al., 2022), and thus enthusiasm could be an important factor under this engagement 
(Patrick et al., 2000) and promote the quality of instructional strategy (Keller et al., 2016); on the other hand, students might have 
more positive feeling toward school as they get more cares from teachers. Thus, adaptive instruction can be a mediator between 
teacher enthusiasm and school belonging. 
2.3. The current study 
Even though existing studies has been able to link teacher enthusiasm to many positive outcomes for students and better teaching 
practices for teachers at the micro-level of the ecological model (Kim & Schallert, 2014; Keller et al., 2016), there is limited research on 
the mechanism of how teacher enthusiasm contribute to students’ school belonging, especially the role of teaching practices between 
them. This research aims to bridge this gap by investigating the mediation effects of teaching practices using Chinese student data from 
PISA 2018 and guided by the teacher-emotion-student-outcome conceptual framework. As displayed in Fig. 2, the mediation model 
starts with teacher enthusiasm as the independent variable, take the four teaching practices as the mediators, and use students’ 
belonging as the dependent variable. To investigate the mechanism, the current study endeavors to use a multilevel mediation model 
to address three research questions: 
RQ1: How much influence does teachers’ enthusiasm have on students’ school belonging? 
RQ2: How important are different teaching practices in predicting students’ school belonging? 
RQ3: How large are the mediation effects of teaching practices between teacher enthusiasm and students’ school belonging? 
3. Method 
3.1. Participants 
This study draws on the data from PISA 2018, which is led by the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development 
(OECD) and held every three years. Its target population is 15-year-old students worldwide (OECD, 2019a). In PISA 2018, there are 
four cities in China participating, that is, Beijing, Shanghai, Zhejiang, and Jiangsu. 361 Chinese schools took part in PISA 2018. 
However, there was one school with only one student, so it was dropped for further analysis, which brought the number of schools 
down to 360 and sample sizes within a school range from 7 to 35. In total, 12,057 Chinese students (47.89 % girls vs. 52.11 % boys) 
were kept, and the average number of students per school of 34.01.

3.2. Measurements 
3.2.1. School belonging 
School belonging (SCHBLNG) was assessed by three positively-worded items (e.g., I make friends easily at school) and three 
negative ones (e.g., I feel awkward and out of place in my school) on a four-point scale from strongly disagree to strongly agree (OECD, 
2019b). The six-item scale was transformed into a single score through item response theory (IRT) models using weighted likelihood 
estimates (WLE). Advantages of IRT scoring can be found in OECD (2019b) and van der Linden and Hambleton (2013). Note that the 
scaling procedure used OECD countries as references by setting the mean and standard deviation of OECD countries as 0 and 1, 
respectively. A higher score indicates a higher sense of school belonging. In terms of reliability, its Cronbach alpha coefficient reaches 
0.83. 
3.3. Teacher enthusiasm 
Teacher enthusiasm (TEACHENT) was assessed by four items on a four-point scale from strongly disagree to strongly agree. Item stems 
are “It was clear to me that the teacher liked teaching us”; “The enthusiasm of the teacher inspired me”; “It was clear that the teacher 
likes to deal with the topic of the lesson”; and “The teacher showed enjoyment in teaching” (OECD, 2019b). Responses are provided by 
students. In the same vein, IRT was applied to scaling with OECD country’s average score as 0 and standard deviation as 1. The 
Cronbach alpha coefficient is 0.90 for this scale. 
3.4. Teacher support 
The scale of teacher support (TEACHSUP) encompasses four items on a four-point scale from every lesson to never or hardly ever. 
Items include showing interest in students’ learning, giving extra help to students in need, helping students’ learning, and teaching 
until students understand. Responses were reversed before they entered IRT models, so a higher WLE means a higher sense of teacher 
support. Likewise, OECD countries’ mean and standard deviation were set to 0 and 1, respectively. Its Cronbach alpha coefficient 
reaches 0.87. 
3.5. Teacher-directed instruction 
PISA 2018 asked students the frequency of four teachers’ directed instruction (DIRINS), including, setting a clear goal, checking 
students’ understanding, giving a summary of previous lessons at the beginning of a new lesson, and informing learning content. 
Options also range from every lesson to never or hardly ever. The same scaling process as the teacher support scale was applied to this 
scale and its Cronbach alpha coefficient is 0.81. 
3.6. Teacher feedback 
There are three items in PISA 2018 assessing students’ perceived teacher feedback (PERFEED). Item stems are “The teacher gives 
me feedback on my strengths in this subject”, “The teacher tells me in which areas I can still improve”, and “The teacher tells me how I 
can improve my performance.” Teacher-directed instruction scale has the same options as the scale for teacher support and underwent 
the same scaling procedure, where a higher WLE indicates a higher level of teacher-directed instruction. Its Cronbach alpha coefficient 
reaches 0.90. 
3.7. Adaptivity of instruction 
PISA 2018 asked three items about adaptivity of instruction (ADAPTIVITY) on the same four-point scale as the teacher support 
scale. Items include adapting the lesson to the class’ needs and knowledge, providing individual help when needed, and changing 
lesson structure when it is difficult for most students. The scaling procedure is the same as the teacher support scale, and its Cronbach 
alpha coefficient is 0.82. 
3.8. Statistical analysis 
Multilevel linear models (MLMs) were applied to analyze the data using teacher enthusiasm as the independent variable, the four 
teaching practices as mediators, and school belonging as the dependent variable (see Fig. 2). Specifically, 1–1–1 mediation models 
were used, as the measurements occur at the student level (i.e., the first level of this study) and thus coefficients and mediation effects 
vary across schools (i.e., the second level of this study). There are two reasons for using multilevel mediation models: (1) PISA used a 
stratified two-stage sampling technique where schools were sampled first and within-school homogeneity regarding school belonging 
is likely to exist; (2) effect sizes might vary across schools, which can be captured by MLMs. 
Mathematically speaking, the multilevel mediation model comprises three sets of multilevel regressions. 
First, teacher enthusiasm was regressed on school belonging to quantify the total effect (Model 0):

4.2. Multilevel mediation 
Table 3 displays the results of multilevel mediation models. The average total effect of teacher enthusiasm on students’ school 
belonging is estimated to be 0.272 in Model 0, which is significant at the 1 % level, and its standard deviation across schools is 
estimated to be 0.102. That is, teacher enthusiasm has a positive effect on students’ school belonging for most schools but varies to a 
relatively large degree. 
Moderate effects of teacher enthusiasm on the four teaching practices were found with coefficients larger than 0.400 and standard 
deviation ranging from 0.051 to 0.111 in Model 1. Their coefficients are significant at the 1 % level. It implies that enthusiastic teachers 
are likely to demonstrate higher levels of teaching practices. For example, one unit increase in teachers’ enthusiasm is estimated to be 
associated with 0.428 units increase in teacher support. In terms of Model 2, three teaching practices have significant average effects 
on students’ school belonging at the 1 % level, except adaptivity of instruction, although their coefficients are small. Among them, 
teachers’ enthusiasm has the largest coefficient. 
For RQ1 concerning the influence of teachers’ enthusiasm on students’ school belonging, the results show that it is an important 
factor. For RQ2 about the impact of teaching practices on students’ school belonging, teacher support and feedback are the two most 
influential factors in predicting students’ school belonging while the two teaching practices directly related to instruction have less 
influence. 
Concerning RQ3, Table 4 displays the average mediation effects of the four teaching practices across schools. They take up 33.14 % 
of the total effects from teachers’ enthusiasm to students’ school belonging, and teachers’ feedback is the most important mediator. 
That is, teachers with higher enthusiasm tend to give more feedback to students, whereby students feel a stronger sense of belonging to 
their school. 
Fig. 3 shows the distribution of the mediation effects across schools using the Monte Carlo Method (Preacher & Selig, 2010), which 
center around the averages given in Table 4. Their 95 % confidence intervals are all above 0. It implies that there exist positive 
mediation effects of the four teaching practices converting teachers’ enthusiasm to students’ school belonging in most schools, but 
their size varies to a moderate extent. 
5. Discussion 
Teachers are an important source for students to acquire support, inclusion, value, and respect in schools, which constitutes their 
sense of belonging to school. Built on previous research and guided by the Bronfenbrenner’s (1994) ecological model and 
teacher-emotion-student-outcome conceptual framework (Frenzel et al., 2021), this study investigates the mechanism underlying 
teacher enthusiasm and student’s school belonging at the micro-level, which represent two of the most important emotions in school. 
MLMs were applied to Chinese student data in PISA 2018, where four teaching practices (i.e., teacher’s support, teacher-directed 
instruction, teacher feedback, and adaptive instruction) were taken as mediators because of their salient role in student-teacher 
interaction. 
First, the micro level of Bronfenbrenner’s (1994) framework emphasizes patterns of social interaction and activities in a given 
setting has profound influence on human development. The given setting of the participants in this study is schools. A positive school 
environment will lead to the enjoyment of students and teachers and produce desirable outcomes (Kunter et al., 2013; OECD, 2019b). 
Our findings further verify the positive connection between teacher enthusiasm and students’ school belonging, i.e., a direct trans­
mission effect. It implies that students can sense at least parts of support, inclusion, respect, and value from enthusiastic teaching 
behavior in school. As discussed Frenzel et al. (2021), when people are placed in the same context, emotion can be directly transmitted 
between each other, and the direct effect can be explained by the relationship of quality and non-verbal message caused by the positive 
emotion. In our study, teacher enthusiasm can help improve teacher-student relationship and generate some welcoming, supportive, 
and inclusive messages to students, which make them feel closer to schools. 
Moreover, this study also reaffirms the association between teaching practices and students school belonging (Allen et al., 2018; 
Allen et al., 2021) and finds that teacher support and feedback are more influential than the other two practices that are directly related 
to instruction, which suggests that the emotional connection between students and teachers can be formed even more strongly through

teachers and students beyond instruction (e.g., Hagenauer et al., 2015). It is also interesting to note that teacher support is often 
researched in the field of school belonging (Allen et al., 2018) probably because there is a support aspect in the definition of school

feedback items in PISA, a major difference is that teacher feedback focuses on acknowledging students’ strengths and encouraging 
students to improve – e.g., the teacher gives me feedback on my strengths in this subject – and teacher support concerns more on the help 
that teacher provide – e.g., giving extra help to students in need (OECD, 2019b). Therefore, conceptually, teachers’ feedback is more 
aligned with the positive affection of school belonging. 
Third, positive mediation effects of the four teaching practices between teacher enthusiasm exist for most schools, their sizes vary to 
a moderate extent in China, which further justifies the use of multilevel mediation models. More importantly, our findings support the 
mediation role of teaching practices in the teacher-emotion-student-outcome conceptual framework (Frenzel et al., 2021) and based on 
the results, we can argue that in the relationship between teacher enthusiasm and students’ school belonging, teachers’ feedback and 
support are important mediators. Thus, in promoting school belonging, teachers can consider including more enthusiastic behaviors 
and words when they provide feedback and support for students. 
Finally, this study underscores the connection between teacher enthusiasm and students’ sense of school belonging. It is note­
worthy to explore strategies for enhancing teacher enthusiasm as a proactive approach to improving students’ school connection. 
Notably, factors such as positive affect, self-efficacy, and job satisfaction have been identified as elements contributing to heightened 
teacher enthusiasm (Buri´c & Moe, 2020). Achieving this requires concerted efforts from schools, governments, and society. For a more 
in-depth understanding, interested readers are encouraged to consult pertinent studies, including those by Buri´c and Moe (2020) and 
Keller et al. (2016).

5.1. Limitations and future directions 
PISA 2018 focuses on reading literacy and thus its background questionnaires center around students’ experiences in language 
classes, so is the case for the teaching practices in this study. Future studies can further collect teaching practices in other classes to 
address this limitation. 
Moreover, listening to teachers’ ideas is also another direction for future study. Although PISA 2018 has a teacher questionnaire, it 
was not administered in China. If teachers’ responses are collected in the future, it is always a good idea to include them in the models. 
Third, even though PISA is the largest publicly available dataset for Chinese students, it still suffers from some representation 
limitation as only four regions in China participated. This study tried its best to extract evidence from PISA dataset and contributed to 
the field, but it is still valuable to collect data from a more representative Chinese students sample though it requires tremendous 
efforts. 
Lastly, PISA is a cross-sectional survey at the student level, which limits the possibility of causal inference and makes it unable to 
address the recursive effect in teacher-emotion-student-outcome conceptual framework (Frenzel et al., 2021) from the statistics point 
of view. Future research can consider longitudinal data to address these issues. 
6. Conclusions 
In conclusion, teacher enthusiasm is a strong predictor of students’ school belonging, where teacher support, direct instruction, 
feedback, and instruction adaptivity serve as mediators and account for 33.14 % of the total effects, and the mediation effect sizes vary 
across schools. The findings imply that to help student to develop a higher sense of belonging, schools can consider improve teachers’ 
enthusiasm by increasing their job satisfaction, etc., and teachers should demonstrate more enthusiastic behaviors and expressions 
when they provide feedback and support to students.
