Abstract
Psychoemotional well-being factors have been recognized to have a significant
impact on students’ reading literacy. However, identifying which key psychoemotional well-being factors most significantly influence students’ reading performance
is still not fully explored. This research examines the psychoemotional well-being
factors that distinguish the reading literacy of high-level students from low-level
ones using machine learning methods in four regions of China, including Beijing,
Shanghai, Jiangsu, and Zhejiang. In total, 3497 samples were drawn from the public database of the PISA 2018, including 2935 high-level students (with proficiency
level at or above Level 5) and 562 low-achieving students (at Level 2 or below). By
applying Recursive Feature Elimination with Cross-Validation feature selection and
Support Vector Machine classifiers approach, this study successfully identifies 15
key factors (e.g., students’ socioeconomic status and learning goals) from the total
25 psychoemotional well-being factors that synergistically distinguish high-level
students from low-level students with a high accuracy score (0.905). Further, using
the Shapley Additive exPlanations method, the feature importance of the features
set is shown, and 10 factors relevant to the psychoemotional well-being show the
feature importance of reading literacy of high-level students. This study provides
important insights into the factors of psychoemotional well-being that influence students’ reading literacy development.
Keywords Students’ reading literacy · Psychoemotional well-being · Machine
learning · Shapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) · PISA 2018

Although previous studies have identified some psychoemotional well-being factors affecting students’ reading literacy, certain limitations do exist. The utilization
of traditional statistical methods in previous research imposes specific requirements
on the sample. For example, binary logistic regression requires the fulfillment of
certain assumptions (Chapman, 2018), including the absence of strong correlations
among explanatory variables and the existence of a clear linear relationship between
these variables and the response variable. Consequently, there is a need to explore
alternative research methodologies to overcome inherent issues associated with traditional statistical approaches, i.e., small survey sample sizes and a limited number
of psychoemotional factors.
The Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA) evaluates the educational performance of students in reading through questionnaires distributed to
students, parents, school principals, and teachers (OECD, 2018). Based on the conceptual framework of PISA, our research framework integrates insights from PISA’s
psychological dimension and school climate in combination with findings from previous studies examining factors related to psychoemotional well-being.

example, research has been conducted by using artificial intelligence techniques
to assess variations in learning styles across different age groups of students (Wei

factors and assessing their impact on differentiating high-level from low-level reading literacy among students employing ML methods in Beijing, Shanghai, Jiangsu,
and Zhejiang (China) (hereafter “B-S-J-Z [China])”.

In this study, we utilized a combination of Recursive Feature Elimination with CrossValidation (RFE-CV) feature selection and Support Vector Machine (SVM) classifiers
based on machine learning to identify optimal psychoemotional well-being factors. These
factors are key in differentiating students with high reading literacy from those with low

Moving into the Modeling phase, we employed the SVM-RFE-CV method, diverging

This study is motivated to explore the essential psychoemotional well-being factors on students’ reading literacy, with a specific focus on distinguishing the literacy
levels between high-achieving and low-achieving students by utilizing ML techniques in B-S-J-Z (China).
To achieve this, the research will address three key research questions:
(1) What ML algorithm is best for identifying the difference in reading literacy
levels among students in B-S-J-Z (China)?
(2) Can psychoemotional well-being factors accurately differentiate secondary
school students with high-level reading literacy from those with low-level reading literacy?
(3) Which combination of psychoemotional well-being factors has the most significant impact on high-level reading literacy among students?

2 Literature review
2.1 Relationship between psychoemotional well‑being and reading literacy
Psychological well-being refers to the existence of positive emotions, satisfaction with life, and absence of negative emotions (Mayer & Salovey, 1993; Pimple,
2023). There is a strong correlation and reciprocal influence between the concepts
of psychological well-being and emotional state (Telef & Furlong, 2017; Wu, 2023).
Previous studies often use the term “psychoemotional” to emphasize the interplay
between psychological well-being and emotional states (Stepanyan et al., 2010).
The correlation between students’ psychoemotional well-being and their academic
performance has been a significant research subject in the field of education. There
have been studies exploring the psychoemotional factors influencing students’ mathematical literacy (Molina-Muñoz et al., 2023).
Reading is a multifaceted activity that involves various factors in cognition, ecology, and psychology (Liu et al., 2023). According to PISA 2018, reading literacy is
defined as “understanding, using, evaluating, reflecting on and engaging with texts

to achieve one’s goals, to develop one’s knowledge and potential, and to participate
in society” (OECD, 2019a).
There have been numerous studies examining the relationship between students’
psychoemotional well-being and reading literacy. It has been found that participants
experienced short-term enhancement in their psychological well-being following
their involvement in a guided reading circle (Pettersson, 2018). Another study was
carried out to examine the psychological and educational research elements related
to reading culture (Pečjak, 2021). The literature on various educational stages has
extensively documented the correlation between students’ psychological and emotional states and reading performance (Xiao & Hew, 2022). These results indicate
that the psychoemotional well-being of students is crucial for their reading literacy.
Consequently, it is essential to identify the influences of psychoemotional well-being
factors on students’ high-level reading literacy.
2.2 Psychoemotional well‑being factors
Psychological well-being and emotional states are integral components of well-being
(Lippman et al., 2009), including three main dimensions: self, school environment,
and out-of-school environment (OECD, 2019c). The overlap between psychological
well-being and certain aspects of well-being is evident in psychological functioning
(Huebner, 1991). Other studies have argued that the components of students’ mental-emotional health can be categorized as personal and relational (Molina-Muñoz
et al., 2023). Another study has shown that adolescents’ perceptions of their school
performance and their competency are correlated with higher perceived psychological well-being (Suldo et al., 2014).
According to the theory of the dynamic model of educational effectiveness
(Creemers & Kyriakides, 2007), the psychoemotional well-being factors are influenced by various levels: the student, family, classroom, and school. The structure
of psychoemotional well-being factors in PISA 2018 primarily consists of three
aspects: psychological well-being, emotional states, and school climate. According
to research, gender and socioeconomic status have been found to have an impact on
students’ psychological well-being (Alivernini et al., 2020). Thus, these two factors
became integral components in evaluating student backgrounds. The influence of the
psychoemotional factors on students’ reading literacy was investigated by conducting a thorough review of the relevant literature.
2.2.1 Psychological well‑being
Psychological well-being can be represented by multiple measures (Ryff, 1995). It
encompasses students’ subjective evaluations and perspectives on various aspects
of their lives, including their level of engagement in school, their aspirations, and
objectives for the future (OECD, 2019b). Psychological functioning includes a range
of factors, such as competence, autonomy, meaning/purpose, optimism, self-acceptance, positive relations with others, personal growth, purpose in life, environmental mastery, and autonomy (OECD, 2013; Ryff, 1995). According to the findings

of PISA 2018, the psychological dimension is a constituent of student well-being,
encompassing students’ life satisfaction and meaning in life, students’ feelings,
self-efficacy, and fear of failure (OECD, 2019b). There are additional studies that

2.2.2 School climate
Research findings indicated a significant correlation between the school climate
and the psychoemotional well-being of individuals (Aldridge et al., 2018; Maxwell
et al., 2017; Rustamov et al., 2023). Research has shown that a positive school climate predicts academic achievement (Demirtas-Zorbaz et al., 2021).
The primary features of school climate are safety, interpersonal relationships,
learning, and teaching, as well as environmental structure (Salle et al., 2015). The
PISA 2018 survey questionnaires explore nine specific aspects within three major
domains: student disruptive behavior (bullying, disciplinary climate, and student
truancy and lateness), teaching and learning (teacher enthusiasm, teachers’ support,
and teaching practices, teacher behavior, and student learning), and school community (student cooperation and competition, sense of belonging at school, parental
involvement in school activities) (OECD, 2019b).
The study revealed that students’ positive psychological well-being was associated with support from family, friends, and significant others (Khan & Husain, 2010).
We can obtain four variables, namely Student Cooperation, Teacher support, Teacher
feedback, and Parents’ emotional support from PISA 2018. Experiencing bullying
adversely affects the mental well-being of students. Existing literature indicates a clear
link between a sense of belonging and psychological well-being (Seijo et al., 2023).
Studies have demonstrated a significant relationship between the value of school and
student performance on the PISA assessments (Yalcin & Tavsancil, 2014).
2.2.3 Emotional states
According to Cognitive Appraisal Theory, emotional states are an individual’s
response to situations that they perceive as either harmful or beneficial (Lazarus,
1970). Emotions can typically be categorized as either positive or negative. Positive emotions exhibit positive psychological traits (Kang et al., 2021), in contrast to
negative emotions which are characterized by negative psychological characteristics
(Fredrickson et al., 2003). The direct impact of emotional intelligence on psychological well-being has been reported to be statistically significant (Kamboj & Garg,
2021). Several studies have examined factors associated with emotional states, such
as Mastery motivation, Motivation to master tasks, Learning goals, Attitudes toward

2.3 Conceptual framework of the study
In this section, we introduce the conceptual framework that serves as the foundation of our study. The framework not only establishes a theoretical base but also
guides the collection of research data. According to the theory of the dynamic
model of educational effectiveness, the evaluative framework of psychoemotional well-being was developed based on PISA 2018. This conceptual framework
includes psychological well-being, emotional states, school climate, and student
backgrounds (Fig. 1). First, psychological well-being comprises Students’ life satisfaction, Meaning in life, Students’ positive feelings, Students’ self-efficacy, and
Fear of failure. Second, school climate encompasses Student disruptive behavior,
Teaching and learning, and School community. Third, emotional states include
Motivation to master tasks, learning goals, Attitudes towards competition, and
Parents’ emotional support. Finally, Student backgrounds include socioeconomic
status (ESCS) and gender. Utilizing the conceptual framework, a variety of psychological well-being factors associated with reading literacy have been discovered. This research employs ML methods to investigate the factors influencing the
psychoemotional well-being that differentiate students with high-level and lowlevel reading literacy across four regions in B-S-J-Z (China).

3 Materials and methods
3.1 Datasets
The Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA), conducted by the
Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD), is a recurring
global assessment that evaluates the educational performance of 15-year-old students in reading, mathematics, and science (OECD, 2018). These assessments take
place every three years.
The sample was extracted from the latest public dataset of the PISA 2018 (https://​
webfs.​oecd.​org/​pisa2​018/​SPSS_​STU_​QQQ.​zip ). The B-S-J-Z (China) sample was
chosen for two main reasons. Firstly, these students demonstrated outstanding performance in reading literacy and attained the highest average scores (555) among
students from 79 countries and economies participating in PISA 2018 (OECD,
2019a). This aspect makes it a suitable sample for investigating psychoemotional
well-being factors associated with reading literacy. Secondly, the sample includes
a higher proportion of high-level students, which enhances its representativeness.
By incorporating a significant number of these students, the sample more accurately
reflects the states of students in B-S-J-Z (China), allowing for a comprehensive analysis of the psychoemotional well-being factors associated with reading literacy.
According to the PISA 2018 dataset, 10 plausible values were used to represent student reading literacy. “There are no significant differences between using
one plausible value or five plausible values, due to the large number of the samples” (OECD, 2009). Therefore, the first plausible value 1 in reading was randomly
selected to represent each student’s reading literacy. In the PISA 2018 assessment,
the cut-off score for performance below level 2 was 407.47 in reading, while the
cut-off score for performance at level 5 and above was 625.61 in reading (Pons &
Houldsworth, 2020). To enhance the clarity of differentiation, students at Level 5
and above are categorized as high-level, while those at Level 2 are classified as lowlevel (Chen et al., 2019; Lezhnina & Kismihók, 2022). This categorization aids in
the interpretation and comparison of study findings. A total of 12058 students from
B-S-J-Z (China) participated in the PISA 2018 test. Following the exclusion of missing values or invalid data, 3497 students (2935 high-level students and 562 low-level
students) were chosen as the sample to identify psychoemotional well-being factors
of students’ reading literacy.

3.3 Analysis procedure
In this study, reading scores were categorized using dummy variables as dependent
variables: scores at or above 625.61 were assigned ‘1’ to high-level reading literacy,
and scores below 407.47 were assigned ‘0’ to low-level reading literacy. Missing
values were addressed using Stata 17.0, employing the ‘drop if’ command with the
‘missing ()’ function to identify and eliminate rows with missing data.
According to the conceptual framework of our study, psychoemotional wellbeing factors were derived from the PISA dataset, serving as independent variables. These variables originate from numerical data modeled by PISA officials.
For example, ‘Teacher Feedback’ has a mean of 0.39, a standard deviation of
1.01, and its values range from − 1.64 to 2.02. A higher value indicates more
frequent teacher feedback. To ascertain the validity of our findings, we selected
pertinent items from the PISA 2018 student and school questionnaires. This
selection was followed by a comprehensive series of statistical analyses including reliability testing and confirmatory factor analysis. For example, the construct
of ‘Teacher Feedback’ was operationalized via three items from the PISA 2018
survey: How often the teacher gives me feedback on my strengths in this subject?
How often the teacher tells me in which areas I can still improve? How often the
teacher tells me how I can improve my performance? Confirmatory factor analysis substantiated the construct’s one-dimensionality, with substantial item loadings on a singular factor: 0.689, 0.870, and 0.883, correspondingly. The scale’s
internal consistency was validated with a standardized Cronbach’s alpha of 0.853,
indicating excellent reliability. The construct also demonstrated satisfactory
convergent validity, as evidenced by a composite reliability (CR) of 0.858 and

The process of data analysis is divided into three phases: Training, Modeling,

Training The initial phase of data analysis involved performing data preprocessing,

(LR), Perceptron (PER), Classification and Regression Tree (CART), Random Forest (RF), Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM), and Support Vector Machines (SVM),
are employed for model training.
Modeling K-fold cross-validation is the main method used for identifying the fitting hyper-parameters to ensure the accuracy of the SVM model (Li et al., 2013).
The study using 5-fold cross-validation proved to be effective in predicting outcomes (Sejuti & Islam, 2023). To find out the suitable cross-validation multiplicity, this study looped through the training dataset ranging from 5 folds to 15 folds
and recorded the accuracy accordingly. The best accuracy value is the most suitable
number of folds. To examine the ability to generalize a model to an independent
dataset, the SVM-recursive feature elimination cross-validation (SVM-RFE-CV)
can be used to find the best hyper-parameters (Chen et al., 2019). In the study, the
RFE-CV feature selection and SVM classifiers approach to determine the optimal
accuracy and corresponding number of features based on the folding number. Subsequently, the optimal feature set was generated as the final output.

In this study, we applied various machine learning algorithms, including LR, PER,
CART, RF, GBM, and SVM, to the Psychoemotional well-being dataset to assess
their predictive abilities and computational efficiency. This dataset comprises 3497
samples, each with 25 features. The analysis of computational complexity revealed

varying theoretical complexities that were reflected in the actual training times. The
ensemble learning techniques, Random Forest and Gradient Boosting, exhibited
higher theoretical complexity (Friedman, 2001), which was evident in their longer
training times of 42.639 and 53.322 s, respectively. In contrast, Logistic Regression
and Perceptron, as linear models, demonstrated faster training speeds due to their

Compared to other models, the SVM algorithm emerged as the optimal choice
for its superior accuracy, achieving 0.90 on the training set and 0.871 on the test
set, along with its suitable computational complexity. According to recent findings,
SVM algorithm classifiers demonstrate excellent recognition accuracy compared
to other classification methods (Hu et al., 2022). The basic idea of the SVM algorithm is to plot data in an n-dimensional space with n number of features and apply
a hyperplane to distinguish the classes that are used for classification and regression
(Kalaiselvi & Thirumurthi, 2020). SVM algorithm constructs a statistical model of
data to explore the relationships between training data and target data, aiming to
identify the relations between variables (Chen et al., 2019). The high accuracy suggests that the selected psychoemotional well-being features effectively differentiate between high and low students’ reading literacy (Fig. 4). As a result, it can be
inferred that the SVM model demonstrates excellent predictive performance for this
dataset. Therefore, we will employ the SVM as the primary predictive model for
further analysis.

4.2 Optimal sets
According to Fig. 5, the cross-validation of the dataset was conducted using a
range of folds, from 5 to 15. The SVM model demonstrated the highest accuracy
of 0.891 during cross-validation when utilizing either 10 folds or 13 folds. Previous studies have commonly used 10-fold cross-validation to enhance the performance of ML methods (Malakouti et al., 2023). Therefore, the optimal set for
this dataset can be determined through either 10-fold or 13-fold cross-validation.
Recursive feature elimination (RFE) selects features recursively by considering smaller and smaller sets of features. Previous studies were done by identifying key features of resilient students in digital reading from 105 contextual features (Zheng et al., 2023). RFECV improves RFE within a cross-validation loop
to identify the optimal number of features. Considering the specific features of
our dataset, the utilization of RFECV is particularly advantageous for our study.
To evaluate the significance of the optimal psychoemotional well-being factors in distinguishing between high and low levels of students’ reading literacy,
RFECV was used to automatically select the appropriate number of features that
yielded the highest average score. The accuracy score reached its peak at 0.905
when the number of features was set to 15 in Fig. 6, along with the output of the
optimal set. Therefore, these 15 features represent the optimal set with the highest accuracy score while minimizing the number of features required.
The 15 features of psychoemotional well-being contributed most to the categorization task for the high-level and low-level of students’ reading literacy.

MASTGOAL, DISCLIMA, STIMREAD, EMOSUPS, ATTLNACT, COMPETE,
BELONG, female, and TEACHSUP can be interpreted as the influencing factors for
the high scoring arrays. Conversely, high values in STULS, EUDMO, DIRINS, and
SBHL can be interpreted as the influencing factors for the low scoring arrays.

5 Discussion
This study investigates the psychoemotional well-being factors that distinguish
between students with high-level and low-level reading literacy employing ML algorithms across B-S-J-Z (China). Through analysis, our study has successfully determined 15 significant factors that have a strong correlation with the psychoemotional
well-being of students. The findings enrich the theory of dynamic models of educational effectiveness from the perspective of psychoemotional well-being.

5.1 Student backgrounds
Factors influencing students’ reading literacy are related to students’ demographic
characteristics, including gender (gender) and socioeconomic status (ESCS).
Gender is a significant factor in students’ reading performance, with females having a particularly positive impact on the performance of high-level students. This phenomenon was also found in previous studies (Torppa et al., 2018). It is important to
emphasize that ESCS stands out as the most influential factor of high-level students
among the selected 15 features. In the PISA 2018, ESCS consisted of three variables:
parents’ highest level of education, highest occupational status, and home possessions
(OECD, 2019b). Socioeconomic and sociocultural factors have the greatest impact on
performance in reading, mathematics, and science literacy (Pehlivan & Aslan, 2023).
The study revealed a stronger relationship between ESCS and reading achievement as
opposed to science and math achievement (Liu et al., 2020).
5.2 School climate
Factors influencing students’ reading literacy at school climate include Teacher
Support (TEACHSUP), Teacher-directed Instruction (DIRINS), Teacher Feedback

(PERFEED), Teachers’ Stimulation of Reading Engagement (STIMREAD), sense
of belonging (BELONG), Value of school (ATTLNACT), Disciplinary Climate
(DISCLIMA), and student behavior hindering learning (SBHL).
Teachers’ instructional behaviors play an important role in enhancing students’ academic performance (Hanushek, 2011). The key factors of teacher
instructional behaviors include teacher support, teacher-directed instruction,
teacher feedback, and teachers’ stimulation of reading engagement. Among
these factors, both teacher support and teachers’ stimulation of reading engagement have an influential impact on high-level students. Existing evidence suggests a strong link between teacher support and academic achievement (Roderick & Engel, 2001).
The study found a positive association between teachers’ stimulation of reading
engagement and students’ reading literacy. In contrast to the findings of the previous
study, which indicated that teacher-directed learning environments were negatively
predictive of reading achievement (Meng et al., 2023).
Both the sense of belonging and the value of school have a positive effect
on reading literacy for high-level students. Previous studies have demonstrated
that sense of belonging plays a crucial role in enhancing academic performance
(Roeser et al., 1996). Both Competitive learning environment and achievement
goals positively predict reading achievement (Meng et al., 2023). Schools are
typically evaluated by looking at aggregated academic results or easily countable measures related to academic results (Mishra & Close, 2020). There is a
direct correlation between the academic performance of students and the value
of the school.
Disciplinary climate positively affects high-level reading students. The study
found that school climate affects achievement (Rizzotto & França, 2022). In contrast, student behavior hindering learning includes behaviors such as skipping
school, skipping classes, and being late to school, which have a negative impact on
academic performance.
It’s worth noting that Student Behavior Hindering Learning and Teacherdirected Instruction are factors affecting the reading performance of low-scoring students. The category of Student Behavior Hindering Learning involves
distractions, negative classroom participation, and student behavioral issues
(Blank & Shavit, 2016). Distractions lead to difficulties in understanding reading materials, affecting cognitive development and reading comprehension
skills (Rance et al., 2023). Research has found that classroom participation and
behavioral problems could make an impact on students’ reading abilities (Tang
& Dai, 2021), potentially leading to a loss of interest in reading as well as a
loss of reading habits.
Teacher-directed instruction can enhance students’ academic performance in
some cases (Werang et al., 2022), but it might also restrict students’ autonomy and
creativity. For instance, studies have shown that teacher-directed learning can create
fear of learning and teachers among students, which is detrimental to their motivation to learn (Wang & Wen, 2023). It might restrict students’ autonomy and creativity, thus affecting their reading literacy.

5.3 Psychological well‑being
Factors influencing students’ reading literacy at psychological well-being include
students’ life satisfaction (STULS) and meaning in life (EUDMO).
The variables of students’ life satisfaction and meaning in life are important features that impact students’ performance. However, they have a positive effect on
predicting low-level students. This observation aligns with findings from previous research indicating a modest yet statistically significant negative relationship
between mathematics and students’ perception of meaning in life (Memisevic &
Biscevic, 2022).
According to the PISA 2018 results, a non-linear connection exists between
students’ life satisfaction, meaning in life, and reading performance. The data
indicates that students with high life satisfaction and meaning in life show a
tendency to have worse reading performance than the average students (OECD,
2019b). The research suggests that while extremely high levels of life satisfaction are essential for developing personal social relationships, moderate
satisfaction is often more beneficial for academic success (Antaramian, 2017).
The Accumulated Local Effects (ALE) method reveals how slight variations in
predictor variables can influence a model’s predictions across the entire distribution of feature values (Apley & Zhu, 2020). This study employs ALE to
delve into the correlation trends between students’ life satisfaction, meaning in
life, and reading performance, with the analytical findings presented in a supplementary document. The analysis shows that when specific thresholds of life
satisfaction and meaning in life are reached, higher levels of these aspects are
related to a tendency towards lower reading literacy.
Students who feel satisfied and happy in life may be more engaged in extracurricular social and hobby-related activities. Although these activities provide
opportunities for socializing, building self-confidence, and developing socioemotional learning skills (Philippe et al., 2023), it is important to consider the
impact of these activities on academic performance. Prioritizing these activities
excessively can hinder students’ learning (Nyorere et al., 2022). This is because
it can divert their attention from academics (Pertegal & Oliva, 2017), affecting their reading literacy to some extent. The research indicates that participation in extracurricular activities can increase students’ life satisfaction, but
it may also lead to a decline in academic performance. Moreover, while life
satisfaction and meaning in life positively impact students’ overall well-being,
it is crucial to understand the balance between extracurricular engagement and
academic focus. The study suggests that moderate engagement in extracurricular activities, when well-aligned with academic goals, can foster a conducive
learning environment, thereby enhancing reading literacy (Coulangeon, 2018).
Therefore, it is crucial for students to maintain a moderate level of life satisfaction and meaning in life to enhance their reading literacy.

5.4 Emotional states
Factors influencing students’ reading literacy at the student level include learning
goals (MASTGOAL), attitudes towards competition (COMPETE), and parents’
emotional support (EMOSUPS).
Learning goals, attitudes towards competition, and parents’ emotional support
positively impact the reading literacy of high-level students. Learning goals have
been determined to exert a considerable influence on academic achievement (Senko,
2019). Competitive learning environments and achievement goals positively predict
reading achievement (Meng et al., 2023). The research suggested that family support
had a positive influence on science achievement (Atasoy et al., 2022).

6 Conclusion and implications
This study explored the utilization of ML techniques to determine how psychoemotional well-being factors effectively differentiate students with high-level and
low-level reading literacy in B-S-J-Z (China). The results identified 15 important
features to differentiate students with high and low levels of reading literacy. Ten
of these features comprised the most influential set of features affecting students’
high level of reading literacy. The discovery of this optimal feature set highlights
the importance of these factors in determining the reading literacy of high-level students. This research provides valuable perspectives on the psychoemotional wellbeing factors that influence the development of students’ reading literacy.
Our study offers innovations and contributions to students’ reading literacy across
three key aspects. Firstly, this study focused on psychoemotional well-being factors, offering a fresh perspective compared to previous studies that mainly considered contextual factors (Xiao & Hu, 2019). Secondly, our study adopts a distinct
approach from traditional statistical analysis (Molina-Muñoz et al., 2023) by utilizing machine learning algorithms. This method adeptly addresses multicollinearity and elucidates intricate interactions among variables in high-dimensional data
(Alivernini & Manganelli, 2015). After evaluating various ML classification algorithms, we find that SVM algorithms are the optimal algorithm for constructing the
model for the dataset. Researchers usually choose either 5-fold or 10-fold cross-validation based on their expertise in previous studies. However, we utilized the RFECV feature selection and SVM classifiers approach, incorporating a broader set of
cross-validation folds ranging from 5 to 15, to identify the most accurate feature
set. Thirdly, the SHAP technique was employed to interpret the outputs of the SVM
model, providing a more comprehensive understanding of its workings. Fourthly,
our study specifically focuses on B-S-J-Z (China) used as our sample to explore the
psychoemotional well-being factors and their impact on Chinese students’ reading
literacy.

In terms of practical implications, teachers’ teaching behavior plays a crucial
role in enhancing students’ learning (Hanushek, 2011). Teachers should offer
teaching assistance to students, thereby enabling them to perceive the teachers’
active engagement in the learning process (Klem & Connell, 2004). In addition,
it is crucial for teachers to offer teaching feedback and foster student involvement in reading. This refers to the information that teachers provide regarding
students’ performance and understanding (Hattie & Timperley, 2007). Teachers
should take into account not only the gender but also the economic, social, and
cultural backgrounds of their students. To cater to the multiple needs and capabilities of the students, teachers can utilize differentiated instruction to meet
the diverse requirements (Reis et al., 2011).
To reduce disruptive behavior that hinders students’ learning, school administrators should reinforce a disciplinary climate and foster a strong sense of
belonging. To enhance their academic achievements, it is essential for students
to establish clear learning goals (Litvinova, 2022). Moreover, fostering a competitive learning attitude among students can serve as a motivation for them
to pursue academic excellence (Baumann & Harvey, 2021). In addition, the
involvement of parents plays a crucial role in ensuring their child’s emotional
well-being. They can provide emotional support by actively engaging in their
child’s education journey (Liu et al., 2022).

7 Limitations
This study has several limitations. Firstly, the sample size of low-achieving students in B-S-J-Z (China) was limited, which could potentially impact the generalizability of the research findings. To enhance the representativeness, future
studies should consider including a larger number of countries and regions to
minimize the disparities between high-level and low-level student groups. Secondly, the 25 factors associated with psychological and emotional well-being
were exclusively derived from surveys conducted among students, schools,
and families. It is recognized that these factors may not provide a comprehensive understanding. Therefore, future research will endeavor to incorporate
additional factors to thoroughly explore the impact of psychoemotional wellbeing on students’ reading literacy. Thirdly, the RFE-CV feature selection and
SVM classifiers approach offers significant advantages in identifying relevant
features. SVM-RFE-CV progressively removes features to identify the optimal feature set, a process that necessitates multiple runs of the SVM model,
thereby substantially increasing the overall computational burden (Ding &
Wilkins, 2006). Furthermore, this complexity intensifies when applied to largescale datasets and high-dimensional data, leading to a marked increase in the
required computational time and resources.
