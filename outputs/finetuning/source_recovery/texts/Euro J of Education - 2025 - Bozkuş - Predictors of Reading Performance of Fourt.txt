ABSTRACT

This study aimed to employ machine learning techniques to uncover the pivotal determinants influencing the reading proficiency of fourth-­grade students across 65 regions, as participants in the PIRLS 2021 assessment. The primary objective was to discern and assess key factors at the student, family and school levels that predict high and low reading performance among these
students. Utilising a machine learning approach, this research analysed data from 204,176 fourth-­grade students encompassing
122 independent variables. The Support Vector Machine (SVM) algorithm was employed to effectively differentiate between
students with high and low reading performance based on the identification of 16 crucial contextual factors. The results revealed
that the most influential factors predominantly resided at the family level, encompassing socioeconomic variables. These factors
pertained to the provision of personalised study environments, facilitated through access to an internet connection, individual
study desks, dedicated study rooms, an assortment of books and personal smartphones. At the student level, significant factors
included reading motivation, gender and age. Meanwhile, school-­level determinants encompassed aspects such as ineffective
classroom regulations, absenteeism rates, the presence of libraries and the availability of digital learning resources.

1   |   Introduction
A broad inquiry that examines both individual and contextual
factors is required to fully understand the elements that affect
fourth-­graders' reading ability. This research aims to investigate the many potential effects of environmental elements on
fourth-­grade readers' reading abilities. There has been a lot
of research on the individual achievements of students, but
the literature on contextual elements that can separate high-performing readers from those who perform poorly (Becker
and Nekrasova-­Beker 2018; Begeny et al. 2011; Chen et al. 2022;
Fang et al. 2022; Hall 2012; Hu et al. 2022, Hu et al. 2023;
Rakhlin et al. 2023; Stole et al. 2020) is in development. The various circumstances surrounding a child's learning environment
are referred to as contextual factors, including things like socioeconomic background, factors that limit instruction and school
resources (Dong and Hu 2019). The development of comprehensive educational policies and targeted intervention measures is

hampered by the lack of a clear understanding of these relationships (Swain-­Bradway et al. 2015).
Furthermore, to effectively allocate resources and interventions,
it is imperative to know which contextual factors have the greatest impact on reading performance, and identifying these factors is not sufficient. The relative importance of each of these
factors in the influence of reading ability is still mostly understood (Lee and Shute 2010). It is important that more research
is done in this area, as unravelling the relative impact of these
factors could considerably influence educational techniques and
policies.
Essentially, the goal of this research is to fill these gaps in our
knowledge of reading performance by investigating the possibility of differentiating high and low performers based on contextual
factors and evaluating the relative importance of these factors. The
lack of information in these areas calls for immediate attention,

Regular school attendance is essential for academic success,

reading. Each instance of absenteeism equals a lost chance to
learn new vocabulary, practice reading and engage in text-­based
critical thinking.

more extensive access to books and diverse reading materials. This exposure to various genres, styles and themes broadens their literary horizons and strengthens their reading skills

other learning spaces, including the availability of reading corners, matters greatly. Students are more inclined to read in well-lit, comfortable and welcoming environments. It is clear that
reading volume plays a role in reading development (Allington
and McGill-­Franzen 2021). Librarians in schools should play the

role of literacy educators and positively impact students' literacy

3   |   Method
This research used machine learning to reveal the key factors
predicting the reading performance of fourth-­graders from 65
regions who participated in PIRLS 2021. The public dataset of
PIRLS 2021 was analysed using R software.

3.1   |   Data
The data of this research were extracted from the public dataset of the PIRLS 2021 (pirls​2021.​org/​data). PIRLS 2021, accomplished successfully in 57 countries and eight benchmarking
bodies, stands apart from previous PIRLS evaluations in a few
ways. Firstly, a considerable amount of effort went into transforming PIRLS 2021 into a state-­of-­the-­art digital assessment
that presents students with 23 vibrant and engaging texts via
a novel group adaptive design. Secondly, the unprecedented
COVID-­19 pandemic took place during the 2 years of PIRLS
2021 data collection. Despite various disruptions to data collection in schools, most countries fulfilled the criteria for high-quality data collection. As a result of this impressive endeavour,
PIRLS 2021 is now the sole international comparison source for
fourth-­grade achievement data collected during the pandemic
(Mullis et al. 2023). Over 4000 students from 150 to 200 schools
in each country participated in PIRLS 2021. Approximately
400,000 children, 380,000 parents, 20,000 teachers and 13,000
schools provided information for PIRLS 2021. A detailed evaluation of the sampling process confirmed that almost all countries met all sampling requirements (Mullis et al. 2023). Nearly
all of the 57 countries and 8 benchmarking participants fulfilled
the coverage requirements of the target population, and most
also satisfied the criteria for low exclusion rates (less than 5%).
In conclusion, the PIRLS 2021 data are of high quality (Mullis
et al. 2023).
Data of each questionnaire for each country were present in
637 separate files. Data from student, teacher, home and school
questionnaires were fetched, and their files were merged into a
single file by a program written in R by the author. The resulting
raw file included data from 413,387 students.
The raw data were pre-­processed in three steps: cleaning, imputation and normalisation, which were also applied by Hu
et al. (2022). First, samples with more than 20% missing data
were deleted. After the cleaning process, the samples were reduced to 289,911. The remaining missing data were then imputed with the k nearest neighbour mean value function, which
fills in all missing values using the k nearest neighbours of each
case with missing values. It uses the values of the neighbours
and obtains a weighted average (by the distance to the case) of
their values to fill in the missing values. The k-­number was selected as 10. Finally, the data were normalised using the centering and variance scaling principles.
The resulting raw file, at the beginning of the data pre-­processing
explained before, included 545 variables. Since machine

3.2   |   Variables
The PIRLS 2021 complex sampling design, including plausible
values, weights and stratification, was addressed as follows. For
the student reading scores, the first plausible value (ASRIBM01)
was selected as a representative measure, a common practice
in PIRLS research (Chen et al. 2019; Gorostiaga and Rojo-Alvarez 2016; Hu et al. 2022). While the study acknowledges
the importance of weights and sampling strata, these were not
directly incorporated into the machine learning analysis. This
decision was made due to the inherent capacity of machine
learning algorithms, particularly SVM, to handle large datasets
and identify complex relationships even without explicit weighting adjustments. However, the study recognises that incorporating these design features in future analyses could further refine
the results and is an area for future research. It is important to
note that the PIRLS 2021 sampling process and quality assurance measures ensure the representativeness and high quality of
the data (Mullis et al. 2023), mitigating potential biases arising
from the complex sampling design.
While existing literature provides a valuable framework for understanding predictors of reading performance, this study specifically aims to explore the predictive power of all contextual
factors included within the PIRLS 2021 dataset, irrespective of
their prominence in prior research. This approach is justified as
it allows for an unbiased assessment of the relative importance
of each factor within this particular international assessment
context, potentially revealing novel predictors or relationships
not previously emphasised. By considering the full spectrum of
available variables, this study seeks to identify the specific factors most salient within the PIRLS 2021 framework, contributing a unique perspective to the existing body of knowledge and
informing future research and policy decisions based on this
specific assessment. This comprehensive approach is crucial for
maximising the utility of the rich PIRLS 2021 data and potentially uncovering previously overlooked influences on fourth-grade reading achievement in a diverse international sample.
Given the exploratory nature of this study and the focus on
identifying the most influential predictors from a large set of
variables, multicollinearity was not explicitly tested for prior to
applying the SVM-­R FE. This decision is supported by the robustness of SVM to high dimensionality and the fact that RFE
itself acts as a form of feature selection, mitigating the impact
of highly correlated predictors. However, acknowledging the
potential influence of multicollinearity on the interpretability of individual feature importance, a post hoc analysis of the
correlations among the top 16 selected features was conducted.
This involved calculating Pearson correlation coefficients for
all pairwise combinations of these variables. This approach ensures that while the predictive power of the model remains robust, potential multicollinearity among the final key predictors

is transparently addressed. Any correlations exceeding a threshold of |0.7| were then further examined to understand the nature

To avoid including duplicate data from IEA-­calculated indices,
a careful review of all 122 variables was undertaken. Variables
identified as components of pre-­existing indices, such as the
Early Home Learning Environment (EHLE) or the School

only the most granular level of data was included in the analysis, preventing redundancy and potential weighting issues.
Furthermore, the PIRLS 2021 user guide and technical documentation were consulted to confirm the composition of each
index and ensure that no duplicated information was inadvertently included in the final set of 122 predictor variables. This
rigorous process guarantees that the analysis utilises the most
direct and non-­redundant data available within the PIRLS 2021
dataset.
The dependent variable of this research was the reading test
scores of the students in PIRLS 2021. To predict the factors
that affect student reading performance, first, an identifier
variable should be created to distinguish successful students.
To do this, the benchmark score (ASRIBM01) of the first plausible value was randomly selected to represent the students'
reading scores, which was also applied in previous studies (Chen et al. 2019; Gorostiaga and Rojo-­A lvarez 2016; Hu
et al. 2022). The benchmark score had 5 categories: (1) below
400 (n = 43,888), (2) at or above 400 but below 475 (n = 50,296),
(3) at or above 475 but below 550 (n = 85,735), (4) at or above 550
but below 625 (n = 80,440), (5) at or above 625 (n = 29,552). This
score is recoded into a new variable, that is, the identifier variable used in machine learning to train a model. The identifier
variable should indicate whether each student is successful or
not. Therefore, the first two categories of the benchmark score
were considered unsuccessful, and the last two categories were
considered successful. The resulting identifier variable had
two categories: (1) low performance (n = 94,184), (2) high performance (n = 109,992). So, the final data set included 204,176
students and 123 variables.

3.3   |   Modelling
To answer the first question, the L2-­regularised SVM with
linear kernel method with three-times repeated 10-fold
cross-­validation (CV) was employed using the caret package
(short for Classification and Regression Training) in The
Comprehensive R Archive Network Repository. The dataset
is randomly divided into 10 equal “folds” for 10-­fold cross-validation. The model is then evaluated on the final fold
after being trained on nine of these folds. This procedure is
carried out 10 times, each fold serving as a test set just once.
The average result across these 10 iterations constitutes the
final model performance. For increased robustness, the entire 10-­fold cross-­validation procedure was performed three
more times with distinct random dataset divisions each time.

score are the most often used indicators in the SVM to evaluate the classification capacity of the model. These metrics

exactly once. Then, an estimation can be created by averaging

4   |   Results

The performance of the SVM model was evaluated through the
confusion matrix, which presents a detailed view of the model's
accuracy by displaying the number of true positive, true negative, false positive and false negative predictions. The SVM
model made a total of 51,252 predictions. Of these, 22,717 were
true negatives and 28,951 were true positives, demonstrating a
high level of correct predictions made by the model. True negatives represent the instances where the model correctly predicted
a student to be a low-­performing reader, whereas true positives
denote cases where the model rightly classified a student as a
high-­performing reader. However, the model did present some
errors. There were 3967 false positives and 5617 false negatives.
False positives indicate instances where the model incorrectly
predicted a student to be a high-­performing reader, whereas
false negatives signify cases where the model wrongly classified
a student as a low-­performing reader. Considering the balance
between the model's correct and incorrect classifications, the
SVM model demonstrates robustness in its predictive capability.

After the test with the 10-­fold CV method repeated 3 times, the
optimised SVM model with a total of 122 contextual factors
(independent variables) reached a relatively high classification
accuracy of 84.3% (95% CI: 0.841, 0.846, p < 2.2e−16); the sensitivity score was 0.80, and the specificity score was 0.88. These
indicate that, based on the 122 contextual factors, the model
was roughly 84.3% accurate in predicting whether a student will
be a high or low performer. The model appears to be doing an
excellent job of capturing the correlations between contextual

5   |   Discussion
In this research, it was discovered that when trained
with PIRLS 2021 data, SVM successfully distinguished between high-­and low-­performing fourth-­g rade students based
on key contextual factors. The top 16 key factors affecting
the fourth-g raders' reading performance were revealed by
SVM-­R FE.

This study makes several unique contributions to the literature
on reading achievement. First, it provides a comprehensive analysis of the predictive power of all contextual factors available
within the PIRLS 2021 dataset, offering a broader perspective
than studies focusing on pre-­selected variables based on existing
literature. This approach allows for the identification of potentially novel or overlooked predictors within the specific context
of this international assessment. Second, by employing a machine learning approach (SVM-­R FE), the study identifies the
16 most salient predictors of fourth-­grade reading performance
within the PIRLS 2021 framework, offering a data-­driven prioritisation of factors for intervention and policy considerations.
Third, the study contributes to the understanding of the relative
importance of these factors in a diverse international sample of
students, providing valuable insights for cross-­cultural comparisons and informing the development of targeted interventions.
Finally, by analysing data collected during the COVID-­19 pandemic, the study offers a timely snapshot of the factors influencing reading achievement during a period of unprecedented
educational disruption, contributing to a better understanding
of the evolving educational landscape. These contributions

6   |   Limitations
Even though this study illuminates the complex relationship
between contextual factors and the reading performance of
fourth-­grade students in 65 regions via the utilisation of machine learning, it is crucial to acknowledge several limitations.
Firstly, the employment of SVM-­R FE, although effective, hinges
on the data provided by PIRLS 2021. The study's inferences are
inherently confined to the factors incorporated in this data set
and may not account for other potentially influential factors either absent or misrepresented in the PIRLS 2021 data. Secondly,
the efficiency of SVM in discerning between high-­and low-achieving students also relies on the precision and comprehensiveness of the PIRLS data. Any inconsistencies or inaccuracies
in this dataset might affect the reliability of the study outcomes.
Thirdly, this study relied on categorising students into high-­and
low-­performance groups, which intrinsically imposes a binary
perspective on the student's performance. This strategy might
oversimplify the complexities and neglect the continuum of student performance in reading. Finally, while the Mann–Whitney
U test was used to assess the statistical significance of differences between low-­and high-­performing groups, this test does
not inherently account for the complex sampling design of
PIRLS 2021. Given the large sample size and the focus on identifying substantial effect sizes, the impact of the sampling design

10 of 13

on statistical significance is expected to be minimal. However,
acknowledging this limitation, future research should consider
incorporating methods that explicitly account for the survey
design, such as survey-­weighted regression models or replicate
weights, to further refine the analysis and ensure the accuracy
of statistical inferences. This approach would provide more robust and generalisable findings regarding the relationships between contextual factors and reading performance within the
PIRLS 2021 framework.

7   |   Suggestions for Policies and Interventions
The findings provide valuable insights that can guide the formulation of effective educational policies and interventions
aimed at enhancing the reading performance of fourth graders. Policymakers and educators should devise and implement
focused interventions based on the top 16 crucial factors revealed by this study. As these factors have a significant influence on reading performance, direct addressing may lead to
notable improvements. The results underline the significance
of a comprehensive approach to education. Interventions should
not solely focus on pedagogical strategies but should also consider broader socioeconomic, familial and individual contexts.
Ensuring teachers are well-­equipped to tackle the key factors
that impact reading performance is a crucial mandate for policies. This could involve integrating professional development
programmes that concentrate on teaching strategies for reading
aimed at diverse student groups and the provision of resources
to support teachers in classroom implementation of these strategies. The enhancement of reading performance is a shared
responsibility that policies should advocate for. Encouraging
collaboration among teachers, parents, community organisations and other involved parties is a crucial step. This collective
approach can foster a nurturing environment conducive to reading development.

7.1   |   Suggestions for Future Research
This study has effectively laid out a wide array of fascinating and
significant possibilities for future research. Each of these directions presents an exciting opportunity to build upon the findings
of this study and to continue expanding our understanding of
the complex factors that influence reading performance in students globally.
Considering that this study's primary focus was fourth-­graders,
expanding the analysis to incorporate other age groups could
prove beneficial. An investigation into whether the key factors
identified in this research also influence reading performance
in other grades would pave the way for a more holistic understanding of student reading development.
A more in-­depth exploration of the identified key factors could
yield valuable insights. More detailed investigations could
probe the processes through which these factors impact reading performance. Such studies might adopt qualitative or mixed
methods approaches, thereby providing a more detailed understanding of the influences on reading performance.
