A B S T R A C T

Keywords:
ICT competency
PISA
Digital reading

Due to the prevalence of information and communication technologies (ICT) among secondary school students
and the increasing significance of digital reading, exploring the impact of ICT on students’ digital reading performance in English seems necessary. The current research aimed to examine the effects of ICT competencies
factors on secondary school students’ English digital reading performance. Data were obtained from 7703 15year-old students from three East Asian economies, Macau, Hong Kong, and Singapore, from the Programme
for International Student Assessment (PISA) 2018 dataset. The results of hierarchical regression analysis revealed
that students’ ICT literacy was negatively correlated with their English digital reading performance, while the
influence of attitudes toward ICT varied across the three East Asia economies. Additionally, males had lower
English digital reading achievement, while students with higher socioeconomic status (SES) had higher English
digital reading performance.

1. Introduction
As digital reading involves a variety of texts and integrates with new
social and cultural practices, it seems critical for learners to be equipped
with adequate digital reading skills (OECD, 2019b) to participate
competently in academic and social communication in this increasingly
digitalized age (OECD, 2019a). Notably, according to previous studies
converging on the reading performance between digital and paper mediums, digital reading is not comparable to conventional reading for
readers of different reading habits (Støle et al., 2020). The shift to digital
reading as well as the rapid development of information and communication technologies (ICT) in education (Lu and Xie, 2022), necessitated an updated exploration between ICT competencies and digital
reading.
ICT competencies, which include students’ ICT literacy (ICT
knowledge and skills) and attitudes toward the use of educational
technologies (OECD, 2022), are essential constructs to be taken into
account when educators and researchers are dealing with issues associated with ICT integration. Given this situation, existing studies have
explored the role of ICT competencies in learners’ reading performance
in the Program for International Student Assessment (PISA) (Hu and Yu,

2021; Kong et al., 2022), a triennial test assessing 15-year-old students’
academic performance in reading, mathematics, and science (OECD,
2019a).
Previous research has highlighted the significant role of ICT on students’ PISA reading performance. For example, those learners with
positive perceptions of ICT-based social media showed greater digital
reading performance in PISA (Hu and Yu., 2021). More recently, Kong
et al.’s (2022) research indicated that students’ attitudes toward the use
of digital devices had a positive impact on digital reading achievement
based on PISA 2018 data. Previous research has also demonstrated the
importance of ICT regarding mathematics and science performance in
PISA (Odell et al., 2020) and reading skills (Batanero et al., 2021).
Owing to the significance of ICT and digital reading achievement, a few
empirical studies have incorporated the effect of ICT competencies to
understand its influence on students’ digital reading performance.
However, a holistic picture of the relationships between ICT competencies, including ICT literacy and attitudes toward ICT, and digital
reading performance in the subject domain of English in the East Asian
context remains underexplored. Another research gap is that there are
contradictory findings regarding the link between ICT literacy, attitudes
toward ICT, and digital reading, which may be attributed to the possible

Both ICT use in and outside classrooms have been documented as crucial

Attitudes are the essence of the learning procedures, which contributes to learners’ development and well-being (EC, 2013). Learners’
attitudes toward ICT have been defined as students’ perceptions of using

their learning process (Li et al., 2019). In PISA, students’ attitudes toward ICT have been regarded as an important component of their ICT
competencies (OECD, 2022). Specifically, according to OECD (2022,
p44), in this research, students’ attitudes toward ICT refer to “students’
motivation to learn ICT-related skills, their openness to new experiences, willingness to collaborate and engage with others using ICT, and
their confidence in conducting certain tasks with ICT,” which contains
four dimensions: interest in ICT, perceived autonomy in ICT use,
perceived ICT competence, and ICT as a topic in social interaction.
Interest in ICT refers to students’ motivation to deal with ICT-related
tasks and activities, which is regarded as one of the conditioning variables to enhance ICT competencies as intrinsic motivation (Goldhammer
et al., 2016; OECD, 2022). Perceived autonomy in ICT use is defined as
learners’ willingness and self-control to engage in ICT-based activities,
which might benefit their readiness in using ICT (Goldhammer et al.,
2016; OECD, 2022). Furthermore, perceived ICT competence is identified as learners’ capabilities of ICT use, while ICT as a topic in social
interaction refers to the sharing of knowledge, activities, and interest
related to ICT in communication and interaction (Goldhammer et al.,
2016; OECD, 2022).
It should be noted that there are mixed findings regarding the relationship between ICT literacy, attitudes toward ICT, and digital reading
performance according to the previous studies. On the one hand, Xiao
et al. (2019) revealed a negative link between students’ subject-related
ICT use at school and their PISA digital reading performance based on
research from Australia, Finland, France, New Zealand, and Singapore.
This was also in line with Hu et al.’s study (2018) among 44 countries
whose results showed that ICT use at school was negatively correlated
with their digital reading achievement. Other studies did not find a
significant association between ICT-related attitudes and digital reading
achievement. For example, results of Skryabin et al. (2015) revealed that
the higher frequency ICT usage during lessons does not have a direct
relationship with students’ academic outcomes. Similarly, Toroujeni
(2022) showed that ICT literacy was not an important factor in predicting students’ English reading achievement in Iran.
As for the association between attitudes toward ICT and reading
achievement, it is revealed that students with higher ICT interest, autonomy, and perceived competence reported better reading achievement through the PISA 2015 data (Hu et al., 2018). However, a
longitudinal study based on data during PISA 2009 and 2018 from 57
economies demonstrated that students’ ICT-based social media use was
negatively associated with their reading performance (Hu and Yu,
2021), which was contradictory with Lim and Jung’s (2019) finding that
reported a positive link between participants’ attitudes toward
ICT-based social use and digital reading proficiency across 15 countries.
These mixed findings may be owing to the possible differences
among economies and countries regarding reading, for example, reading
habits and strategies (Wu and Peng, 2017). Notably, different economies
embrace diverse education systems and cultures, which may also subsequently influence adolescents’ perspectives of technologies (Kong
et al.,2022). Thus, this research tried to address this research gap by
investigating how ICT literacy and attitudes toward ICT factors impact
students’ English digital reading achievement in the context of East Asia.
Moreover, as the competence to effectively use ICT plays an essential
role in teaching and learning (Senkbeil, 2022), it is necessary to employ
multidimensional ICT competencies constructs to examine their
respective impact on learning achievements (Hu et al., 2018). However,

differences among economies concerning students’ reading habits,
reading strategies (Wu and Peng, 2017), understanding of technologies
(Kong et al.,2022), and education systems (Støle et al., 2020). As an
example, Sarbaini et al.’s (2019) research found that culture affected
students’ adoption of e-learning technologies. Given the research gaps,
the present research aimed to examine the effect of ICT competencies on
English digital reading achievement in three East Asian economies.
2. Literature review
2.1. Digital reading achievement in English in PISA
The PISA test started to evaluate learners’ digital reading performance since PISA 2009. In PISA 2018, the role of digital reading has
been highlighted (OECD, 2019a). The PISA digital reading test contains
a broad range of resources, such as articles from textbooks, e-mails, and
blogs, to assess how well learners interact with the texts for specific
purposes (OECD, 2022). To finish the digital reading test, students ought
to read in a fluent, accurate, and automatic way to have a deep understanding of the main content (OECD, 2022). Moreover, students also
need to be equipped with abilities of locating information (OECD, 2022),
reflecting on the text concerning the content and form, critically evaluating the quality and reliability of the information, and dealing with
contrast (OECD, 2016).
In PISA, participants complete their reading test using different
languages (OECD, 2016), which means their digital reading achievement has been assessed in diverse languages across economies. For
example, some students in a few East Asian regions or countries use
English as their second language to complete the PISA test (OECD,
2019b).
English, an international language in multilingual settings, is an
effective tool for people to communicate (Matsuda, 2017). In the East
Asian context, for example, in Hong Kong and Singapore, English is used
as an official language such as in government and education (Zhang
et al., 2021). Across the East Asian regions, distinct differences exist in
the domain of English education, involving English educational policies,
students’ English proficiency (Bolton and Bacon-Shone, 2020) and English medium instructions (Chiu and Walker, 2007), which might result
in secondary learners’ diverse English reading achievements (Zhang
et al., 2021). Given the crucial role of English and the various English
learning contexts across East Asia (Luo et al., 2024; Saraceni, 2020), it
seems interesting to explore students’ digital reading achievement in
English using the large-scale dataset PISA 2018 and see if factors like ICT
competencies can have an impact on it.
2.2. ICT competencies and its relationship with digital reading
ICT competencies refer to students’ technical skills or knowledge in
using information and communication technologies in learning, as well
as their attitudes toward the use of educational technologies (OECD,
2022). This definition implies that ICT competencies contain ICT literacy and attitudes toward ICT. OECD further delineates that ICT literacy
is learners’ knowledge and skills in “accessing, evaluating and managing
information and data; sharing information and communicating; transforming and creating digital content; individual and collaborative
problem-solving in a digital context, and computational thinking; and
appropriate use of ICT" (OECD, 2022, p.45). In particular, in the PISA
test, it is assumed that people are equipped with the necessary technical
abilities of utilizing digital technologies effectively, recognize related
procedures as well as acknowledge ICT literacy as a necessity to help
people fully engage in the digital era (OECD, 2022). Accordingly, the
ICT framework proposed by OECD (2022) lays the groundwork for
integrating ICT literacy as a distinct category in the PISA test.
The ICT literacy in PISA 2018 is composed of two dimensions:
subject-related ICT use during lessons and subject-related ICT use
outside of lessons (OECD, 2022). Subject-related ICT during lessons

To address the research gaps, the present study aims to explore the
relationships between ICT competencies and students’ digital reading
achievement in English in three Asian economies. It provides insights
into the association between ICT competencies and English digital
reading achievement as well as offer practical implications on how ICT
use might help improve students’ digital reading achievement in the
East Asian context. We addressed the following research questions with
specific hypotheses.
1. Does ICT literacy have a positive impact on English digital reading
achievement in Macau, Hong Kong, and Singapore respectively?
Hypothesis 1. Subject-related ICT use during lessons has a positive
impact on English digital reading achievement.
Hypothesis 2. Subject-related ICT use outside of lessons has a positive
impact on English digital reading achievement.
2. Does attitudes toward ICT have a positive impact on English digital reading achievement in Macau, Hong Kong, and Singapore
respectively?
Hypothesis 3. Students’ interest in ICT has a positive impact on English digital reading achievement.
Hypothesis 4. Perceived autonomy in ICT use has a positive impact
on English digital reading achievement.
Hypothesis 5. Perceived ICT competence has a positive impact on
English digital reading achievement.
Hypothesis 6. ICT as a topic in social interaction has a positive impact
on English digital reading achievement.
3. Which ICT literacy factor best predicts English digital reading
achievement after controlling the influence of SES and gender in Macau,
Hong Kong, and Singapore respectively?
4. Which attitude toward ICT best predicts English digital reading
achievement after controlling the influence of SES and gender in Macau,
Hong Kong, and Singapore respectively?

2.3. The East Asian context
There exist competitive and high-performing educational systems in
Hong Kong, and Singapore (Lee and Manzon, 2014) and socioeconomic
status heavily influences the quality of schools (Chiu and Walker, 2007).
According to the PISA 2018 dataset, the mean average of students’
digital reading achievement in Singapore, Macau, and Hong Kong, was
549, 525, and 524, which ranked second, third and fourth, respectively,
among the 76 economies and regions (OECD, 2019b). Confucian culture,
which emphasizes the importance of learning for personal growth and
social mobility might account for the high achievement of these East
Asian economies (King et al., 2018).
Multiculturalism, as a common and significant characteristic of the
education systems in Singapore, Macau, and Hong Kong (Arat et al.,
2022; Bernardo et al., 2016; Ustun et al., 2022), results in the diverse
population in these East Asian economies (Lian, 2016) contained with a
large proportion of immigrants (Hirschmann, 2020). It should be noted
that English is not the first language in Macau and Hong Kong, and only
a small portion of the participants in Macau and Hong Kong completed
the PISA 2018 test in English. In Singapore, English is the official language rather than the mother language. It may be interesting to explore
the factors that might contribute to students’ digital reading achievement in English PISA 2018 in Macau, Hong Kong, and Singapore. Thus,
student participants who used English as the test language from these
three East Asian economies were included in this research.
2.4. SES and gender
To examine the effect of ICT competencies on digital reading
achievement, SES was considered a control variable since it was found to
exert a significant effect on students’ reading proficiency (Bernardo,
2022) and academic achievement (Wang et al., 2022). The existing
literature revealed that SES was positively related to students’ English
learning and reading proficiency in the Philippines using the PISA 2018
data (Bernardo, 2022). Students with lower SES were more likely to be
disadvantaged in learning resources and instruction of English in schools
(Bernardo, 2022)).
The consideration to include gender as a control variable in this
current research was also inspired by the previous literature. OECD
(2019c) reported that female students significantly outperformed males
in reading performance, which indicated the crucial part of gender in
students’ reading comprehension (Abdorahimzadeh, 2014). According
to Kong et al. (2022), there is a positive correlation between students’
attitudes about using digital devices and their achievement in digital
reading. This correlation is more pronounced for male students in
Finland and the United States. Singaporean male students showed a
higher correlation between digital reading achievement and the frequency of reading on digital devices than did their female counterparts.
(Kong et al., 2022).
Since students’ SES and gender were found to be closely associated
with language achievement, these two factors were included as control
variables to rule out alternative explanations. It allowed us to examine
the unique predictive role of ICT competencies on English digital
reading achievement.

4. Methodology
4.1. Participants
The databases from the most recent PISA evaluation, PISA 2018
(URL: http://www.oecd.org/pisa/data/), provided the data for this
study. Since some students in Macau, Hong Kong, and Singapore
completed PISA reading in English through computers, their PISA
reading data were chosen to represent students’ PISA English digital
reading performance. Based on the PISA 2018 data, 7703 participants
who adopted English as the test language were selected. The sample of
this current study contained students from Macau (n = 679, 18 %), Hong
Kong (n = 348, 5.8 %), and Singapore (n = 6676, 100 %).

4.2.1. Dependent variable
4.2.1.1. Digital reading achievement in english. The plausible value

According to OECD (2019c), participants were asked to complete items

and capabilities, such as locating, evaluating, and reflecting

4.3. Independent variables
4.3.1. ICT literacy
Based on the definition of ICT literacy by OECD (2018), thirty-four
items that measured students’ ICT literacy were directly obtained
from the ICT Familiarity Questionnaire for PISA 2018. The items consisted of two aspects: subject-related ICT use during lessons and subject-related ICT use outside of lessons. The items were rated on a 4-point
Likert scale, ranging from “never or hardly ever” to “every day,” with the
sample item “Downloading, uploading or browsing material from my
school’s website (e.g., timetable or course materials).” The internal
consistency reliability (Cronbach’s alpha) for ICT literacy was 0.75.

According to the definition of attitudes toward ICT (OECD, 2022),

7. Discussion

7.2. Attitudes toward ICT

This current study investigated the impacts of ICT literacy and attitudes toward ICT on English digital reading achievement through the
hierarchical linear regression among 7703 15-year-old students from
Macau, Hong Kong, and Singapore based on the PISA 2018 dataset.
The findings of this current research contributed to the present
studies in three main aspects. First, the small increase in students’ digital
reading performance was explained by subject-related ICT use during
lessons and subject-related ICT use outside of lessons after controlling
the role of gender and SES, suggesting the little effect of ICT literacy on
English digital reading performance to students of all three economies in
East Asia. Second, ICT literacy showed a negative impact on English
digital reading achievement in the three economies. Finally, the influence of attitudes toward ICT varied but with similar findings among the
three economies, adding to the findings of previous literature that only
concentrated on some aspects of ICT competencies.

In terms of attitudes toward ICT, the results of this research revealed
that students’ interest in ICT, autonomy in ICT use, and perceived ICT
competence, had positive effects on their digital reading achievement in
English among the three East Asian economies. It means that those reported higher interest, perceived ICT autonomy, and competence were
more inclined to achieve better English digital reading. These positive
relationships resonate with the existing literature (e.g., Petko et al.,
2017; Hu et al., 2018). A probable explanation is that those with greater
ICT interest, autonomy and competence tended to actively participate in
online reading tasks (Lee and Wu, 2012) compared to those with
negative attitudes. Positive involvement in online reading tasks may
practice learners’ digital skills and reading abilities.
Conversely, ICT as a topic in social interaction negatively correlated
with students’ English digital reading performance in the three East
Asian economies. It is indicated that those adolescents who reported
negative attitudes toward ICT as a topic in social interaction tended to
have better performance in the English digital reading scores in the East
Asian context. This finding is novel and is not aligned with previous
studies (e.g., Lim and Jung, 2019; Hu and Yu, 2021) that showed a
positive correlation between attitudes toward ICT-based social media in
social interaction and digital reading achievement. It might be because
students with positive attitudes toward ICT as a topic in social interaction might frequently utilize social media. The frequent use of digital
devices may take out students’ time for learning (Gilleece and Eivers,
2018) and distract their attention from learning materials (Hu and Yu,
2021). Consequently, the positive attitudes toward ICT as a topic in
social interaction may lead to worse performance in digital reading
scores in English. This finding illustrates the importance of guidance on
students’ use of ICT in social interaction.

7.1. ICT literacy
The impacts of ICT literacy were found to be similar among the three
economies. Both subject-related ICT used during lessons and outside of
lessons were negatively related to English digital reading achievement
in Macau, Hong Kong, and Singapore. This finding partly confirmed the
results of Gubbels et al. (2020) and Kong et al. (2022) that
subject-related ICT use had a negative association with digital reading
performance. It also went a step further to uncover the specific impact of
subject-related ICT use during lessons and outside of lessons on digital
reading performance in the subject domain of English in the East Asian
context.
This finding revealed that students participating in ICT during and
outside of lessons frequently were more inclined to show lower performance in the digital reading assessments compared to those seldom use
ICT (Hu and Yu, 2021). One possible explanation is that students’ digital
devices use at school and home might occupy their time concentrating
on learning (Gilleece and Eivers, 2018). Their attention may also easily
be distracted from lessons involving digital reading activities (Hu and
Yu, 2021). The fact that subject-related ICT use required learners’

8. Implications
The results of this current research yield several practical implications. To enhance students’ English digital reading achievement,
teachers and parents need to pay more attention to students’ ICT use
both at school and outside school. For example, teachers and parents
could provide students with adequate guidance on the beneficial purposes of ICT use (Hu and Yu, 2021), monitor students’ time spent not
related to digital reading (Kong et al., 2022), and place more emphasis
on students’ self-regulation of ICT-related assignments.
To help students achieve better English digital reading performance
through the assistance with attitudes toward ICT, instructors and parents are also advised o cultivate learners’ interest, autonomy, and
perceived competency in ICT use. For example, utilizing White and
Hungerford-Kresser’s (2014) pedagogical strategy that applied ICT to
character journaling was found to improve adolescents’ interest in
digital reading and participation in multiple activities with sufficient
digital reading materials (Hu and Yu, 2021).
Additionally, teachers and parents need to realize the possible influence of students’ gender and SES differences on digital reading
achievement in English to better tailor strategies to meet students’
learning demands. Teachers could facilitate female students’ autonomy
to read using ICT and improve females’ ICT competence while guiding
male students on how to use ICT effectively in digital reading.
Although it is not feasible for all students to obtain sufficient digital
reading materials (Kong et al., 2022), teachers and parents should still
try their best to provide more learning resources to students, especially
those with lower SES, to help realize digital reading equality.

9. Conclusions and limitations
The current research revealed that students’ ICT literacy had a
negative association with their English digital reading achievement;
students’ interest, autonomy, and competence in ICT use showed diverse
degrees of positive influence on their English digital reading achievement, while ICT as a topic in social interaction negatively correlated
with digital reading outcomes in English among the three East Asian
economies. This study has made several contributions. First, it added to
the existing literature by providing a comprehensive perspective concerning the impact of ICT competencies factors on digital reading
achievement in the subject domain of English in the East Asian context
using the latest PISA 2018 data. Compared with the existing studies,
most of which concentrated on a few factors in ICT competencies, this
research conducted a more holistic examination of all the ICT competencies factors mentioned in the PISA 2018 questionnaire. It is hoped
that the findings on the basis of secondary data and multi-dimension
factors could offer insights into the associations between adolescents’
ICT competencies and English digital reading achievement as well as
promote effective educational practices, particularly in this digital era
that witnessed the significance of ICT literacy and attitudes toward ICT
in affecting English digital reading.
However, the results of this research should be interpreted with
caution. First, this research only included three economies from East
Asia. Future studies are encouraged to select more economies and pay
attention to digital reading achievement in other languages. Additionally, the data were driven from PISA 2018 dataset, with the results of the
relationships were drawn only from the PISA 2018 database. We suggest
future work consider longitudinal studies to investigate the impacts of
ICT competencies over time. Besides, there was a negative link between
ICT literacy and digital reading performance in English in East Asia.
Qualitative methods could be used in future research to help find the
hindered reasons. Last but not least, it should be noted that ICT competencies factors may be affected by other environmental factors (OECD,
