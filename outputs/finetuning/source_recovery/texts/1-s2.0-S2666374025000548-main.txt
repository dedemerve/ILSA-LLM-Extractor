A B S T R A C T

Keywords:
Covid-19

This study examines the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on non-cognitive skills using survey data from the
Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA). We analyzed survey findings from PISA 2022 and
compared these results with previous test administrations to evaluate the influence of school closures on noncognitive outcomes across Canada, the United States, Australia, New Zealand, and Europe. Our findings indicate that students in the 2022 PISA cohort experienced a contrasting short- and-long term effect on their sense of
belonging. In the short term, there was a loss of 0.040 standard deviations (SD) in their sense of belonging to
school. However, in the long term, there is evidence of a recovery of 0.049 SD, particularly in the USA and
Canada. Additionally, the pandemic was associated with a 0.08 SD decline in growth mindset and a 5 %
reduction in the likelihood of being frequently bullied, compared to past student cohorts. The study reveals that
the COVID-19 pandemic disproportionately affected non-cognitive skills among girls, migrants, and economically disadvantaged students, exacerbating bullying and prompting a shift towards a fixed mindset, particularly
among female students.

1. Introduction
The COVID-19 pandemic disrupted the education of >1.5 billion
children and youth around the world (UNESCO, 2022). Not surprisingly,
the historic disruption to in-person learning and related school closures
had a deleterious impact on student learning. A growing number of
studies such as those in the United States (Bailey et al., 2021; Dorn et al.,
2020), Mexico (Hevia et al., 2022), Brazil (Barthalo et al., 2023), Germany (Schult et al., 2022), Belgium (Maldonado & De Witte, 2022;
Gambi & De Witte, 2024), The Netherlands (Engzell et al., 2021; Haelermans et al., 2022), as well as other parts of Europe (Blazko et al.,
2021) and the developing world (Azevedo et al., 2022) suggest learning
stalled during the pandemic. Perhaps more importantly, the duration of
school closures influenced the magnitude of these learning deficits (De
Witte & François, 2023; Jakubowski, Gajderowicz & Patrinos, 2025). In
higher education, recent evidence also suggests that the pandemic
exacerbated grade inflation, particularly among weaker students, with
lenient grading practices becoming more common in response to academic disruptions (François & De Witte, 2025). Research in select

education jurisdictions such as Sweden, which did not close schools
during the pandemic, indicate no learning deficits were sustained by
their student population, when national administrative data were
analyzed (see Fälth et al., 2021; Hallin et al., 2022; Patrinos et al., 2022).
While extensive research has examined the pandemics´s effects on
cognitive outcomes, little is known about the pandemic effect on noncognitive outcomes. This study fills this void by examining the impact
of the COVID-19 pandemic on non-cognitive skills.
A substantial body of research has demonstrated the importance of
noncognitive skills in school achievement and other school outcomes
(Agasisti et al., 2018; Erberber et al., 2015; Ye et al., 2021; Sisk et al.,
2018; Aarntzen et al., 2023). These skills— such as motivation,
engagement, self-beliefs about abilities, are particularly important in
post-pandemic contexts where resilience and well-being have emerged
as key protective factors (Gambi & De Witte, 2024; OECD, 2024;
Schnepf et al., 2024). According to the OECD’s Program for International Student Assessment (PISA), academic resilience refers to students
who, despite being from low socio-economic backgrounds (bottom 25 %
of the ESCS index), perform in the cognitive test (Level 3 or higher in

reading, math, or science) significantly above average in their respective
countries. Building on this definition, we extend the concept of resilience to include the preservation and recovery of a sense of belonging,
growth mindset, and frequent exposure to bullying, which are key variables associated with students’ adaptation during periods of high uncertainty and drastic changes in the way students learn.
Beyond their role as outcomes, a growing body of research emphasizes the predictive power of non-cognitive skills for academic
achievement. As Balart et al. (2018) point out, performance on cognitive
tests, including those for mathematics, reading, and science, is not only
the result of cognitive ability but also influenced by non-cognitive attributes. While it is recognized that the relationship between cognitive
and non-cognitive skills is bidirectional, the directionality remains
complex and underexplored, as recently emphasized by Sakellariou
(2025). Nevertheless, several school-based experimental interventions
have demonstrated that fostering growth mindset can lead to improved
academic performance. For example, students who received such interventions performed significantly better on standardized tests up to a
year later, compared to those in control groups (Blackwell et al., 2007;
Yeager et al., 2019). This evidence further strengthens our choice to
focus on non-cognitive indicators as key mechanisms through which
students cope with and recover from large-scale educational shocks.
This study focused on three variables: sense of belonging to school,
growth mindset, and bullying. The choice of these three variables is
determined by conceptual relevance, but mainly by availability in the
different rounds of the PISA tests. These three variables were measured
consistently in the last rounds of the PISA tests, which allowed for longitudinal and robust comparisons between countries. Although there are
multiple non-cognitive variables in the same PISA tests, it is impossible
to make comparisons across years, since in some rounds they are not
considered, as in the case of self-efficacy.
The sense of belonging refers to perceptions of phrases such as "I feel
like I belong to school" or "I feel like an outsider at school." Based on
Dweck’s (2006) framework, growth mindset captures students’ beliefs
about the malleability of intelligence. Finally, bullying is measured
through the frequency of being exposed to relational, verbal, physical,
and extortive forms of victimization, such as being teased, hit, or
excluded at school. These variables allow us to analyze how
non-cognitive outcomes were affected in the aftermath of the pandemic.
Given the global scale of school closures and the psychological strain
experienced by children and youth, there is strong reason to believe that
non-cognitive outcomes deteriorated during the pandemic. Concerns
about students’ emotional well-being and mental health have increasingly moved to the forefront of the public policy agenda. Research
examining topics such as emotional well-being and student mental
health is increasingly a topic of public policy relevance (e.g., Kishida
et al., 2021; Luthar, Pao & Kumar, 2021; Mazrekaj & De Witte, 2024;
Volante, Lara, Klinger, & Siegel, 2022). For example, Pathways to
School Success’ adopted by EU Member States in November 2022,
provides a new focus in education policy making by increasingly
stressing the importance of students’ mental well-being in school.
This study makes three key contributions. First, it examines how
pandemic related school closures have influenced the non-cognitive
domain and explores the variation among countries. Therefore, we
rely on the OECD 2012–2022 PISA test. PISA provides comparative data
in relation to various non-cognitive skills such as growth mindset, students’ sense of belonging at school and the propensity to be frequently
bullied. More specifically, we examine non-cognitive characteristics in
students from Canada, United States, Australia, New Zealand, and European countries. Collectively, these industrialized nations represent a
robust cross-section of western education systems. Since many largescale national assessment programs were postponed or modified during the pandemic, our study offers researchers and policymakers with a
detailed analysis of their respective student population using a wellestablished international benchmark. To date, there is no study that
has juxtaposed the significant cross-national variations in school

closures against PISA’s non-cognitive survey results. In terms of methodology, our regression model accounts for time varying observed heterogeneity, an overall time trend, country fixed effects, and a country
specific time trend.
Second, we examine how socioeconomic background, migrant status, and gender (as individual characteristics), the duration of total
school closures (as a pandemic characteristic), and teacher shortages (as
an education system characteristic) influence the observed changes in
the non-cognitive skills analyzed resulting from the COVID-19
pandemic.
Third, this paper contributes to literature by examining the role of
the non-cognitive skills in cognitive testing of mathematics, reading, and
science in the context of the COVID-19 pandemic. A substantial body of
scholarly work has been dedicated to scrutinizing the influence of noncognitive skills on economic outcomes, spanning academic performance, economic growth, and labor market outcomes (Duckworth et al.,
2011; Heckman, Pinto & Savelyev, 2013, 2006). However, as the
non-cognitive skills might be a mediator for cognitive skills, examining
their relationship is relevant. These investigations contribute valuable
insights into the multifaceted impact of non-cognitive skills, underscoring their significance in shaping educational and economic
trajectories.
The remainder of the paper unfolds as follows. The next section
outlines the methodological approach utilized to analyze PISA results.
Subsequently, descriptive statistics are presented, followed by the results of the econometric specifications. Finally, we present the discussion and conclusions.

2.2. Data
The analysis of non-cognitive tests relies on the Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA) tests. The sample consists of
980,403 students who took the PISA tests between 2012 and 2022 in the
selected countries. Most of these students are from Europe, and to
mitigate potential biases, the analysis implements weights at student
level. This ensures that the results accurately represent the national
population and facilitate comparisons across countries. All selected
countries are industrialized and developed nations from the global
North with consistent participation in PISA and comparable education
systems, which strengthens the internal validity of the analysis.
In the various PISA waves different measures of non-cognitive skills
have been included, but not consistently overtime for comparisons after
2018. Thus, we focus exclusively on three variables: sense of belonging
at school (BELONG), growth mindset, and being frequently bullied.
Importantly, due to data availability, the variable relative to the sense of
belonging (BELONG) is available from 2012 onward, while for the case
of the growth mindset variable and being frequently bullied, the data are
available from 2018 onward. The total number of students that sat the
PISA test in the selected countries was 255,202 in 2018 and 253, 393 in
2022.
The variable BELONG is measured using the Item Response Theory
(IRT) based on the following questions: (1) Thinking about your school: I
feel like an outsider (or left out of things) at school. (2) Thinking about
your school: I make friends easily at school. (3) Thinking about your
school: I feel like I belong to school. (4) Thinking about your school: I
feel awkward and out of place at my school. (5) Thinking about your
school: Other students seem to like me. (6) Thinking about your school: I
feel lonely at school.
The second variable considered is growth mindset (Growth Mindset).
As a proxy measure for this variable, the following question is standardized, with response options using a Likert scale: “Agree/Disagree:
Your intelligence is something about you that you can’t change much.”
The valid responses include: (1) Strongly disagree; (2) Disagree; (3)
Agree; (4) Strongly agree. In line with the approach of Jain and Stemper
(2023), these responses are transformed into integer values ranging
from 0 to 3, and are then standardized with a mean of 0 and a standard
deviation of 1.
According to Gouëdard (2020), disagreement with this statement is a
precursor to a growth mindset, as people who believe intelligence is
mutable are more likely to actively pursue self-improvement. Namely,
the original scale reflects a fixed mindset, while inverting it could be
interpreted in terms of a growth mindset. In the inverted scale, lower
values indicate a stronger growth mindset, with “Strongly disagree” (1)
being recorded as 4.
Finally, “being frequently bullied” is measured according to the
OECD (2023). To construct the bullying index students answered a
question (ST038) regarding how often, during the 12 months prior to the

6. Discussion and conclusion
The COVID-19 pandemic likely constitutes the most significant
disruption to global educational systems since World War II, leading to
substantial changes not only in daily routines but also in the modality of
delivering classes. During the periods of school closures in the analyzed
countries, for instance, 51 % of students received daily real-time (also
referred to as synchronous) classes from their teachers (see Fig. A4). The
lack of physical interaction resulted in interpersonal challenges among
students, translating into psychological problems such as anxiety,
loneliness, and demotivation. Using the PISA 2012–2022 administrations, this study explored the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic on noncognitive skill outcomes, particularly on the sense of belonging to
school, growth mindset, and self-reported prevalence of bulling. The
results of students who took the tests in 2022 were compared with
previous cohorts in Australia, New Zealand, the United States, Canada,
and Europe. In doing so, we control observed heterogeneity, a time
trend, country fixed effects, and country specific time trends. This study
is pioneering in investigating the impacts of COVID-19 on non-cognitive
performance with comparable data over successive test administrations.
We found that students in the 2022 PISA cohort experienced a contrasting short- and-long term effect on their sense of belonging. In the
short term, there was a loss of 0.040 standard deviations (SD) in their
sense of belonging to school. However, in the long term, there is evidence of a recovery of 0.049 SD, particularly in the USA and Canada.
Additionally, the pandemic was associated with a 0.08 SD decline in

a sense of belonging. However, gender differences in sense of belonging
outcomes can be explained from various perspectives, as Rippon (2023)

We also observe that COVID-19 amplified bullying for girls
compared to boys. Given that the pandemic and related school closures
increased online interactions, this finding converges with previous
research indicating girls are more likely to be victims of cyber and online
bullying (Gao, Zhao & McJunkin, 2016; OECD, 2019; Smith et al.,
2019). The latter suggests physical distancing and lockdown measures
might have reduced physical altercations for school-aged populations,
and boys in particular, but teenage girls are an especially vulnerable
group in national and global contexts where technology is emphasized.
Coupled with the statistically significant shift towards a fixed mindset
for girls compared to boys, the present study suggests the adverse effects
of the pandemic on non-cognitive characteristics are particularly acute
for female students. The results in terms of resilience approached when
relating the ESCS index to changes before and after COVID highlight the
counterintuitive result of growth mindset, those students in the lower
quartiles of the index, i.e., those from lower socio-economic conditions,
experienced a more minor increase in fixed mindset versus their more
affluent peers. These results reinforce the importance of broadening the
concept of academic resilience to include not only cognitive, but also
non-cognitive dimensions, especially under crisis conditions similar to
those caused by COVID-19.
Finally, we observe a strong interaction between the non-cognitive
and cognitive outcomes. Academic setbacks among students with a
high sense of belonging, compared to students with a low sense of
belonging, correspond to a loss of about a quarter of an academic year in
reading and mathematics. We did not observe significant differences for
science. The interaction between high levels of a growth mindset and the
pandemic led to a substantial 30 % reduction in the academic year for
mathematics. The study also highlights that students experiencing
frequent bullying perform substantially lower, particularly in reading,
with effects comparable to nearly a full academic year lost.
This paper has a few limitations. First, it is worth acknowledging that
our study was reliant on PISA survey results, and as such, is unable to
account for the fully range of non-cognitive skills that are associated
with student learning outcomes. Indeed, literature reviews suggest a
broad cadre of non-cognitive skills, including self-perceptions, motivation, perseverance, self-control, metacognitive strategies, social competencies, reliance and coping, and creativity, are all correlated with
academic outcomes and financial stability in adulthood (Gutman &
Schoon, 2013). Additionally, there are important differences in how
researchers and international organizations label, operationally define,
and measure non-cognitive skills (Cinque, Carretero & Napierala, 2021).
Collectively, the lack of shared conceptual clarify in the field, coupled
with the recognition that all international surveys, including PISA, offer
researchers and policymakers cognitive and non-cognitive data in select
areas, should be considered by policymakers before making sweeping
statements of their student populations.
In developing countries, where socioeconomic inequalities are more
pronounced, non-cognitive skills may be more effectively targeted for
interventions to reduce these disparities. Unlike traditional cognitive
skills, non-cognitive skills such as the sense of belonging, growth
mindset, and creative thinking may be less correlated with socioeconomic status. According to the PISA 2022 Results (Volume III): Creative

Minds, Creative Schools (OECD, 2024), socioeconomic gaps in creative
thinking are consistently smaller than those observed in mathematics,
reading, and science. These results suggest boosting non-cognitive skills,
such as those studied in this research, can help balance inequalities
among disadvantaged students.
In terms of policy implications, the nature and scope of education
policies and programmes to ameliorate the short- and long-term negative impacts of the pandemic will undoubtedly need to account for
unique national, cultural, economic, political, and economic circumstances. Nevertheless, emergent research in the wake of the pandemic is
beginning to document the effectiveness of various pandemic-initiated
education policies, albeit largely in relation to cognitive student outcomes (see De Witte & François, 2023; Harmey & Moss, 2023; Schnepf
et al., 2024). Our study underscores the increased salience that should
also be ascribed to non-cognitive outcomes, which have historically
been less prominent in media reports and league tables – but are
nevertheless equally important for future success. Finally, the interactions we have noted between the role of non-cognitive factors and
cognitive performance further highlight the importance of a more
comprehensive view of academic resilience. In their effort to promote
evidence-based policies, international policymakers will benefit from
future research that examines these complex interrelationships over
time.
