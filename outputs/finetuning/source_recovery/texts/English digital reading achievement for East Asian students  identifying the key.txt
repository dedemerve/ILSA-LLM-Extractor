ABSTRACT

ARTICLE HISTORY

Students’ digital reading literacy has attracted increasing attention in the
current digital era; however, few studies have been conducted to explore
how factors at different levels influence students’ digital reading achievement. Grounded by socio-ecological theory, the current comprehensively
explored the relative importance of 24 individual, microsystem, and mesosystem variables in predicting English digital reading achievement
through the machine learning approach (i.e. random forest regression).
The secondary data were retrieved from the Program for International
Student Assessment (PISA) 2018, including 7,703 15-year-old students
from Macao, Hong Kong, and Singapore. Our study identified 12 key
factors that best predicted East Asian students’ English digital reading
achievement. Among them, students’ socioeconomic status, subjectrelated ICT use during lessons, and interest in ICT ranked as the top
three factors. The disparities in the roles played by disciplinary climate,
gender, being bullied, immigrant status, and home language among the
three economies were discussed.

Introduction
With the rise of information and communication technologies (ICT), digital reading has been
increasingly crucial (F. Chen et al., 2022). Digital reading has become an essential approach for
learners to acquire knowledge and to participate successfully in social and cultural communication in
this increasingly digitalized age (OECD, 2019a, 2019c). Studies have consistently found that digital
reading is different from traditional paper-based reading, as it involves different reading habits and
mediums (Støle et al., 2020; Yu et al., 2022). For example, G. Chen et al. (2014) revealed that university
students in their study achieved significantly higher grades when reading on paper than on screen.
Similarly, Støle et al. (2020) found that students performed better on a paper-based test than one on
a screen, suggesting that reading habits may also influence students’ reading performance. Given
the concern about the factors that produce the best digital reading outcomes (Erdem et al., 2023),
Audrin and Audrin (2022) called for more studies on the role of digital reading literacy.
In 2009, the Program for International Student Assessment (PISA) test organized by the
Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) made the first attempt to assess
15-year-old students’ digital reading performance across economies (OECD, 2019a). In PISA 2018, the
definition of reading literacy was updated with a major revision, highlighting the important role of
digital reading abilities in dynamic materials (OECD, 2019c). Consequently, English digital reading
literacy is defined as “understanding, using, evaluating, reflecting on and engaging with digital texts

in order to achieve one’s goals, to develop one’s knowledge and potential and to participate in
society” in English (OECD, 2019a, p. 26). Digital reading literacy not only focuses on learners’ ability to
understand and interpret reading materials but also considers students’ digital reading skills (OECD,
2016).
The predominant studies have examined the impact of a limited set of factors regarding digital
reading achievement (Bernardo, 2022; Hu & Yu, 2021). For example, Bernardo (2022) showed
a significant positive association between students’ growth mindset and their digital reading
achievement. In another study, Hu and Yu (2021) found that students’ attitudes towards the use of
social media were positively related to their digital reading achievement. However, most studies
have focused on a limited number of factors, providing a fragmented understanding of digital
reading. This gap may be related to the wide application of classical statistical approaches, for
example, hierarchical linear modelling in data analysis, which cannot analyse a large set of variables
owing to the overfitting and multicollinearity problems (Yarkoni & Westfall, 2017). Machine learning
is a novel approach used in educational studies (Shin & Shim, 2021), which is suitable for simultaneously analysing multiple variables (Breiman, 2001). In addition, it can help researchers obtain
a holistic understanding of the indicators affecting students’ achievement (Haw & King, 2023;
Hopfenbeck et al., 2018; F. Wang et al., 2022).
As digital reading is complicated and affected by multiple-level variables, it is essential to identify
the key variables and provide a comprehensive understanding of predictors that contribute to
students’ digital reading achievement. Accordingly, this study investigated how factors at different
levels were related to English digital reading achievement using the machine learning approach. The
socio-ecological theory was used to rank the importance of predictors across levels (Bronfenbrenner,
1979). This study used data from Macao, Hong Kong, and Singapore due to their high levels of
reading performance and multilingual context.

Literature review
Digital reading in English in PISA
The PISA digital reading assessment comprises a wide range of reading materials and resources
assessing how well readers engage with the contexts for purposes (OECD, 2022). To succeed in
completing the digital reading assessment, students need to read fluently, accurately, and automatically understand the whole text (OECD, 2022). For example, students were asked to respond to
integrative questions or write a recommendation based on a specific context within the PISA reading
units (OECD, 2019a). The PISA digital reading assessment also requires students to locate information, including obtaining information within a short text and finding-related information (OECD,
2022). Evaluation and reflection in PISA reading tasks requires students to reflect on the text
regarding the content and form, critically assess the quality and reliability of the content, and deal
with conflicts among various texts (OECD, 2016).
In the PISA test, the test language is selected by the participating economies (Wahlström, 2023).
Western countries whose official or native language is English generally choose English as the test
language, but some East Asian economies choose more than one language due to their multilingual
contexts. For example, Hong Kong chose both Chinese and English (OECD, 2019c).
English, as an international language in multilingual contexts, provides individuals with a tool to
communicate effectively (Matsuda, 2017). In some Asian regions, English serves as one of the official
languages in government, law, and education, for example, in Hong Kong and Singapore, while it is
also positioned as an international language for global connections in Macao (Zhang et al., 2021). In
East Asian economies, there are also distinct and diverse differences in English education regarding
students’ proficiency in English skills, the development of English policies, and English teaching and
learning practices (Bolton & Bacon-Shone, 2020). These distinctions can lead to different reading
outcomes in English (Zhang et al., 2021). Given the importance of English and the diverse English

Socio-ecological theory
The socio-ecological theory, put forward by Bronfenbrenner (1979), assumes that students’ learning
process is shaped by factors from different systems, such as individual, microsystem, and mesosystem (Allen et al., 2021; Bronfenbrenner, 1977, 1979). The individual factor, which refers to students’
biological and dispositional characteristics, is the core of the socio-ecological system
(Bronfenbrenner, 1979). The microsystem factor has been defined as the learners’ surrounding
environment, including family, peers, and teachers. The mesosystem factor refers to the school
climate, for example, resources and policies in schools (Bronfenbrenner, 1979). The exosystem and
macrosystem factors represent the political, economic, and cultural characteristics of the social
system (Bronfenbrenner, 1979).
The literature has revealed that learners’ reading achievement is affected by factors at diverse
levels (Hu & Yu, 2021; Kong et al., 2022), leading to the use of socio-ecological theory as the
theoretical framework to explain the effect of multi-level factors on digital reading achievement.
The present research focused on the role of individual, microsystem, and mesosystem factors
because they are the most relevant factors influencing students’ digital reading performance. As
the three research regions shared a similar cultural background, the exosystem and macrosystem
factors were not included.

Individual factors
As individual factors pertain to demographic variables and individual behaviour, we included
students’ perceived growth mindset, self-efficacy, ICT literacy, attitudes towards ICT, attitudes
towards learning activities, and gender because these factors were measured by PISA 2018 and
have been shown to play critical roles in digital reading achievement.
Studies have indicated that the growth mindset is related to English academic learning (Bai &
Wang, 2020; Bernardo, 2022). Regarding self-efficacy, Tsai (2019) demonstrated that students with
low self-efficacy tend to attribute failure to a lack of ability. Based on OECD (2022), ICT literacy refers
to students’ knowledge and skills in ICT use, which contains two aspects: subject-related ICT use
during lessons and subject-related ICT use outside of class. Attitudes towards ICT refer to students’
interest in ICT, perceived autonomy related to ICT use, perceived ICT competency, and ICT as a topic
in social interaction (OECD, 2022). Studies have suggested that students’ ICT literacy and attitudes
towards ICT enhances English reading comprehension skills (Toroujeni, 2022) and reading achievement (Sarimanah et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2022). Hu and Yu’s (Yu et al., 2022) longitudinal study revealed
that students’ use of ICT-based social media at school harmed their digital reading achievement,
while students’ attitudes towards ICT-based social media made a positive contribution to their
reading performance.

Microsystem factors
In the present study, microsystem factors involve classroom and family contexts, composed
of socio-economic status, immigrant status, home language, language exposure, and perceived parental emotional support. Studies have provided empirical evidence supporting the
positive influence of socio-economic status (SES), immigrant status, and home language on
students’ reading achievement (Bernardo, 2022; Strand, 2023). Language exposure was also
found to be a significant indicator of students’ language development (Persici et al., 2022),
which may be attributed to the fact that native speakers predominantly tend to produce
richer child-directed speech with a diverse type (Hoff et al., 2020). On the contrary, students
who receive language exposure at home from parents or siblings with low proficiency were

more inclined to report less vocabulary knowledge (Lauro et al., 2020) and fewer literacy
skills (Puglisi et al., 2017).
While attention has been directed to investigating the role of language exposure in bilingual
students regarding vocabulary and reading skills, relatively little is known about the role of English
exposure from parents, siblings, and peers from non-bilingual students’ linguistic backgrounds.
Persici et al. (2022) observed that research on language exposure should be conducted on larger
sample sizes assessing both mothers’ and fathers’ language skills as well as their socio-economic
status to thoroughly examine the effect of language exposure on students’ language acquisition.

Mesosystem factors
As the mesosystem factor refers to factors at the school level, disciplinary climate, sense of belongings at school, being bullying, perceived school competition, and perceived school cooperation were
selected for our study. For example, disciplinary climate can improve students’ reading performance
(Ning et al., 2015). Students with a higher sense of school belonging and less exposure to bullying
were more likely to achieve higher grades (Huang, 2020).
Taken together, although the literature has covered the influence of a few factors concerning
reading achievement, the limited set of explanatory variables has impeded the ability of previous
studies to provide a comprehensive picture of key predictors of digital reading achievement. Since
socio-ecological theory has highlighted the importance of considering variables across levels simultaneously, our study aimed to address this research gap by considering the individual, microsystem,
and mesosystem factors based on the PISA 2018 dataset.

The East Asian context
Among East Asian economies, the education system in Macao, Hong Kong and Singapore is highperforming and highly competitive (H. W. Wong et al., 2020). In PISA 2018, the mean scores of
students’ digital reading achievement in Singapore, Macao and Hong Kong were 549, 525, and 524,
ranking second, third, and fourth, respectively, among the 76 participating economies (OECD,
2019c). It demonstrated that the education systems in Singapore, Macao and Hong Kong succeeded
in providing high-quality educational opportunities for students, which may be attributed to the
Confucian heritage culture that emphasizes the significance of learning and standards.
Regarding English education, in Macao and Hong Kong, school is the main context in which
students acquire English (Evans & Morrison, 2018), and compared with Chinese-medium schools,
English-medium secondary schools are favoured by parents with higher socio-economic status (Lo &
Lo, 2014).
According to OECD (2019d), Macao, Hong Kong, and Singapore tended to group learners into
schools or classes according to their reading performance, while social segregation in terms of socioeconomic status in Singapore seemed heavier than that in Macao and Hong Kong.
Multiculturalism is also one of the most significant characteristics of the education system in
Singapore, Macao, and Hong Kong (Arat et al., 2022; Bernardo et al., 2016; Ustun et al., 2022). Due to
the multiethnic structure, the population in these East Asian economies, although small, is diverse
(Lian, 2016), with a large proportion of immigrants (Hirschmann, 2020).
Few studies have been conducted to comprehensively investigate the role of the multiple
variables that predict students’ digital reading achievement in English using PISA 2018 data
among the three East Asian economies. Owing to the shared Chinese Confucian heritage culture,
features of the education system including grouping and social segregation, English education, and
multiculturalism that characterize Macao, Hong Kong, and Singapore, investigating the nature of the
participants in these three economies may help explain digital reading achievement in English.
Notably, although mainland China, Taiwan, Japan, and Korea also belong to Confucian heritage
cultures, students from these economies completed the PISA 2018 reading test mainly using their
native languages, namely Chinese, Japanese, or Korean. Therefore, we included samples only from
Hong Kong, Macao, and Singapore.

Machine learning
We used machine learning in this research, which can accurately predict outcomes by automatically
learning from the data while also finding significant relationships, trends, patterns, exceptions, and
oddities that might be underexplored (Sumathi & Sivanandam, 2006). Researchers have often used
machine learning in the educational context to explore relationships among variables of interest
(King et al., 2024Sinclair et al., 2021; F. Wang et al., 2024).
Machine learning has advantages that outweigh classical statistical approaches. First, classical
statistical techniques often face overfitting issues when models are improperly adjusted to adapt
to sample-specific noise (Yarkoni & Westfall, 2017). The k-fold cross-validation in the machine
learning approach can reduce this problem by splitting and cross-validating datasets (MartinezAbad et al., 2020). Additionally, the process of cross-validation refines models by selectively
including variables that significantly contribute to the accuracy of outcome predictions.
Moreover, the results of machine learning are less affected by statistical assumptions, such as
normality and collinearity (Lu & Ishwaran, 2018), making this approach effective for managing
large-scale datasets with complex combinations of variables.

The present study
Guided by socio-ecological theory, we explored how individual, microsystem, and mesosystem
factors predict students’ digital reading achievement in English in the East Asian context. We
compared the relative importance of these three factors and identified the most important predictors contributing to students’ English digital reading performance based on the multiple variables
measured in PISA 2018. Accordingly, our study was guided by two research questions:
(1) What was the relative importance of the different individual, microsystem, and mesosystem
factors contributing to students’ English digital reading achievement?
(2) Which factors were the most crucial factors that best predict students’ English digital reading
achievement?
The conceptual model based on the socio-ecological theory was illustrated in Figure 1.

Methods
Participants
The data was retrieved from the PISA 2018 dataset after considering the availability of information
about students’ digital reading performance and the independent variables (e.g., ICT literacy,
attitudes towards ICT, self-efficacy, growth mindset, SES, language exposure, and disciplinary climate). The purpose of our study was to explore the factors of students’ English digital reading
performance. Hence, we selected participants from Macao, Hong Kong, and Singapore who wrote
the English version of the test. Based on the PISA 2018 data, 7,703 students from Macao (N = 679,
18%), Hong Kong (N = 348, 5.8%), and Singapore (N = 6676, 100%) comprised the dataset.

Measures
Dependent variable
The reading achievement scores in PISA 2018 data were used as the measure of students’ digital
reading performance. Students were required to answer items in different types of texts designed to
evaluate their reading abilities, for example, the ability to locate information, evaluate, and reflect
(OECD, 2019b).

Independent variables
The selection of independent variables was based on the following criteria: 1) they all had to be available
in the PISA 2018 student main survey and ICT familiarity questionnaire, 2) they had to be guided by the
socio-ecological theory, and 3) there had to be empirical evidence showing that the independent
variables were significant predictors of achievement. Consequently, 24 variables were chosen as the
independent variables. The internal reliability and validity of the scales were acceptable (OECD, 2019c).
The selected variables were categorized into individual, microsystem, and mesosystem factors as shown
in Figure 1.

Data analysis
Missing data
Prior to the data analysis, data preprocessing was performed. To deal with missing data, random
forest was applied. This method has been widely recommended and used in PISA-related studies to
enhance the estimate accuracy (Scherer & Siddiq, 2015).

Random forest regression
The random forest analysis could identify the key predictors and rank their relative importance in
predicting students’ digital reading achievement in English (Liaw & Wiener, 2018). The

ASIA PACIFIC JOURNAL OF EDUCATION

7

“randomForest” package in the R program was performed to test a random forest regression model
with 24 independent variables.
Several indices were applied to examine the model’s accuracy and variable importance (Biau &
Scornet, 2016). First, to measure the degree of model fit and model accuracy, the coefficient of
determination (R2) and root mean square error (the differences between values predicted by the
model and the values observed; RMSE) were obtained. A higher value of R2 and a low value of RMSE
in the model explain more variance in the dependent variable if the R2 is higher with the low value of
RMSE (Biau & Scornet, 2016). Then, the variable importance was evaluated by the mean decrease in
accuracy (MDA). Previous literature has revealed that those variables are more crucial with larger
MDA. The variable importance metrics helped select the top predictors of English digital reading
achievement. We also performed a tenfold cross-validation with five repeats to empirically choose
the most crucial predictors of English digital reading achievement. This k-fold cross-validation
process assesses the prediction performance of a model by sequentially decreasing the number of
predictors in the model and calculating the mean decreased mean square error. The k = 10 using the
out-of-bag mean squared error (MSE) was adopted as the estimate of prediction error since it is
almost unbiased in the tenfold cross-validation (Simon, 2007).

Discussion
Through the machine learning analysis of 7,703 students’ data from the PISA 2018 digital reading
assessment, we mapped a comprehensive picture of the factors based on the socio-ecological theory
predicting students’ English digital reading achievement in three Asian economies. It addressed the
research gap that called for studies investigating the effects of variables on reading achievement
using diverse datasets and concentrated on the subject domain of English.
This study ranked the relative importance of 24 predictors at multiple levels of students’ English
digital reading achievement in Macao, Hong Kong, and Singapore using the machine learning
approach. Among the top 12 indicators identified, SES, ICT-related factors and language exposure
mainly stood out. Furthermore, the differences in the effects of disciplinary climate, gender, being
bullied, immigrant status, and home language that existed among the three economies have been
discussed.

Relative importance of the factors
Among the factors, the top 12 predictors of East Asian students’ English digital reading achievement
were mainly derived from individual and microsystem factors. The results indicated that although
English digital reading achievement is affected by multiple factors, individual and microsystem
factors seemed to exert more impact than mesosystem factors. Thus, it appears that the individual
and microsystem variables were closer to the learning process and tended to directly influence
learners’ achievement compared with that of the mesosystem (Bronfenbrenner, 1989). As mesosystem factors may influence individual and microsystem factors, the relationships among these
variables were more complicated.
Students’ SES was the most important predictor of English digital reading achievement, which
aligned with the findings of Zhang et al. (2021). This finding confirmed that students’ SES was
positively related to their target language learning outcomes (also see Ghorbani & Golparvar, 2020).
Students with higher SES were more inclined to have sufficient opportunities to obtain language
learning resources and receive English instructions at schools than those with lower SES (Bernardo,
2022). The importance of SES implies that more attention should be paid to socio-economically
disadvantaged students regarding English reading. Educators who seek to help students with lower
SES improve their digital reading achievement in English should offer more English instruction as
well as English learning materials.
We also found that SES played a more essential role in predicting English digital reading
achievement in Hong Kong (r = 0.491**) and Singapore (r = 0.377**) than in Macao (r = 0.344**),
which indicated that students’ English digital reading achievement relied more on their socioeconomic status in Hong Kong and Singapore compared to that of Macao. This may be because
Hong Kong and Singapore are more economically developed than Macao (Kim & Kim, 2023), which
may influence the students’ English digital reading achievement. Future exploration of the influence
of SES on English digital reading achievement should consider the interrelationships between SES
and political factors, for example, investigating how SES intertwined with language exposure factors
predict students’ English digital reading achievement.
Regarding the individual factors, the factors related to ICT literacy and attitudes towards ICT (e.g.,
ICT use as a topic for social interaction, interest in ICT, and subject-related ICT use during lessons)
played a critical role in predicting East Asian students’ digital reading achievement in English. This
finding is not surprising and resonates with previous studies showing that students’ attitudes
towards ICT positively affected their English reading comprehension (Toroujeni, 2022) and digital
reading achievement (Kong et al., 2022). The significant role of ICT literacy and attitudes towards ICT
on English digital reading achievement verified by our findings implies that to enhance students’
digital reading achievement in English, educators should make special efforts to improve students’
ICT knowledge and skills, raise their awareness of the importance of ICT integration in English
reading (Dashtestani & Hojatpanah, 2022), and cultivate their motivation in using ICT. For example,
practitioners should choose a suitable learning mode to integrate ICT and learning materials into the
reading tasks for students to improve their reading skills (Lin et al., 2020).
Notably, the three variables of language exposure, i.e., language exposure from schoolmates,
mother, and father, ranked in the top 12 in the three East Asian economies. This finding revealed that
the role of language exposure in English digital reading achievement should be highlighted,
corroborating with the finding of Persici et al. (2022). This finding indicates that language exposure
had a strong impact on students’ language development, which may be attributed to the multiculturalism of Singapore, Macao, and Hong Kong (Arat et al., 2022; Bernardo et al., 2016; Ustun et al.,
2022). In the three economies, some students were immigrants or came from multilingual families,
and some students learned at international schools through English as the medium of instruction,
which means they usefully encounter English exposure in daily contact. English exposure from native
speakers was more likely to provide more abundant child-directed speeches (Hoff et al., 2020;
K. M. Wong & Neuman, 2021), which may positively affect students’ English vocabulary size. In

contrast, those who received language exposure at home from parents or siblings with low English
proficiency were more likely to know fewer English words (Lauro et al., 2020) and had lower literacy
skills (Puglisi et al., 2017). This finding suggests that adequate English exposure from families and
peers with satisfactory proficiency would be beneficial to students’ English digital reading
performance.

Differences among the East Asian economies
Despite some variables that predicted students’ digital reading achievement in English among the
three economies in Asia, differences should not be neglected.
It is not surprising that the disciplinary climate in language lessons in schools played a more
predominant role in Singapore and Hong Kong students’ digital reading achievement than in Macao.
The results in Hong Kong and Singapore are consistent with the findings of Lau and Ho (2016) and
Huang (2020) which showed that disciplinary climate was significantly correlated with students’
reading achievement. One possible explanation is that the highly competitive educational system in
Hong Kong and Singapore influenced parental beliefs about academic performance (Lee & Manzon,
2014; H. W. Wong et al., 2020). In Singapore, parents pressured their children to perform well
academically which led to stratified schools with different stereotypes and discrimination
(H. W. Wong et al., 2020). Those students with higher academic performance were more likely to
be grouped and tended to produce a better disciplinary climate (H. W. Wong et al., 2020).
It has also been speculated that the influence of Confucian heritage in teaching instruction on
reading and disciplines has resulted in a stronger relationship between disciplinary climate and
digital reading achievement in Hong Kong. Hong Kong teachers were more inclined to assume great
authority and offer limited opportunities for student autonomy, impeding the development of selfregulated learners (Lau & Ho, 2016). Students who received more instructions on disciplines and
knowledge input tended to show better digital reading achievement. However, some medium and
high performers have shown a disruptive disciplinary climate in Macao, while some low performers
reported an orderly disciplinary climate (Ning, 2019). As students’ academic performance in Macao
has not benefitted or suffered from the disciplinary climate (Ning, 2019), it may lead to a weaker
association between disciplinary climate and digital reading achievement compared to their peers
from Hong Kong and Singapore. Considering the relationship between disciplinary climate and
English digital reading achievement, educators could seek a balance between controlling students
through disciplinary strategies (Ning, 2019) and cultivating students’ autonomy in English reading
with various strategies depending on the cultural and social context.
Likewise, the influence of gender varied across the East Asian economies. An interesting finding is
that the effects of gender on English digital reading achievement in Macao and Hong Kong were
stronger than in Singapore. Such a difference may be associated with disparities in gender values
and that female students prioritize reading achievement (Acar‑Erdol & Akin‑Arikan, 2022). Another
reason may be because the prevalence of English exposure in Singapore may provide similar
opportunities for girls and boys to learn English reading, which would, as a result, lessen the
influence of gender there. Thus, English teachers in Macao and Hong Kong should make extra effort
to equally encourage both female and male students in English reading.
Our results indicated that students’ experience of being bullied imposed a more important role in
English digital reading achievement in Macao and Hong Kong than in Singapore. This indicated that
being bullying was not as prevalent in Singapore as Macao and Hong Kong (OECD, 2019c), suggesting education authorities in Macao and Hong Kong need to make efforts in this regard to protect
students.
The findings revealed that immigrant status exerted a more crucial impact on English digital
reading achievement in Hong Kong and Singapore than in Macao. In PISA 2018, immigrant status
referred to where students’ parents were born (OECD, 2018). Thus, parents’ differing English
proficiency and language skills in these economies may explain this phenomenon (Gu et al., 2023).

The greater influence of home language on English digital reading achievement in Singapore
than that in Macao and Hong Kong partly aligned with the result of O’Brien et al.‘s (2020) study
showing that a large proportion of Singapore parents provided students with reading materials at
home and language literacy-related activities to cultivate their engagement and enjoyment in
language reading and promote their reading literacy. Thus, the difference concerning the association
between home language and digital reading achievement in English may be because of diverse
perceptions of the English reading materials and literacy-relevant activities offered by parents.
Future research is warranted to explore this speculation with empirical studies conducted in these
three regions.

Implications
Our findings have several implications. Theoretically, this study extended previous research by
showing how a wider range of factors simultaneously appear to contribute to students’ English
digital reading literacy among the three East Asian economies. Compared with existing literature
that has focused only on a limited set of variables, the simultaneous examination of multiple
individual, microsystem, and mesosystem factors provided a more holistic understanding of the
relative importance of different factors.
Furthermore, the utilization of socio-ecological theory may help researchers understand how
diverse individual, microsystem, and mesosystem factors predict English digital reading literacy
among the three East Asian economies. This study addressed the limitations of past studies that
only focused on a limited set of explanatory variables. Notably, as socio-ecological theory is a broad
and effective theory, it is suggested that middle-range theories related to English education, reading,
or digital reading could be paid attention to when considering the assessment of students’ English
digital reading literacy. However, there seems to be a lack of middle-range theory related to English
reading that could investigate individual, microsystem, and mesosystem factors simultaneously. For
example, the simple view of the reading framework put forward by Hoover and Gough (1990)
emphasized that reading comprehension served as the product of word decoding and listening
comprehension, while the schema theory of reading highlighted the interaction between readers,
texts, and reading context (Norris & Phillips, 1987). Thus, future research could consider developing
middle-range theories associated with English reading that cover possible factors at diverse levels.
The identification of key factors of English digital reading literacy helped researchers and
educators target the most cost-effective variables to cultivate students’ digital reading achievement
in English. The role of certain factors, i.e., gender and home language, have been widely investigated
in English reading in the existing literature (e.g., Acar‑Erdol & Akin‑Arikan, 2022). However, our
findings suggest such variables were less important compared to others such as SES, language
exposure-related factors, and ICT-related variables.
In addition, this present research added to prior studies by adopting the machine learning
approach to provide a new holistic view of the factors contributing to East Asian students’
English digital reading achievement. As the machine learning approach can be used to analyse
numerous variables in large-scale educational data without the issues of multicollinearity and
overfitting (Yarkoni & Westfall, 2017), it offers opportunities for researchers to rank the relative
importance of a wider range of factors for predicting English digital reading achievement. In
recent years, the machine learning approach has been widely used in diverse domains,
including politics (e.g., McAlexander & Mentch, 2020), psychology (Depren & Depren, 2021),
and mathematics (F. Wang et al., 2023), with relatively little application in the language
education area. Accordingly, this research also sheds light on the applicability of the machine
learning approach in language education as it can identify numerous predictors from largescale international assessments. Furthermore, the similarities and differences regarding the
predictors of digital reading achievement in East Asia may provide insights for policymakers
and educators.

Limitations
Our results should be interpreted with caution due to some limitations. First, the sample in this
research only included three economies in the East Asia. It is because those students whose test
language was not English in the East Asian countries/regions or without data in the variables
mentioned in this study could not be included in this research. The indicators of digital reading
achievement in other languages should also be investigated. Second, this study did not include the
mesosystem and macrosystem factors because they represent the political, economic, and cultural
characteristics of the social system that would be difficult to measure. Future research should
consider including mesosystem and macrosystem factors to deepen the understanding of the factors
affecting digital reading. Third, causal conclusions were made based only on only one PISA assessment test. Thus, researchers should conduct longitudinal studies to explore the influence of predictors on digital reading achievement in English over more than one assessment period. Fourth,
because we did not explore the relationship between the predictors of English digital reading
achievement, we encourage researchers to investigate and uncover the relations between the key
predictors in diverse contexts. Fifth, as the PISA 2018 sample from Hong Kong and Macao contained
only a few participants who used English, while all the participants in Singapore used English, the
sample lacked good balance, which affected the generalizability of the findings. Future work could
consider involving balanced samples from different economies. Lastly, this research used the socioecological theory as the theoretical framework, which is not specifically designed to understand
students’ reading behaviours. A middle-range theory within socio-ecological theory, directly associated with digital reading, is recommended for future work to facilitate a deeper understanding of
the variables and hypotheses related to digital reading.

Conclusion
The present study identified and ranked the relative importance of 24 predictors at multiple levels of
students’ English digital reading achievement in Macao, Hong Kong, and Singapore using the
machine learning approach. Among the top 12 indicators identified, SES, ICT-related factors, and
language exposure mainly stood out. This study provides evidence-based information for researchers, practitioners, and policy-makers who are interested in understanding and optimizing students’
English digital reading achievement.
