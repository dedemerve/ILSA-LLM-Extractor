ABSTRACT

ARTICLE HISTORY

Effective teaching practices are essential for enhancing classroom
instruction and student learning. However, a comprehensive understanding of the factors influencing teachers’ instructional practices
remains elusive. This study employs machine learning (ML) to identify the most significant predictors influencing the instructional practices of secondary school teachers in Shanghai, China. Using data
from the Teaching and Learning International Survey (TALIS) 2018,
we identified key factors that distinguish teachers with high and low
levels of instructional practices. The study combined data-driven and
domain knowledge methods to identify the most influential features,
and then analysed their impact on teachers’ instructional practices
using the Shapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) and Gradient
Boosting Machine (GBM) interpretative framework. The findings
identified 10 key features, such as Small group work (tt3g42g) and
Quieten the class for the lesson (tt3g42l), that distinguished teachers
with high and low levels of instructional practices. This discovery
enhances classroom teaching quality and teacher performance.

Introduction
Teaching practice is a systematic and reflective teaching behaviour, including curriculum
design, selection of teaching strategies, student interaction, and evaluation (Grossman,
Hammerness, and McDonald 2009). Teachers’ instructional practices are often considered
the standard for measuring the quality of teaching (Ainley and Carstens 2018). Many
studies have been conducted on teachers’ instructional practices. For example, the
research demonstrated that teachers’ self-efficacy significantly shapes their instructional
practices in Taiwanese secondary schools (Chen et al. 2020). The study showed that
effective assessment and feedback enhance instructional practices, promote student
engagement, and improve learning outcomes (Ole and Gallos 2023). The principal aim
of the study is to identify the key factors influencing teachers’ instructional practices.
On the one hand, previous research has primarily focused on investigating a few
factors influencing teachers’ instructional practices. However, these studies

overlooked the combined influence of multiple factors within a comprehensive

factors influencing instructional practices. Danielson’s Framework for Teaching (FFT)
is widely used to evaluate teachers’ instructional practices. It offers a structured
rubric for evaluating specific instructional behaviours across multiple dimensions.
Danielson’s Framework comprises four domains of responsibility: planning and preparation, classroom environment, instruction, and professional responsibilities
(Danielson 2007). Stronge’s Qualities of Effective Teachers framework encompasses
professional knowledge, instructional planning, instructional delivery, assessment,
learning environment, and professionalism (Stronge, Tucker, and Hindman 2004).
This framework provides a foundation for examining the contextual factors influencing teachers’ instructional practices. The TALIS is a comprehensive survey of teachers, school leaders, and their teaching environments. The TALIS 2018 includes
professional characteristics and pedagogical practices at the school and teacher
levels (Ainley and Carstens 2018).
On the other hand, traditional methods may not sufficiently capture the complexity and interactivity among these factors in a multifaceted educational context.
ML methods are proficient in dealing with multidisciplinary and high correlations
among numerous variables, which are difficulties commonly faced by traditional
statistical methods (Senaviratna and Cooray 2019). For example, ML technologies
were used to identify key factors impacting educational outcomes (Christensen,
Nielbo, and Gümüş 2024; Zhai et al. 2024). Our research aims to integrate the
theoretical frameworks of Danielson and Stronge to investigate the factors influencing teachers’ instructional practices across three dimensions: individual, instructional, and school environment. Using data from TALIS 2018, ML techniques are
employed to identify the key factors significantly impacting instructional practices
within these dimensions. To achieve this, the research will address three key
research questions:
Research question 1 (RQ1): Within the TALIS 2018 database, which factors at the
individual, instructional, and school environment level influence teachers’ instructional
practices?
Research question 2 (RQ2): Which optimal sets of features serve as the primary
predictors of teachers’ instructional practices?
Research question 3 (RQ3): How do individual predictor factors influence the variation
in teachers’ instructional practices?

Literature review
This section reviews the existing literature on teaching practices, the factors contributing
to teachers’ instructional practices, and the current study. It is organised at three levels:
individual, instructional, and school environment. It provides a structured overview of the
diverse influences on teaching practices.

Teaching practice
Teaching practices refer to teachers’ strategies, techniques, and methods to facilitate
students’ learning and achieve educational goals. These practices are often conceptualised into three key dimensions: cognitive activation, classroom management, and student engagement (Blömeke, Gustafsson, and Shavelson 2015). Cognitive activation is
teaching strategies that challenge students’ thinking, promoting higher-order reasoning
and problem-solving skills. This dimension is critical in fostering deep learning and
preparing students for complex tasks (Baumert et al. 2010). Classroom management, on
the other hand, focuses on creating a structured and organised environment conducive to
learning. Effective classroom management minimises disruptions, enabling students to
focus on academic tasks (Pianta et al. 2008). Lastly, student engagement pertains to how
students are behaviourally, emotionally, and cognitively involved in learning activities,
directly impacting their academic outcomes (Fredricks, Blumenfeld, and Paris 2004).
The literature widely recognises the importance of these dimensions. For example,
cognitive activation has been linked to improved student performance in mathematics
and science (Lipowsky et al. 2009), while effective classroom management has been
identified as a predictor of student engagement and success (Marzano, Marzano, and
Pickering 2003). These teaching practices are critical to student achievement and form the
basis for teacher evaluation and professional development programmes (Danielson 2007;
Stronge, Tucker, and Hindman 2004). The TALIS survey systematically measures teaching
practices through carefully designed survey items. For example, questions focusing on
difficult tasks effectively elicit cognitive activation, which offers a comprehensive framework for examining key dimensions of teaching practices. TALIS data provide an extensive
set of variables for analysing effective instructional practices.

Factors contributing to teachers’ instructional practices
Individual level factors
The influence of individual factors on teachers’ instructional practices is extensive.
Teaching experience significantly impacts instructional efficacy, with experienced teachers often demonstrating higher competence in managing classroom dynamics
(Thorsen 2016). Personality traits and teacher behaviours are key factors in educational
effectiveness, influencing teacher quality and student outcomes (Clinton, Aston, and
Dawson 2023). Teacher characteristics such as gender, educational background, and job
satisfaction are additional facets that shape effective teaching practices (Opdenakker and
Van Damme 2006). Research has shown that professional development impacts teachers’
instructional practices. Improved teacher professional development involves participating
in subject specific training programmes, establishing mentor relationships, and utilising
professional development resources (Avalos 2011). Research demonstrated that professional development positively impacts teachers’ teaching ability (Borko 2004). While these
factors are generally used to measure teaching quality, research has often been limited to
isolated factors impacting teachers’ instructional practices. However, limited resources
and time constraints can hinder professional development, negatively affecting instructional practices (Lofthouse and Hall 2014). Engagement in mentoring and induction
programmes is particularly impactful, enhancing teachers’ effectiveness and professional

growth (Ingersoll and Strong 2011). Furthermore, the variety of topics covered, incentives,
and perceived value greatly impact a teacher’s engagement (Geringer 2003). Participation
hours in professional development activities, types of development undertaken, and their
perceived impact play a role in shaping teachers’ instructional practices (Hutchison and
Rea 2011).

Instructional level factors
Various factors influence teachers’ instructional practices at the instructional level.
Teachers’ content and pedagogical knowledge are foundational to students’ academic
success (Day 2023). The integration of ICT, teaching strategies, and digital resources
significantly influences instructional methodologies (Fantilagan 2022). Teachers’ selfefficacy, motivation, innovative teaching strategies, feedback, and professional collaboration are important factors that contribute to teachers’ instructional practices. Research
demonstrates that teacher self-efficacy mediates teacher cooperation and teaching practices, while professional collaboration affects teaching practices through self-efficacy (Xie
et al. 2023). Teaching motivation positively influences teachers’ job performance and work
experience (Kumari and Kumar 2023; Wahyudi, Armanu, and Suryadi 2023). Furthermore,
positive feedback from teachers enhances student motivation to learn, while negative
feedback can lead to discouragement and self-motivation (Rui and Lo 2023).
School environment level factors
The school environment plays a critical role in shaping instructional practices. Teachers’
perceptions of their work environment, including the learning climate, school culture, and
leadership engagement, significantly affect their instructional methods (Abbas, Javed,
and Waseem 2022). The school climate, leadership styles, and workload also impact
teachers’ instructional practices (Yan, Chiu, and Cheng 2022). School leadership significantly influences teachers’ instructional practices using TALIS data (Bellibaş, Gümüş, and
Liu 2021). Research shows that class size affects teachers’ perceptions and teaching
practices (Almulla 2015). In addition, a change in school facilities can influence teacher
instructional practices and student academic measures (Earthman 2002). The research
discovered that the school climate is related to the personal growth and development of
teacher leaders (Gningue et al. 2022). Another study found that the school principal’s
leadership style and work ethic significantly impact teachers’ instructional methods
(Sambomalilin and Ni’matullah 2021). The research highlighted the importance of principals adopting collaborative leadership and fostering a supportive work environment in
middle school to improve teaching effectiveness (Arina et al. 2023). Job satisfaction
positively impacts the performance of elementary and secondary school teachers (Ker,
Lee, and Ho 2022). Conversely, teachers who experience high levels of stress are more
likely to have lower occupational well-being, which can negatively affect their instructional quality (Rodríguez-Jiménez et al. 2022).

factors influencing teachers’ instructional practices. Danielson emphasises four core

Materials and methods
Datasets
This study utilises the TALIS 2018 data from the Organization for Economic Co-operation
and Development (OECD). The survey involved 3976 secondary school teachers across
198 schools in 16 districts of Shanghai, China, a recognised region for its distinctive
educational system and exceptional performance in international assessments. TALIS
offers a comprehensive dataset including instructional practices, school leadership, teacher professional practices, teacher education and initial preparation, school climate, job
satisfaction, and teacher self-efficacy (OECD 2019).

Dependent variables
Three dimensions are important in defining high-quality instructional practices: wellstructured classroom management, supportive student-oriented classroom climate, and
cognitive activity with challenging content (Doğan and Yurtseven 2018). The TALIS 2018
official conducted a fitting of the dimensions related to teachers’ instructional practices,
referred to as T3TPRA. The study used the indicator as the dependent variable. We
operationalised this indicator by categorising data points above the mean as 1, indicating
teachers with a high level of instructional practices, and data points below the mean as 0,
signifying teachers with a low level of instructional practices. The classification of high and
low instructional practice groups follows a data-driven threshold approach, aligning with
prior studies that segment participants based on predefined proficiency levels in largescale educational assessments (Hu, Peng, and Ma 2021; Zheng, Cheung, and Sit 2023). To
ensure the validity of the findings, we conducted a series of statistical analyses. These
analyses included internal consistency testing, confirmatory factor analysis, variance
testing, and composite dimension fitting. The reliability of the sub-dimensions was
assessed using Cronbach’s alpha, and satisfactory results were obtained with values of
0.91 for Clarity of Instruction, 0.88 for Cognitive Activation, and 0.91 for Classroom
Management. Moreover, a composite dimension named “Teaching Practices” was also
generated by combining these sub-dimensions, and it demonstrated high internal consistency with a Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.91.

Analysis procedure
The workflow utilised a comprehensive ML approach involving data collection, preprocessing, feature selection, and model interpretation, as illustrated in Figure 2. This
methodological approach ensures a rigorous analysis of the TALIS 2018 data, employing
ML techniques to uncover the complex interrelations among the factors influencing
instructional practices.

Datasets and pre-processing
The workflow begins with teachers’ instructional practices feature collecting in the TALIS
2018 dataset. The TALIS dataset provides comprehensive international data on teachers
and teaching environments (OECD 2019). The data obtained in pre-processing was
initially exported using Stata/SE 17.0 to ensure that all data was presented in numerical
format. Subsequently, the mode imputation was chosen as the primary method for
handling missing data. Mode imputation is particularly suitable for categorical data as it
maintains the original distribution and prevents bias caused by outliers (Batista and
Monard 2003; Little and Rubin 2002). The dependent variable was recoded into a binary
outcome: values above the mean of t3pra were coded as 1, and values below the mean as
0. This simplification helps the classification process (James et al. 2021).
Feature selection
Feature selection can reduce input variables by identifying key features significantly
contributing to the predictive variable (Dakshinamurthy, Gera, and Kayarvizhy 2023).
The study employed a two-stage variable selection process. First, a data-driven feature

Model interpretation
In the interpretation stage, the top 10 features are ranked by their SHAP values, which
reflect their contributions to the model’s predictions (Ergün 2023). The SHAP Summary
Plot shows each feature’s contribution and the direction of its impact. The SHAP
Dependence Plot reveals interactions between features (Yi et al. 2023).

Results
The optimal feature sets (RQ 2)
Various feature selection methods exist, each with strengths and weaknesses. This study
employed a three-tier composite feature selection strategy, including feature importance
evaluation using ML models, domain knowledge, and variable modelling score analysis.

Feature importance evaluation using ML models
In this phase, we employed three ML algorithms: Random Forest (RF), Gradient Boosting
Machine (GBM), and eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost). These algorithms were chosen

for their proven advantages in feature selection and their exceptional performance across
various datasets (Badal and Sungkur 2023; Elbawab and Henriques 2023; Khoudi,
Nachaoui, and Lyaqini 2024). We utilised a combination of three ML algorithms and the
RFECV method to evaluate the feature importance for each model separately and identified the top 15 features for each model.
For the RF model, the accuracy was 0.8191, where the top features were tt3g42l,
tt3g42g, and CDC. The GBM model had an accuracy of 0.8354; the top features were
tt3g42g, tt3g42l, and tt3g42a. The XGBoost model had an accuracy of 0.8178 with top
features tt3g42g, tt3g42l, and tt3g42a. Notably, tt3g42g and tt3g42l emerged as key
features across all three models, demonstrating their consistency in prediction. The
heatmap shown in Figure 3 visually illustrates the importance scores across different
models. This graphical approach was designed to quickly identify the key features in each
model and visualise their relative contributions (Wilkinson and Friendly 2009).

Domain knowledge
Domain knowledge can address the limitations of data-driven methods for features that
algorithms may overlook (Ji et al. 2023). We evaluated the significance and impact of each
feature using domain knowledge and the ranking of the top 15 features identified by
three ML algorithms. This improves the model’s interpretability and utility.
The role of teaching strategies is particularly prominent in the selected features, such
as quieten the class for the lesson (tt3g42l), summarising lesson content (tt3g42a),

Interpretation of key factors (RQ3)
Comparison of model
As shown in Figure 5, all models perform well in the training set, with accuracy exceeding
80%. Among them, GBM demonstrates the best performance on the training set with an
accuracy of 0.844, while XGBoost and RF have slightly lower accuracy of 0.821 and 0.832,
respectively. However, there are some differences in model performance on the test set.
GBM achieves an accuracy of 0.807 on the test set, slightly lower than RF’s accuracy of
0.809 and higher than XGBoost’s accuracy of 0.786. Although RF shows slightly higher
accuracy on the test set, the difference between the two models is insignificant. However,
GBM displays better stability with a standard deviation 0.040 compared to RF’s 0.044.
Therefore, considering both the accuracy and stability of the models, GBM emerges as the
optimal choice.
The feature importance
Figure 6 shows the average influence of each feature on the effectiveness of predictive
models utilising SHAP with the GBM algorithms. The red bars represent the average
absolute SHAP values for each feature, with higher SHAP values indicating a greater
influence on the model’s predictive accuracy (Lundberg and Lee 2017). The features
tt3g42g, tt3g42l, and AF impact model predictions most, with SHAP values of approximately 0.7, 0.63, and 0.53, respectively. Other features CDC, tt3g42a, and tt3g42o possess
diminishing effects, while tt3satat, tt3g38, and QEP have the smallest impact, close to 0.1.
The interpretation of these SHAP values helps us to understand which features have
a greater impact on model predictions, allowing for more accurate model interpretation.

increases, suggesting that a higher CDC score may hinder teachers’ performance.

Discussion
Individual level factors related to teachers’ instructional practices
At the individual level, the high value of the QEP positively impacts teachers’ instructional
practices. Teachers’ academic qualifications and teaching preparation are directly related
to their effectiveness in the classroom (Darling-Hammond 2000). Teachers with advanced
degrees often have a more profound comprehension of the subject matter they teach
(Wayne and Youngs 2003). In addition, teachers’ preparation involves a deep understanding of curriculum content, familiarity with teaching resources, awareness of student’s learning needs, and thoughtful selection of teaching methods (Ingersoll and Strong
2011). Comprehensive preparation allows teachers to respond flexibly to diverse classroom challenges and effectively meet the individual learning needs of their students.
Furthermore, as a key component of teacher preparation, continuous professional development enhances teachers’ skills, supports ongoing learning, and contributes to
improved teaching quality (Desimone 2009).

Instructional level factors related to teachers’ instructional practices
At the instructional level, factors influencing teachers’ instructional practices included
tt3g42g, tt3g42l, AF, tt3g42a, tt3g42n, tt3g42o, and tt3g38 under the teaching strategy.

Small group work
The high value of the tt3g42g is positively correlated with teachers’ instructional practices,
indicating that small group work is considered an effective teaching strategy in the
classroom (Jackson et al. 2014). It motivates individual and group development, promoting self-directed learning and teamwork dynamics (Hammar Chiriac 2014). The positive
impact of small group work on enhancing students’ critical thinking and problem-solving
abilities is apparent (Springer, Stanne, and Donovan 1999). This study shows that teachers
generally perform better when using small group work strategies more frequently.
Research suggests that small group work can enhance learning depth and improve
students’ communication and social skills (Lou, Abrami, and d’Apollonia 2001).
Therefore, group work is critical in improving teachers’ effectiveness in the classroom
and is considered a significant strategy for enhancing teaching quality.

Quieten the class for the lesson
The high value of the tt3g42l is positively correlated with teachers’ effective instructional practices, suggesting that a quiet classroom environment may directly contribute to teaching effectiveness. The effectiveness of classroom management
significantly impacts students’ learning outcomes (Maharani 2023). To create a quiet
classroom environment, teachers must establish a positive atmosphere by providing
clear instructions, timely feedback, and engaging with students effectively (Emmer and
Stough 2001). Therefore, quieting the class for lessons is critical in promoting teachers’
effective instructional practices. This requires that teachers continuously develop and
improve classroom management methods to create and sustain an environment that
fosters learning.
Summarizing lesson content
In teaching practice, the role of the tt3g42a is to help consolidate students’
learning and improve the effectiveness of classroom teaching. The high value of
the tt3g42a is positively correlated with teacher performance in instructional
practices. An effective summary should stimulate students’ reflection and encourage them to connect new knowledge with existing knowledge (King 1994). This
study shows that teachers who frequently summarise course content exhibit
improved teaching performance. Therefore, summarising lesson content is a key
strategy to enhance teachers’ instruction practices performance. To summarise
effectively, teachers must know the timing, manner, and frequency of summarising
in the classroom.
Project work
The higher value of the tt3g42o is positively correlated with teachers’ instructional
practices, indicating that project work can improve teaching effectiveness. Project work
provides students with practical and contextualised learning experiences that enable
them to apply theoretical knowledge to practice situations (Helle, Tynjälä, and
Olkinuora 2006). The SHAP dependency plot reveals that the impact of tt3g42o on
teachers’ instructional practices could differ based on various implementation conditions.
The research suggests that teachers should consider the project’s complexity, students’
abilities and interests, and the availability of resources when they design and implement
project work (Blumenfeld et al. 1991). Therefore, project work can improve teachers’
performance in instructional practices. Teachers should motivate students to actively
engage in projects and offer sufficient assistance and direction to ensure the effectiveness
of the work.
Mastery of content
The high value of the tt3g42n is positively correlated with teachers’ instructional practices.
Teachers’ deep understanding of the subject material is an effective factor (Ahmed and
Shogbesan 2023). To effectively master content, teachers need to possess a deep understanding of the subject matter and be able to adjust their teaching content and methods
to meet the varying needs and backgrounds of students (Ball et al. 2008). Therefore,
teachers should continuously update their subject knowledge and be able to apply this
knowledge in different teaching contexts flexibly.

Target class size
The high value of the tt3g38 is positively correlated with teachers’ instructional
practices. This finding highlights the importance of smaller class sizes in enhancing
teaching quality and student learning outcomes. Reducing class sizes allows teachers to deliver more personalised instruction and cater to the unique learning
needs of students (Laitsch, Nguyen, and Younghusband 2021). The SHAP dependency plot shows that the level of teachers’ instructional practices begins to
decline at a certain class size. The study suggested that reducing class sizes can
enhance academic performance, while larger class sizes may lead to difficulties for
teachers in effectively managing instruction (Dynarski, Hyman, and Schanzenbach
2013).
Assessment and feedback
The high value of the AF shows a negative correlation with teachers’ instructional
practices. Frequent or excessive assessment and feedback could harm teaching practices,
while moderate evaluation and feedback can help students understand their learning
progress (Selvaraj and Azman 2020). The SHAP dependency plot reveals that while the
impact of AF is not significant initially, the SHAP value increases when a certain AF
frequency is reached. Complex nonlinear relationships may exist between the factors of
assessment and feedback and teachers’ instructional practices.
School environment level factors related to teachers’ instructional practices
At the school environment level, factors influencing teachers’ instructional practices
included t3satat under the job satisfaction and CDC under the school climate.

Teachers’ job satisfaction with autonomy in their target class
The high value of the t3satat positively impacts their instructional practices. Job satisfaction is directly related to teachers’ teaching motivation and commitment levels (Hidayat,
Suriansyah, and Noorhapizah 2022). When teachers have high autonomy in their classrooms, they usually feel more satisfied and respected (Friedman 1995). Furthermore,
autonomy can enhance teachers’ control over their work environment, reduce occupational burnout, and ultimately improve teaching quality and student learning outcomes
(Pearson and Moomaw 2005). Therefore, teachers’ autonomy and job satisfaction in the
school environment are crucial factors in enhancing instructional practice performance.
Classroom disciplinary climate
There is a negative correlation between the high CDC values and teachers’ instructional
practices. Research indicates that teachers’ passive and exclusionary discipline methods
may negatively impact the overall classroom environment (Mitchell and Bradshaw 2013).
This may suggest that the performance of teaching practices could be limited in classroom environments where discipline and control are overly emphasised. The SHAP
dependency plot demonstrates that the higher CDC scores may hinder teachers’ instructional practices performance. Hence, the effectiveness of instructional practices may be
limited in classroom environments that overemphasise discipline and control. Therefore,
teachers should strive to create an atmosphere conducive to classroom discipline.

It is worth mentioning that school leadership and teachers’ cooperation were not identified
as key factors influencing teachers’ instructional practices in this study. For instance, the
research demonstrated that distributed and instructional leadership positively impact the
quality of teachers’ classroom practices (Bellibaş, Gümüş, and Liu 2021). Similarly, the study
emphasised that teacher collaboration effectively enhances teaching efficacy and student
academic outcomes (Ronfeldt et al. 2015). This may be attributed to the study employing
machine learning-based feature selection, which identifies variables based on their statistical
significance in predicting instructional practices rather than theoretical assumptions. As
a result, despite the well-established importance of school leadership and teacher collaboration in traditional research, they did not emerge as key factors within the analytical framework
of this study.

Conclusion and implications
This study utilised ML methods to identify and interpret the optimal factors influencing
instructional practices among secondary school teachers in Shanghai, China. The study
revealed 10 key factors that differentiate teachers with high instructional practices from
those with low instructional practices. Among these, 8 factors have been identified to
have a positive impact on promoting teachers’ high level of instructional practices. The
identification of this optimal feature set emphasises the significance of these factors in
shaping the instructional practices of high-level teachers. Our study offers innovations
and contributions to teachers’ instructional practices. Firstly, this study identified three
dimensions that influence teachers’ instructional practices: individual, instructional, and
school environment level. “Small group work”, “Quieten the class for lessons”,
“Summarizing lesson content”, “Mastery of content”, “Project work”, “Target class size”,
“Qualification elements and preparedness”, and “Job satisfaction with target class autonomy” proved to be key predictors of teachers’ instructional practices. This study’s findings
enrich the theoretical frameworks of Danielson and Stronge by highlighting the significant role of diverse instructional strategies and the importance of professional development (Morris-Mathews et al. 2021). In the study, the significance of factors such as “group
work” and “project-based learning” reflects the positive impact of diverse instructional
strategies on teaching quality. Stronge’s view on the role of teachers’ personal qualities in
teaching effectiveness is supported by the empirical importance of “Qualifications and
Preparedness” and “job satisfaction” (Mitchell and Bradshaw 2013). Secondly, this study
employs ML to identify and interpret key factors influencing teachers’ instructional
practices, improving upon traditional statistical approaches. Using a combination of
RFECV and three ML algorithms, we identified the top 15 features contributing to
predictive accuracy, which were further refined to 10 key factors with the domain knowledge. These features were validated through variable model scoring analysis. Additionally,
GBM algorithm and SHAP values were applied to interpret the model, providing deeper
insights into how these factors influence instructional practices. This approach offers
a novel perspective on examining teachers’ instructional practices.
The study’s findings have significant implications for teacher development. First, teachers’
qualifications and preparedness provide the foundation for effective teaching (DarlingHammond 2000), highlighting the importance of sustained professional development to
meet evolving educational demands. Second, school administrators should actively support

teachers in utilising diverse teaching strategies to improve instructional effectiveness. For
instance, implementing small group collaboration and project-based learning fosters student
interaction and teamwork (Rai, Chhetri, and Rigdel 2023). Effective classroom management
further ensures a supportive learning environment (Yadav 2022), and summarising course
content effectively can help students better retain key concepts and improve their understanding (Dunleavy et al. 2022). Third, school administrators should focus on managing class
sizes effectively and improving teacher satisfaction by granting more autonomy in the classroom. This will help to create a more positive and supportive school environment for teaching
and learning. Reasonable class sizes allow teachers to effectively cater to the individual
learning requirements of each student (Bondebjerg et al. 2023), while satisfaction with classroom autonomy enhances teachers’ capacity to implement effective instructional strategies
(Dİlekçİ 2022).

Limitations and future directions
While this study provides valuable insights, there are still several limitations that suggest
directions for future research. First, the TALIS 2018 dataset might not have covered all the
potential factors influencing teachers’ instructional practices. In addition, missing values in the
dataset were handled through mode imputation, which could somewhat improve the validity
of the findings. Therefore, future research could consider integrating qualitative research
methods to obtain more diverse data (Kane and Kahwati 2023). Second, the study focused
exclusively on secondary school teachers in Shanghai. While this focus allowed for a detailed
analysis of instructional practices within a specific context, it may limit the generalisation of
the findings to other regions. Future research could employ longitudinal data to address the
limitations of cross-sectional data and explore additional contextual variables, enabling
a more comprehensive understanding of factors influencing teachers’ instructional practices
(Stewart 2012). Third, while the ML methods have shown excellent performance in identifying
key factors and enhancing model interpretability using SHAP, these explanations primarily
reveal the correlations among the factors. Future research could employ causal inference
models to gain a deeper understanding of the impact of various factors on teachers’ instructional practices.
