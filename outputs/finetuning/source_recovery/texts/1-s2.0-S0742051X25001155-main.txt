This study explores the impact of workload and time allocation on Chinese secondary school teachers’ prioritization of creative pedagogies using PISA 2022 data and hierarchical linear models. Findings reveal that
workload has no direct effect on creative pedagogies. However, time allocation for teaching-related tasks follows
a U-shaped relationship at the teacher level and an inverted U-shaped pattern at the school level. Digital resources enhance creative pedagogies while mitigating the effects of workload and time allocation. These results
highlight the need for balanced time management and strategic digital integration to foster creativity in teaching.
Limitations and future research directions are discussed.

1. Introduction
The escalating workload of secondary teachers has emerged as a
pressing global challenge, threatening the sustainability of education
systems worldwide. Over the past two decades, mounting policy pressures to improve student outcomes have precipitated significant increases in teachers’ working hours. For example, in Quebec, secondary
teachers reported dedicating 5 additional weekly hours beyond their
contractual obligations, often under strict administrative surveillance
(Riel & Messing, 2012). Similarly, German secondary educators exhibit
alarming workload trends, with 40 % exceeding the European Union’s
48-h weekly limit and 15 % working over 55 h (Kreuzfeld et al., 2022).
Globally, the OECD’s Teaching and Learning International Survey
(TALIS 2018) revealed that lower- and upper-secondary teachers
worked 712 and 778 annual hours, respectively, reflecting a 10 % surge
over twenty years (OECD, 2020).
Despite policymakers’ assumptions that extended hours bolster
pedagogical quality, a growing body of research demonstrates that
excessive workloads not only compromise teachers’ physical and
emotional well-being (Lawrence et al., 2019; Sanchez-Carracedo et al.,
2018; Wang, 2024a,b), but also hinder their prioritization of creative
pedagogies (Salkovsky et al., 2015). Defined as a dynamic combination
of teaching creatively and teaching for creativity, creative pedagogies
are often recognized as a series of learner-centered, open-ended, and
knowledge-connected practices essential for students’ creativity, critical
thinking, and problem-solving skills (National Advisory Committee on
Creative and Cultural Education, 1999; Jeffrey & Craft, 2004; Li & Li,

2019; Barajas & Frossard, 2018). However, these pedagogies demand
significant temporal and cognitive resources such as high engagement in
iterative lesson design, formative feedback loops, and reflective
collaboration (Swanzy-Impraim et al., 2023). According to Job
Demands-Resources (JD-R) theory (Bakker & Demerouti, 2024), excessive demands (e.g., long working hours) deplete teachers’ cognitive and
emotional resources, thereby crowding out the resources needed for
pedagogical innovation.
This tension is especially acute in secondary education, where
advanced subjects like physics and mathematics inherently demand
creative teaching to cultivate higher-order thinking. Concurrently, secondary teachers face unrelenting pressures from high-stakes testing regimes, institutional expectations, and societal demands. Nowhere is this
paradox more evident than in China, where the entrenched exam culture
surrounding the Gaokao and Zhongkao (Chen & Brown, 2013; Cheng &
Hamid, 2025) propels teachers to routinely surpass the 40-h workweek
and devote disproportionate time to administrative tasks and student
supervision (Gao, Zhou, & Leong, 2025). Ironically, despite China’s top
rankings in Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA), its
students underperform in creative thinking (Park et al., 2021; Yi et al.,
2013)—a gap that recent curriculum reforms have sought to address by
mandating creative pedagogies, paradoxically exacerbating teacher
workloads (Yan, 2015). This unique context positions Chinese educators
as a critical cohort for investigating the workload’s impact on pedagogical innovation.
Despite these challenges, three key research gaps persist. First, while
JD-R theory and existing studies have illuminated workload effects,

2. Literature review

While workload and time allocation significantly influence creative
pedagogies, these relationships must be examined within the broader
context of educational digitalization. Drawing on JD-R theory, digital
resources may serve as valuable job resources that potentially mitigate
work demands through task streamlining and efficiency gains. Empirical
evidence reveals a complex, dualistic impact that varies across educational contexts.
Research demonstrates digital resources’ capacity to support creative pedagogy through multiple mechanisms. Information and Communications Technology (ICT) tools could streamline tasks and reduce
workload burdens (Selwood & Pilkington, 2005), while ChatGPT facilitate personalized lesson planning and collaborative teaching approaches (Hashem et al., 2024). These technologies may conserve
teachers’ cognitive resources, creating capacity for pedagogical innovation. However, the implementation process may introduce new demands, including the need for continuous professional development and
the management of more complex classroom dynamics (Odegaard et al.,
2024; Wohlfart et al., 2023).
This paradox is particularly evident in China’s educational context.
While policy frameworks position digital technologies as workloadreduction tools, their actual implementation frequently increases
teacher burdens through mandatory training and documentation requirements (Li & Christophe, 2024) This creates a fundamental tension
between the empowering potential of digital resources and their controlling function within the education system. Liu’s (2019) large-scale
survey further reveals that teachers’ adoption of digital resources varies significantly based on their workload levels, subject areas, and individual attitudes, suggesting that digital resources act as conditional,
rather than absolute, moderators in the relationship between workload
and creative pedagogy. Given the mixed findings in existing research,
and based on JD-R theory, we propose the following hypotheses:

This study reviews literature from three aspects: teachers’ working
hours and time allocation patterns, the impact of workload and time
allocation on creative pedagogies, and the moderating role of digital
resources in these relationships. Building upon this comprehensive review and grounded in JD-R theory, we develop an integrated research

2.1. Workload and time allocation
Teachers’ workload generally refers to the quantity of work performed during specific periods, with total working hours serving as the
primary measurement indicator (OECD, 2020). Researchers typically
collect such data through self-reported surveys that document educators’ daily, weekly, or annual working patterns (Allen et al., 2021; Zhao
et al., 2023). Closely related to workload, time allocation examines how
teachers distribute their working hours across different professional
responsibilities, commonly assessed through structured questionnaires
(Creagh et al., 2023; Milem et al., 2000).
Current scholarship demonstrates considerable diversity in classifying teachers’ professional activities. Some researchers employ a binary
framework that differentiates between teaching-related tasks (including
lesson preparation, classroom instruction, and student assessment) and
non-teaching obligations (such as administrative work, meetings, and
paperwork) (Johnson et al., 2022; Van Droogenbroeck et al., 2014).
Alternative approaches offer more nuanced categorization systems,
exemplified by Jerrim and Sims’ (2021) six-dimensional classification
and Kong et al.’s (2023) four-domain model. To maintain analytical
clarity while capturing potential non-linear effects, this investigation
adopts the binary classification approach, following the methodological
precedent established by Van Droogenbroeck et al. (2014).

3.2. Variables
The dependent variable in this study is creative pedagogies,
measured by teachers’ self-reported ratings of the importance they place
on implementing creative teaching methods in the classroom. Examples
include activities such as “Finding ideas through brainstorming” and
“Debating ideas or current issues.” The scale comprises 7 items and has a
Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.814, indicating satisfactory reliability.
Responses were originally collected on a 4-point Likert scale and subsequently transformed into a synthetic variable, CREATPED, using Item
Response Theory (IRT). Higher CREATPED scores indicate greater
attention to and frequency of creative pedagogical practices in the
classroom. At the school level, the mean CREATPED score was calculated by aggregating teacher-level data using CNTSCHID to create a
school-average measure of creative pedagogies.

3. Methods
3.1. Data
The data used in this study is derived from the PISA 2022. PISA assesses the academic and non-academic performance of 15-year-old students nearing the completion of compulsory schooling. The PISA 2022
dataset offers several advantages, including a rigorous sampling
framework that ensures representativeness, a multi-level data collection
approach that enables the examination of both individual and schoollevel effects, and detailed time allocation metrics that allow for a precise assessment of how workload influences creative pedagogies.
This study specifically examines the responses of teachers and principals from Hong Kong and Macao, two Special Administrative Regions
of China, where high-stakes examinations and educational reforms
shape distinct teaching environments. The teacher questionnaire provides comprehensive background information, including teachers’ professional experience, working hours, time allocation, and teaching
practices, while the principal questionnaire offers school-level contextual variables such as school size and class size.

4. Results
4.1. Differences in workload and time allocation in Hong Kong, Macao,
and the OECD
Table 2 presents an analysis of differences in teachers’ weekly
workload and time allocation among Hong Kong, Macao, and the OECD
average, using ANOVA tests. The results indicate that teachers in Hong
Kong and Macao dedicate significantly less time to teaching-related
tasks, particularly classroom teaching, compared to their counterparts
in OECD countries. Conversely, they allocate more time to non-teachingrelated activities, such as student career counseling, extracurricular
involvement, and general administrative duties. Notably, Hong Kong
teachers bear substantially heavier overall workloads than their peers in
Macao and OECD countries, despite allocating significantly less time to
teaching-related tasks. These results highlight significant differences in
how teachers distribute their work hours, raising important questions
about how these variations may affect their pedagogical approaches.

5. Discussion
Grounded in JD-R theory, this study examines the relationship between workload, time allocation, and the prioritization of creative
pedagogies among secondary school teachers, utilizing PISA 2022 data
from Hong Kong and Macao, with a particular emphasis on the moderating role of digital resources. The research seeks to understand how
total working hours and their distribution across teaching-related and
non-teaching-related tasks influence teachers’ prioritization of creative
pedagogies in an increasingly digitalized environment. The study presents four key findings, contributing to JD-R theory by extending its
applicability and offering a more nuanced understanding of the underlying mechanisms.
Firstly, the study reveals that workload, when controlling for
teachers’ time allocation strategies, does not significantly correlate with
the prioritization of creative pedagogies at either the individual or
school level. This finding challenges previous research that emphasized

the teacher level, this relationship follows a U-shaped pattern, indicating
that teachers prioritize creative pedagogies more effectively when they
allocate a higher percentage of their time to teaching-related tasks.
Specifically, teachers who allocate more than 80 % of their time to
teaching-related work tend to be deeply committed to innovation,
demonstrating a higher prioritization of advanced and deliberate creative strategies. This finding aligns with previous research suggesting that
sustained effort and time are necessary for redefining teachers’ professional identity and refining instructional skills (Conradty & Bogner,
2022; Loerts & Belcher, 2024). Conversely, teachers who dedicate
minimal time to teaching-related activities may resort to superficial
creative methods, such as brainstorming or group discussions, to
compensate for inadequate lesson preparation. While these strategies
may add an element of engagement to student learning, they lack depth
and do not necessarily foster the development of students’ creative
thinking (Assemakis, 2022). However, this also suggests that when
teachers face high pressure due to long working hours and the high
demands for innovation, they may resort to these superficial methods.
This finding raises important concerns for schools and policymakers,
emphasizing the need to balance workload and provide sufficient support for meaningful pedagogical innovation.
Thirdly, at the school level, the study uncovers an inverted U-shaped
relationship between the time teachers dedicate to teaching-related
tasks and their prioritization of creative pedagogies. The highest level
of creative pedagogy prioritization was observed when teachers, on
average, devoted approximately 60 % of their time to teaching-related
activities. This balance enables teachers to engage in pedagogical
innovation while still having sufficient time for professional collaboration, interdisciplinary learning, and reflective practices—all of which
are crucial for fostering creativity in schools. However, as the proportion
of time spent on teaching-related tasks becomes excessively high, the
prioritization of creative thinking begins to diminish. In such cases,
teachers may experience cognitive overload, reduced flexibility, and a
diminished willingness to experiment with new teaching methods, ultimately limiting their ability to prioritize creative pedagogies (Moos &
Pitton, 2014). This finding is consistent with one recent research, which
emphasizes that greater autonomy fosters more creative thinking among
teachers (Orakci & Durnali, 2023). Therefore, it is reasonable to
conclude that teachers can only allocate time and cognitive resources to
creative pedagogies when they can effectively manage their workload.
This optimal allocation aligns with JD-R theory, which posits that resources like collaborative time and professional autonomy are essential
in balancing the demands placed on teachers, thereby enabling them to
prioritize creativity.
Unlike the U-shaped relationship at the individual level, which
suggests that a significant time investment is necessary for effective
creative teaching, the inverted U-shaped pattern at the school level
underscores the importance of work distribution and collaboration.

Innovation requires substantial individual effort, but it also thrives in
collaborative environments. If all teachers devote excessive time solely
to teaching, opportunities for professional exchange, interdisciplinary
collaboration, and collective innovation are diminished, weakening the
overall creative culture within the school. More importantly, a strong
culture of innovation reduces the individual time cost of creative
teaching. In schools where creative pedagogies are actively encouraged,
teachers can draw on collective expertise to adopt and refine innovative
strategies more efficiently. This explains why, at the school level, an
overemphasis on teaching time can suppress creativity. Innovation is not
merely a product of individual effort but also depends on an open,
cooperative, and resource-sharing environment. The contrasting findings at the individual and school levels significantly extend JD-R theory,
demonstrating that job demands at the individual level can be mitigated
through effective time management, while at the school level, the
availability of collaborative resources and a supportive work culture
play an equally vital role in fostering creative pedagogy.
Fourthly, this study highlights the crucial role of digital resources in
enhancing creative pedagogies and moderating the relationship between time spent on teaching-related tasks and creative teaching practices at both the teacher and school levels. This finding reinforces JD-R
theory, showing that digital resources can buffer the negative effects of
long working hours and foster positive work outcomes. In secondary
education, digital resources are particularly effective in streamlining
instructional procedures before class, facilitating dynamic learning experiences during class, and extending support beyond the classroom.
Before class, tools like ChatGPT assist teachers in automating routine
tasks, optimizing lesson planning, and refining resource allocation. By
reducing the time spent on administrative work, these tools enable educators to focus more on developing innovative teaching strategies and
fostering creativity in their instruction (Hashem et al., 2024). During
class, digital resources enhance creative teaching by providing diverse
instructional materials, facilitating real-time student feedback, supporting the design of assessments and interactive activities such as
quizzes and educational games, and integrating multimedia elements
like films, audio, and virtual simulations to enrich the learning experience (Villasmil, 2024). These resources not only make lessons more
engaging but also allow teachers to tailor content to students’ needs,
fostering a more personalized learning environment. They facilitate the
creation of interactive learning experiences that help students structure,
organize, and construct knowledge in meaningful ways (Engeness &
Edwards, 2017). By effectively integrating these tools, educators can
cultivate a more engaging, participatory, and intellectually stimulating
classroom environment.
6. Limitations and future research
Several limitations should be noted when interpreting these findings.
First, the use of cross-sectional data restricts the ability to infer causality
among the examined variables. Future research should incorporate
longitudinal designs and qualitative approaches, such as classroom

provide detailed guidance for teachers on optimizing their time management to support creative teaching practices. Third, this study does
not include the use of artificial intelligence (AI) in education, as PISA
2022 did not capture data on AI usage. Given the rapid advancement of
AI technologies, future research should examine their potential impact

was limited to Hong Kong and Macao, which may constrain the generalizability of the findings to other provinces in China or broader international contexts. Future research could expand the sample to include a

7. Conclusion

Data availability statement

This study investigates the working hours and time allocation of
secondary school teachers in an era defined by increasing digitalization,
high work demands, and a push for creativity-driven education, utilizing
PISA 2022 data from Hong Kong and Macao. The findings reveal that
overwork is prevalent in both regions, with teachers dedicating a smaller
proportion of their time to teaching-related tasks compared to their
OECD counterparts. A curvilinear relationship emerges between the
time spent on teaching-related activities and the prioritization of creative pedagogies, exhibiting a U-shaped relationship at the teacher level
and an inverted U-shaped pattern at the school level. Particularly, at the
school level, maintaining a balanced workload proves crucial, as allocating approximately 60 % of working hours to teaching-related tasks
results in the most favorable outcomes. Moreover, digital resources play
a significant role in enhancing teachers’ ability to prioritize creative
pedagogies, showcasing the potential of these tools in alleviating the
negative effects of overwork.
This study contributes to the JD-R theory by offering a more nuanced
understanding of the interplay between job demands, resources, and
outcomes. By disaggregating workload into specific time allocations, we
reveal a curvilinear relationship between teaching demands, digital resources, and creative pedagogical prioritization. This challenges the
conventional assumption that workload always inhibits innovation. The
contrasting findings at the teacher and school levels further expand the
scope of JD-R theory. At the individual level, teachers may require a
substantial time investment to prioritize creative teaching, while at the
school level, excessive time spent on teaching-related tasks can suppress
creativity due to the lack of collaborative resources and a supportive
work environment. Additionally, the study highlights the relevance of
JD-R theory in the Chinese context, where the secondary education
system faces intensifying workload pressures, high expectations for
pedagogical advancement, and rapid digital transformation. Digital resources play a critical moderating role by helping teachers manage
workload pressures, balance administrative tasks, and foster creative
teaching practices. The findings underscore that high demands can
challenge teachers’ prioritization of creative pedagogies, but these
challenges can be mitigated through effective time management and
strategic use of digital tools, making JD-R theory particularly applicable
in rapidly evolving educational contexts.
The study offers several policy implications, particularly for education systems characterized by standardized testing and rigid curricular
structures, which often pose challenges for fostering creativity in
teaching. In these contexts, policymakers must focus on optimizing
teachers’ time allocation, reducing unnecessary administrative burdens,
