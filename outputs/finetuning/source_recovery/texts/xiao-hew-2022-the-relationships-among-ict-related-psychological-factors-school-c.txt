Abstract
Evolving information and communication technology (ICT) reshapes people’s reading
activities by popularizing digital reading. Past studies focused on the relationships
between students’ frequency of ICT use and reading performance but neglected the
possible interrelationships between students’ ICT-related psychological factors, school
contextual factors, and reading performance. This study applied the Self-determination
Theory (SDT) to explore the relationships between students’ ICT-related psychological factors (perceived interest, autonomy, competence, and social interaction in
using ICT) and reading performance. The possible moderation effects of schools’
contextual variables (schools’ support in ICT devices and schools’ support in teachers’
capacity to integrate technology in teaching) were also investigated. Data from
222,293 secondary students from 10,103 schools in 47 economies who participated in
the Program for International Student Assessment (PISA) 2018 were analyzed using
three-level hierarchical linear modeling. The results provided support for the SDT by
conﬁrming the positive correlations between students’ ICT-related interest, autonomy
and competence and reading performance, and the accentuating moderation effect of
schools’ contextual factors. However, students’ perceived social interaction in ICT use

Introduction
Reading literacy can be deﬁned as the ability to understand the written language forms
required by a society or valued by the individual (Mullis & Martin, 2019). In recent
years, reading literacy has been expanded to include digital reading literacy since
evolving information and communication technology (ICT) has rapidly reshaped
people’s reading processing practices (Gan et al., 2015). Digital reading literacy is not
merely concerned about the ability to understand the written content but also involves
the ability to use basic ICT skills (e.g., scrolling or clicking on links), as well as
cognitive skills unique to the ICT context (e.g., building coherence among different
hyperlinks).
The transformation of the reading literacy concept has drawn international attention,
particularly within the research framework of international assessments. The Program
for International Student Assessment (PISA) is the ﬁrst international assessment to
evaluate secondary students’ ability to read digital texts (OECD, 2010).
Program for International Student Assessment is a triennial large-scale assessment
launched by the Organization of Economic and Cultural Development (OECD) since
2000, aiming to evaluate 15-year-old teenagers’ literacy in three domains, that is,
reading, mathematics, and science. PISA started to evaluate secondary students’
reading of digital texts by assigning a computer-based reading assessment in 15 participating economies in 2009. PISA 2018 further redeﬁned reading literacy by including digital reading literacy, underscoring the reading of dynamic texts (e.g.,
embedded hyperlinks) in the digital environment.
Program for International Student Assessment has also been offering an optional
ICT Familiarity Questionnaire asking students about their experiences with ICT since
2000. Given this situation, several studies investigating the relationships between
possible ICT-related factors and reading performance have been conducted (Juhaňák
et al., 2019; Kitisak & Piriya, 2020; Naumann & Sälzer, 2017; Petko et al., 2017), with
some focusing on ICT use for different purposes (academic vs. social) and reporting a
positive relationship between ICT academic use and reading performance but an insigniﬁcant or negative relationship between ICT entertainment use and reading performance (Kitisak & Piriya, 2020), some focusing on ICT use at different locations
(school vs. home) and reporting the negative relationship between ICT use at school

and reading performance (Juhaňák et al., 2019; Petko et al., 2017), and some reporting
that students who perceived ICT a useful learning tool achieved better reading performance (Naumann & Sälzer, 2017).
Despite the efforts to explore the potential inﬂuence of ICT-related factors on
reading performance, more research along this line is needed for two main reasons. First
and foremost, the majority of prior studies on ICT-related factors have focused on
investigating whether students’ frequency of ICT use improved or hindered their
reading performance (Juhaňák et al., 2019; Kitisak & Piriya, 2020; Vázquez-Cano
et al., 2020). A few have shed light on the inﬂuence of students’ ICT-related psychological factors (e.g., students’ interest and self-efﬁcacy in using ICT) on reading
performance (Lee & Wu, 2012; Naumann & Sälzer, 2017), or the inﬂuence of ICTrelated contextual factors at school (e.g., availability of ICT devices) on reading
performance (Hu et al., 2018). To the best of our knowledge, none have investigated the
possible interactions among individuals’ ICT-related psychological factors and
schools’ ICT-related contextual factors, and how these interactions may affect student
reading performance. The mutual interaction between students’ strong psychological
motivation and schools’ supportive context cannot be ignored in effective learning
(Deci & Ryan, 2015).
Second, previous PISA-related studies have been criticized for being under-theorized
(Eryilmaz et al., 2020), which has resulted in a long list of possible factors inﬂuencing
secondary students’ academic performance, including sociodemographic factors (e.g.,
socioeconomic status, gender), affective factors (e.g., enjoyment, self-efﬁcacy in
learning), and contextual factors (e.g., school climate, material resources) (Hopfenbeck
et al., 2018). However, these factors are often examined in isolation. Rarely are they
examined in combination under a theoretical framework to explore the relationships
among them. Research in this area is in desperate need of applying a unifying theoretical
framework to move the discussions beyond veriﬁcations of these isolated relationships to
capture the multidimensional nature of literacy development (Eryilmaz et al., 2020;
Hopfenbeck et al., 2018).
Therefore, this study aims to ﬁll some of the lacunae by drawing on the Selfdetermination Theory (SDT), a widely-used classical macro-theory in the educational
ﬁeld. Speciﬁcally, this study conducted multi-level modeling on the latest global data
provided by PISA 2018 assessment and its ICT Familiarity Questionnaire to (a) link the
student-level ICT-related psychological factors with their reading performance, and to
(b) elucidate the moderation effect of school-level ICT-related contextual factors on the
relationships between the ICT-related psychological factors and reading performance.
Overall, the results indicated that students’ perceived autonomy and competence in
using ICT predicted better reading performance, while students’ perceived social
interaction in using ICT predicted worse reading performance. When schools gave
more quantity-oriented support in ICT devices, the positive relationship between
students’ perceived autonomy in using ICT and their reading performance was accentuated. When schools provided more quality-oriented support in terms of teachers’
capacity to integrate technology in teaching, the positive relationship between students’

Literature Review
Student-Level ICT-Related Factors and Reading Performance
Previous studies on student ICT-related factors have largely concentrated on the relationships between the frequency of ICT use at different locations (e.g., at home or
school), or for different purposes (e.g., for entertainment or academic purposes), and
students’ reading performance (Gumus & Atalmis, 2011; Juhaňák et al., 2019; Kitisak
& Piriya, 2020; Petko et al., 2017; Vázquez-Cano et al., 2020), and to a lesser extent the
relationships between students’ interest in ICT, or students’ self-efﬁcacy in ICT, and
their reading performance (Lee & Wu, 2012; Naumann & Sälzer, 2017).
Speciﬁcally, ICT use at home was found to be signiﬁcantly positively correlated with
8th-grade secondary students’ reading performance (Skryabin et al., 2015), while ICT
use at school was found to be signiﬁcantly negatively related to their reading performance using the database of PISA (Juhaňák et al., 2019; Petko et al., 2017). On the
contrary, Vázquez-Cano et al. (2020) indicated that prolonged use of ICT at home was
negative for the development of secondary students’ reading performance.
Meanwhile, studies exploring the relationships between students’ ICT use for
entertainment or academic purposes and their reading performance showed inconsistent
results. Some studies reported that ICT entertainment use predicted better reading
performance while ICT academic use was negatively related to reading performance
(Gumus & Atalmis, 2011). On the other hand, some studies discovered an opposite
pattern, which indicated that ICT entertainment use played an insigniﬁcant or negative
role in reading performance while ICT academic use played a positive role (Kitisak &
Piriya, 2020).
Other studies attempted to unravel the relationships between students’ interest in
using ICT and reading performance. Low-achieving readers reported lower interest in
using computers in comparison to high-achieving readers. Meanwhile, students with
more conﬁdence in using ICT were found to achieve higher reading scores (Lee & Wu,
2012). Further, Naumann & Sälzer (2017) found that students’ perceptions of the
effectiveness of ICT as a learning tool had a predominant signiﬁcant positive effect on
students’ reading performance.

School-Level ICT-Related Factors and Reading Performance
In contrast to the substantial number of research on the relationships between studentlevel ICT-related factors and reading performance, research on school-level ICT-related
factors remains scant even though schools occupy a central position within the

1170

Journal of Educational Computing Research 60(5)

education system. At the school level, the availability of ICT devices was found to be
positively correlated with reading performance (Hu et al., 2018).
In addition to ICT devices, the teacher is also considered an important factor in
school-level reforms to integrate technology into the classroom (Scherer et al., 2019).
Previous studies discovered a comprehensive and detailed account of teachers’ capacity
to integrate technology in teaching, which includes support from technology specialists
(Razak et al., 2018), conﬁdence and comfort using technology (Anderson & Putman
2020) and beliefs on technology usefulness (Nelson & Hawk, 2020). Enhancing
teachers’ capacity to integrate technology in teaching is a complex process, which takes
systematic schools’ support for a collective set of factors (Dogan et al., 2021). The
extent of schools’ support for teachers’ capacity to integrate technology in teaching is
still extremely varied and such variance was one of the reasons for students’ differences
in reading performance (OCED, 2020).
Most previous large-scale surveys centered on gathering information about the
adequacy of ICT devices at school. Few investigated the teachers’ capacity to integrate
technology in teaching at school. As in PISA, only in the latest cycle of PISA 2018 did
relevant information about the degree to which schools support teachers’ capacity to
integrate technology in teaching was included in its school questionnaire (OECD,
2019).

Theoretical Rationale and Conceptual Framework
In PISA 2015, a new ICT engagement scale was introduced to the ICT Familiarity
Questionnaire by Zylka et al. (2015) and continued to be used in PISA 2018, the
construct of which was theoretically based on the self-determination theory (SDT). The
SDT was ﬁrst proposed by Edward Deci and Richard Ryan in the 1980s, which
identiﬁes three basic psychological needs: autonomy (individuals’ inner endorsement
of their own actions), competence (individuals’ ability to be effective in interacting with
the environment) and relatedness (individuals’ craving for establishing meaningful
connections with others) (Deci & Ryan, 1985, 2000).
In correspondence to the three psychological needs, four composite indexes
measuring students’ perceived autonomy, competence and relatedness when using ICT
were included in the ICT familiarity questionnaire. Speciﬁcally, students’ ICT interest
and students’ perceived autonomy in using ICT reﬂect their feelings of control and selfdirectedness when using ICT (autonomy). “Autonomy” refers to a person’s desire to be
self-initiating and to have a sense of control in accord with one’s own choices (Deci &
Ryan, 1985), and interests (Wang & Peverly, 1986). “Interest” is understood to be a
preference for certain activities, reﬂecting a sense of acting in accord with one’s own
desire (Schiefele, 1991), which is closely related to autonomy (Reeve & Jang, 2006;
Vlachopoulos & Michailidou, 2006; Wang et al., 2016). We therefore explored “interest” together with the dimension of autonomy in this study.
Students’ perceived competence in using ICT reﬂects their feelings of mastery of
ICT-related knowledge and skills (competence); Students’ perceived social interaction

in using ICT reﬂects their feelings of connectedness and belongingness to others when
using ICT (relatedness) (OECD, 2017). A high degree of satisfaction of the basic
psychological needs is conducive to increasing intrinsic motivation, which is more
likely to bring about improved performance in ICT-related activities (Hamari et al.,
2016).
The SDT theoretical model is also expanded to include school contextual factors.
The context providing nutriments for individuals’ psychological needs will stimulate
participants’ stronger intrinsic motivation in conducting activities and accordingly
bring about sustained personal growth. The context that fails to nurture or even thwarts
these psychological needs will induce adverse consequences (Deci & Ryan, 2012).
Therefore, it is the “dialectic between the active, growth-oriented organism and the
social context that is the central explanatory nexus for development” (Deci & Ryan,
2015, p. 487). Correspondingly, students educated at a school where ICT is abundant
and effectively integrated into teaching tend to achieve better ICT-related academic
performances and vice versa. In PISA 2018, school-level ICT contextual factors
(i.e., schools’ support in ICT devices, schools’ support in teachers’ capacity to integrate
technology in teaching) were newly introduced, reﬂecting the degree of support from
school to students’ ICT-related psychological needs.
As a leading theory in human motivation, the SDT has been widely applied within
traditional paper-based reading education, providing a framework for investigating
students’ reading intrinsic motivation construction (Baker & Wigﬁeld, 1999; Wigﬁeld
& Guthrie, 1997; Guthrie et al., 2005) and corresponding supportive classroom
practices (De Naeghel et al., 2014; Guthrie & Cox, 2001; Jutarat, 2016). However, the
SDT has not yet been used to examine reading activities in the digital age. The development in PISA assessment and questionnaire provides a timely opportunity to ﬁll
this gap by mapping the propositions of SDT onto reading activities in the digital age.
Therefore, in line with the SDT, the conceptual framework of ICT-related selfdetermination development on reading performance among secondary school students can be illustrated in Figure 1.
Drawing from the research lacunae summarized from previous literature, the current
study centers around the following two research questions (RQs) in view of the twolevel propositions of the SDT:
Research question 1: What are the relationships between ICT-related psychological
factors and reading performance among secondary students?
Research question 2: How would school-level contextual factors moderate the
relationships between secondary students’ ICT-related psychological factors and their
reading performance?
According to the propositions emphasized by the SDT, the present study put forward
two corresponding hypotheses as follows:

Method
Data
The main data source of the current study was obtained from the latest released cycle of
PISA 2018 (URL: http://www.oecd.org/pisa/data/2018database/). The reading assessment of PISA 2018 updated its assessment framework by fully integrating the
concept of digital reading literacy and assigning the computer-based format of assessment (CBA) in the majority of participating regions (70 out of 79) (OECD, 2019).
Speciﬁcally, the deﬁnition of reading literacy in PISA 2018 is expanded to include
“understanding, using, evaluating, reﬂecting on and engaging with digital texts in order
to achieve one’s goals, to develop one’s knowledge” (OECD, 2019, p. 28). The optional
ICT Familiarity Questionnaire was administered in 52 CBA economies. In the present
study, only the economies that completed this questionnaire were selected. Additionally, two compulsory questionnaires assigned in PISA 2018 (i.e., the Student
Background Questionnaire and the School Questionnaire) were employed for collecting students’ demographic information and schools’ contextual information. Data

Variables
As for student-level explanatory variables, four continuous composite variables of the
ICT engagement scale contained in the ICT Familiarity Questionnaire were selected.
The composite variables were derived from students’ responses to the statements on a
four-point Likert scale, ranging from “strongly disagree” to “strongly agree”.
Speciﬁcally, students’ interest in using ICT (INTICT) were derived from students’
degree of agreement with six statements, for example, “I am really excited discovering
digital devices or applications”; students’ perceived autonomy in using ICT (AUTICT)
were derived from student’s degree of agreement with ﬁve statements, for example, “If I
need new software, I install it by myself”; students’ perceived competence in using ICT
(COMPICT) were derived from students’ degree of agreement with ﬁve statements, for
example, “I feel competent using digital devices that I am less familiar with”; students’
perceived social interaction in using ICT (SOIAICT) were derived from student’s
degree of agreement with ﬁve statements, for example, “I like to meet friends and play
computer and video games with digital devices”. The four composite variables indicate
the degree of satisfaction of students’ three basic psychological needs in conducting
ICT-related activities, namely, autonomy (indicated by INITICT and AUTICT),
competence (indicated by COMPICT), relatedness (indicated by SOIAICT). The scale
reliabilities of the four composite variables were all high, with Cronbach’s Alpha
coefﬁcients above 0.8. Detailed information with regards to the derivation of the
composite variables can be found in the ofﬁcial 2018 PISA technical report (OECD,
2020).
As for school-level moderating variables, 11 items from the School Questionnaire
formed two factors of schools’ support for ICT integration into education using exploratory factor analysis (varimax rotation). These items were based on principals’
agreement or disagreement with relevant statements on a four-point Likert scale and
were developed for the ﬁrst time in PISA 2018. The Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin measure
(0.916) veriﬁed the sampling adequacy for the analysis and the Bartlett’s test was

Data Analysis
Hierarchical linear modeling (HLM) was employed in this study considering the hierarchical structure of the data in the current study (i.e., students are nested within
schools and schools are nested within countries). If continuing to use the multiple
regression model that assumes data at a single level, the possible correlations among
residuals of cases sampled from similar contexts might violate the assumption of
independence and cause inaccurate estimation (Hofmann, 1997; Raudenbush & Bryk,
1992). HLM is particularly designed to deal with nested data since this model simultaneously estimates the relationships between different levels of predictors and the
outcome variable and at the same time keeps these predictors at the appropriate analysis
level (Kozlowski & Klein, 2000; Raudenbush & Bryk, 1992).
In using HLM, the relationships among variables can be computed by the following
equations
Yijk ¼ γ000 þ π ajk Aijk þ β0bk B0jk þ γ00c C00k þ eijk þ r0jk þ u00k
where
Yijk is the reading performance for the student i in school j in country k;
γ000 is the intercept of the model;
π ajk is the slope of A student-level variables for student a in school j in country k;
β0bk is the slope of B school-level variables for school b in country k;
γ00c is the slope of C country-level variables for country c;
eijk is the residues for student i in school j of country k;
r0jk is the residues for school j in country k;u00k is the residues for country k;
In this study, HLM was performed in R computing language version 3.5.0 (URL:
https://www.r-project.org/) using the lme4 package. Following the recommendation of
Hox et al. (2017), this study applied the “bottom-up strategy” to build models with the
selected variables. Altogether, this study applied a ﬁve-step data analysis procedure.
First, a null model (baseline model) that contains no predictors but only with the
intercept to vary across contexts was built to test how many levels should be employed
in HLM. Intraclass correlation (ICC) value was calculated for each level to see if the
variances were signiﬁcant, namely, the higher-level variations explained partial outcome variable. Second, explanatory variables at the student level were added to the
model. As recommended by Studenmund and Cassidy (1987), control variables
(Gender, ESCS, SCHSIZE, SCHLOCA, LnGDP) were added to the model at ﬁrst, and
then main explanatory variables at the student level (INITICT, AUTICT, COMPICT,
SOIAICT) were added into the model. The ﬁrst two steps were used to solve RQ1, with
three following steps implemented to deal with RQ2.

Third, the random effect of slopes was added by entering into the model the schoollevel main predictors (SCHICTD, SCHICTT), which means that the inﬂuence of
school’s contextual support in ICT on students’ ICT-related psychological factors
varied across schools. Fourth, the interaction terms which reﬂect the interaction effect
between schools’ contextual support in ICT and students’ ICT-related psychological
factors were added to the model. The signiﬁcance of the interaction term reﬂects
whether there is a signiﬁcant difference between the slopes of the regression equations
with different levels of moderators (Dawson, 2014). Fifth, in order to further investigate
the exact strength and orientation of the moderation effect, the simple slope analysis
was conducted for the interaction term whose regressing coefﬁcient was statistically
signiﬁcant. The dependent variable (READ) was regressed on the student-level main
explanatory variables, high- and low-level of moderators, and the statistically signiﬁcant interactions simultaneously. Here, the high- and low-level of moderators were
computed as 1 standard deviation (SD) above and below the mean of the moderators
following common practices (e.g., Cohen & Cohen, 1983; Nastasa et al., 2021). In
addition, to display the nature of the investigated moderation effect intuitively, the
prediction lines of moderators at different levels were plotted.

Discussion
The aim of this study is two-fold: (a) to investigate the relationships between secondary
students’ ICT-related psychological factors and their reading performance, and (b) to
examine the moderation effect of school-level ICT-related contextual factors on such
relationships. Accordingly, two hypotheses were formulated. The discussions below
address the two hypotheses corresponding to the two research questions explored in the
present study.
H1 postulated that secondary students’ ICT-related psychological factors are signiﬁcantly positively related to their reading performance. The results of HLM provide
strong support for this hypothesis. According to Table 4, signiﬁcant positive correlations were found between students’ perceived interest, autonomy and competence
and their reading performance. Such ﬁndings are consistent with Lee and Wu (2012)’s
study, according to which students with a higher interest in using computers and more
competence in performing high-level ICT tasks achieved better academic performance
in reading. Possible reasons are that stronger intrinsic motivation to use ICT might
stimulate students to be more devoted to online reading activities, such as digging into
manuals of e-readers for effective utilization and selecting appropriate e-reading applications for better user experience and so on (Hyman et al., 2014). On the contrary, for
students whose intrinsic motivation to use ICT was low, chances are that they will not

In terms of schools’ provision of ICT devices, the positive relationship between

However, the negative relationship between students’ perceived social interaction in

students’ attention in reading activities, which leads to worse reading performance.
More access to ICT devices at school will offer students greater autonomy to read,
which can improve their reading performance. For example, Internet connectivity has
made access to reading materials more affordable and easier. Access to the Internet
avails the opportunity to students to ﬂexibly access almost all print books in the digital
format (Mlay et al., 2015). In addition, with the aid of the online learning platform such
as Moodle in reading courses, students can access any form of learning resources at
their convenience, read and reread the assigned reading materials at any time, and
become more responsible for their own learning, which contributes to their reading
comprehension improvement (Radia, 2019). The features of ICT can also strengthen
students’ autonomous engagement in reading activities, which brings about better

reading performance. For example, interactive e-books stimulate students’ stronger
willingness to interact with the text by highlighting or underlining words or text

and images when reading, which stimulates students’ eagerness to read what is on the
screen (Nikolopoulou et al., 2019).
We found that the positive relationship between students’ perceived competence in
using ICT and reading performance was strengthened when schools provided more
support in teachers’ capacity to integrate technology in teaching. Meanwhile, the
negative relationship between students’ perceived social interaction in using ICT and
reading performance was attenuated under this circumstance.
Although the bulk of ﬁnancial investments in schools’ educational programs focusing on ICT integration are allocated to ICT devices installations and renewal,
support for systematic improvement of teachers’ capacity to use technology remains
insufﬁcient (OECD, 2020). Teachers are the key to effectively integrate technology into
the classroom (Scherer et al., 2019). Regular training sessions aiming to develop not
just teachers’ necessary technical skills to use ICT devices (e.g., computers, interactive
whiteboards and tablets) but also their innovative ICT-empowered reading pedagogical
practices (e.g., strategies for navigating graphic novels using a dual coding approach)
will lay a solid foundation for successful ICT integration in the reading classroom
(Albion et al., 2015; Jack & Higgins, 2019; McClanahan & Nottingham, 2019). In
addition, teachers’ exchange of novel ideas and rewarding experiences in teaching
practices with their colleagues in regular forums is expected to facilitate sustainable and
scalable ICT integration in education (Prestridge & Tondeur, 2015). In participating in
these activities, teachers’ conﬁdence in employing ICT when conducting teaching
activities is largely boosted, so does their willingness to utilize ICT in a more effective
way (Bhattacharjee & Deb, 2016).
The above-discussed studies provide possible reasons for the accentuated positive
correlation between students’ perceived competence in using ICT and reading performance when schools gave more support in teachers’ capacity to integrate technology in
teaching. Likewise, these studies might also explain why the negative correlation between
students’ perceived social interaction and reading performance could be buffered when
schools provided more quality-oriented support in teachers’ capacity to integrate technology in teaching, while on the contrary, worsened when schools gave more quantityoriented ICT devices. In sum, the impact of ICT devices on student reading performance
has remained limited because schools’ support in ICT devices has not been accompanied
by schools’ support in teachers’ capacity to integrate technology in teaching.
Therefore, in order to improve the overall capacity-building for ICT integration into
reading education, it is important for schools to construct a long-term teacher training
program, instead of merely providing more ICT resources in schools. The teacher
training program should focus on the development of their technical and pedagogical
skills as well as positive attitudes towards technology use in the classroom (Ungar &
Baruch, 2016).

Conclusion
Based on the two-layer propositions of the SDT, the current study aims to construct a
multifaceted picture of secondary students’ reading performance in the digital age by
investigating the relationships between students’ ICT-related psychological factors and
their reading performance as well as the moderation effect of schools’ contextual
factors on such relationships. Accordingly, a three-level HLM was adopted in this study
to explore the two research questions based on data from 47 economies in the latest
global database of PISA 2018. Major ﬁndings were (a) Students’ perceived interest,
autonomy and competence in using ICT predicted better reading performance while
students’ perceived social interaction in using ICT predicted worse reading performance; (b) When schools gave more quantity-oriented support in ICT devices, the
positive relationship between students’ perceived autonomy in using ICT and their
reading performance was accentuated while the negative relationship between students’
perceived social interaction in using ICT and reading performance was more strongly
negative; (c) When schools gave more quality-oriented support in teachers’ capacity to
integrate technology in teaching, the positive relationship between students’ perceived
competence in using ICT and reading performance was strengthened, meanwhile, the
negative relationship between students’ perceived social interaction in using ICT and
reading performance was attenuated.
Theoretically, the present study provides support for the applicability of the SDT to
reading in the digital age by conﬁrming the integral role of students’ ICT-related
psychological factors in improving reading performance, and in particular, the signiﬁcance of schools providing contextual support to support students’ ICT-related
psychological needs. Methodologically, taking advantage of the updated PISA
2018 assessment which fully integrates digital reading literacy into its analytical
framework and PISA 2018 School Questionnaire which newly introduces schools’
ICT-related contextual factors (i.e., schools’ support in ICT devices, schools’ support in
teachers’ capacity to integrate technology in teaching), the present study constructs a
multifaceted proﬁle of secondary students’ reading performance. Three-level HLM
conducted on the latest global data increases the generalizability of the research
ﬁndings. Practically, this study highlights that in reading education, it is of fundamental
signiﬁcance for teachers to regulate teaching activities catering to students’ interest,
autonomy and competence in ICT use. Meanwhile, proper scrutiny and timely guidance
by teachers and parents is also needed for monitoring students’ ICT-related social
activities. As for school principals and policymakers, in addition to increasing the
quantity-oriented support in ICT devices, increasing attention should be paid to the
quality-oriented support in teachers’ capacity to integrate technology in teaching.
This study has a few limitations. Firstly, the self-reported responses from participants in PISA employed in this study carry the risk of subjectivity and bias. Future
studies may adopt multiple sources of data (such as gathering responses from both
students and teachers to measure students’ ICT-related psychological factors) to address this problem if feasible for a large sample size around the globe. Secondly, the
