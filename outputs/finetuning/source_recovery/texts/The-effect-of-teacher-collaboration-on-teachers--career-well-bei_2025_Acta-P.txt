The mechanisms and boundary conditions through which teacher collaboration influences teachers career wellbeing remain insufficiently explored. To address this gap, this study aimed to examine the relationship between
teacher collaboration and teachers' career well-being based on the conservation of resources theory, revealing the
mediating role of self-efficacy between them, as well as the moderating role of distributed leadership in this
mediation model, utilizing a sample of 3159 Shanghai teachers from the 2018 Teaching and Learning International Survey (TALIS) database. The results showed that teacher collaboration was significantly and positively
related to both teachers' career well-being and self-efficacy. Self-efficacy was significantly and positively related
to teachers' career well-being. Self-efficacy mediated the relationship between teacher collaboration and
teachers' career well-being. Distributed leadership not only positively moderated the relationship between
teacher collaboration and teachers' career well-being but also positively moderated the relationship between
teacher collaboration and teachers' self-efficacy. In addition, distributed leadership moderated the mediating
effect of self-efficacy between teacher collaboration and teachers' career well-being. These findings offered
important theoretical and practical implications for promoting teachers' career well-being, which were discussed
in detail.

through which teacher collaboration affects teachers' career well-being
remain unclear. According to COR theory, teacher collaboration, as a
conditional resource (Wang & Bai, 2025a), can enrich teachers' resource
pool by transforming it into personal characteristic resources (e.g., selfefficacy) (Halbesleben et al., 2014; Hobfoll, 1989), thereby enhancing
their career well-being. Collaborative practices enable teachers to share
teaching experiences and expertise (Xie et al., 2023), thereby enhancing
their self-efficacy and enabling them to respond positively to teaching
challenges and experience greater positive emotions, such as happiness.
Moreover, the acquisition and maintenance of teacher collaboration and
self-efficacy, as critical resources for teachers, are influenced by the
school environment (e.g., distributed leadership). Distributed leadership
emphasizes the distribution of power and responsibility (Wang & Bai,
2025a) and fosters a school climate characterized by support, trust, and
collective participation (Lin, 2022). Such an environment not only
promotes teacher collaboration and self-efficacy but also amplifies their
positive effects. Therefore, self-efficacy and distributed leadership may
function as mediating and moderating variables, respectively, in the
relationship between teacher collaboration and career well-being.
In conclusion, understanding how teacher collaboration affects
teachers' career well-being remains limited. Drawing on COR theory,
this study aims to examine the relationship between teacher collaboration and teachers' career well-being within the Chinese context, while
revealing the mediating role of self-efficacy and the moderating role of
distributed leadership. This study contributes to the existing literature in
two significant ways. First, it underscores the crucial role of teacher
collaboration in promoting teachers' career well-being and empirically
examines this relationship to offer new insights into teacher well-being
research. Second, as the underlying mechanisms and contextual
boundary conditions of this relationship have not been fully explored,
this study addresses this gap by highlighting the mediating role of selfefficacy and the moderating role of distributed leadership. In doing so, it
provides both theoretical support and practical strategies for enhancing
teachers' career well-being.

2. Literature review and hypotheses
2.1. Teacher collaboration and teachers' career well-being
Career well-being is defined as the long-term satisfaction experienced by individuals regarding their career outcomes, accomplishments,
and changes (Coetzee et al., 2021). Career well-being, which differs
from job satisfaction, represents a deeper and broader psychological
state. It encompasses not only satisfaction with a specific job but also
higher-level experiences such as career meaningfulness, achievement,
value, sense of belonging, self-realization, and personal growth.
Teachers' career well-being refers to a positive psychological experience
through which they realize their career aspirations and personal values
within their career trajectories. This well-being is closely related to the
degree of alignment between teachers' career experiences and career
expectations (Liu, Qiang, & Kang, 2023). Teacher collaboration, as a key
factor in promoting career development and personal growth
(Muckenthaler et al., 2020), has a substantial impact on teachers' career
well-being. It involves active, regular, and ongoing cooperative activities among teachers (Vangrieken et al., 2015; Wang & Bai, 2025a).
According to COR theory (Halbesleben et al., 2014; Hobfoll, 1989),
teacher collaboration, as a critical conditional resource within the
school context (Wang & Bai, 2025a), can help teachers enrich their
resource pools, mitigate the negative consequences of resource depletion, and thereby enhance their career well-being. Specifically, through
collaboration, teachers can share and optimize resource allocation, such
as collective lesson planning, the exchange of teaching strategies, and
high-quality lesson plans, enhancing teaching efficiency, reducing task
pressure, and increasing job satisfaction (Jang et al., 2023). Moreover,
collaboration promotes the establishment of a professional learning
community among teachers, fostering an atmosphere of trust, harmony,
and support. Emotional support and instrumental support provided by
colleagues can effectively help teachers cope with career challenges,
reduce burnout risk, and enhance career well-being. Therefore, teacher
collaboration is likely to be strongly associated with teachers' career
well-being. Liang, Song, and Sun (2022) found that professional learning
communities were positively associated with both hedonic and eudaimonic well-being among teachers. Liu, Cai, and Zhang (2024) showed
that teacher collaboration was significantly and positively related to job
satisfaction, which, in turn, significantly enhanced teachers' well-being
(Li, Ju, Kong, & Zheng, 2024). Based on this analysis, the following
hypothesis is proposed:
H1. Teacher collaboration is significantly and positively related to
teachers' career well-being.
2.2. The mediating effect of self-efficacy
Teacher self-efficacy refers to teachers' confidence in their professional competence and their belief in their ability to guide students toward achieving desired learning outcomes (Aalto et al., 2024; Wang &
Bai, 2025a). According to the resource flow perspective of COR theory
(Halbesleben et al., 2014; Hobfoll, 1989), teacher collaboration, as an
external conditional resource, can be gradually internalized into
teachers' internal personal characteristic resources through transformative effects, positively affecting their work status (Wang & Bai,
2025a). Therefore, self-efficacy is considered an important personal
characteristic resource (Halbesleben et al., 2014), and higher levels of
teacher collaboration can enhance teachers' self-efficacy (Çoban et al.,
2023). As Bandura (1986) noted, self-efficacy is shaped by three main
sources: direct mastery experiences, indirect vicarious experiences, and
social persuasion—each of which can be facilitated through collaboration. In collaborative settings, teachers have opportunities to learn new
instructional methods and strategies, leading to innovative practices and
the accumulation of direct experience (Liang, Song, & Sun, 2022).
Through interactions and knowledge sharing, they can benefit from

Meanwhile, according to COR theory, self-efficacy, as a personal
characteristic resource (Halbesleben et al., 2014; Hobfoll, 1989), enables teachers to manage stress and improve their well-being. Teachers
with high self-efficacy are confident in their ability to accomplish
instructional tasks, and this confidence motivates them to proactively
invest time and energy in enhancing their professional knowledge and
skills. When confronted with instructional challenges, they tend to
perceive them as manageable rather than overwhelming and actively
adopt problem-solving strategies, thereby avoiding the passive depletion of psychological resources. Higher self-efficacy also fosters greater
work engagement (Cai et al., 2022), allowing teachers to derive deeper
appreciation for the social value and meaning of their work. This contributes to enhanced job satisfaction and well-being (Fang & Qi, 2023;
Wang et al., 2024), maintains a positive psychological state and career
enthusiasm, and mitigates negative emotions such as burnout
(Haghayeghi & Moghadamzadeh, 2025). Therefore, self-efficacy may be
significantly and positively associated with teachers' career well-being.

correlated with the subjective well-being of special education teachers.

and self-efficacy for productive skills were significantly and positively
correlated with English as a foreign language learners' well-being. In a
study involving 375 Chinese secondary English teachers, Wang et al.
(2024) demonstrated that self-efficacy significantly and directly predicted teachers' well-being and also had an indirect effect through job

3.2.5. Control variables
Referring to related studies, this study included gender, teaching
experience, and educational background as control variables to more
accurately examine the impact of teacher collaboration on teachers'
career well-being.

3.2.2. Career well-being
Career well-being was assessed using eight items from the teacher
questionnaire (TT3G53A, TT3G53B, TT3G53D, TT3G53H, TT3G54B54E), such as “The advantages of being a teacher clearly outweigh the
disadvantages,” “If I could decide again, I would still choose to work as a
teacher,” “Teachers' views are valued by policymakers in this country/
region,” and “Teachers can influence educational policy in this country/
region.” A four-point Likert scale was used, ranging from “1 = strongly
disagree” to “4 = strongly agree,” with higher scores indicating higher
levels of teachers' career well-being. Liu, Qiang, and Kang (2023) used
these items to assess teachers' career well-being, demonstrating good
reliability and validity. The Cronbach's alpha coefficient for the scale in
this study was 0.852.

Teacher well-being is crucial for both individual development and
the overall stability of the education system (Dreer, 2023; Zhou et al.,
2024). However, previous studies rarely explored the mechanisms and
boundary conditions through which teacher collaboration influences
teachers' career well-being. Guided by COR theory, this study empirically investigated the relationship between teacher collaboration and
teachers' career well-being in the Chinese educational context, revealing
the mediating role of self-efficacy between them and the moderating
role of distributed leadership in this process. This section discusses these
findings in detail in relation to the existing literature.
First, the results indicated that teacher collaboration was significantly and positively associated with teachers' career well-being. This
suggests that higher levels of collaboration correspond to greater career
well-being among teachers. This finding aligns with the principles of
COR theory. From the perspective of COR theory (Halbesleben et al.,
2014; Hobfoll, 1989), teacher collaboration serves as a critical conditional resource (Wang & Bai, 2025a) that enables teachers to accumulate
and preserve the resources necessary to effectively navigate workplace
challenges and pressures, thereby enhancing their career well-being.
Moreover, this result is consistent with previous similar studies (Liang,
Song, & Sun, 2022). Through collaboration, teachers support one
another and work collectively to develop effective solutions to instructional and administrative problems (Xie et al., 2023). This cooperation
not only alleviates individual workload but also enhances their resilience in the face of work-related stress. Particularly in the context of
China's collectivist culture, the value of teacher collaboration extends
beyond the exchange of teaching strategies and resources, encompassing
collective identity, emotional bonds, and a shared sense of belonging.
Teachers often receive emotional support and positive reinforcement
from their peers, and this collective sense of achievement and trust
significantly enhances their career well-being. It is important to note,
however, that teacher collaboration within the Chinese education system is often facilitated by administrative mechanisms, such as the
mentor-apprentice system, “jiaoyanzu” (teaching groups), and collective
lesson planning (Liu, Keeley, et al., 2021; Xie et al., 2023). While these
administratively driven initiatives have increased the frequency of
collaborative activities, they may overlook the organic and voluntary
nature of collaboration, leading to formalized practices and passive

6. Implications and limitations
This study has important practical implications and provides targeted strategies for enhancing teachers' career well-being. First, school
administrators should acknowledge the pivotal role of teacher collaboration and prioritize the establishment of structured mechanisms to
support it. For instance, schools can utilize digital platforms to create
repositories for professional knowledge exchange, enabling timely access to and dissemination of instructional resources. Additionally, a
formal system of collaborative teaching should be implemented,
including weekly sessions for collective lesson planning, case analyses,
experience-sharing, and teaching log exchanges to promote professional
reflection. Second, it is essential to strengthen teachers' self-efficacy, a
critical factor influencing their career well-being. Schools should
develop a comprehensive professional development system that offers
diverse training formats and content to systematically enhance teachers'
instructional competence. Furthermore, timely feedback mechanisms
are indispensable. For example, open class systems can provide opportunities for instructional demonstration and improvement, while student learning portfolios can inform data-driven teaching adjustments.
Finally, distributed leadership serves as a vital institutional safeguard
that facilitates teacher collaboration, self-efficacy, and career wellbeing. School administrators should aim to enhance distributed leadership and encourage moderate decentralization of authority. For
instance, the staff congress system can be enhanced to encourage
teachers to actively participate in school affairs and decision-making
processes. Additionally, leadership training initiatives can also foster
teachers' leadership capacity, enabling them to assume professional responsibilities with greater confidence. Collectively, these measures
contribute to cultivating a collaborative and supportive school climate
that fosters teachers' long-term well-being.
This study has several limitations. First, using cross-sectional data to
explore the relationship between teacher collaboration, self-efficacy,
career well-being, and distributed leadership limits the ability to
determine causal relationships, suggesting that future research could
employ experimental or longitudinal studies for further analysis. Second, quantitative methods were used to reveal the roles of self-efficacy
and distributed leadership in the relationship between teacher collaboration and teachers' career well-being, resulting in a lack of qualitative
data. It is recommended that future research utilize mixed methods to
gain deeper insight into the process by which teacher collaboration affects teachers' career well-being. Finally, this study was conducted
within the Chinese cultural context, which may constrain the generalizability of the findings. Future research should validate and extend
these findings across different cultural or educational contexts.
