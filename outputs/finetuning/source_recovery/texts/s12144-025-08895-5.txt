Abstract
This study examines the relationships among the use of information and communication technology (ICT), reading enjoyment, reading metacognition, and reading achievement among Hong Kong students. The sample comprised 5,164 students who participated in the Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA) 2018 in Hong Kong. Descriptive
statistics, Pearson’s correlation coefficient, hierarchical regression and bootstrapping were used for data analysis to verify
the multiple parallel mediation effect. The results demonstrate that the use of ICT at school negatively predicts reading
achievement. However, reading enjoyment does not mediate the relationship. Use of ICT negatively predicts reading literacy through reading metacognition. The positive chain mediation effect of reading enjoyment and metacognition in the
relationship between the use of ICT at home for schoolwork and reading literacy is supported. Reading enjoyment and
metacognition negatively mediate the relationship between the use of ICT at home for entertainment and reading literacy.
Educators in Hong Kong should transcend the paradigm of achievement-focused education and improve reading metacognitive proficiency and performance by stimulating reading enjoyment and conducting metacognitive training through ICT.
Keywords ICT use · Reading literacy · Reading enjoyment · Reading metacognition · Serial multiple mediation · Chain
mediation model

Literature review
Theoretical framework
Information and communication technology and reading literacy Since 2009, the Programme for International Student
Assessment (PISA) has integrated the use of ICT into its
assessment system. Many studies have examined the impact
of ICT use on academic performance but have obtained
inconsistent findings. To address conceptual ambiguity, this
study adopts Pekrun’s (2006) control-value theory (CVT)
of achievement emotions as its theoretical framework. CVT
posits that learners’ appraisals of control (over learning
activities/outcomes) and value (attached to those activities/
outcomes) directly influence their achievement emotions
(e.g., enjoyment), which in turn affect cognitive and metacognitive processes and ultimately academic performance.
This framework is particularly apt for explaining the serial
mediation pathways (ICT use → reading enjoyment →
metacognition → reading literacy) central to our hypotheses, as it explicitly links environmental antecedents (ICT
contexts), affective processes (enjoyment), self-regulated
learning components (metacognition), and outcomes (literacy) within a coherent motivational architecture (Alobaid,
2025).
Within this framework, we clarify that ICT refers specifically to digital devices and applications categorized by their
primary usage context in PISA 2018:
ENTUSE (Entertainment/Social Use): Leisure-oriented
activities via smartphones, portable gaming devices, social
media platforms, and online video/music streaming services. HOMESCH (School-related Use at Home): Use of
desktop/laptop computers, tablets, printers, and educational

Current Psychology (2026) 45:180

software/platforms (e.g., learning management systems,
online databases) for completing homework or self-study.
USESCH (Educational Use at School): Utilization of
school-provided ICT infrastructure (e.g., computer labs,
classroom desktops/laptops, educational software, online
collaborative tools) under teacher guidance for instructional
purposes.

Information and communication technology and
reading literacy
Recent studies have demonstrated the significant impact of
ICT use on students’ reading literacy (Lei & Zhao, 2007;
Agasisti et al., 2020). A Chinese study revealed a negative
association between USESCH and reading performance. Lei
and Zhao (2007) discovered a negative correlation between
computer study exceeding three hours and grade point average (GPA), including reading scores. Empirical studies have
shown that ICT detrimentally impacts reading achievement
based on PISA 2009 (Lee & Wu, 2012). Numerous studies
in Western countries have shown that ENTUSE positively
impacts reading literacy, whereas USESCH and HOMESCH
negatively impact reading literacy. For example, Gumus and
Atalmiş (2011) found that the use of computers for entertainment is positively linked to reading achievement, while
the use of computers for learning is negatively connected
to reading achievement. Agasisti et al. (2020) claimed that
more frequently ICT use to complete homework at home is
associated with lower reading scores. However, the influence of HOMESCH and ENTUSE on students’ reading literacy in China remains unknown. Therefore, it is imperative
to investigate whether the impact of these three types of ICT
usage on reading literacy aligns with the findings of studies
in Western countries. Building upon the conclusions drawn
from studies in Western countries, we posit the following
hypothesis.
H1a USESCH negatively predicts reading literacy.
H2a HOMESCH negatively predicts reading literacy.
H3a ENTUSE positively predicts reading literacy.

The mediating role of reading enjoyment
There is no consensus on the definition of reading enjoyment in the literature. Pekrun (2006) introduced the concept
of academic emotion and distinguished negative emotion
from positive emotion. Reading enjoyment is a positive
academic emotion of enjoyment or satisfaction obtained
by curious individuals through reading activities (OECD,
2019). Academic emotions impact cognitive processes,

Page 3 of 13 180

decision-making, and motivation, playing a critical role in
education (Pekrun, 2006; Goetz et al., 2008).
Many studies have indicated a positive relationship
between academic enjoyment and academic achievement
(Raccanello et al., 2019; Zaccoletti et al., 2020). In particular, empirical studies have demonstrated a positive correlation between reading enjoyment and reading literacy
(Zaccoletti et al., 2020). Specifically, students who derive
enjoyment from reading are more inclined to choose challenging texts and engage in deep reading activities and are
more open to adopting effective reading strategies (Park,
2011; Ma et al., 2021). Therefore, individuals with higher
levels of reading enjoyment demonstrate greater reading literacy (Rogiers et al., 2020).
Previous research has indicated that USESCH and
HOMESCH can increase reading enjoyment, whereas
ENTUSE detrimentally impacts reading enjoyment (Park,
2011; Raccanello et al., 2019; Rogiers et al., 2020). An
empirical study has also demonstrated that the use of video
games has no positive effect on academic enjoyment (Goetz
et al., 2006). However, other studies found that utilizing
ICT for academic purposes both within and outside school
can increase reading enjoyment (Park, 2011), which in turn
affects reading achievement (Park, 2011; Raccanello et al.,
2019; Rogiers et al., 2020). The following hypotheses are
proposed.
H1b Reading enjoyment positively mediates the relationship between USESCH and reading literacy.
H2b Reading enjoyment positively mediates the relationship between HOMESCH and reading literacy.
H3b Reading enjoyment negatively mediates the relationship between ENTUSE and reading literacy.

The mediating role of reading metacognition
Reading metacognition is “a person’s ability to think about
and control his or her reading and comprehension strategies” (OECD, 2019). Metacognitive strategies used in reading tasks include setting goals, adjusting reading strategies
according to those goals, knowing how to summarize a text
or remember basic information, monitoring comprehension, and knowing how to solve comprehension problems
(OECD, 2019).
Numerous studies have found a positive relationship
between metacognition and reading literacy (Jairam &
Kiewra, 2010; Lee & Wu, 2013; Wu et al., 2017). Research
has demonstrated that students who receive metacognitive strategy training outperform those who are exposed
to only traditional teaching methods in terms of reading

180 Page 4 of 13

comprehension (Jairam & Kiewra, 2010). Furthermore, this
positive effect is particularly pronounced in online reading
as opposed to traditional paper reading (Lee & Wu, 2013).
Azmuddin et al., 2017) elucidated the importance of metacognition in reading online texts for academic purposes
through experiments and online surveys. Wu et al. (2017)
investigated the reading literacy and behavior of 15-year-old
students from various countries and regions and found that
reading metacognition positively impacts reading literacy.
However, many studies found that three types of ICT use
have negative effects on metacognition (Wu, 2014; Florit et
al., 2023). Empirical studies have indicated that engaging in
entertainment activities (e.g., online gaming and chatting)
through ICT negatively impacts metacognition based on
PISA 2009 (Wu, 2014). Based on the positive relationship
between metacognition and reading literacy, metacognition
has been shown to have negatively the relationship between
ICT use for recreational activities and reading literacy (Wu,
2014). Additionally, Li and Wang (2022) discovered a significant negative mediating effect of metacognition on the
relationship between frequent ICT use and reading literacy
via PISA 2018 data. This is attributed to the potential cognitive burden associated with limited familiarity with digital devices for learning purposes, as well as reading from
screens. (Ackerman & Lauterman, 2012; Florit et al., 2023).
Therefore, the following hypotheses are proposed in this
study.
H1c Metacognition positively mediates the relationship
between USESCH and reading literacy.
H2c Metacognition positively mediates the relationship
between HOMESCH and reading literacy.
H3c Metacognition negatively mediates the relationship
between ENTUSE and reading literacy.

The mediating effects of reading enjoyment and
reading metacognition
A review of previous research on reading indicates that an
increasing number of studies have explored the impacts
of ICT use, reading enjoyment, and reading metacognition on reading ability. Efklides (2018) proposed a model
of self-regulated learning that incorporates metacognitive
and affective factors, asserting that self-regulated learning
is a dynamic process in which the choice of strategies is
influenced by one’s abilities, metacognitions, motivations,
and emotions. Positive academic emotions (e.g., enjoyment,
curiosity) stimulate metacognitive and cognitive resources
utilized by students and ultimately affect academic performance (Daniels & Stupnisky, 2012).

In an online learning environment, previous research
has revealed that students experiencing greater enjoyment
employ more metacognitive strategies (Butz et al., 2015;
Price et al., 2018). Artelt et al. (2010) conducted a study on
German students in the PISA 2009 and found that metacognition is the strongest predictor of reading ability, followed
by reading enjoyment. Nevertheless, there is still a deficiency in reading research on the impact of reading enjoyment on the metacognition and reading literacy of middle
school students, especially within ICT usage contexts in
Hong Kong. The following hypotheses are presented.
H1d Reading enjoyment and metacognition positively mediate the relationship between USESCH and reading literacy.
H2d Reading enjoyment and metacognition positively
mediate the relationship between HOMESCH and reading
literacy.
H3d Reading enjoyment and metacognition negatively
mediate the relationship between ENTUSE and reading
literacy.

The present study
The present study aims to explore the relationships among
ICT use, reading enjoyment, reading metacognition, and
reading achievement, as displayed in Fig. 1. The literature
has shown that reading achievement is affected by ICT use,
reading enjoyment, and reading metacognition (Daniels &
Stupnisky, 2012; Kong et al., 2022). USESCH negatively
impacts reading performance among students in Hong
Kong. However, there is a lack of relevance in the existing
research to the relationships among HOMESCH, ENTUSE
and reading literacy in Hong Kong. According to Western
literature, ENTUSE positively impacts reading literacy,
whereas USESCH and HOMESCH negatively impact reading literacy (Gumus & Atalmiş, 2011; Agasisti et al., 2020).
The present study provides new insight into reading literacy
to the international research community by considering the
case of Hong Kong.
In the reading literature, the impact of ICT on reading
literacy remains poorly understood, with limited understanding of direct and indirect effects. The role of reading
enjoyment and metacognition in this context is largely unexplored. Reading enjoyment determines learning motivation,
strategies, and the metacognitive and cognitive resources
that students use and ultimately determines student academic performance (Zaccoletti et al., 2020). Metacognition
is a malleable variable that can be strengthened by supportive teaching and classroom practice (Brozo & Simpson,
2007). Some studies have shown that reading enjoyment

Data collection
ICT use

Methods

In the PISA 2018, the ICT Familiarity Questionnaire
included three types of ICT usage: ENTUSE (13 items),
HOMESCH (12 items), and USECH (10 items). Students
were asked to indicate the frequency of digital device usage
on a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (never or hardly
ever) to 5 (every day). This study utilized standardized
composite variables from the PISA 2018 (OECD, 2019).
The internal consistency coefficients (Cronbach’s α) of the
ENTUSE, HOMESCH, and USECH scales were 0.84, 0.94,
and 0.94, respectively.

Participants

Reading enjoyment

The present study used the Hong Kong sample from the
PISA 2018. The PISA 2018 placed particular emphasis
on reading and acknowledged the important role of ICT
in education, including an ICT Familiarity Questionnaire.
The PISA employs a two-stage stratified and proportional
sampling methodology. In the initial stage, schools are categorized into different strata based on school type (government, funded, and independent) and student ability level
(high, medium, and low), utilizing information provided
by the Hong Kong Education Bureau. The use of stratified
sampling ensured that schools from diverse backgrounds
are adequately represented in the sample. In the second
phase, an online survey was conducted with 35 randomly
selected 15-year-old students from each sampled school.
To account for sampling errors and measurement errors, the
PISA assigns specific sampling weights to both individual
students and sampled schools. According to the OECD’s
sampling criteria, a total of 6,037 students from 152 schools
in Hong Kong completed the PISA 2018. Students who
completed both the student questionnaire and ICT familiarity questionnaire were retained for analysis. After excluding
cases with incomplete information, such as on ENTUSE,
HOMESCH, USECH, reading enjoyment, reading metacognition or reading literacy, a sample of 5,164 students
was obtained. Among them, male students accounted for
49.4% (N = 2552), while female students accounted for
50.6% (N = 2612). Among the participating students, 69.0%
(N = 3563) were in Grade 9, 24.6% (N = 1270) were in Grade
9, and the remaining 6.4% (N = 331) were in Grades 7, 8, or
11. All students were surveyed in their first language, with
5.6% (N = 288) reporting English as their first language and
94.4% (N = 4876) reporting Chinese as their first language.

Reading enjoyment was assessed by five items (e.g., “Reading is one of my favorite hobbies”, “I like talking about
books with other people”) rated on a four-point Likert scale
ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 4 (strongly agree). The
internal consistency coefficient (Cronbach’s α) for this scale
was 0.70.
Reading metacognition
Reading metacognition encompasses understanding and
remembering (UR), summarizing (SM), and assessing
credibility (AC). Each aspect is assessed with a reading
task accompanied by a set of strategies. The UR scale was
derived from the ST164, which is composed of 6 reading
strategies. The SM scale was extracted from ST165, which
comprised 5 reading strategies. The AC scale stemmed from
ST166 and included 5 reading strategies. The three indices
were standardized across OECD economies, with a mean of
0 and a standard deviation of 1 (OECD, 2019). The Cronbach’s α values for the UR, SM, and AC scales were 0.79,
0.85, and 0.60, respectively. To calculate the score for reading metacognition, the scores of the three dimensions were
summed and averaged. The average score represented the
individual’s level of reading metacognition.
Reading literacy
The concept of reading literacy in the PISA 2018 encompasses three dimensions. First, the purpose of reading
extends beyond knowledge acquisition and potential development to encompass the achievement of personal goals
and societal engagement. Second, reading entails not only

comprehension, application, and reflection but also evaluation and active participation in reading activities. Third,
the scope of reading materials transcends traditional paper
texts to encompass all forms of readable, visible, and audible texts (OECD, 2019). The test results are presented with
10 plausible values. As these 10 plausible values yielded
almost identical results, this study reported a result based on

Data analysis
The statistical software package SPSS 25.0 was used to
conduct preliminary data sorting, transformation, descriptive statistics, and correlation analysis. To test the multiple
mediation effect model, Model 6 was employed in the PROCESS 3.3 program developed by Hayes. The deviationcorrected percentile bootstrap method was used to sample
5000 times.

Discussion
The present study is the first to simultaneously investigate
the correlations between three forms of ICT usage and the
reading literacy and the mediating role of reading enjoyment and metacognition in students in Hong Kong based
on the PISA 2018. This research contributes to a better
understanding of the impact of ICT on reading literacy
and investigates potential variations in this impact within
a performance-oriented educational environment, providing valuable insights for implementing effective interventions in educational institutions and households. Based on
the literature, this study proposes and tests three hypotheses.
In particular, new hypotheses (H2 and H3) are proposed to
bridge the research gap in Hong Kong. The main results are
discussed below.

The relationship between ICT use and reading
literacy
This study confirms that USESCH is not conducive to
improving reading literacy among students in Hong Kong
(H1a). However, the direct influence of HOMESCH and
ENTUSE on reading literacy is not significant (-H2a, -H3a),
which is inconsistent with findings in Western countries
(Agasisti et al., 2020; Park et al., 2015).

pronounced when computers are perceived as a supplement to traditional instruction rather than a substitution. In
essence, computers prove most effective when utilized to
extend learning time and practical opportunities, enabling
students to autonomously pursue individualized learning
objectives and fostering collaborative learning. This finding
underscores the suboptimal efficacy of ICT implementation
in Hong Kong schools, resulting in USESCH negatively
impacting reading literacy. This may also have contributed to the decline in Hong Kong students’ reading scores
observed on the PISA. Second, teachers continue to assign
homework in a traditional way, and students generally do
not rely on electronic devices to complete their assignments outside school (Moorhouse, 2018). Consequently,
HOMESCH does not significantly directly impact reading scores. Finally, due to the long time students spend in
schools in Hong Kong, they have little time to use ICT for
entertainment. Therefore, ENTUSE has no significant direct
effect on reading achievement.

The mediating effect of reading enjoyment
This study confirms that reading enjoyment negatively
mediates the relationship between ENTUSE and reading
literacy (H3b). In other words, ENTUSE negatively impacts
reading enjoyment, which subsequently affects reading literacy. This finding is in line with the conclusions drawn by
Li and Zheng (2018). In addition, reading enjoyment positively mediates the relationship between HOMESCH and
reading literacy (H2b). However, reading enjoyment does
not mediate the relationship between USESCH and reading
achievement (-H1b) due to the lack of significant improvement in reading enjoyment as a result of USESCH, which
contradicts research findings in Western countries (Roorda
et al., 2017).
This finding partially supports the literature suggesting
that differences in intrinsic incentives for students to use
ICT may account for this result (Raccanello et al., 2019).
When students utilize ICT for entertainment, such as online
chat and games, they may not prioritize reading, leading to
a potential decrease in their reading enjoyment. However,
students who use ICT for learning outside school without
teacher guidance have higher levels of reading enjoyment
and higher scores. Nevertheless, the lack of a mediating role
of reading enjoyment in the relationship between USESCH
and reading achievement may be attributed to the following factors. Educators in Hong Kong prioritize knowledge
transfer and academic achievement based on a utilitarian

educational philosophy (Zhang, 2019). Although ICT has
been integrated into the classroom, teachers often utilize
it primarily for knowledge dissemination and learning
resources rather than leveraging its potential to provide an
engaging and captivating reading experience. The findings
indicate that the utilitarian-focused educational approach in
Hong Kong has influenced the impact of ICT education.

The mediating effect of reading metacognition
To further explore the mechanism of ICT use on reading
literacy, this study confirms the mediating role of metacognition. Consistent with the results of Li and Wang (2022),
reading metacognition negatively mediated the relationship
between ICT use and reading literacy (H1c, H2c, H3c). This
indicates that ICT can impair reading metacognition, further
affecting reading performance. These findings align with
research results in Western countries (Ackerman & Lauterman, 2012; Florit et al., 2023).
The above findings may be attributed to several factors. First, regardless of whether students utilize electronic
devices for learning or entertainment, there is a decrease
in the amount of sustained attention and deep reading they
engage in during this process (Florit et al., 2023). Second,
computerized learning produces an environment in which
individuals tend to invest less cognitive effort or activate
less effective learning habits (LaRose, 2010). This suggests
that the adverse impact of ICT on metacognition may not
be attributed to inadequate technological support provided
by the medium but rather to learners’ insufficient access to
metacognitive resources required for successful task completion. Ultimately, ICT diverts students’ attention, underutilizes their metacognitive resources, and impacts reading
comprehension. Under heavy academic pressure, students
in Hong Kong habitually accept all the knowledge taught by
teachers without thinking (Zhang, 2019). This study indicates that ICT amplifies this detrimental cognitive pattern
in Hong Kong.

The serial multiple mediating effects of reading
enjoyment and reading metacognition
The present study confirms the positive chain mediating
effect of reading enjoyment and reading metacognition on
the relationship between HOMESCH and reading literacy,
addressing a previous research gap (H2d). Moreover, the
negative chain mediating effect of reading enjoyment and
metacognition on ENTUSE and reading literacy is confirmed (H3d). However, this chain mediating effect is not
significant for the influence of USESCH on reading literacy
(–H1d). This is caused by the nonsignificant relationship
between USESCH and reading enjoyment.

This study importantly reinforces the concept that reading enjoyment serves as a protective factor in the development of reading metacognition. Consistent with the
conclusions of Price et al., our findings indicate that heightened reading enjoyment is predictive of elevated levels of
reading metacognition (Price et al., 2018; Daniels & Stupnisky, 2012). This aligns with Efklides’ metacognitive and
affective models of self-regulated learning, which emphasize the interplay between positive academic emotions and
metacognitive development (Efklides, 2011). The findings
of this study underscore the significance of reading enjoyment as a protective factor mitigating the adverse effects
of ICT use on reading metacognition, subsequently influencing reading achievement. However, it is possible that
educators in Hong Kong prioritize knowledge transfer over
utilizing ICT to cultivate an engaging reading experience
(Zhang, 2019). Consequently, USESCH does not influence reading literacy through the mediating effect chain of
reading enjoyment and metacognition. Additionally, our
findings reveal that HOMESCH (school-related ICT use
at home) demonstrates potential to enhance reading enjoyment, which subsequently promotes metacognitive strategy
engagement and reading performance. This aligns with
Control-Value Theory (Pekrun, 2006): Structured academic
ICT use may foster perceived control (e.g., mastering digital
learning tools) and task value (e.g., recognizing homework
relevance), thereby triggering positive achievement emotions. Conversely, ENTUSE (entertainment-focused ICT)
significantly reduces reading enjoyment, ultimately impairing metacognitive development and reading proficiency.
This divergence likely stems from fundamentally different cognitive-affective engagements: While HOMESCH
encourages goal-oriented textual processing (e.g., synthesizing information from educational apps), ENTUSE prioritizes instant gratification through fragmented, multimodal
content (e.g., social media snippets, gaming narratives),
inadvertently training shallow processing habits that conflict with sustained reading comprehension (Georgiu, 2012;
Florit et al., 2023). Crucially, Hong Kong’s performancedriven education context amplifies these effects. Students
may perceive ENTUSE as “guilty pleasure” undermining
study goals, further devaluing reading activities (Daniels
& Stupnisky, 2012). These insights necessitate nuanced
interventions: Rather than uniformly restricting recreational
ICT, educators could leverage HOMESCH’s motivational
benefits by designing scaffolded digital reading tasks (e.g.,
curated online libraries with interactive annotations) to
bridge entertainment and academic literacies. Parents might
co-establish “digital balance” plans—preserving ENTUSE
for leisure while framing HOMESCH as autonomy-supportive skill development.

Implications and limitations
The findings yield three key theoretical and practical implications that extend existing frameworks on ICT-mediated
learning.First, our results substantiate Control-Value Theory (CVT) in digital contexts: The serial mediation (ICT
→ enjoyment → metacognition → literacy) empirically
validates Pekrun’s (2006) proposition that environmental antecedents (structured HOMESCH/USESCH) boost
achievement emotions through heightened perceived control (e.g., mastering digital tools) and task value (e.g.,
recognizing reading relevance). This challenges technodeterministic views by demonstrating that ICT’s positive
effects materialize only when pedagogically scaffolded to
foster cognitive-affective engagement—not merely through
increased usage time. Second, we refine Self-Determination
Theory’s application to digital pedagogy: The critical role
of reading enjoyment suggests that performance-driven
ICT integration (e.g., automated homework systems) may
undermine intrinsic motivation unless balanced with autonomy-supportive designs (e.g., student-curated digital libraries). This implies educators should reconceptualize ICT not
as efficiency tools but as affective amplifiers that modernize
reading experiences when aligned with psychological needs
(competence, relatedness, autonomy; Ryan & Deci, 2017).
This study has several limitations. First, the use of crosssectional data prevents establishing causal relationships
among the variables. It is challenging to make definitive
assumptions about the directional relationships among
ICT use, reading enjoyment, metacognition, and reading
literacy. Nonetheless, given the substantial sample size
employed in this study, the results hold value as a point of
reference. Future research should explore the causal relationships among these variables through longitudinal and
qualitative studies. Second, all the questionnaires used in
this study, except for the reading ability measure, relied on
self-report scales completed by the students. This introduces
the possibility of social desirability bias, as the accuracy of
self-reported data depends on the honesty of the participants
(Van de Mortel, 2008). To better examine the relationship
between the variables in this study, future research should
incorporate alternative measures such as teacher reports or
in-depth observations (Raccanello et al., 2013). Furthermore, the study assessed the frequency of only three specific ICT usage activities. In future research, it would be
beneficial to include additional factors such as individuals’
interest in ICT, self-perceived ICT skills, and exposure to
ICT-related resources to explore their relationships with
reading literacy in a more comprehensive manner.
