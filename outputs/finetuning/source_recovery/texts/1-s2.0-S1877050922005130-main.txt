2. Method
This study uses TIMSS 2019 database from Moroccan students Fourth-grade. It relates to 257 schools with 7500
students (3649 girls and 3851 boys), for which the average age is 10.15 years. We used in our application the R
Core Team [22], SPSS 20 and the IEA international Database (IDB) Analyzer1 . To answer the research questions, we
applied the ”One-way random effect ANOVA model” and ”Intercepts and Slopes as Outcomes Model” [23]. We used
the lme4 package [3] to estimate the parameters of these models and the lmerTest package [14] to test the significance
of fixed and random parameters. To create the GSR and the RMI index we used the partial credit model (PCM) [17],
and the R mice package [6] to impute the missing data.
2.1. Measures and Covariates
Data. TIMSS 2019 database from Moroccan students Fourth-grade 2
2.1.1. Dependent variables
The dependent variable is the average scores of students’ achievement in mathematics (from the five plausible
values) obtained by the methods of item response theory [18].
2.1.2. Explanatory variables
The variables in this study are aggregated into two levels, student level and school level [18].
Level 1 : Student variables : Gender (ITSEX) and Age (ASDAGE)
Level 2 : School variables : General School Resources index and Resources for Mathematics Instruction index
A. General School Resources index.. This index consists of 9 items: (1) Instructional materials (e.g., textbooks): ScQ13Aa (ACBG13AA); (2) Supplies (e.g., papers, pencils, materials): ScQ-13Ab (CBG13AB); (3) School buildings
and grounds): ScQ-13Ac (ACBG13AC); (4) Heating/cooling and lighting systems: ScQ-13Ad (ACBG13AD); (5)
Instructional space (e.g., classrooms): ScQ-13Ae ( ACBG13AE); (6) Technologically competent staff: ScQ-13Af
(ACBG13AF); (7) Audio-visual resources for delivery of instruction (e.g., interactive white boards, digital projectors:
ScQ-13Ag (ACBG13AG); (8) Computer technology for teaching and learning (e.g., computers or tablets for student
use): ScQ-13Ah (ACBG13AH); Resources for students with disabilities: ScQ-13Ai (ACBG13AI).
Response options: ”1” = Not at all, ”2” = A little, ”3” = Some, ”4” = A lot.
B. Resources for Mathematics Instruction.. This index consists of 5 items: (1) Teachers with a specialization in
mathematics: ScQ-13Ba (ACBG13BA); Computer soft(2) ware/ applications for mathematics instruction: ScQ-13Bb
(ACBG13BB); (3) Library resources relevant to mathematics instruction: ScQ-13Bc (ACBG13BC); (4) Calculators
for mathematics instruction: ScQ-13Bd (ACBG13BD); (5) Concrete objects or materials to help students understand
quantities or procedures: ScQ-13Be (ACBG13BE).
Response options: ”1” = Not at all, ”2” = A little, ”3” = Some, ”4” = A lot.

4.1. Schools Difference
The variance explained at the school level is 39.41% and the average score in mathematics achievement is γ00 =
376.84, S D = 3.75). The test of homogeneity of variance H0 : τ200 = 0 vs Ha : τ200 > 0 is significant at the .05 level.
Thus, the average score varies significantly across schools.
4.2. Student’s Age
At student’s level: the student’s age is negatively correlated with student’s achievement and is significant at the .05
level and wen the age increases by one unit the MathAchi j decrease by 4.84 units (γ10 = −4.84, S D = 1.19, p < .05).
(b) Across schools: the test H0 : τ211 = 0 vs Ha : τ211 > 0 is significant at the .05 level. Thus, the age effect is
heterogeneous through school.
4.3. Gender differences
The girls are favored over boys by 4.45 points, this difference is significant at .05 level (γ20 = −4.42, S D =
1.68, p < .05).
4.4. Resources for Mathematics Instruction
The RMI index is positively correlated with student’s achievement in mathematics and when RMI increases by one
unit, the student?s achievement increases by 8.23 units (γ30 = 8.23, S D = 2.43, p < .05)
4.5. General School resources
The GSR index is positively correlated with student?s achievement in mathematics and when this index increases
by one unit, the student?s achievement increases by 14.37 units (γ40 = 14.37, S D = 8.2, p = .08).
5. Discussion
Student level. After adding the variable ASDAGE in the Model 1, the proportion of variance between the school
increases slightly by .11% (PRV = −.0011). The result of the one-way random effect model : AS DAGEi j = γ00 +
µ0 j + εi j , µ0 j ∼ N(0, τ200 ), εi j ∼ N(0, σ2 ), affirms that, the student’s Age varies significantly across school, because
the test H0 : τ200 = 0 vs Ha : τ200 > 0 is significant (LRT = 266.3, d f = 1, p < .01). The heterogeneity in average age
is due to student failure, on the other hand, the Age effect is heterogeneous across school. After adding the variable
ITSEX to Model 2, the proportion of the variance varies from .11% (PRV = −.0011) to .01% (PRV = −.0001) it is a
negligible value.
School level. In the Model 4, the Resources for Mathematics Instruction decrease the proportion of inter-school
variance by 3.92% (3.91+0.01), but has nothing to change on the age effect in the presences of the RMI effect.
(γ10 (M2 ) = γ10 (M3 ) = −4.75), and a simple difference between the boys and the girls in the presences of the RMI
effect. (γ20 (M2 ) = −4.42 vs γ20 (M3 ) = −4.45). General School Resources decrease the proportion of inter-school
variance from 3.92 to 5.98. Thus, RMI index and GSR index explain 5.99% of the inter-school variance so that
PRV = (5.98 + 0.01)%. These resources have positive effects on student achievement, which confirms the study by
[7, 11].
6. Conclusion
This work studies the effect of some context variables like Age, Gender, School Resources (General Resources
and Resources for Mathematics Instruction) on students? achievement in Mathematics. This study shows that the
students? achievement is negatively correlated with the age and has a significant difference in gender. It also affirms
