A B S T R A C T
Machine learning approaches have emerged as powerful analytical tools for understanding student learning, 
which have become increasingly popular in recent years to complement traditional statistical analyses. However, 
there remains a limited understanding of how machine learning approaches are applied. This scoping review 
synthesizes 57 empirical studies that applied machine learning techniques to analyze data from international 
large-scale educational assessments. Specifically, the review explored the types of machine learning approaches 
used, the research questions addressed, the theoretical frameworks adopted, and the methodological advantages 
of machine learning approaches. Findings indicated that machine learning approaches, such as random forests, 
boosting algorithms, and support vector machines, were the most commonly employed approaches. Machine 
learning was primarily used to select important features, identify key predictors across student subgroups, and 
benchmark predictive performance across approaches. Notably, 28 out of 57 studies lacked explicit theoretical 
frameworks, while the remaining studies predominantly relied on macro- and meso-level theoretical frameworks. 
This review highlights several strengths of machine learning, including its superior accuracy in predicting out­
comes, capacity to handle large sets of potentially correlated variables, and ability to generate exploratory in­
sights that complement traditional methods. Concomitantly, we highlight the need for more theory-driven and 
methodologically robust applications of machine learning approaches in future studies. Given the growing 
popularity of machine learning, this review has promising implications for researchers who might want to draw 
on these approaches to advance research on student learning.
1. Introduction
International large-scale educational assessments have become 
valuable resources for educational research and policymaking as they 
provide rich and comparable data on student learning across diverse 
education systems (Kirsch & Braun, 2020; OECD, 2023). Extensive 
secondary analyses have been conducted using these datasets to inves­
tigate factors associated with academic achievement (see Hern´andez-­
Torrano & Courtney, 2021, for a review). However, most of these studies 
have relied on traditional statistical methods, such as multiple regres­
sion, structural equation modeling, and hierarchical linear modeling (e. 
g., Guo, Marsh, Parker, & Hu, 2024; Martin, Collie, & Malmberg, 2024). 
Although traditional statistical methods have been useful in generating 
important insights from these large-scale assessments, these approaches 
face several limitations, including issues of replication crises (e.g., 
p-hacking), multicollinearity, assumption of linearity, and an over­
reliance on a priori hypotheses, which may compromise the predictive 
power, reliability, and validity of research findings (Dwyer, Falkai, & 
Koutsouleris, 2018).
A small but growing body of research has highlighted the potential of 
machine learning approaches to complement and address the weak­
nesses associated with traditional statistical methods (Yarkoni & West­
fall, 2017). Compared to traditional statistical methods, machine 
learning approaches emphasize cross-validation and out-of-sample 
prediction, which could mitigate issues related to replication and 
reduce the risk of p-hacking (Yarkoni & Westfall, 2017). These

These strengths are particularly important when analyzing data from 
international large-scale assessments, which include a wide range of 
student-, school-, and system-level factors of student learning. By 
applying machine learning approaches, researchers can not only 
improve the prediction of academic outcomes but also identify key 
predictors of academic achievement that may not have been found 
through traditional statistical methods, enabling a deeper understanding 
of student learning. Recognizing the increasing adoption of machine 
learning approaches in educational research (e.g., King, Wang, Fu, & 
Leung, 2024; Pan & Cutumisu, 2024; Zheng, Cheung, & Sit, 2024), there 
is a growing need to systematically examine how these approaches have 
been applied in international large-scale educational assessments.
Compared with traditional statistical methods, the application of 
machine learning approaches in educational research remains at a 
nascent stage, with a small but growing body of studies beginning to 
adopt these methods. This trend is more pronounced in work drawing on 
international large-scale educational assessment data (e.g., F. Wang, 
King, Fu, & Leung, 2024; Zheng et al., 2024). Hence, a scoping review 
will help researchers “establish how a particular term is used in what 
literature, by whom, and for what purpose” (Anderson, Allen, Peckham, 
& Goodwin, 2008, p. 8), providing a comprehensive overview of an 
emerging and rapidly evolving field. By systematically mapping existing 
studies, this review responds to the growing need to move beyond iso­
lated methodological applications toward a more comprehensive un­
derstanding of how machine learning has been applied in research using 
international large-scale educational assessment datasets.
This scoping review synthesizes empirical studies that employ ma­
chine learning approaches, focusing on the methods used, the research 
questions addressed, the theoretical frameworks adopted, and their 
analytical advantages, providing a foundation for more theoretically 
informed and methodologically rigorous research in this emerging field.
2. Literature Review
2.1. International large-scale educational assessments
International large-scale educational assessments, such as the Pro­
gramme for International Student Assessment (PISA), Trends in Inter­
national Mathematics and Science Study (TIMSS), and Progress in 
International Reading Literacy Study (PIRLS), are standardized inter­
national evaluations designed to assess students’ learning outcomes 
across diverse countries/regions (Hern´andez-Torrano & Courtney, 
2021). International large-scale educational assessments typically 
involve high-dimensional datasets, comprising large sample sizes and a 
wide range of variables. For example, PISA 2022 collected data from 
nearly 700,000 students across 81 countries/regions, encompassing 
hundreds of student-, teacher-, school-, and system-level variables 
(OECD, 2023). The most recent TIMSS 2023 dataset includes more than 
350,000 fourth-grade students from 64 countries/regions and more than 
290,000 eighth-grade students from 47 countries/regions (von Davier 
et al., 2024).
These assessments provide rich and comparable data that enable 
researchers to conduct secondary analyses to deepen our understanding 
of student learning. International large-scale educational assessments 
offer unique advantages over primary studies as they are specifically 
designed to provide cross-nationally comparable data on student 
learning outcomes, attitudes, and contextual factors, enabling re­
searchers and policymakers to benchmark performance across educa­
tional systems. The breadth of their scope and the large, representative 
samples ensure a high degree of external validity, making findings 
generalizable across diverse settings. Furthermore, the standardized 
instruments for measuring student learning and achievement in inter­
national large-scale educational assessments allow for robust cross- 
country comparisons and advanced statistical analyses.
To date, hundreds of studies have been published using data from 
international large-scale educational assessments, with most employing 
traditional statistical methods, such as multiple regression, hierarchical 
linear modeling, structural equation modeling and other types of 
multilevel analyses (e.g., Guo et al., 2024; King & Wang, 2025; Martin 
et al., 2024; F. Wang & King, 2025; Zhang, Russell, & Kelly, 2022). 
While these studies have substantially deepened our understanding of 
factors that might affect student learning, they are limited by method­
ological challenges arising from the data characteristics of international 
large-scale educational assessments, such as the presence of highly 
correlated variables and potential non-linear relationships among 
variables.
One of the biggest concerns in analyzing international educational 
assessment data using traditional statistical methods is the risk of p- 
hacking, whereby researchers may unintentionally increase the proba­
bility of statistically significant findings through extensive analytic 
flexibility and selective reporting (Head et al., 2015). The risk of 
p-hacking has led to a replication crisis in the social sciences literature, 
including education (Malich and Munaf`o, 2022). Given the large sample 
sizes in international large-scale educational assessments, even trivial 
effects can become statistically significant, increasing the likelihood of 
p-value significance hunting (Gelman & Loken, 2014). Meanwhile, the 
large number of variables and potential model specifications may 
encourage post hoc hypothesis generation and testing, thereby 
increasing the risk of p-hacking.
Furthermore, most traditional statistical methods are built on the 
assumptions of linearity, independence of errors, and the absence of 
multicollinearity (Yarkoni & Westfall, 2017). These assumptions are 
often difficult to meet in international large-scale assessment datasets. 
For example, multicollinearity is a common issue given the large number 
of highly correlated factors measured in international large-scale 
educational assessments, which would distort the estimation of regres­
sion coefficients and distort the interpretability of research findings (e. 
g., Nalipay, King, & Cai, 2020). These statistical limitations highlight the 
need for more robust and flexible analytical approaches beyond tradi­
tional statistical methods.
2.2. Machine learning approaches
Machine learning refers to “a set of methods that can automatically 
detect patterns in the data and then use the uncovered patterns to pre­
dict future data” (Murphy, 2012, p. 1). Machine learning approaches are 
commonly categorized into supervised and unsupervised methods, with 
the primary distinction being the presence or absence of labeled 
outcome variables (Alloghani, Al-Jumeily, Mustafina, Hussain, & Aljaaf, 
2020). Supervised learning techniques (e.g., random forest) are applied 
to datasets that include both predictors and outcome variables,3
enabling the model to learn the relationships between them and accu­
rately predict outcomes based on the input predictors.
In contrast, unsupervised learning methods (e.g., k-means clustering) 
are used with unlabeled data to uncover underlying structures or pat­
terns, such as clusters or latent dimensions. Given that most studies 
employing international large-scale educational assessments aim to 
identify factors that predict student learning outcomes, which aligns

with the goals of supervised learning, this review specifically focuses on

Machine learning approaches are particularly well-suited to 
analyzing high-dimensional datasets collected from international large- 
scale educational assessments due to their capacity to manage complex 
relationships between variables without relying on strong parametric 
assumptions (Yarkoni & Westfall, 2017). Compared to traditional sta­
tistical methods, which require a-priori assumptions, machine learning 
approaches such as random forests, gradient boosting, and support 
vector machines operate within a bottom-up, exploratory paradigm, 
prioritizing predictive accuracy rather than hypothesis testing (Dwyer 
et al., 2018; Hong, Jacobucci, & Lubke, 2020). This flexibility enables 
machine learning to uncover the most important factors in student 
learning that traditional statistical methods might overlook. Moreover, 
many machine learning approaches incorporate built-in mechanisms for 
cross-validation, effectively mitigating overfitting and enhancing 
out-of-sample generalizability (Shmueli, 2010).
Machine learning also offers a remedy to address the problem of p- 
hacking by shifting the emphasis from null hypothesis significance 
testing to predictive performance (Yarkoni & Westfall, 2017). Rather 
than relying solely on p-values to determine the significance of pre­
dictors, machine learning models are evaluated based on their 
out-of-sample accuracy using cross-validation, bootstrapping, or 
hold-out sets. This paradigm prioritizes generalizability over statistical 
significance, ensuring the replicability of research findings. By empha­
sizing prediction over post hoc explanation, machine learning reduces 
the likelihood of spurious conclusions that do not hold up under inde­
pendent replication (Bzdok & Ioannidis, 2019).
Recent studies also highlight that machine learning approaches are 
more effective in addressing the problem of multicollinearity, a common 
issue in analyzing data from the international large-scale educational 
assessments. Unlike traditional statistical methods that produce unsta­
ble estimates when predictor variables are highly correlated, machine 
learning approaches eliminate redundant features or assign lower 
weights to less informative variables during model training (Yarkoni & 
Westfall, 2017). As a result, machine learning offers a more reliable 
analytical framework for exploring the relationship between predictors 
and outcomes.
Last, machine learning promises to enhance predictive accuracy 
compared to traditional statistical approaches (Yarkoni & Westfall, 
2017). Traditional statistical methods, such as linear regression or lo­
gistic regression, are often constrained by their underlying assumptions, 
such as linearity, normality, and independence of predictors, which may 
not hold in real-world educational data. In contrast, machine learning 
approaches are inherently more flexible and can model non-linear re­
lationships, high-order interactions, and complex data structures 
without requiring strict parametric assumptions (Hong et al., 2020). 
This flexibility allows machine learning approaches to capture subtle 
patterns and dependencies in the data that traditional statistical 
methods might miss. Hence, while traditional statistics are often more 
interpretable, their focus on parameter estimation may come at the 
expense of predictive accuracy in complex settings. By contrast, machine 
learning’s focus on iterative optimization and out-of-sample perfor­
mance makes it well-suited for applications where predictive power is 
paramount. Together, these advantages make machine learning a useful 
tool to complement past studies that relied mostly on traditional sta­
tistical techniques.
To date, machine learning approaches have been widely applied in 
educational studies (e.g., Gabriel, Signolet, & Westwell, 2018; Hu, Peng, 
& Ma, 2022a; Sanzana, Garrido, & Poblete, 2015; Zhang & Cutumisu, 
2024). These approaches serve as a valuable complement to traditional 
statistical methods and have provided novel insights into understanding 
student learning.
2.3. Previous reviews
While previous reviews have explored the use of machine learning in 
domains such as higher education and educational technology (e.g., 
Luan & Tsai, 2021; Fahd, Venkatraman, Miah, & Ahmed, 2022), few 
studies have reviewed its application in international large-scale 
educational assessments, despite their increasing policy relevance and 
data complexity. For example, Luan and Tsai (2021) reviewed 40 
empirical studies regarding machine-learning-based education. They 
found that most studies were carried out in online or blended learning 
environments among university students majoring in computer science 
or STEM. Fahd et al. (2022) reviewed 89 studies and outlined a 
comprehensive overview of the application of machine learning in 
higher education contexts towards a positive impact on student aca­
demic performance, at-risk, and attrition. In another study, Albreiki, 
Zaki, and Alashwal (2021) reviewed 78 studies and found that machine 
learning was used to predict the propensity of students to become at-risk 
and dropout. students at risk and dropout rates.
Although these review studies have offered valuable insights into the 
role of machine learning in educational research, they often overlook the 
unique characteristics and methodological challenges associated with 
international large-scale educational assessments (e.g., high dimen­
sionality). Few studies have systematically examined how machine 
learning approaches have been applied to international large-scale 
assessment data such as PISA, TIMSS, or PIRLS, which involve high- 
dimensional data characterized by highly correlated variables and 
potentially non-linear effects on academic learning outcomes. The scale 
and structure of these datasets pose significant challenges to traditional 
statistical methods, highlighting the need for machine learning ap­
proaches. Furthermore, previous studies have mostly focused on higher 
education contexts (e.g., Fahd et al., 2022) and targeted a limited range 
of outcome variables (e.g., dropout in Albreiki et al., 2021).
2.4. The present study
This scoping review systematically examined empirical studies that 
applied machine learning approaches to data from international large- 
scale educational assessments. Specifically, we identified the types of 
machine learning approaches employed, the research questions these 
approaches addressed, the theoretical frameworks underpinning the 
studies, and the key advantages of using machine learning in this 
context.
3. Methods
This study was conducted following the scoping review guideline of 
Arksey and O’malley (2005), which consists of five stages: (1) identi­
fying the research question, (2) identifying relevant literature, (3) study 
selection, (4) charting the data, and (5) collating, summarizing, and 
reporting the results.
3.1. Step 1: Identifying the research question
This scoping review study was guided by the following research 
questions:
RQ1: What machine learning approaches have been employed to 
study student learning using data from international large-scale educa­
tional assessments?
RQ2: What types of research questions can be effectively addressed 
through machine learning approaches in these studies?
RQ3: What theoretical frameworks have been adopted to guide the 
application of machine learning in this context?
RQ4: What are the strengths of applying machine learning ap­
proaches to the analysis of international large-scale educational assess­
ment data?

3.2. Step 2: Identifying relevant literature
To comprehensively identify relevant studies, four major databases 
in educational research were utilized, including Web of Science, Scopus, 
PsycINFO, and ERIC. Each database was searched for literature pub­
lished up to December 31, 2025.4 Boolean operators (AND, OR) were 
used to combine search terms to refine the search strategy. Table 1
presents the search strategy applied across the four databases.
3.3. Step 3: Study selection
We followed the Preferred Reporting of Items for Systematic Reviews 
and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) Statement to guide the selection of 
included studies (Tricco et al., 2018). As shown in Fig. 1, a total of 2969 
articles were identified through searches across the four databases. After 
removing 422 duplicates, we screened the abstracts of 2547 studies, 
excluding 1821 that did not meet the eligibility criteria. Additionally, 10 
studies were removed due to the unavailability of full text. For the 
remaining 716 papers, we applied the following exclusion criteria: (1) 
did not use quantitative designs, (2) did not use any machine learning 
approaches, and (3) did not draw data from international large-scale 
educational assessments. A total of 40 articles were included at this 
stage.
In addition to the primary electronic database searches, supple­
mentary strategies were employed to identify additional relevant sour­
ces of evidence. These included backward and forward citation 
searching of included studies, hand-searching reference lists of key 
publications, and consultation with field experts. These approaches 
identified 17 additional records after screening. As shown in Fig. 1, a 
total of 57 articles were included in the final scoping review.
3.4. Step 4: Charting the data
The first and second authors chart the data independently. All au­
thors recorded and reconciled the discrepancies. The following infor­
mation was extracted from the selected studies: publication journals, 
publication year, data sources, sample sizes, type of machine learning 
approaches, research questions, and theoretical frameworks in the 
model. Details of the included studies are provided in the Supplementary 
Materials.
3.5. Step 5: Collating, summarizing, and reporting the results
A total of 57 articles were published across 39 journals. The most 
frequently represented journals were Education and Information Tech­
nologies (N = 6), International Journal of Science Education (N = 3), and 
International Journal of Science and Mathematics Education (N = 3).
Regarding publication year, 19 articles (33%) were published in 
2024, followed by 16 (28%) in 2025, seven (12%) in 2022, seven (12%) 
in 2023, four (7%) in 2021, two (4%) in 2020, one (2%) in 2019, and one 
(2%) in 2018.
Regarding data sources, 44 articles (76%) used data from PISA, eight 
(14%) used data from TIMSS, five (9%) used data from PIRLS, and one 
(2%) drew data from the Survey on Social and Emotional Skills (SSES).5

Among these studies, 32 articles (56%) drew samples from multiple

4. Results
4.1. RQ1: The types of machine learning approaches
Fig. 2 shows the frequency of machine learning approaches used in 
the included articles. A total of ten machine learning approaches were 
identified in the 57 reviewed articles. The three most commonly used 
methods were random forests (N = 38), boosting algorithms (N = 24), 
and support vector machines (N = 23).
4.2. RQ2: The types of research questions
Machine learning approaches were employed to address three types 
of research questions. The first involved feature selection and impor­
tance analysis, aiming to assess the relative importance of various fac­
tors in predicting academic performance (N = 54). The second focused 
on comparative analyses to identify key predictors of academic perfor­
mance across different groups, such as gender, countries, and academic 
disciplines (N = 16). The third examined the predictive accuracy of 
different machine learning approaches, comparing their performance in 
order to improve prediction accuracy (N = 16). 6

4.4. RQ4: The strengths of applying machine learning approaches
Several strengths of applying machine learning approaches to large- 
scale databases have been identified. First, several articles in our review 
that conducted model comparisons found that machine learning ap­
proaches outperform traditional statistical methods in predicting aca­
demic performance and classifying students into subgroups with greater 
accuracy (e.g., J. Chen, Zhang, & Hu, 2021b; Khoudi et al., 2024).
Second, machine learning approaches are capable of analyzing a 
large number of predictors, even when these predictors are highly 
correlated. Among the 57 reviewed articles, the number of predictors 
included in the models ranged from 9 (You, Hong, Zhu, & Zhenhan, 
2025) to 486 (Khoudi et al., 2024), demonstrating the capacity of ma­
chine learning in handling complex, high-dimensional data.
Third, machine learning approaches complement traditional statis­
tical methods by supporting exploratory research. The research ques­
tions in most of the reviewed articles are developed without predefined 
hypotheses, allowing researchers to identify key predictors of student 
learning that may not be anticipated by existing theories (e.g., Arroyo 
Resino, Constante-Amores, Gil Madrona, & Carrillo L´opez, 2024; Gil-­
Madrona et al., 2025; Luo, King, Wang, & Leung, 2024; Zhang & 
Cutumisu, 2024).
5. Discussion
This scoping review aimed to synthesize empirical research on the 
application of machine learning approaches to understanding student 
learning using data from international large-scale educational assess­
ments. By systematically analyzing 57 studies, the review identified the 
types of machine learning approaches employed, the types of research 
questions addressed, the theoretical frameworks adopted, and the key 
strengths associated with using machine learning in this context.
5.1. The types of utilized machine learning approaches
Our review identified a range of machine learning approaches 
employed to analyze data from international large-scale educational 
assessments. Among them, random forests, boosting algorithms, and 
support vector machines emerged as the three most commonly used 
approaches due to their respective advantages.
Random forest is frequently employed due to its strong predictive 
performance, robustness against overfitting, and relative insensitivity to 
multicollinearity in high-dimensional data, making it particularly suit­
able for analyzing international large-scale educational assessment 
datasets (Breiman, 2001).
Boosting algorithms (e.g., XGBoost, LightGBM, and Adaptive 
Boosting algorithms) are supervised ensemble learning strategies that 
sequentially combine multiple weak learners to construct a strong pre­
dictive model, with each learner focusing on correcting the errors of its 
predecessors (Ferreira & Figueiredo, 2012). This approach offers several 
advantages, including improved predictive accuracy, effective bias 
reduction, and robustness to overfitting through regularization, which 
could improve the predictive accuracy of student learning.

Support vector machines are especially effective for classification

5.2. The types of research questions addressed
Based on the reviewed studies, we identified three types of research 
questions that machine learning approaches have been widely employed 
to address in analyzing data from international large-scale educational 
assessments. These applications highlight the methodological flexibility 
of machine learning approaches in advancing the theoretical under­
standing of student learning and facilitating evidence-based decision- 
making.
First, machine learning approaches are widely used for feature se­
lection by ranking the relative importance of a wide range of factors that 
influence student learning (e.g., Gil-Madrona, Guerrero-Muguerza, 
Infantes-Paniagua, & Martínez-L´opez, 2025; Luo et al., 2024; F. Wang, 
King, & Leung, 2022). This approach capitalizes on the strengths of 
machine learning in analyzing a broad range of variables while being 
less affected by the problem of multicollinearity (Yarkoni & Westfall, 
2017). The key factors identified through these approaches could help 
policymakers prioritize intervention targets and allocate resources more 
effectively based on the relative significance of different factors.
Second, the use of machine learning approaches in comparative 
analyses enables the exploration of similarities and differences in the 
factors influencing student learning across subgroups, such as gender, 
academic disciplines, and cultural backgrounds (King et al., 2024; F. 
Wang, King, & Leung, 2023). These analyses deepen our understanding 
of how various factors contribute differently to academic learning across

5.3. Theoretical frameworks used in machine learning approaches
In the reviewed studies, we found that 28 out of 57 studies did not 
adopt a specific theoretical framework, which raises important consid­
erations regarding the conceptual underpinnings of machine learning 
studies in education. The absence of explicit theoretical frameworks 
limits researchers’ ability to explain why certain results emerge, leading 
to findings that are descriptively rich but theoretically shallow. Hence, 
there is a growing need for theory-informed applications of machine 
learning in educational research.
For the remaining studies, macro- and meso-level theoretical 
frameworks such as socio-ecological theory, the process-person-context- 
time (PPCT) model, and the opportunity-propensity framework were 
frequently employed to guide variable selection and interpret findings. 
One commonality of these frameworks is their emphasis on examining 
factors at different levels, from the individual to broader family, school, 
and system contexts (Bronfenbrenner, 1979). This multilevel perspec­
tive aligns well with the strengths of machine learning approaches, 
which are particularly well-suited for analyzing high-dimensional data 
involving a wide range of potentially correlated variables (Yarkoni & 
Westfall, 2017). The adoption of macro- and meso-level theoretical 
frameworks would also allow for the simultaneous consideration of a 
broad set of predictors, enabling researchers to get a holistic under­
standing of factors affecting student learning.
5.4. The strengths of applying machine learning approaches
This review study revealed several advantages of machine learning 
approaches in analyzing international large-scale educational assess­
ment databases. First, machine learning approaches demonstrate supe­
rior predictive accuracy compared to traditional statistical methods, 
making them particularly effective for predicting student achievement 
and classifying subgroups (J. Chen et al., 2021b; Khoudi et al., 2024). 
This advantage may stem from the capability of machine learning to 
handle multicollinearity more effectively and capture complex, 
non-linear relationships between predictors and outcomes (Hong et al., 
2020).
Second, machine learning approaches enable researchers to simul­
taneously analyze a wide range of predictors while minimizing issues 
related to multicollinearity and overfitting (Yarkoni & Westfall, 2017). 
This advantage is particularly important when analyzing data from in­
ternational large-scale educational assessments, which often involve 
numerous variables across multiple levels.
Third, machine learning provides an exploratory and data-driven 
analytical framework, which complements the confirmatory logic of 
traditional statistical models that rely on pre-specified hypotheses 
(Hindman, 2015; Hong et al., 2020). By facilitating the generation of 
novel findings that may not be anticipated by theory-driven approaches, 
machine learning contributes to a deeper understanding of the complex 
factors underlying student learning.
5.5. Implications and directions for future research
This study has both theoretical and practical implications. Theoret­
ically, this scoping review extends prior research by offering a 
comprehensive overview of how machine learning approaches have 
been applied in studies using international large-scale educational as­
sessments. Rather than focusing on isolated empirical findings, our 
study maps the evolving landscape of machine learning-based research. 
By situating machine learning within broader theoretical and method­
ological conversations in educational research, this study advances the 
field by clarifying how machine learning can deepen our understanding 
of student learning.
Furthermore, this scoping review highlights the underutilization of 
theoretical frameworks in current applications of machine learning ap­
proaches. Although machine learning provides powerful tools for 
analyzing data collected from international large-scale educational as­
sessments, many studies rely heavily on data-driven approaches with 
limited engagement with educational theories to guide research design, 
variable selection, and result interpretation. This finding argues for a 
more theory-informed use of machine learning, in which analytical 
choices are explicitly aligned with established conceptual frameworks to 
enhance interpretability, cumulative knowledge building, and theoret­
ical contribution.
Practically, the review provides clear insights into the types of ma­
chine learning methods used, the research questions addressed, the 
theoretical frameworks adopted, and the methodological advantages of 
machine learning approaches. These findings serve as a roadmap for 
researchers to consider the application of machine learning approaches 
in their studies with particular research objectives and data character­
istics. These findings are particularly relevant for educational re­
searchers seeking to examine the factors shaping student learning. This 
review clarifies the types of research questions that can be addressed 
when using machine learning approaches, such as assessing the relative 
importance of multiple predictors, identifying key factors associated 
with academic performance across diverse student groups and contexts,

advantages, this review offers a clearer framework for leveraging ma­

particularly in the context of international large-scale educational

This review is also important for policymakers and practitioners. 
This study highlights the advantages of machine learning in addressing 
key methodological challenges commonly encountered in using inter­
national large-scale educational assessments. By leveraging the flexi­
bility of machine learning approaches, policymakers can obtain more 
robust and reliable findings, strengthening the empirical foundation for 
evidence-informed decision-making.
Furthermore, the reviewed studies are grounded in a set of seminal 
works that have shaped current applications of machine learning ap­
proaches. For example, Yarkoni and Westfall (2017) emphasized the 
value of prioritizing prediction over explanation, arguing that machine 
learning methods can offer substantial benefits, including reducing bias 
associated with p-hacking, improving research efficiency, enabling sys­
tematic evaluation of model performance, and enhancing interpret­
ability. Hong et al. (2020) further advanced this perspective by 
proposing a deductive data-mining framework that integrates theoret­
ical reasoning with data-driven modeling, demonstrating how machine 
learning can complement rather than replace theory-driven inquiry. In 
addition, Lones (2021) and Pargent, Schoedel, and Stachl (2023) pro­
vided practical methodological guidance for the research design, eval­
uation, and interpretation of machine learning models, emphasizing 
transparency, robustness, and alignment between research questions 
and analytical choices. These studies provide practical guidance for re­
searchers interested in applying machine learning approaches in their 
work.
Despite its contributions, this study has several limitations. First, the 
scope of the review is confined to the international large-scale educa­
tional assessments due to the distinct characteristics of these datasets. 
However, machine learning approaches have also been increasingly 
applied in other domains, such as higher education (Basnet, Johnson, & 
Doleck, 2022; Demeter et al., 2022) and educational technology 
(Jebbari, Cherradi, Hamida, & Raihani, 2024; Zhai, Yuan, Liu, & Wang, 
2024). Future reviews could adopt a broader scope by including studies 
from different research domains to provide a more comprehensive 
overview.
Second, this review emphasizes the application trends and research 
contributions of machine learning approaches, rather than providing 
technical guidelines for model development and evaluation. As the 
application of machine learning becomes increasingly popular in 
educational research, it is critical to ensure the rigor and transparency of 
model development. Future reviews may consider incorporating meth­
odological quality assessments to evaluate the robustness and trans­
parency of machine learning applications in education research.
6. Conclusion
Machine learning approaches are powerful tools for researchers to 
understand student learning using data from international large-scale 
educational assessments. Based on a synthesis of 57 published studies, 
this scoping review provides a comprehensive overview of the types of 
machine learning approaches employed, the research questions 
addressed, the theoretical frameworks integrated, and the methodo­
logical strengths of using machine learning approaches. Moreover, the 
review highlights how machine learning can complement traditional 
statistical methods by supporting exploratory, data-driven analysis 
while also aligning with broader theoretical perspectives to deepen our 
understanding of student learning. As the availability of international 
large-scale educational assessment data continues to grow, machine 
learning stands to play an increasingly central role in advancing theory 
and informing policy.
