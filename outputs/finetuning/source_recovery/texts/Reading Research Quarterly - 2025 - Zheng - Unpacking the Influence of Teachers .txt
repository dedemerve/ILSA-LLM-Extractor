ABSTRACT

This study examines teacher reading instruction strategies, defined as specific teaching activities aimed at developing students'
reading comprehension skills, including guiding students to identify main ideas, make inferences, predict text content, self-monitor understanding, and evaluate text credibility. Using PIRLS 2021 data from primary schools, the study analyzes how the
frequency of these instructional strategies affects students' reading achievement. Regression results show a significant positive
relationship between the use of diverse reading instruction strategies and student reading performance. Advanced methods
such as double machine learning (DML) and instrumental variable (IV) methods further confirm a robust causal impact of these
teaching strategies, even after controlling for potential biases and endogeneity. The effect is particularly strong when teachers
engage in daily reading for pleasure and when students have a solid literacy foundation. Additionally, mediation analysis reveals
that teacher assessment strategies partially mediate the positive impact of reading instruction, highlighting the role of formative
assessment in supporting reading development. These findings emphasize the importance of equipping teachers with effective
reading instructional practices and fostering teacher reading engagement to enhance literacy outcomes. The study offers practical implications for educational policymakers aiming to improve reading achievement through evidence-­based instructional
and assessment strategies.

1   |   Introduction
Reading proficiency is widely recognized as a cornerstone of academic success and a fundamental skill necessary for lifelong
learning (Cadiz-­Gabejan and Quirino 2021; Hanemann 2015;
Horbec 2012). According to the Organisation for Economic
Co-­operation and Development (OECD 2023a), students who
attain higher levels of reading proficiency are better equipped
to meet real-­life challenges and perform effectively across various subjects. Reading skills are not only essential for language-related disciplines but also play a crucial role in subjects such
as science and mathematics, where students need to interpret

written materials and apply them to problem-­solving scenarios (Akbasli et al. 2016; Nation 2019; Zhu 2022). Furthermore,
UNESCO (2020) emphasizes that literacy is a fundamental
human right and the foundation for sustainable development,
highlighting its importance in improving health and nutrition
and reducing poverty worldwide.
Given the foundational importance of reading proficiency for
educational success, it becomes critical to examine how certain
education systems consistently achieve high reading outcomes.
East Asian systems, in particular, provide valuable cases for understanding how instructional and cultural factors interact to

Beyond cultural commonalities, the structural and policy characteristics of East Asian regions offer further insight into how
reading instruction is shaped by both convergence and local
differentiation. As rapidly developing knowledge economies,
Hong Kong, Macau, Taiwan, and Singapore represent four of
the most influential and policy-­innovative education systems in
East Asia. Despite the diverse sociopolitical and linguistic contexts of these four regions, their approaches to reading education
reveal convergent tendencies rooted in shared cultural heritage
and institutional legacies (Chien 2012; Curdt-­Christiansen and
Silver 2013; Reynolds et al. 2024; Wan 2016; Wu 2012). Against
this backdrop, a “pooling and differentiation” approach to regional reading education reform becomes conceptually and
practically significant. Pooling refers to the consolidation of
shared cultural and institutional resources—such as Mandarin
as a dominant instructional language, Confucian traditions
of text-­based learning, and shared colonial educational legacies—while differentiation allows for the contextual adaptation
of pedagogy based on specific regional needs and educational
maturity.
Research comparing Hong Kong, Macau, Taiwan, and Singapore
reveals that while all four contexts value reading literacy, differences in instructional practices—such as Taiwan's reliance on
reading aloud, Hong Kong's emphasis on in-­school independent

reading, Macau's focus on bilingual instruction, and Singapore's
integrated approach to reading and writing—have measurable
impacts on student outcomes (Chan 2019; Hong et al. 2024;
Shegar and Ward 2016; Tse et al. 2013, 2016). Thus, integration
efforts should not aim at homogenization but rather at strategic
collaboration that respects diversity while leveraging structural
commonalities to address challenges such as learner heterogeneity, instructional equity, and curriculum alignment across
systems. This regional framework, supported by differentiated
instruction theory and comparative data, provides a compelling
rationale for a coordinated yet flexible approach to reading education in East Asia.
Within these distinct educational settings, the quality and nature of reading instruction become central to explaining student
achievement differentials. Teacher reading instruction strategies play a pivotal role in shaping students' reading comprehension and academic success (Carlisle et al. 2011; Judd 2024; Meng
et al. 2017). Research has consistently shown that when teachers implement explicit and strategic reading instruction—such
as identifying main ideas, drawing inferences, making predictions, self-­monitoring, and evaluating texts—students demonstrate significantly improved reading outcomes (Brevik 2019;
Rollins et al. 2022; Ukrainetz 2015). For example, explicitly
teaching students to identify main ideas and support them with
text evidence significantly enhances comprehension outcomes,
especially on standardized assessments (Geleta et al. 2022).
Instruction that includes inferencing, prediction, and evaluating the author's intent has been shown to improve reading engagement and deeper understanding of text (Kim et al. 2021;
Oyetunji 2013; Tarchi 2015).
Complementing instruction, assessment practices offer another powerful mechanism through which teachers can influence reading development, particularly when implemented
systematically. Teacher assessment strategies are increasingly recognized as a critical component of effective reading
instruction (Afflerbach 2025; Blair et al. 2007; Nordström
et al. 2019). These strategies guide instructional decisions and
offer continuous feedback loops that enhance student learning. In high-performing classrooms, assessment is not an
endpoint but a dynamic instructional tool that helps teachers
adapt their teaching to student needs and fosters student engagement in the learning process (Cameron and Piercy 2013;
Prastikawati et al. 2024; Yan and Chiu 2023). Similarly, when
teachers interpret and apply assessment results to adjust their
teaching practices, students tend to achieve better outcomes,
particularly in reading comprehension (Denton et al. 2007).
Through these mechanisms, teacher assessment practices become a powerful lever for supporting student reading growth,
reinforcing the effectiveness of instruction while advancing
individualized student outcomes (Afflerbach 2016; Hao and
Johnson 2013; Knowles 2022). To build on the existing literature and address the specific instructional dynamics in high-performing East Asian systems, the present study sets out to
examine these mechanisms with greater analytical precision.

students' reading achievement within high-­performing East
Asian contexts. By employing advanced analytical methods

2   |   Background
2.1   |   The Multifaceted Impact of Teacher
Instruction Strategies on Students
Teacher instruction strategies have a profound impact on students' academic achievement, including reading and subject-specific performance. For instance, content-­based discussion
and scaffolded guidance improve comprehension and test
scores by ensuring students are deeply engaged with material
(Brophy 2001; Martin et al. 2010; Ramos-­A lvarez and Luque-Agulló 2024). Student-­centered approaches like cooperative
learning and guided discussion are also effective in promoting
critical thinking and academic success (Bhardwaj et al. 2025;
Guzman 2016; Martin et al. 2010). In reading specifically,
teacher instruction strategies that emphasize decoding, vocabulary development, and reading comprehension through
interactive tasks have been shown to improve outcomes
(Cubukcu 2008; Nasri and Biria 2017; Silverman et al. 2020).
Teachers who adjust digital reading learning systems and
use evidence-­based teaching methods can further enhance
students' reading performance (Chen et al. 2010; Koch and
Spörer 2017).
Beyond academic performance, teacher instruction strategies
also influence students' emotional, behavioral, and social outcomes. Instruction strategies that promote autonomy, competence, and relatedness enhance prosocial behavior (Collie 2021,
2022; Hensley et al. 2020; Su and Wang 2022). Strategic use of behavior prompts and goal-­setting reduces externalizing behaviors,

and culturally adapted interventions show promise for underrepresented populations (Campbell et al. 2024; Werunga and
Lo 2023). Instruction that emphasizes autonomy and intrinsic
motivation, based on self-­determination theory, is associated
with greater student engagement and well-­being (Bardach and
Klassen 2021; Lunkina et al. 2023; Wang et al. 2016).
These findings suggest that instruction strategies do not merely
transmit knowledge but shape students' holistic development.
Therefore, understanding their varied effects—both academic
and socio-­emotional—is central to educational effectiveness.

2.2   |   Factors Influencing Student Reading
Achievement
Student reading achievement is shaped by cognitive, emotional,
instructional, and contextual factors. At the individual level,
metacognitive reading strategies—such as summarizing and
assessing source credibility—are strong predictors of performance, especially in digital contexts (Aghaie and Zhang 2012;
Liu et al. 2024; Muhid et al. 2020). Motivation and reading
self-­efficacy mediate the relationship between background and
outcomes (Chen et al. 2021; Lee and Jonson-­Reid 2016; Yang
et al. 2018), while reading enjoyment is linked to higher achievement (Cheema 2018; Dadandı and Dadandı 2022; Wingard
et al. 2020). Preferences for novels and informational texts, especially from school libraries, also improve scores (Bouchamma
et al. 2013; Li et al. 2018; Vannelli 2012).
The family environment matters as well. Predictors include the
number of books at home, parent–child reading frequency, and
early literacy engagement (Geske and Ozola 2020; Park 2008;
Vázquez-­Cano et al. 2020). SES and parental attitudes toward
reading further moderate development (Alivernini et al. 2010;
Andersen et al. 2021; Dolean et al. 2019). At the school level,
teacher experience, instructional quality, library use, and school
climate matter, especially when combined with student motivation (Lopes et al. 2022; Thomure et al. 2025; Wu et al. 2019).
Other distinguishing features include administrative leadership
and teacher satisfaction, which explain why high-­performing
schools are more effective in developing reading skills (Dittrich
and Van Staden 2025; Kim et al. 2011; Zhu et al. 2020).
Collectively, these factors emphasize that reading achievement
is an ecosystemic outcome, shaped by home, school, and personal elements that interact with instructional practices.

Supporting Reading Achievement
Assessment strategies—formative assessments, observation records, reading profiles, diagnostics, and student self-assessment—are essential tools in early reading instruction.
Rather than merely measuring, they help teachers adapt lessons in real time, identify struggling readers, and offer targeted support (Afflerbach et al. 2018; Nordström et al. 2019;
Wu et al. 2014). Integrated practices enhance responsiveness and support vocabulary growth and comprehension

2.4   |   Theoretical Framework: Guided Instruction
and Formative Assessment
To explain the mechanisms behind these instructional effects,
the study draws on Guided Instruction Theory and Formative
Assessment Theory (see Figure 2). Guided Instruction Theory
highlights the teacher's role in designing scaffolding and structuring learning activities (Kirschner et al. 2006). For reading,
this includes helping students locate and evaluate information,
identify main ideas, make predictions and inferences, and assess
authorial intent. Teachers also foster digital literacy by guiding
students to assess the credibility of online sources. For example,
when teachers model how to judge whether online information
is trustworthy, students not only improve comprehension but
also critical reading skills.
Meanwhile, formative assessment theory emphasizes the
continuous use of assessment to adapt teaching (Black and
Wiliam 1998). Feedback and monitoring not only inform teaching but also enhance its effectiveness. Teachers who regularly
check for understanding through formative assessments can
better tailor instruction to student needs, resulting in more personalized and effective learning experiences.
By integrating these perspectives, this study conceptualizes instruction and assessment as co-­dependent processes that jointly
influence literacy development.

2.5   |   Research Questions and Study Focus
While much is known about the value of teacher instruction
and assessment strategies, existing evidence is often limited by
methodological or contextual constraints. Prior research frequently isolates one aspect—as well as instructional quality or
student motivation—without accounting for the dynamic interplay between instructional strategies, assessment practices, and
school-­level differences. To address this complexity, this study

proposes a more integrative framework to examine three key
research questions:
RQ1: What is the effect of teachers' reading instruction
strategies on primary students' reading achievement?
RQ2: Is there heterogeneity in the impact of teachers'
reading instruction strategies on reading achievement?
RQ3: Do teacher assessment strategies mediate the
relationship between reading instruction strategies
and students' reading achievement?

3   |   Methods
3.1   |   Data
This study utilized data from PIRLS 2021. PIRLS is an international comparative study of students' reading achievement
conducted on a regular 5-­year cycle since 2001, and PIRLS 2021
includes student achievement data as well as student, home,
school, teacher, and national context data for 57 countries and
eight benchmarking participants. The database includes records
for 395,295 students, 384,001 parents, 13,509 school principals,
19,741 teachers, and the National Research Coordinators of each
participating country (Fishbein et al. 2024). This study selected
East Asian regions (Hong Kong SAR; Macau SAR; Taiwan) from
the PIRLS 2021 dataset as the sample for this study. Although
Singapore is geographically part of Southeast Asia, it is often
included in comparative research on East Asian or Confucian-heritage education systems because of its shared cultural and institutional characteristics with Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan
(La Londe and Verger 2022; Marginson 2011a, 2011b). The final
study cohort comprised 21,198 participants from 200 primary
schools. The demographic characteristics of the participants are
presented in Table 1.

3.2   |   Variables
3.2.1   |   Dependent Variable
The dependent variables of this study are primary school students' reading achievement. In the PIRLS 2021, primary school
students' reading achievement was measured by five plausible values. In adherence to the recommendations outlined
in the PIRLS 2021 User Guide for the International Database
(Fishbein et al. 2024), this study employs all plausible values in
the analysis.

4.2   |   Double Machine Learning (DML)
DML is an advanced econometric technique designed to estimate
causal effects robustly in the presence of high-­dimensional confounders (Chernozhukov et al. 2018). Its core innovation lies in a
“double-­debiasing” procedure. First, flexible machine learning algorithms (e.g., random forests, lasso regression, neural networks)
are used separately to model the outcome and the treatment,
conditioning on a potentially large set of control variables. The
residuals from these initial predictions are then used in a final,
typically simple (e.g., linear), regression to estimate the causal effect of the treatment on the outcome (Chernozhukov et al. 2018).

4.3   |   IV Methods

In the second stage, this study constructs a regression model
with the original treatment variable being replaced by the treatment prediction estimated in the previous stage. Concurrently,
the study maintains the same set of covariates at the student,
teacher, and school levels (Baltagi 2011).

To address potential endogeneity and bias in estimating the effect of teachers' reading instruction strategies on primary school
students' reading achievement, this study employs IV methods.
IV methods are a classical econometric approach used to identify
causal effects when the treatment variable is suspected to be endogenous (Wooldridge 2010). This endogeneity could arise from
reverse causality, omitted variable bias, or measurement error
(Stock and Watson 2020). IV estimation relies on finding a valid
instrument: a variable that (1) is correlated with the endogenous
treatment variable, (2) affects the outcome only through its effect
on the treatment and not directly or via other unobserved channels, and (3) is uncorrelated with the unobserved error term in
the outcome equation (Angrist and Pischke 2009). The IV estimator uses this instrument to isolate the exogenous variation in the
treatment and estimate its causal effect on the outcome.

PIRLS. While we control for numerous observed confounders

IV methods provide a pathway to causal identification under

IV methods alongside DML, we subject our findings of a positive
causal effect of diverse instructional strategies to a different and
stringent identification strategy, significantly strengthening the
robustness and credibility of our causal inference against potential hidden biases.

5.1   |   Results of Regression Model
The results of the regression model (see Table 3) reveal that
teachers' reading instruction strategies have a significant positive effect on primary school students' reading achievement
(β = 4.653, p < 0.001), which means teachers' reading instruction strategies are strongly associated with higher reading
achievement among primary school students. This coefficient
indicates that for every one-­unit increase in the composite
index of strategy frequency, students' reading achievement
increases by approximately 4.6 points on average, holding all
other covariates constant. Since the index was constructed by
summing the frequency ratings of 12 instructional strategies
(each scored on a 1–4 Likert scale), the variable represents
a cumulative measure of how often various comprehension strategies are used in the classroom. Furthermore, students' age (β = 125.848, p < 0.001), teacher's teaching years
(β = 1.870, p < 0.001), location of school (β = 6.639, p = 0.040),

and population of the area where the school is located
(β = 29.082, p < 0.001) have significant positive effects on primary school students' reading achievement, which implies
that older students, taught by more experienced teachers, in
schools situated in urban areas with larger populations tend
to achieve greater reading success. Additionally, students'
gender (β = −61.287, p < 0.001), students' parents' highest education (β = −77.721, p < 0.001), teacher's age (β = −14.708,
p = 0.002), and rate of students who come from economically
disadvantaged homes (β = −16.011, p < 0.001) have significant
negative effects on primary school students' reading achievement, which implies that male students, students whose parents have higher educational attainment, students taught by
older teachers, and students in schools with a higher rate of
students from economically disadvantaged backgrounds experience lower reading achievement.

The results of model 4 (see Table 7) show that teacher's assessment strategies have a significant positive effect on primary

influence on primary school students' reading achievement,

achievement.

and Bolger 2002; Sobel 1982).

5.6   |   Results of Threshold Analysis
The results of threshold analysis (see Table 11) show that compared to the low-­frequency group, students whose teachers reported a medium frequency of reading instruction strategies
scored on average 60.9 points higher (p < 0.001), while those in
the high-­frequency group scored 71.4 points higher (p < 0.001).
The incremental gain from moving from the medium to the high
group was only 10.5 points, suggesting diminishing returns at
higher frequencies. These findings indicate that while more
frequent use of instructional strategies is associated with better student performance, the effect plateaus beyond a certain
threshold.

6   |   Discussion
6.1   |   RQ1: Impact of Teacher Reading Instruction
Strategies on Student Reading Achievement
This study provides robust evidence that teacher reading instruction strategies positively influence primary students' reading
achievement. Regression, DML, and IV analyses all demonstrate
significant and consistent effects, even after controlling for various student, teacher, and school factors. However, the threshold

analysis reveals a nonlinear pattern in the relationship: moving
from low to moderate levels of strategy use is associated with a
substantial gain in reading achievement, whereas the additional
benefit from high-­frequency use is comparatively smaller, indicating diminishing marginal returns at higher intensity levels
of instruction. These findings align with prior research emphasizing the critical role of explicit instructional strategies in enhancing reading performance (Brevik 2019; Rollins et al. 2022;
Ukrainetz 2015). The causal relationship confirmed here supports Guided Instruction Theory, which posits that teacher-­led
scaffolding is essential for developing students' reading comprehension skills (Kirschner et al. 2006). Moreover, this study
extends existing knowledge by addressing endogeneity and bias
through rigorous methods, providing stronger causal inference than many previous correlational studies (Chernozhukov
et al. 2018; Pokropek 2016).
The significance of these findings lies in their practical implications for literacy education, particularly in high-­performing
East Asian contexts. The results underscore the necessity of
equipping teachers with evidence-­based reading instructional
strategies that promote active student engagement and cognitive processing. This is consistent with research emphasizing
that teacher quality is a core determinant of literacy success
across instructional contexts (Rupley 2011). Crucially, the diminishing returns observed beyond medium implementation
frequency suggest policy should aim for consistent, moderate
use of effective strategies rather than maximal frequency alone.
Previous meta-­analyses have similarly highlighted that teacher
professional development targeting reading strategies yields
measurable gains in student achievement (Didion et al. 2020;
Dugasa et al. 2022). Thus, policy efforts should prioritize
both initial teacher training and ongoing professional development focusing on diverse and explicit reading instruction
to sustain and improve literacy outcomes (Gersten et al. 2010;
McMaster et al. 2021).

7   |   Conclusions

This study's mediation analysis confirms that teacher assessment strategies partially mediate the positive relationship
between reading instruction strategies and student reading
achievement. This aligns with formative assessment theory,
which emphasizes the role of continuous assessment and feedback in guiding instructional decisions and improving learning outcomes (Black and Wiliam 1998; Denton et al. 2007).
By enabling teachers to monitor students' comprehension and
adapt teaching responsively, assessment strategies serve as an
important mechanism that strengthens the efficacy of instructional methods. Similar empirical work has demonstrated
that formative assessments can increase student motivation,
engagement, and achievement, particularly in reading literacy (Afflerbach et al. 2018; Hao and Johnson 2013; Lyon and
Weiser 2009).

This study elucidates the effect of teachers' reading instruction strategies on primary school students' reading achievement. Initial regression analyses reveal that teachers' reading
instruction strategies significantly enhance reading achievement, a finding further corroborated by robust DML and 2SLS
methods, which account for potential biases and endogeneity.
Heterogeneity analyses demonstrate that the impact is most
pronounced when teachers read for enjoyment frequently and
in schools with students who have higher basic literacy skills,
highlighting the interplay between teacher habits and student
readiness. Mediating effect analyses further show that teacher
assessment strategies partially mediate this relationship, suggesting that evaluation practices amplify the benefits of instructional strategies. These findings underscore the critical role of
methodological rigor and contextual factors in evaluating pedagogical practices. The observed pattern of diminishing returns
beyond medium implementation frequency further suggests
an optimal range for strategy intensity rather than maximization alone.

8   |   Implications
The findings of this study offer significant theoretical implications for the field of education. The robust positive association
between teachers' reading instruction strategies and students'
reading achievement, consistently observed across various regression models, reinforces the theoretical framework that effective instructional methods are pivotal in shaping academic
outcomes. The heterogeneity analysis further enriches our understanding by highlighting that the impact of these strategies
is moderated by factors such as teachers' personal reading habits and students' literacy levels. This suggests that educational
theories should consider the interactive dynamics between
educators, students, and their environment. Additionally, the
mediating role of teachers' assessment strategies provides a nuanced perspective, indicating that the pathway from instruction
to achievement is multifaceted, which calls for more integrated
theoretical models in educational research.
From a practical standpoint, the results have direct implications for educational practice. Teachers and school administrators should prioritize the implementation of effective reading
instruction strategies, as they are strongly linked to improved
reading achievement. Encouraging teachers to develop personal
reading habits, especially frequent enjoyment reading, can enhance the effectiveness of their instruction. Moreover, schools
should focus on improving students' basic literacy skills, as a
higher proportion of students who have basic literacy skills leads
to a more significant impact of reading instruction. The findings
also underscore the importance of integrating assessment strategies with instruction, as they play a crucial role in mediating the
achievement outcomes. By adopting these insights, schools can
create more conducive learning environments to boost students'
reading proficiency.

9   |   Limitation and Future Directions
This study has several limitations. Firstly, the data is cross-sectional, which restricts our ability to establish causal relationships between teacher strategies and student achievement. We
cannot determine if effective strategies lead to higher achievement or if higher-­achieving students influence the strategies
teachers employ. Secondly, the PIRLS data, while rich, relies
on teacher self-­reports regarding instructional strategies, which
may introduce reporting biases, and summing across diverse
items assumes an additive and continuous relationship among
strategies and may oversimplify differences in pedagogical emphasis or instructional intent.
Additionally, the study focuses on a specific cohort and context,
potentially limiting the generalizability of the findings to other
educational systems or student populations with different cultural backgrounds. Finally, while this study pools data across
Hong Kong, Macau, Taiwan, and Singapore to leverage shared
cultural and institutional features, caution is warranted due to
contextual heterogeneity. Differences in demographic composition and school-­level socioeconomic stratification may affect
the comparability of instructional contexts. Although we statistically control for school-­level socioeconomic disadvantage in all
regression models, unobserved cultural or systemic differences
may still influence the results.
A further limitation arises from the linguistic diversity across
the participating systems. For example, in Hong Kong, reading
instruction at the fourth grade is conducted mainly in Cantonese
and/or Putonghua, with English used for English language lessons (IEA 2022; Li 2017; The University of Hong Kong 2023).
In contrast, Taiwan uses Zhuyin Fuhao as a phonetic system to
support Mandarin instruction (Huang 2019; IEA 2022), while
Singapore adopts English as the primary language of instruction despite Malay, Mandarin, and Tamil also being official languages (IEA 2022).
Consequently, the influence of English must be emphasized:
Singapore's post-­1987 “English Use Policy” established English
as the main medium of instruction and fostered an “English-knowing bilingualism” among students, highlighting its pervasive role in education and nation-­building (An 2022). In Hong
Kong, English-­Medium Instruction (EMI) is prevalent in secondary education, with evidence suggesting that EMI students
perform better in English proficiency, though sometimes at the
cost of content learning (Lin and Morrison 2010). These differences underscore a dilemma: although PIRLS administers
assessments in the local language of instruction to ensure accessibility and contextual validity, such practices may reduce strict
comparability across education systems. For instance, in Hong
Kong, PIRLS 2021 employed separate Chinese reading booklets
as part of the comprehension materials (The University of Hong
Kong 2023).
Future research should address these limitations. Longitudinal
studies are needed to explore the causal pathways and the enduring impact of specific reading instruction strategies over time.
Employing mixed-­methods approaches, incorporating classroom
observations alongside survey data, could provide a more nuanced understanding of how these strategies are implemented
