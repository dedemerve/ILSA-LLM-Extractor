The COVID-19 pandemic has provided educational institutions with an opportunity to enhance classroom
teaching, empower teachers, and address professional development needs, fostering collaboration across diverse
economic contexts. This study investigates the pandemic’s impact on teacher competencies, professional
development needs, and collaboration using PISA data from 2019 to 2023, involving 128,866 teachers across 24
countries. Teacher competencies in empowerment, management, adaptability, and professionalism were assessed
using rating scales. Statistical analyses, including means, t-tests, ANOVA, and Structural Equation Modeling,
were conducted to evaluate changes before and after the pandemic. Significant improvements were observed in
teachers’ competencies, particularly in empowerment, management, and adaptability, with teachers from lowand middle-income countries demonstrating notable gains in fostering student confidence. While
professionalism-related needs declined, there was an increased demand for training in teacher-parent cooperation and inclusion. Enhanced collaboration among teachers underscored the value of robust collaborative
practices. This study offers a comprehensive analysis of the pandemic’s effects on teacher competencies and
provides practical insights for improving instructional design and collaboration in education.

1. Introduction
The COVID-19 pandemic reshaped global education, compelling
teachers, policymakers, and researchers to adapt strategies to mitigate
its impact on academic outcomes (Bozkurt et al., 2022; Jiang et al.,
2024). This shift highlighted the need to strengthen teacher competencies through targeted professional development (Ghimire & Khanal,
2022). Competence encompasses knowledge, skills, attitudes, and
values to address complex educational challenges (TKCOM, 2018). The
shift to remote learning emphasized key competencies, including
empowerment, management, adaptability, inclusion, and collaboration
(Sato et al., 2024). Alenezi et al. (2022) stressed the importance of new
professional development and collaborative practices, while Busuttil
and Farrugia (2020) noted inconsistent online teaching approaches,
with teachers relying on trial, training, and varying skill levels. Some
educators resisted these changes, expressing skepticism about online

education’s effectiveness (Joshi, Khanal, & Dhakal, 2023).
Post-pandemic, many returned to face-to-face teaching but retained
technology skills and continued using online platforms (Ortega, 2023).
Comparative education literature reveals a significant research gap
in teacher education and competencies, a field that has only recently
gained attention (Tatto, 2023). Kennedy and Ferdig (2018) highlight
this gap, noting the limited understanding of how teachers’ competencies, particularly in empowerment, management, adaptability, inclusion, and collaboration, have evolved globally. It remains unclear
whether these competencies have changed uniformly across contexts or
if notable variations exist. This study analyzes PISA data from 24
countries, spanning Lower Middle-Income (LMICs), Upper
Middle-Income (UMICs), and High-Income Countries (HICs) in 2019 and
2023, to examine these competencies across economic contexts.
The dataset includes responses from students, principals, teachers,
and parents, enabling an evaluation of key teacher competencies such as

& Muluye, 2020). Digital inclusion fosters educator autonomy and
resilience, and policies should address skill gaps while promoting
multi-stakeholder collaboration for equitable lifelong learning (Digital
Inclusion Policy Brief, 2024).

1.1. This study addresses three key research questions
1. How have teachers’ competencies in empowerment, management,
and adaptability evolved from pre-to post-COVID-19 across LMICs,
UMICs, and HICs?
2. What are the post-COVID-19 variations in professional development
needs related to professionalism and inclusion among teachers in
LMICs, UMICs, and HICs?
3. How has teacher collaboration changed post-COVID-19, and what is
the quantifying impact of professional development needs on overall
teacher competencies?

1.4. Teachers’ empowerment related competencies
Empowerment in teaching is closely tied to teachers’ belief in their
ability to make positive changes (Lötter, 2021). This self-belief enhances
motivation and performance, leading to more effective teaching and
greater student engagement (Luo et al., 2024). Empowered teachers
promote critical thinking, motivate students, and create dynamic
learning environments (Praneetpolkrung & Supakicco, 2023). Teachers
who view themselves as capable perform better and encourage students
to independently overcome challenges (Shukla & Arora, 2023). A global
survey during the COVID-19 pandemic revealed that empowered
teachers handled remote teaching challenges more effectively (Jelińska
& Paradowski, 2021). A comparative study between HIC (United States)
and UMIC (South Africa) found that South African teachers demonstrated greater resilience despite facing more challenges and fewer resources (Crompton et al., 2023). Empowered teachers, even when
lacking autonomy or involvement in decision-making, maintain strong
relationships with students and a passion for teaching (Nulman &
Alkalay, 2023). However, institutional barriers can hinder efforts to
achieve teacher empowerment (Kolber, 2022). While empowerment is
positively linked to job satisfaction, increased autonomy and status can
sometimes have a negative impact (Ahrari et al., 2021). A literature
review by Singh and Gautam (2024) demonstrates that empowerment
significantly enhances teacher job satisfaction. During the pandemic,
teacher competencies, including empowerment, likely shifted, influenced by economic factors, resource availability, and resilience, with
disparities evident across LMICs, UMICs, and HICs (Morrison-Smith &
Ruiz, 2020).

1.2. Literature review
TKCOM (2018) defines teacher competence as the integration of
knowledge, skills, attitudes, and values essential for handling complex
professional situations. The OECD (2023a, 2023b) highlights key competencies, including empowerment, management, adaptability, professionalism, inclusion, and collaboration. The COVID-19 pandemic
disrupted traditional teaching, revealing gaps in both required competencies and available resources. This study examines changes in these
competencies, focusing on empowerment (e.g., fostering student
self-belief and critical thinking; Khoo & Kang, 2022), management (e.g.,
handling disruptive behavior), and adaptability (e.g., implementing
diverse assessment strategies; Erickson & Noonan, 2022).
Professionalism-related needs include subject knowledge, pedagogical
skills, and teacher-parent collaboration (Evans, 2015), while inclusion
needs encompass teaching students with special needs and promoting
equity and diversity (Codina & Robinson, 2024).
1.3. Teachers’ competencies across economic backgrounds: pre- and postCOVID-19
Before COVID-19, teacher competencies aligned with school
improvement goals, varying across economic contexts (Perry, 2023).
The pandemic disrupted 90% of students globally, driving a shift to
remote and hybrid learning (Ratten, 2023). Teachers adapted by
developing digital pedagogies, highlighting both strengths and areas for
growth (Bozkurt et al., 2022), while fostering collaboration and
socio-emotional competencies (Özdemir & Babadoğan, 2023). In HICs,
low-income students—particularly second-generation and male students—experienced declines in motivation (Alivernini et al., 2023).
Teachers in LMICs and UMICs demonstrated strong instructional techniques, subject expertise, and classroom management skills (Hu, 2024).
The pandemic improved teachers’ adaptability to new pedagogies and
student needs (Wilkins, 2020), although time constraints and resource

1.5. Teachers’ management related competencies
Management-related competencies in teaching encompass the skills
required to maintain an organized and productive classroom environment, including controlling disruptive behavior, communicating expectations, and ensuring adherence to rules (Gümüş, 2022). Effective
management also involves calming disruptions to create a conducive
learning atmosphere, which is essential for fostering a respectful and
structured classroom dynamic (Zoder-Martell et al., 2023). Effective
classroom management is closely tied to overall teaching performance,
with competencies in this area enhancing both teacher and student
outcomes (Granada & Oco, 2024). Internationally, teacher competency

1.6. Teachers adaptability-related competencies

The transition to remote education during the COVID-19 pandemic
highlighted the urgent need for digital competencies among educators
and students. While the importance of empowerment, management, and
adaptability remains, the significance of digital skills is increasingly
recognized (Ghimire & Khanal, 2021; Jian et al., 2024; Joshi, Khanal, &
Dhakal, 2023). Digital competencies encompass the effective use of
digital tools, content creation, and an understanding of digital safety and
ethics, all essential for successful remote education (Cook et al., 2023).
Digital literacy, the ability to critically navigate and create information
using digital technologies, is crucial for both educators and students, as
it enhances educational outcomes and engagement in remote learning
during COVID-19 (Sillat et al., 2021).
Technological inequalities, or the digital divide, particularly impact
low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), limiting effective remote
education for those without reliable access to digital tools and the
internet during the pandemic (Otto et al., 2024). These regions face
specific challenges, including limited access to devices, inadequate
internet connectivity, and low digital literacy among educators and
students (Barrot et al., 2024). Research shows that these issues weaken
the effectiveness of online education and deepen existing educational
inequalities (Khanal, 2024; Koh & Daniel, 2022). The digital divide,
marked by disparities in access to technology and the internet, is especially pronounced in low-resource settings, where it restricts student
participation, leading to lower engagement and poorer learning outcomes (Zhang, 2023). Addressing these barriers requires strategies to
improve digital literacy and close the technology gap, such as providing
affordable devices, strengthening internet infrastructure, and offering
digital training for educators and students. Policies aimed at reducing
these disparities are crucial for promoting equitable access to remote
education (Hehir et al., 2021).
Despite these challenges, many teachers have demonstrated
remarkable adaptability during this transition, quickly acquiring skills
in digital tools, creating online materials, and implementing innovative
pedagogical strategies to engage students in virtual settings across
LMICs, UMICs, and HICs (Sum & Oancea, 2022). This adaptability has
been vital for maintaining educational continuity during the pandemic
(Barrot et al., 2024). Additionally, the shift to online teaching has
created opportunities for educational innovation and improvement,
fostering new teaching methods and digital tools that enhance learning
experiences and expand access to education, particularly for students in
remote or underserved areas across all income contexts.

1.9. Challenges and opportunities in remote education during the COVID19 pandemic: the role of digital competencies and technological
inequalities

Adaptability in teaching involves responding effectively to change,
novelty, and uncertainty (Martin et al., 2015). Aligned with Heckhausen’s lifespan theory of control (Heckhausen et al., 2010), it requires
adjusting instructional approaches to meet diverse student needs
through varied assessments, explanations, and methods (van Geel et al.,
2023). Adaptability enhances classroom management, resilience, and
teacher commitment (Collie et al., 2020), extending beyond instruction
to overall professional functioning (Hargreaves, 2005). It serves as a
protective factor, fostering cognitive, behavioral, and emotional flexibility (Fuchs et al., 2024; Martin et al., 2015). The COVID-19 pandemic
intensified the need for adaptability, with teachers leveraging synchronous and asynchronous learning, though resource disparities across
LMICs, UMICs, and HICs influenced their effectiveness (Alvarez-Rivero
et al., 2023; Zhao & Watterston, 2021).
1.7. Teachers’ professional development needs and inclusion in pre- and
post-COVID-19
Professional development for teachers is crucial for effective teaching and continuous improvement, involving formal and informal practices, flexible environments, and social interactions (Pylväs et al., 2022).
Key areas include subject knowledge, pedagogical skills, curriculum
mastery, student assessment, classroom management, individualized
learning, evaluation utilization, and parent collaboration (Bottiani et al.,
2018; Randel et al., 2016; Đurišić & Bunijevac, 2017). The pandemic
highlighted disparities in development needs across economic contexts.
Teachers in LMICs face resource limitations, requiring support for
remote and blended learning (Rahman & Siddik, 2022), while those in
UMICs and HICs struggle with student engagement and digital tool
integration. High turnover during COVID-19 underscored the need for
enhanced development to improve retention and stability (Bacher-Hicks
et al., 2023). Post-pandemic, teachers face anxiety, scheduling issues,
and student progress challenges (Wakui et al., 2021), alongside stress
from lockdowns and safety concerns (Ozamiz-Etxebarria et al., 2021).
Inclusion-focused development is essential for addressing diverse student needs, managing multicultural classrooms, and promoting equity
(Ali et al., 2024), with implementation varying by resource availability.

2.2. Reliability and validity
The reliability of the instrument was ensured using the composite
reliability (CR) method, with values ranging from 0.70 to 0.84,
exceeding the threshold criterion of 0.70 (Hooper et al., 2008; Kline,
2016). Additionally, the validity of the instrument was assessed using
both convergent and discriminant validity methods. The Average Variance Extracted (AVE) values were all greater than 0.40, while composite
reliability values exceeded 0.60, confirming convergent validity (Yang
et al., 2023). Discriminant validity was verified through Heterotrait–Monotrait (HTMT) analysis, with all values falling below the
threshold of 0.90 (Joshi, Adhikari, et al., 2023).
2.3. Data analysis
This study utilized two PISA teacher databases (“CY07_MSU_TCH_QQQ” and “CY08MSP_TCH_QQQ”), which were merged based
on common variables. The data were cleaned before analysis using
several statistical techniques, including mean, standard deviation (SD),
independent sample t-tests, Analysis of Variance (ANOVA), and Structural Equation Modeling (SEM). The X-bar and R control charts analyzed
teachers’ competencies in relation to Country Economic Levels (CEL)
and the periods before and after COVID-19. The mean values assessed
item status pre- and post-COVID-19 and across different economic
levels, with results visualized in a 3D bar chart. Centrality plots diagnosed network stability, variability, and influence, while clustering plots
evaluated data structure and performance. Flexplots illustrated data
distribution and the relationships between Professional Development
Needs (PR), Instructional Needs (IN), and Contextual Influences (CI)
with Teachers’ Competencies (COM) based on pre- and post-COVID-19
periods and CEL. The independent sample t-test evaluated empowerment, management, adaptability, professionalism, inclusion, and
collaboration before and after the pandemic, while one-way ANOVA
assessed these variables across economic levels. SEM examined the
impact of professional development needs (professionalism and inclusion) and teachers’ collaboration practices on competencies (empowerment, management, and adaptability).

2. Methodology
This study analyzed PISA data from 2019 (pre-COVID-19, dataset
“CY07_MSU_TCH_QQQ”) and 2023 (post-COVID-19), covering 128,866
teachers (62,589 pre-COVID; 66,277 post-COVID) across 24 countries.
Countries were classified based on the World Bank’s 2022–2023 income
levels: LMICs (per capita income $1136–$4465), UMICs ($4466–
$13,845), and HICs ($13,846+). The dataset included information from
24 countries, with income classifications aligned with the World Bank’s
2022–2023 standards (https://tinyurl.com/4phrxwbv). Morocco was
classified as an LMIC; Albania, Azerbaijan (Baku), Brazil, Colombia,
Costa Rica, the Dominican Republic, Georgia, Kosovo, Malaysia, and
Peru as UMICs; and Australia, Chile, Taiwan (Chinese Taipei), Germany,
Hong Kong, Korea, Macao, Panama, Portugal, Spain, the UAE, the UK,
and the USA as HICs. Comparing pre- and post-pandemic data across
income groups and countries provides unique insights into shifts in
teacher competencies, highlighting global trends with a large, diverse
sample.
2.1. Research instrument
In this study, we analyzed 28 items from the PISA dataset across six
distinct domains (see https://tinyurl.com/yc62pz9c). The study examined competencies related to empowerment, management, and adaptability, as well as professional development needs in professionalism
and inclusion, and explored collaboration within schools. Empowerment, management, and adaptability competencies were assessed on a
four-point scale, while professionalism and inclusion needs were rated
using the same scale. Collaboration was evaluated on a six-point scale.
Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) was employed to validate the six
domains.
Empowerment-related competencies involve fostering students’
belief in their abilities (EM01), appreciation for learning (EM02), ability
to formulate questions (EM03), motivation for disinterested students
(EM04), and critical thinking skills (EM05). Management-related competencies include handling disruptive behavior (MT01), setting clear
behavior expectations (MT02), enforcing rules (MT03), and addressing
classroom disruptions (MT04). Adaptability-related competencies cover
using varied assessments (AD01), providing alternative explanations
(AD02), and implementing new instructional approaches (AD03). Professional development needs focus on subject knowledge (PR01–PR03),

increased significantly, with more sharing of teaching materials and
standardized evaluations, despite a decline in team conference attendance. HICs exhibited the highest levels of collaboration, and this trend
was observed across all regions. The study highlights the strong
connection between professional development, collaboration, and
teacher competencies, with professional development positively
impacting both collaboration (Beta = 0.13) and competencies (Beta =
0.12), particularly in areas such as empowerment, adaptability, management, inclusion, and collaboration. The pandemic fostered greater

in key competencies. However, continuous professional development

and UMICs, such as in Ghana (Abakah et al., 2023), highlighting the
need for targeted interventions.

6. Limitations
This study highlights significant variations in teacher competencies
across economic backgrounds but may not generalize to all regions. Only
one country was taken as an LMICs, which limits the study’s generalizability. The reliance on self-reported data introduces potential biases,
and the study does not fully account for external factors such as government policies and socio-economic conditions. Future research should
explore long-term sustainability, qualitative teaching practices, and the
psychological impact of stress and burnout on teacher competencies.
While PISA data provides insights, it doesn’t directly measure
teacher competencies, limiting conclusions about teacher development.
The cross-sectional design also restricts causal claims, as longitudinal
data is needed to directly link changes in competencies to the pandemic.
Without follow-up data, it remains unclear if these improvements will
last over time. The tendency to generalize across economic groups
without considering contextual differences, like resource access or
educational policies, is a limitation. Professional development effectiveness is often influenced by local policies, funding, and needs, which
this study does not fully address. Using PISA data for cross-national
comparisons poses challenges, as it doesn’t capture local factors like
regional policies, cultural influences, and socio-political dynamics.
Future studies could incorporate local surveys or qualitative assessments
to offer a more nuanced understanding of how teacher competencies are
shaped by context-specific challenges and resources.
As education systems evolve post-pandemic, digitization will shape
teacher professional development. Future research should explore how
digital literacy and technology integration impact teacher competencies,
particularly in lower-middle-income countries. Investigating digital
platforms for collaboration and peer learning could offer valuable insights. Incorporating recent research and providing clear guidelines for
targeted professional development in digital literacy and inclusive
practices would strengthen recommendations, offering a comprehensive
analysis of challenges and opportunities in the evolving educational
landscape.

5. Implication
Sustainability of Teacher Competencies Post-COVID-19: One of
the key findings of this study is the significant improvement in teachers’
empowerment-related competencies post-COVID-19, particularly in
adapting to innovative pedagogies, digital tools, and evolving student
needs. However, whether these improvements are sustainable remains
uncertain. While the pandemic prompted short-term adaptations,
further research is needed to determine if teachers will maintain these
enhanced skills beyond the crisis. Continued use of digital tools and
hybrid teaching models may require ongoing support, professional
development, and adequate resources to prevent skill erosion.
Long-Term Implications for Teacher Empowerment and Management: Empowerment and management skills saw notable improvements, especially in LMICs like Morocco. These competencies may
reflect either temporary crisis management strategies or lasting changes
in teaching practices. Sustaining these gains will depend on providing
ongoing support and professional development, particularly in resilience and adaptability beyond crisis situations. The rise in management
skills, particularly post-COVID, emphasizes the importance of building
long-term strategies for teacher growth.
Policy Recommendations for Different Economic Contexts: The
findings emphasize the need for education policies tailored to different
economic contexts. In LMICs, equitable access to resources and training
is essential. For example, Rwanda’s Digital Ambassadors Program boosts
digital literacy by training local volunteers, while India’s DIKSHA
platform offers teachers digital resources and professional development.

learning opportunities, and Ghana’s National Teachers’ Standards focus
on digital literacy. In HICs, innovation in teaching methods and technologies is encouraged, as seen in Finland’s phenomenon-based learning
