The increasing importance of learning context digitalization has necessitated investigations into
the effects of information and communication technology (ICT)-related factors in blended
learning settings on students’ reading performance. Using data from 32 Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development countries from the latest cycle of the Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA) 2018 reading dataset, this study combines an artificial
intelligence (AI) algorithm and hierarchical linear modeling (HLM) to examine the effects of
student-level and school-level ICT-related factors in the blended learning setting on students’
reading performance. Results show that students’ perceived autonomy and competence in ICT,
preference for different reading formats, quality of school ICT resources and school ICT use
policies are positively related to reading performance, whereas school ICT use support for
teachers and the teaching of ICT skills are negatively related to students’ reading scores. The
quadratic effects of students’ interests in ICT, family and school ICT resources, and ICT uses are
identified, showing an inverted U-shaped trend. The possible linear and quadratic moderation
effects of school-level ICT-related features are also investigated. This study invites consideration
of a combination of analytical methods for a comprehensive exploration of reading education and
sheds light on how the delicate association of multilevel ICT factors in blended learning settings
can contribute to the improvement of reading education. Instructors in blended learning settings
should effectively integrate technology and appropriately apply pedagogies, and the design of
blended learning should be sustainable and learner-friendly to exploit students’ learning
potential.

1. Introduction
The rapid development of information and communication technology (ICT) has brought researchers into closer contact and affinity with reading education in increasingly digitized classrooms (e.g., Cho et al., 2021). Implementing screen technology across
reading curricula often reduces the use of print textbooks while producing a more dynamic and interactive reading scenario (van der
Weel & Mangen, 2022). Evidence from previous studies suggests that properly utilizing elements of advanced digital technology is
conducive to reading instruction and students’ reading proficiency (Macaruso et al., 2020). Blended reading classrooms are an
effective system that integrates the benefits of both technology-enhanced reading practices and conventional classroom interactions
(Lin et al., 2020). In blended learning environments, students, teachers and information and communication technology (ICT) are
intertwined, collectively shaping students’ reading proficiency. ICT plays an indispensable role in the successful launching of blended
learning, without which the blended learning setting cannot be built (Ashraf et al., 2022). How to effectively embed ICT into the
blended learning setting has become a critical part of reading education. Thus, the ICT-related factors that may influence adolescents’
reading performance in blended learning environments are subject to discussion.
The development of international reading assessments has enabled researchers to examine the complicated interactions between
students and various elements of blended educational settings. In the PISA 2018 reading literacy framework, reading is defined as
“understanding, using, evaluating, reflecting on and engaging with texts in order to achieve one’s goals, to develop one’s knowledge
and potential and to participate in society” (OECD, 2019, p. 28). The PISA 2018 assessed students’ reading proficiency in a
computer-based environment, which expanded the assessment in previous cycles by 1) including multiple-source texts, 2) providing
multimodal reading materials emulating reading online, and 3) emphasizing information processing (van der Weel & Mangen, 2022).
It modeled large-scale reading assessment as “internet reading”, which is a necessity for students to participate in modern society
(OECD, 2021). The affordance of technologies in the assessment and the abundant contextual information about whether and how
technologies are used in classrooms and in schools can help researchers examine the elements of blended settings that may influence
students’ reading performance (Hahnel et al., 2022).
Previous studies have accumulated abundant knowledge about the factors that may influence students’ reading performance using
international large-scale reading datasets. These studies have widely applied multilevel analysis and provided a holistic picture of the
effects of different factors/variables on students’ reading performance. For instance, Cho et al. (2021) used new reading literacy as a
framework to investigate factors that predict elementary students’ reading comprehension in digital assessments. First, although there
has been fruitful discussion of the factors that may influence students’ reading performance, these effects in blended reading classrooms await further exploration. Second, most of the recent studies on blended learning focus on the effects of school-wide blended
learning programs on students’ reading gains (e.g., Hurwitz & Macaruso, 2021; Macaruso et al., 2020; Prescott et al., 2018) and
provide in-depth knowledge about the specific design and systematic planning of blended courses. However, the limited sample size
may reduce the generalizability and transferability of the results to larger groups. Additionally, the ubiquity of the blended learning
setting calls for a more comprehensive analysis of the ICT-related factors that may influence students’ reading performance. Third, due
to the limitation of the conventional analytical method, nonparsimonious models can only measure significance indices rather than
indices of the size of the combined effects (Gamazo & Martínez-Abad, 2020). The rapid development of large-scale educational
assessment calls for a holistic methodology to produce well-supported results and valuable conclusions (Costa-Mendes et al., 2021).
To address the issues presented above, this study adopted a sequential explanatory design to explore the effects of multilevel ICT
factors on 15-year-old adolescents’ reading performance in a blended learning environment using two mutually enriching methods:
random forests (RF), an artificial intelligence (AI) algorithm, and a hierarchical linear model (HLM) based on secondary panel data
from the PISA 2018 results. The RF algorithm can help to identify the optimal ICT-related feature set and the relative importance
ranking to predict students’ reading performance in the blended learning context, and the HLM takes the embedded data structure into
consideration to examine the nuanced effects and cross-level moderation of these factors. This study can contribute to the understanding of how ICT-related elements in the blended reading context collectively shape 15-year-old students’ reading performance.
Given the effects of multilevel ICT-related factors in the PISA 2018 framework and the conceptualization of ICT-related factors on
reading performance in previous research, this study focuses on the following four research questions:
Research question 1. (RQ1): What is the optimal ICT-related feature set, its efficiency, and the relative importance of these factors
in blended learning settings for predicting students’ reading performance?
Research question 2. (RQ2): What are influences of student-level ICT-related factors in blended learning settings on students’
reading performance?

2. Literature review
2.1. Blended learning environment
The definition of blended learning varies according to different researchers. According to Graham (2006), blended learning is
2

defined as the integration of traditional classroom methods with online activities. The hallmark of blended learning is the integration
of teacher-led instruction with digital technology in the learning environment (Macaruso et al., 2020), which aligns well with the focus
of our research, namely, the effects of ICT-related factors in the blended learning setting. In this research, we adopted the definition by
Mitchell and Forer (2010), which emphasizes technology use: blended learning refers to a learning environment in which ICT is
developed to complement rather than replace traditional forms of learning. More specifically, blended learning is regarded as a hybrid
model that allows the presence of traditional direct teaching methods alongside modern ICT-supported activities (Littlejohn & Pegler,
2006). Teachers in blended learning environments can leverage technologies and design classroom activities to meet the specific needs
of students with diverse learning profiles (Prescott et al., 2018).
Researchers have reported the effects of the blended learning approach on students’ reading literacy skill gains (e.g., Yang, 2012),
thereby confirming the positive impact of digitalized classrooms. Some investigations have been conducted with blended learning
programs implemented at the school level that have produced valuable and detailed information about the effects of factors in blended
settings on students’ reading performance (e.g., Macaruso et al., 2019; Macaruso et al., 2020; Prescott et al., 2018). Blended learning
has penetrated reading classrooms and is popular among students in various learning phases, such as the kindergarten level (Macaruso
et al., 2019), the elementary level (e.g., Macaruso et al., 2020; Prescott et al., 2018; Schechter et al., 2017), the secondary level
(composed of middle and high school students) (e.g., Huang & Hong, 2016; Hurwitz & Macaruso, 2021), and the tertiary level (e.g.,
Divya & Mohamed, 2020). Regarding specific factors that are correlated with students’ reading performance in blended learning
environments, researchers have identified teachers’ ICT proficiency (Paleczek et al., 2022), students’ time spent on learning (Kundu
et al., 2021), and design-, strategy-, and learner-related guidelines (Lin et al., 2020) as essential. However, previous investigations have
also identified a negative relationship between the use of computers for reading practice in class and reading scores (OECD, 2021). The
contentious findings of the impacts of ICT-related factors in blended settings on students’ reading performance require further
exploration.
In recent years, the frames of many large-scale educational surveys have taken the technology use in class into consideration,
providing helpful data resources for the blended learning research. In PISA 2018, the student questionnaire included the questions
about students’ and teachers’ technology use in class, as well as students’ technology use after class (OECD, 2019), which are
indispensable for a blended learning setting. Accordingly, the combination of different analytical methods has been applied in
large-scale educational survey to examine the relationships between ICT-related factors and students’ academic outcomes, which
provide new perspectives for the examination of blended learning settings. For example, Olga and Gábor (2022) used RF and multilevel
models to examine the association between students’ attitudes toward ICT and their mathematical and scientific literacy based on the
PISA 2015 and 2018 tests. They concluded that the former method conduces to finding generalizable patterns of the variables (e.g., the
relative variable importance), and the latter is more about testing hypotheses (e.g., the detailed effects of students’ attitudes towards
ICT). It is suggested that conventional statistical methods and educational data mining techniques have yet to be further integrated
especially in decoding blended reading education for a more comprehensive analysis. Based on the literature, the following hypotheses
were formulated:
Hypothesis 1. (H1): With student-level ICT-related factors showing the most importance (e.g., Olga & Gábor, 2022), the optimal
feature set, produced by AI algorithms, composed of student- and school-level ICT factors in blended learning settings can effectively
predict students’ reading performance.
2.2. ICT-related factors that influence students’ reading outcomes
2.2.1. Student-level ICT-related factors
2.2.1.1. Students’ attitudes toward ICT. The importance of students’ affective domains related to ICT and reading activities has been
highlighted in previous investigations. Lim and Jung (2019) found that students’ attitudes toward computers and their self-confidence
in high-level ICT tasks can boost their reading performance in computer-based environments. Another study using data from three
countries participating in the PISA 2018 examined the relationship between students’ attitudes toward ICT use and their reading
performance and found that positive attitudes toward digital devices contribute to reading development (Kong et al., 2022). In
addition, students’ attitudes toward ICT can positively mediate the relationship between family economic status and reading performance (Sarimanah et al., 2022). Other attitudinal factors such as students’ ICT interest, self-efficacy and autonomy, are positively
correlated with their reading proficiency, while their enjoyment of social interaction around ICT negatively affects their reading
performance (Wang et al., 2023).
Another factor that has received attention in the blended reading education setting is students’ preference for reading formats. The
provision of digital reading materials is a vital part of the blended reading environment, but whether students like reading in digital or
print format is susceptible to various factors (e.g., reader characteristics reading task characteristics, and text features), and these
complex interactions affect students’ performance in digital or print reading (Salaz & Mizrachi, 2022). Research has shown that
students’ experience with e-formats is harmful to their favorable attitudes toward digital reading (Mizrachi & Salaz, 2022). Regarding
the relationship between reading format preference and academic performance, a study revealed that students’ reading format
preference is associated with their academic rank, with students with a higher academic rank showing a higher preference for digital
reading (Mizrachi et al., 2018). However, there is a paucity of research that examines the effects of middle school students’ different
preferences for reading formats on their reading performance.

2.2.1.2. Student ICT use. The results from Naumann and Sälzer (2017) suggested that students’ general ICT use has an inverse
U-shaped relationship with their reading proficiency. In terms of various purposes and contexts of ICT use, researchers have found that
social interaction in ICT use is negatively correlated with reading performance (Xiao & Hew, 2022), while ICT use outside of school for
entertainment contributes to students’ performance (Gómez-Fernández & Mediavilla, 2021). Students with moderate use of ICT at
school or outside school for schoolwork have the highest digitally assessed reading performance (Gubbels et al., 2020). A moderated
analysis revealed that the relationship between different ICT use types (at home and at school for recreational activities and for
schoolwork) and reading literacy is closely intertwined with the intensity of ICT use, i.e., the increasing intensity of ICT use decreases
the positive effects of these ICT use scenarios (Li & Wang, 2022). Bhutoria and Aljabri (2022) investigated the optimal level of ICT use
for students and cognitive development in reading and concluded that traditional learning and ICT-based learning should be complemented by traditional ways of teaching in blended learning settings.
2.2.1.3. Family ICT resources. Previous research has provided mixed findings about the association between the availability of family
ICT resources and students’ reading performance. Using data from PISA 2009, Lee and Wu (2012) examined this intricate relationship
and found that access to ICT at home has a direct negative effect on students’ reading literacy, with many subsequent studies confirming this finding (e.g.,Hu et al., 2022b; Hu et al., 2018). However, some studies have also identified family ICT resources as a
contributing factor to students’ reading performance in PISA (e.g., Sayans-Jimenez et al., 2018). Researchers have attempted to
reconcile these results through the exploration of the effects of different amounts of ICT resources at home, and found that students’
reading performance increases according to the type and quantity of devices at home but decreses drastically when the value of the ICT
resources variable exceeds a certain point (Vázquez-Cano et al., 2020), calling for a moderate and sustainable quantity of ICT resources
(Gubbels et al., 2020). Based on the aforementioned studies, the following relationships were hypothesized:
Hypothesis 2. (H2): In the blended learning environment, following relationships exist: students’ positive attitudes toward ICT (e.g.,
Lim & Jung, 2019) and preference for reading on digital devices (e.g., Mizrachi et al., 2018) contribute to their reading performance,
while ICT as a topic in students’ social interaction is negatively related to their reading performance (Wang et al., 2023). There are
inverted U-shaped effects of students’ interests in ICT (e.g., Bhutoria & Aljabri, 2022; Chiu, 2022), ICT use (e.g., Bhutoria & Aljabri,
2022; Chiu, 2022), and family ICT resources (e.g., Gubbels et al., 2020) on their reading performance.
2.2.2. School-level ICT-related factors
2.2.2.1. School ICT resources and ICT use promotion and regulation policies. Previous studies have documented mixed results about the
effects of the availability of ICT resources at school on students’ reading performance. Hu et al. (2018) found that students’ access to
ICT at school was positively correlated with their reading scores, while Li and Petersen (2022) examined the effects of ICT resources
and students’ ICT use at school and showed that the two factors exerted a direct negative effect on students’ academic achievements. In
addition to the provision of ICT resources, the effect of school ICT use promotion and regulation policies and related activities on
students’ learning outcomes has been explored by previous research, with intriguing results. Gorjón and Osés (2022) used data from
PISA 2018 to capture the U-shaped relationship between school ICT use intensity and students’ mathematics performance and
concluded that policies regarding the frequency of ICT use at school should not interfere with students’ learning process. Regarding
students’ reading performance, Fernández-Gutiérrez et al. (2020) examined the effects of different policies on the use of ICT at school
and found that ICT promotion policies have insignificant effects on students’ reading performance, calling for a careful evaluation of
school policy oriented toward increasing the educational use of ICT. In the blended learning setting, considering that school policies
and regulations about ICT use are complex and involve the promotion and support of teacher ICT use, the management and coordination of ICT resources, and ICT use standards and regulations, further studies should be conducted to explore their detailed effect on
students’ reading performance.
2.2.2.2. Teacher ICT support from school and ICT instruction. It is argued that the mere provision of ICT resources cannot guarantee the
development of students’ learning outcomes and may inhibit their deep learning engagement, which implies the need to encourage
teachers to properly use technology in the blended learning setting (Ünal et al., 2022). On the one hand, there are mixed results about
the effects of schools’ support for teacher ICT use. Some researchers have demonstrated that the provision of ICT training plans for
teachers by schools can help teachers utilize digital devices properly in class (e.g., Wu et al., 2022; Zagouras et al., 2022), while other
studies have discovered the opposite pattern: ICT training and opportunities for teachers provided by schools did not significantly
increase their intention to use technology (Hsu & Lin, 2020), which is an indicator of a teacher’s ability to do so (Kessler, 2007). More
specifically, teachers’ ICT-related work and pedagogical orientation toward the use of ICT had no statistically significant effects on
their capability to teach in a digital environment (Claro et al., 2018).
Teaching ICT knowledge and skills is an important factor that should not be neglected in the blended learning setting. Pilgrim et al.
(2019) found that teaching digital skills (e.g., reliability reasoning) can help to improve students’ reading performance, while some
researchers have shown that it is not the frequency of teaching digital skills to students but the quality of ICT-related instruction that
can help students better navigate computer-based reading environments (Hu & Wang, 2022a). Recent research has found that
small-scale interventions in ICT skills are not sufficient to enhance students’ online literacy, but continuous practice in different
reading contexts during their school years can contribute to students’ digital literacy (Alamettala & Sormunen, 2021). In addition,
researchers have argued that the successful implementation of ICT requires teaching practical skills about ICT to students and helping
them gain basic knowledge regarding ICT ethics (Ünal et al., 2022). The following relationships has thus been hypothesized:

Previous research has found some significant moderation effects of school-level factors on the relationship between student-level
factors and reading performance. School-level variables not only directly influence students’ academic performance but also moderate
the influences of individual’s self-regulation skills on learning outcomes (Yerdelen & Sungur, 2019). Li and Wang (2022) found that
factors that moderate the effect of ICT on student performance include the instructor and the intervention duration. Particularly,
researchers have found that school ICT resources could moderate the relationship between student-level factors and reading outcomes.
For instance, Xiao and Hew (2022) explored the ICT-related factors influencing students’ reading performance based on the PISA
dataset and found that the negative correlation between students’ ICT use for social interaction and their reading performance may be
worsened with increasing school provision of ICT devices. Against this backdrop, the interaction between school-level ICT-related
factors and those factors at the student level within the blended learning setting warrants further study. Thus the following hypotheses
was formulated:
Hypothesis 4. (H4): School-level ICT-related factors in the blended learning setting can moderate the relationship between studentlevel ICT-related factors and reading performance.
2.3. Rationale for the current study
Research on reading performance and blended learning is gaining momentum, with researchers developing a solid knowledge
foundation for the effects of features (the core factors/variables in the AI algorithm) at various levels on students’ reading outcomes.
However, despite the substantial efforts made by researchers in this field, the factors that may influence students’ reading performance
have been examined less frequently in the context of blended learning environments. Furthermore, the limited number of samples in
previous research that participated in specific blended learning programs may reduce the generalizability of the results to a global
scale. Finally, although a series of advanced analytical approaches have been introduced to this field and have provided innovative
perspectives, but the combination of these methods still awaits further exploration (Olga & Gábor, 2022).
Drawing from the research lacunae of previous research, the current study adopts a hybrid methodology to examine the collective
effects, the relative importance of ICT-related factors (using an AI algorithm), and the direct and moderating effects of student- and
school-level ICT-related factors (using a multilevel model) in the blended learning setting on students’ reading performance. The ICTrelated factors/variables examined in this analysis are collected from the PISA 2018 dataset, and are selected and modeled according to
the PISA data structure. This study aims to specify the effects of ICT-related features in blended settings on students’ reading performance, as depicted in Fig. 1.
Compared with previous research, this study adds value to the field of reading education in the following two ways. First, regarding
the data, this study takes advantage of the international educational dataset on a systematic scale, contributing to the knowledge and
development of adolescents’ reading education in blended learning settings. Second, to our knowledge, few studies have that

3. Methods
3.1. Research context and samples
Three of the PISA 2018 background questionnaires receive special attention in this analysis: the student questionnaire, the school
questionnaire and the ICT familiarity questionnaire. Students from 32 OECD countries who completed the optional ICT familiarity
questionnaires in PISA 2018 were selected. The demographic descriptions of the samples are provided in Table 1. Overall, the reading
scores of 152,218 adolescents from 9087 schools situated in blended test language classes were used according to their responses to
question IC152Q01HA. This question helps to classify students’ test language learning environment into four classes, namely, a course
where 1) teachers alone used ICT, 2) students alone used ICT, 3) both teachers and students used ICT, and 4) neither the teacher nor the
students used ICT. Considering that the blended learning environment consists of a variety of forms and components (e.g., teachers
alone using ICT-integrated teaching strategies, students alone using computers for homework, and teacher-student communication
through digital devices after class) (Ashraf et al., 2022), students who selected 1), 2) and 3) were all included in this research.
Previous PISA-related research has generally regarded student and school background characteristics and national development
indicators as potential confounding factors that influence students’ academic performance (e.g., Uzun & Kilis, 2019). The analysis and
interpretation of the results in this study controlled for two demographic variables of students, namely, student gender (GENDER) (e.
g., Hu & Wang, 2022a; Petko et al., 2017) and family economic, social and cultural status (ESCS) (e.g., Sarimanah et al., 2022;
Sayans-Jiménez et al., 2018), three school-level factors, namely, school size (SCHSIZE) (e.g., Hu & Wang, 2022a) and class size
(CLSIZE) (e.g., Fenollar et al., 2007), and student-teacher ratio (STRATIO), and one country-level factor, namely, the logarithm of GDP
per capita (lnGDP) (e.g., Wang et al., 2023).

(SOIAICT), and their preference for reading formats, namely, preference for a digital reading format (DIGIPRE), for a paper reading
format (PAPERPRE), and equal interest in paper and digital reading formats (EQUAFORM). The ICT use variables included ICT use at
school in general (USESCH), subject-related ICT use outside of lessons (ICTOUTSIDE), subject-related ICT use during lessons (ICTCLASS), ICT use outside of school for entertainment (ENTUSE) and the use of ICT outside of school for schoolwork activities

At the school level, the PISA 2018 dataset provides two factors that delineate the quantity of ICT resources, namely, ICT available at
school (ICTSCH) and the number of available computers per student (RATCMP1), and one factor that describes the quality of ICT
resources, namely, the proportion of available computers connected to the internet (RATCMP2).
For our research purpose, four additional latent school-level factors were constructed in the present study instead of being directly
extracted from the PISA 2018 dataset. The 7 items from the student questionnaire and 19 items from the school questionnaire formed
four school-level factors, namely, ICT skills taught by teachers (ICTSKIT), quality of school ICT resources (SCHICTQUA), teacher ICT
use support from school (TEACHICT), and school ICT use promotion and regulation policies (SCHICTPO) (see Table S2 in Supplementary Materials). A confirmatory factor analysis was conducted to ensure the validity of the four latent constructs, the process of
which is further illustrated in the following section. Additionally, to ensure continuity between the classification of factors, the group
means of the ICTSCH and ICTSKIT collected from the student questionnaire were aggregated to the school-level factors in the
multilevel analysis (e.g., Marôco, 2021). It is proposed that the quadratic model is helpful for detecting combined effects of factors on
students’ academic performance (Kamarova et al., 2016). Considering that previous studies have observed the curvilinear effect of ICT
use (e.g., Bhutoria & Aljabri, 2022; Chiu, 2022), students’ interests in ICT, and the quantity of ICT resources (Gubbels et al., 2020) on
students’ reading performance, we have also developed quadratic terms in the multilevel model with the following variables: 1) ICT
use: USESCH, ICTOUTSIDE, ENTUSE, HOMESCH, and ICTCLASS; 2) students’ interests in ICT: INTICT; 3) family ICT resources: ICTRES
and ICTHOME; and 4) the quantity of school ICT resources: ICTSCH and RATCMP1. The GDP per capita of 32 OECD countries was
collected from the official website of the World Bank (URL: https://databank.worldbank.org/home.aspx).
Regarding the dependent variable used in this research, the ten plausible values that represent students’ reading performance in
PISA 2018 were used. In PISA 2018, 10 plausible values (i.e., PV_1 to PV_10) were developed for unbiased estimates of students’
reading performance. A plausible value is defined as the random values from the posterior distribution, which can help reduce error in
making inferences about the target population (OECD, 2009). Rather than using the sole score for the estimation of students’ reading
ability θ, PISA estimated a probability distribution for θ, and 10 plausible values were randomly selected from this distribution to
represent students’ possible range of reading ability (OECD, 2009). This is thus an effective way to represent the performance of
students for educational assessment at the national and international levels. The 10 plausible values were included in our analysis for a
10-round RF and HLM analysis, as recommended by the PISA Data Analysis Manual (OECD, 2009). In PISA-based analysis, multiple
imputation (MI) is recommended when dealing with missing data (OECD, 2009), as widely applied in previous studies (e.g., Hu &
Wang, 2022a; Lim & Jung, 2019). The number of MIs is established according to the number of plausible values provided in each cycle
of PISA, with each plausible value, i.e., PV_1, PV_2, PV_3, …, PV_10, combined with an MI dataset, i.e., MI_1, MI_2, MI_3, …, MI_10.
These MI datasets are imported into the HLM simultaneously to pool the results for a comprehensive analysis of students’ reading
performance.
3.2. Research model and procedure
3.2.1. Random forests
Conventional statistical methods focus mainly on model estimation, inference and fit, while AI centers on developing computationally efficient machine learning algorithms with high accuracy that can make predictions and serve human decisions (Shmueli,
2010). Compared with other algorithms (e.g., support vector machine or artificial neural networks), which are often denounced as
“black boxes” (e.g., Gorostiaga & Rojo-Álvarez, 2016), the DT algorithm provides results with increased interpretability and visible
processes. Researchers have developed an ensemble algorithm composed of several randomized DTs, namely, the RF, to obtain the
empirical distributions of feature importance ranking (Breiman, 2001). The working principle of RF is to examine the different voting
results of the DTs and select the best among the subset of variables for a more accurate judgment of the final decision (Alzahrani &
Abdullah, 2019). Recent studies have found that RF significantly outperforms other AI algorithms in predicting students’ academic
performance in a technology-enhanced environment (Brahim, 2022; Goundar et al., 2022). This powerful AI algorithm method could
help to identify the importance of interconnected factors that are most relevant to students’ reading performance in the blended
learning environment.
3.2.2. Hierarchical linear modeling
Multilevel models were used to consider cluster effects since students nested within schools tend to share common characteristics
(Raudenbush & Bryk, 2002). This study further explored the effects of ICT-related factors using Hierarchical Linear & Nonlinear
Modeling 8 (HLM 8, User License ID: 13437). This software allows for the simultaneous use of the 10 plausible values for students’
reading scores in PISA, student weights, and the analysis of the residual of the variance in three levels (Martínez-Abad et al., 2020).
Previous studies using HLM and considering cross-level interaction provided a solid basis for our study (e.g., Yerdelen & Sungur, 2019;
Zhong, 2011). To obtain a more parsimonious model, we applied models with random effects only to student intercepts, school intercepts, and student slopes with cross-level interactions (Zhong, 2011). The final mixed model used in this analysis is as follows:

3.3. Instruments used and their validation
The best-fitting RF regression model was developed according to the measures of mean square error (MSE), mean absolute error
(MAE), and R2 (Singh & Bawa, 2022). According to Cohen (1988), an R2 between 0.20 and 0.50 is considered moderate, and an R2
higher than 0.5 is considered effective; however, researchers in the field of social science consider an R2 between 0.1 and 0.5
acceptable provided that most of the predictors or explanatory variables are statistically significant (Neter et al., 2012). In addition,
k-fold cross-validation was adopted in our analysis; k = 10 has been proven to be effective in various related works (e.g., Chui et al.,
2020; Kokkinos & Margaritis, 2018). Thus, 10-fold cross-validation was applied to relieve the problem of overfitting in this performance prediction model (e.g., Gorostiaga & Rojo-Álvarez, 2016).
For the validation of the models, the Gini importance of every feature in the RF model (e.g., Alzahrani & Abdullah, 2019) and the f 2
in the HLM model (e.g., Hu & Wang, 2022a) were calculated to estimate the effect sizes of the variables. The former represents the sum
of the Gini impurity reductions of all nodes split on that feature (i.e., showing which variables are the most effective in prediction), and
the latter indicates the proportion of the original remaining variance that is accounted for by the added variables. The values of f 2 at
0.02, 0.15, and 0.35 indicate small, medium, and large effects, respectively (Hox, 2010). The mathematical functions and explanations
for the two indicators are provided in Supplementary Material 3.

3.4. Data analysis
The data analysis process was as follows. First, data were extracted from the PISA 2018 dataset and the World Bank dataset and
merged using SPSS 23. Second, we conducted a data cleaning process, which included the deletion of repeated values and outliers and
the MI of the missing variables (e.g., Wang et al., 2023; Scherer & Siddiq, 2015). The variable with the largest number of missing values
in this analysis was SOIAICT (12.7%). In this regard, m = 10 was adopted in the imputation to ensure the quality of the data; it
achieved an efficiency above 98% for 12.7% missing data and thus was considered appropriate in this study (Schafer & Olsen, 1998).
Third, based on previous research (e.g., Hu & Wang, 2022a; Chen, Zhang, & Hu, 2021; Lin et al., 2017), CFA was applied to decode the
relationship between a set of measured and latent variables in this study, namely, ICTSKIT, SCHICTQUA, TEACHICT, and SCHICTPO.
The lavaan package in R (Rosseel, 2012) was used to measure the construct validity of the four features. The scores of the model fit
indices all met the criteria that CFI and TLI were above 0.90, RMSEA was below 0.08, and SRMR was below 0.10 (Hu & Bentler, 1999),
i.e., 0.046 for RMSEA, 0.072 for SRMR, 0.931 for CFI, and 0.945 for TLI, showing that the construction of these factors was acceptable.
The standardized factor loadings and detailed descriptions for the four latent variables are presented in Table S2 in the Supplementary
Materials. Fourth, the data conversion was conducted using SPSS 23. The categorical variables were converted to dummy variables.
For the RF analysis, the continuous variables were z-scored, while for the multilevel analysis, the student-level factors were
group-centered, and the school-level factors were grand-centered (Hofmann & Gavin, 1998).
The two-stage sequential analysis is provided as follows in Fig. 2. In Stage 1, RF with 10-fold cross-validation (i.e., nine folds were
used as the training data, and the remaining fold was used as the testing data) was performed to obtain the ranking of the ICT-related
features. The number of trees in the RF model was set to 100 to achieve high efficiency of classification and economy of computation
(Oshiro et al., 2012). The code for analysis was uploaded to GitHub for ease of reference. In Stage 2, multilevel analysis was conducted
for a detailed analysis of the impacts of ICT-related factors on students’ reading performance. The student weights were implemented
to account for the design effects shown in the PISA data (Hu & Wang, 2022a). Additionally, to ensure the reliability of the results, HLM
assumptions were checked (Hox, 2010). The correlation matrices of the derived independent variables were calculated, accompanied
by the values of variance inflation factors (VIFs), which helped to identify multicollinearity (see Tables S3–S5 provided in Supplementary Material 4). After modeling, the normality of the residuals of the three levels and homoscedasticity of the level-1 residual were
examined using the Q-Q plot (e.g., Wang et al., 2023), the results of which are provided in Supplementary Material 5.
4. Results
4.1. The optimal feature set with high efficiency
Stage 1 in this study utilized RF to build a regression model with acceptable fit levels, providing the ranking of the relative
importance of the ICT-related factors in the blended learning setting. The results under 10-fold cross-validation are provided below.
The RF model composed of 27 factors showed acceptable efficiency (i.e., MSE of 6319.33, MAE of 63.19, and R2 of 0.38). Previous
investigations have found that the optimal feature set is composed of 20–30 factors (e.g., Hu et al., 2022b; Gorostiaga & Rojo-Álvarez,
2016). Our experiment retained 27 factors as the optimal number of factors with data-driven evidence (see Fig. 3) (e.g., Gorostiaga &
Rojo-Álvarez, 2016) and provided their relative importance in the prediction of students’ reading outcomes. The rank and detailed
descriptions of the features are provided in Fig. 4 and Table 2 for ease of reference.
4.2. The heterogeneous effects of the multilevel factors
In the one-way ANOVA model, intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs) of the three levels were all above 0.059 (see Table 3), which
indicated the existence of variations and proved the validity of conducting HLM analysis (Cohen, 1988). A small percentage of variance
due to school-level differences is common in research involving psychological constructs (e.g., Wang et al., 2023; Yerdelen & Sungur,
2019).

Quadratic effects of four variables, namely, interest in ICT (INTICT), ICT use outside of school for entertainment (ENTUSE), ICT

ICT resources, respectively. Regarding quadratic effects of INTICT and ENTUSE, they corresponded to the parts on the inverted

declined gradually and leveled up when students showed higher interests in ICT (see Fig. 5a) and using ICT more frequently outside of
school for entertainment (see Fig. 5b), while there was no prominent negative return. However, the quadratic effects for ICTSCH and
ICTHOME can be plotted as those parts shown on the inverted U-shaped curves that concave downward, i.e., monotone decreasing. It
means that when the ICTSCH and ICTHOME increased, only small reading performance return was observed, and the excessive ICT
resources at school (see Fig. 5c) and at home (see Fig. 5d) negatively predicted students’ reading performance.
To explore the detailed nature of cross-level linear interactions in the final model, simple slope analyses were also conducted to test
the significance of the independent variable (e.g., Murayama & Elliot, 2009; Qian & Lau, 2022). The quantity of school ICT resources
was found to significantly moderate the relationship between students’ reading format preference and their reading performance. The
two simple slope analyses revealed that when ICTSCH became higher, both the positive relationship between the preference for a paper
format (PAPERPRE) and students’ reading performance, and between equal interest in paper and digital reading formats (EQUAFORM) and students’ reading performance were strengthened (see Fig. 6a and b).
In addition, quadratic two-way interactions were examined. The simple slope analyses in Fig. 7 revealed that the negative quadratic
effects of ICT use at school in general (USESCH) (see Fig. 7a) and the use of ICT outside of school for schoolwork activities (HOMESCH)
(see Fig. 7b) on students’ reading performance were buffered when the quality of school ICT resource (SCHICTQUA) was higher. The
relationship between the subject-related ICT use outside of lessons (ICTOUTSIDE) and reading performance could be plotted as an
inverted U-shaped curve. When the proportion of available computers connected to the internet (RATCMP2) became higher, the curve
witnessed a vertical stretching with the increasing variation and the maximum threshold value (see Fig. 7c). The simple slope analysis
showed that higher RATCMP2 magnified the effects of ICTOUTSIDE.
5. Discussion
The aim of this research was fourfold: 1) to identify the optimal ICT-related feature set and their relative importance for reading in
the blended learning setting; 2) to investigate the relationships between student-level ICT-related factors and students’ reading performance; 3) to examine the association between school-level ICT-related factors and students’ reading performance; and 4) to probe
into the moderation effect of school-level ICT-related factors on the relationship between student-level factors and reading performance. Accordingly, four hypotheses were formulated. The discussions below address the four hypotheses corresponding to the four
research questions.

5.1. Responses to RQ1: The optimal ICT-related feature set and feature importance ranking
Our results supported H1 through the identification of the optimal ICT-related features set that retained the efficiency in the RF
model. The amalgamation of these ICT-related features were substantiated to collectively predict students’ reading performance. It
meant that the complex ecology of blended learning settings could be affected by the ICT-related feature set as a whole group. These
results further supported the importance of the organic integration rather than the simple mixture of various ICT-related elements in
the blended learning setting, echoing the perspective of ecological system in education (Bronfenbrenner, 1979).
Our research identified the four most important factors at the student level. In addition to the effect of the control variable ESCS
(ranking the 1st), the use of ICT at school in general (USESCH) was identified as the most important feature that received concerns in
our study (ranking the 2nd), followed immediately by students’ interest in ICT (INTICT) (ranking the 3rd), use of ICT outside of school
for schoolwork activities (HOMESCH) (ranking the 4th) and ICT use outside of school for entertainment (ENTUSE) (ranking the 5th).
Past years have witnessed the importance of teachers’ instruction on students’ reading performance, with teachers telling students how
to find information they need in an encyclopedia and whether they should recognize that information as accurate. The ubiquity of
blended learning settings and the rapid advancement of ICT presents reading materials in a more complex way, requiring young
readers to develop the capacity to navigate through dazzling ambiguity, to triangulate between different viewpoints and to understand
complicated content (OECD, 2021). In terms of students’ reading format preference, our study showed that a preference for paper
reading (PAPERPRE) was the most important, followed by equal interest in digital and paper reading formats (EQUAFORM) and a
preference for paper reading (PAPERPRE), echoing previous studies that highlighted the advantage of reading on paper in the
increasingly digitalized world (Goodwin et al., 2020).
Regarding the school-level factors, except for the high importance of the control variable SCHSIZE (ranking the 10th), the ICT
available at school (ICTSCH) (ranking the 7th), ICT skills taught by teachers (ICTSKIT) (ranking the 9th), and school ICT use promotion

5.2. Responses to RQ2: The influence of student-level ICT-related features on reading outcome
Results of this study verified H2 in general, and further specified the inverted U-shaped effects of students’ interests in ICT, ICT use,
and family ICT resources. Although the effects of student-level ICT-related factors on their academic performance were delineated in
previous research, some nuanced differences were observed within the blended learning setting.
Students perceived autonomy and competence related to ICT use and preference for different formats were all positively related to
their reading performance in the blended learning environment, while ICT as a topic in students’ social interaction was detrimental to
reading performance. Our results confirmed the findings of Kong et al. (2018), who systematically examined the association between
ICT and adolescents’ reading achievement. This is because students who show positive attitudes toward ICT use are more likely to
actively explore the use of digital equipment and develop familiarity with the reading formats on digital media and thus perform well
in the PISA 2018 digital assessment of reading literacy. However, ICT as a topic in students’ social interaction indicates diverted
attention from schoolwork and from deep reading, which is detrimental to students’ reading performance (Hu et al., 2023). In addition,
our study further showed that the specific nature of the blended learning setting and the increasing digitalization of the reading
environment reinforced the importance of students’ interests and autonomy in technology-penetrated environments (Garrison &
Vaughan, 2008).
It is interesting to find that the effects of students’ interests in ICT (INTICT), ICT use outside of school for entertainment (ENTUSE)
and ICT available at home (ICTHOME), though generally categorized as inverted U-shaped relationships in previous studies (e.g.,
Gubbels et al., 2020), showed subtle differences. The effects of the former two variables corresponded to the first half of the concave
function, i.e., a monotone increasing trend with decreasing return. Students interests in ICT can help them stay curious and motivated
when navigating through the blended learning setting, contributing to their academic performance. However, the educational returns
of this factor decrease because excessive interest in ICT may divert students’ attention from study. Similarly, the decreasing returns of
the effect of ENTUSE is noteworthy. Items for this variable include students’ frequency of using ICT to play online computer games,
sending emails, reading news online, and searching for information for leisure, which are beneficial for their understanding about the
digital world, whereas indulgence into the virtual world is detrimental for students’ physical and mental development. On the contrary, the effect of ICT available at home, however, shows a monotone decreasing trend. Students’ use of ICT resources at home is
generally without the supervision of teachers or parents, which may result in addiction to computers (Bandres & Benedi, 2022). This
study has recognized the importance of students’ autonomy in acquiring digital skills and building a sustainable and supportive web
environment for young readers, which broadened the scope of blended learning from the classroom to family and the web society.
Teachers in the blended reading classroom should make the best of the diverse useful web applications to create a helpful learning
environment for students after class, thus improving students’ self-dependency in learning.

5.3. Responses to RQ3: The influence of school-level ICT-related features on reading outcome
Results of our analysis partially support H3, while school ICT use support for teachers and teaching of ICT skills in the blended
learning setting negatively predict students’ reading performance.
Regarding the contentious role of the provision of ICT resources at school in previous studies, our study provided a new perspective:
the effect of ICT resources quantity at school aligns with the second half of the U-shaped curve (i.e., a monotone decreasing trend),
while the quality of ICT resources at school was significantly positively related to students’ reading performance. The former further
specified the general U-shaped curve identified in previous studies (e.g., Zhu & Li, 2022; Ünal et al., 2022), highlighting its negative
effect in the blended learning setting. This is possibly because the ICT resources such as media and online learning materials are not
integrated with effective teaching strategies or instructional design in the classroom (Uzun & Kilis, 2019), thus calling for the
appropriate use of ICT in the blended classroom to ensure the efficiency of reading education. The proportion of ICT connected to the
internet (RAMTCMP2) and the quality of ICT resources (SCHICTQUA) covers the hardware and software quality (e.g., adequate
software, effective online learning support platform, high computing capacity of devices, fast internet speed, and computers of quality
for instruction), respectively. The availability of internet connections for digital equipment may aid students in accessing digital books
(Radia, 2019), and the supportive online learning platform, as a vital part of the blended learning setting, provides flexible learning
instructions that students need to carefully read by themselves, which contributes to students’ autonomy of learning. It worth
mentioning that the differentiated effects between the quantity and quality of school ICT resources on students’ reading performance
provide a new outlook for the examination of the impact of ICT resources at school. Schools should highlight both the quality and
quantity dimensions of ICT resources in building the blended learning environment, which could enhance the educational effectiveness. This is also in line with the educational effectiveness framework proposed by Creemers and Kyriakides (2008).
Another important finding in our research lies in the positive effects of school ICT use promotion and regulation policies for
teachers and students. The items about ICT-related policies answered by the school principals included the school-level standard about
ICT use, the ICT use program for certain subjects, guidance and regular discussions about teacher and student ICT use at school. First,
the supplementary policies could provide a sustainable and helpful ICT use environment that contributes to teachers’ effective ICT
integration in class and students’ proper use of ICT resources. Second, the effectiveness of school ICT use policy reflects the technology
leadership roles of school principals, which have a positive effect on teachers’ effective ICT utilization and students’ academic performance (Ghavifekr & Wong, 2022). Furthermore, the scope of the blended learning setting is expanded from the classroom to the soft
ICT use environment at school. The efficiency of bended learning setting is thus not only related to the teaching practices of teachers,
but also benefits from the support of schools’ ICT use promotion policies. However, since the items in PISA 2018 did not specify the
frequency and approaches of ICT use suggested by school policies, researchers should further explore the detailed effect of different ICT
promotion and regulation policies in the future.
Contrary to most of the previous findings and part of our research hypothesis, our study identified negative relationships between
teaching ICT skills to students and reading performance and between school support for teachers’ ICT use. The first result is in
accordance with our previous findings that teaching ICT skills to students cannot guarantee reading excellence (Hu & Wang, 2022a).
First, considering that the items for constructing this variable are dichotomous (i.e., yes or no questions), the quality of ICT skills
teaching cannot be meticulously measured. Second, researchers have found that it is not the teaching of digital approaches but the
task-oriented use of ICT that is a key factor for students’ learning performance, reinforcing the importance of designing learning
scenarios that promote actual effective behavioral engagement (Lima et al., 2022). Future research should thus delineate specific ways
of teaching ICT skills to students (e.g., teachers plainly show the students how to use digital devices in class, or students themselves can
use ICT to accomplish tasks under the guidance of teachers in class) and further examine the effect of teachers’ instruction quality on
their reading performance. The second negative association, however, reflects the nonequivalence between school ICT support for
teachers perceived by principals and instructors. Items such as “there are professional resources for teachers to learn how to use digital
devices” for this variable were answered by the school principals, but teachers’ perceptions of this question could be different, and
their actual support gained from schools cannot be determined.
5.4. Responses to RQ4: The moderation effects of school-level ICT-related factors
H4 in this study postulated that there were significant cross-level moderating effects, which was supported by the results from the
moderation model. Our results not only identified linear cross-level moderation effects about ICT resources at school and students
reading format preference, but also further complemented the proposed hypotheses through the identification of the quadratic twoway cross-level interactions.
Different from previous studies, our analysis newly delineated the interesting school-level moderations between different reading
format preferences and students’ reading performance. Students’ preference for paper reading was identified as the most contributive
feature of students’ reading performance, followed by equal interest in paper and digital reading. The provision of high-quality ICT
resources at school strengthened this relationship, and a similar moderation effect was found in the positive relationship between
students’ equal interest in paper and digital reading. This is because the provision of digital reading formats and texts supported by ICT
can motivate students to acquire different reading skills and experience different cognitive processes (OECD, 2021). Consequently,
appropriate ICT use can help students who prefer paper reading gain the basic digital competence that are required to persist in their
future studies, particularly when ICT plays a significant role in young students’ academic and nonacademic lives. Schools should
provide high-quality digital equipment and use motivating ICT resources to encourage reading in schools and improve students’
reading proficiency.

For one thing, the monotone decreasing quadratic effects of ICT use at school in general (USESCH) and ICT use outside of school for
schoolwork activities (HOMESCH) were buffered by higher quality of school ICT resources. For another, the effects of subject-related
ICT use outside of lessons (ICTOUTSIDE) on students’ reading performance can be described with a more complete inverted U-shaped

quality of ICT resources and school and access to the internet at school in the blended setting could provide students with more opportunities for self-reading practice and more flexible reading patterns (Xiao & Hew, 2022), which could promote their reading
performance. Schools and teachers should thus not only cooperate with parents to put young students’ ICT use under supervision with
optimal frequency, but also invest on high-quality ICT resources and provide effective technology-rich activities for students in the
blended setting.
6. Conclusion, limitations and future research
This study combined an AI algorithm and a multilevel analytical approach to examine the effects of multilevel ICT-related factors in
blended settings on students’ reading performance. Results showed that 1) the optimal feature set, composed of student- and schoollevel ICT factors in the blended learning setting, collectively predicted students’ reading performance efficiently, with student-level
factors showing the most importance; 2) students’ perceived autonomy and competence in ICT, preference for different formats
were positively related to their reading performance, and the inverted U-shaped effects of interests in ICT, family ICT resources, ICT use
for recreational purposes and for schoolwork were identified, showing subtle differences; 3) the quality of school ICT resources and
school policies for promoting and regulating ICT use were positively related to students’ reading performance, while school ICT use
support for teachers perceived by principals and teaching of ICT skills negatively predicted students’ reading performance; and 4) the
quality of ICT resources at school in the blended learning could moderate the linear relationship between students’ preference for
reading formats and their reading performance, and the quadratic effects of ICT use at school, ICT use outside of school for schoolwork
activities and subject-related ICT use outside of lessons.
Different from previous studies, this research provided the optimal feature set identified with the AI algorithm and the linear and
curvilinear effects produced by the multilevel models, which collectively contributed to a comprehensive understanding about the in
the blended learning setting. On one hand, our findings were in line with previous results regarding the positive effects of students’
positive attitudinal ICT-related factors, the detrimental effect of ICT as a topic in social interaction, and the quadratic effects of ICT
resources at home and provided by the school, and ICT use on students’ reading performance (e.g., Bhutoria & Aljabri, 2022; Kong
et al., 2022). On the other hand, some innovative findings were identified: 1) students’ interests in ICT and use of ICT for recreational
purposes corresponded to the first half of the U-shaped curve, showing the monotone increasing trend; 2) the quality dimension rather
than the quantity dimension of ICT resources in the blended learning settings provided by schools could contribute to students’ reading
performance; and 3) the effects of students’ reading format preferences were all directly positively related to students’ reading performance, and the positive effects paper format preference and equal interest were strengthened by school ICT resources quantity.
This research has the following implications. Regarding methodology, this research invites consideration of the combination of
conventional analytical methods and AI algorithms to predict students’ reading performance in the blended learning environment,
providing new perspectives for future reading research based on a large educational dataset. Theoretically, this study identifies the
importance of ICT-related features and their different effects on students’ reading performance in the blended learning environment,
conceptualizing the ICT-related influence mechanism within the discipline of reading. Practically, using the PISA 2018 dataset, this
research examines the effects and cross-level moderation of multilevel factors related to adolescents’ reading performance in the
blended learning environment, lending support to more nuanced guidance for reading classrooms with technical penetration. Balance
and moderation should be taken into consideration, and various ICT-related elements should be organically integrated to give full play
to the advantages of blended learning and realize the optimization of teaching. Schools should provide more high-quality ICT resources
to both students and teachers, and teachers in the blended learning environment should effectively integrate technology into reading
instruction and apply appropriate pedagogies. Additionally, school principals, teachers and parents should make concerted efforts to
build a sustainable and helpful blended ecosystem to stimulate students’ cognitive processes, develop their critical thinking skills,
provoke their learning interests, and fully exploit their learning potential.
The findings in this research should also be considered with a few limitations. First, the PISA data used in our analysis are crosssectional and based on individual responses, and the effects of some important and contentious variables could not be examined.
Second, in this secondary analysis of PISA, the data-driven methods may only focus on the patterns and trends in the big data while
neglecting some important aspects and the reasons. Due to the limitation of the sole use of quantitative research methods, some of the
explanations of the results should be treated with caution. A mixed-methods design is suggested to reveal the specific reasons underlying the phenomenon and provide a more comprehensive picture of the association between factors in blended learning settings
and students’ reading proficiency (e.g., Farkas & Jang, 2019). Third, as a cross-sectional dataset, some items in the PISA questionnaire
provided only limited information, and the long-term effects of some variables could not be investigated. Future studies should
consider designing longitudinal experiments (e.g., Macaruso et al., 2019) to explore the impact of blended learning on students’
reading performance.
