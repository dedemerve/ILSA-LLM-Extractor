The impact of reading metacognitive strategies on mathematics learning
efficiency and performance: An analysis using PISA 2018 data in China
Weizhong Zhang , Rong Lian *, 1
School of psychology, Fujian Normal University, Fuzhou, Fujian 350007, China

A R T I C L E I N F O

A B S T R A C T

Keywords:
Mathematics learning efficiency
Mathematics performance
Reading metacognition
PISA
Potential profile analysis

The current study employed latent profile analysis to examine the application patterns of students’ reading
metacognitive strategies using PISA 2018 data in China. Subsequently, it explored the differences in students’
mathematics learning efficiency and performance. The results revealed that: (1) Six types of reading metacognitive strategies application patterns were identified: “Novice - indifferent,” “Veteran - average,” “Novice low evaluating,” “Veteran - skilled,” “Novice - mixed,” and “Novice - arbitrary.” (2) The primary factors that
affect the classification of reading metacognitive strategies application patterns were gender, and family economic, social, and cultural statuses (ESCS). (3) Mathematics learning time could positively predict performance
overall, but the mathematics learning time of “Veteran - skilled” and “Novice - mixed” students had no significant
correlation with their mathematics performance. The findings suggests that educators should not blindly increase
students’ mathematics learning time but instead provide appropriate guidance based on their mastery patterns of
reading metacognitive strategies to enhance mathematics learning efficiency and performance.

1. Introduction

1.1. Reading metacognitive strategies enhance learning efficiency

Reducing students’ academic burden by cutting learning time has
sparked controversy. Some researchers indicate that there is no significant correlation between learning time and performance in mathematics, and blindly extending mathematics learning time may not yield
desired outcomes (Tang & Fu, 2007; Wang et al., 2014). However,
alleviating students’ learning burden through a universal reduction of
learning time may have negative effects on the interests of disadvantaged students and exacerbate educational inequality, as students’ socioeconomic and cultural backgrounds, as well as school-related factors,
significantly influence their academic performance (Zhao et al., 2020).
Therefore, some researchers argue that alleviating the academic burden
on students should prioritize reducing quantity while maintaining
quality, rather than simply reducing knowledge content and learning
time for all students (Jiang & Saito, 2022). Given these considerations,
the foremost task for educational practitioners was to employ appropriate methods to enhance students’ learning efficiency and ensure that
students from diverse backgrounds can achieve their optimal
performance.

Students can enhance their learning effectiveness by acquiring
appropriate reading strategies. Numerous studies have shown that
reading plays a vital role in facilitating students’ understanding of textbased knowledge and information, and it is strongly connected to their
cognitive ability and academic performance in diverse disciplines
(Cunningham & Stanovich, 1997; McGeown et al., 2014; Stanovich,
2017). However, up to 61 % of students aged 12 to 15 lack fundamental
reading skills according to the UNESCO Institute for Statistics (UIS)
(UNESCO, 2017). These students who struggle with reading may experience unsatisfactory academic performance despite investing a significant amount of time in learning. This is because poor readers face
challenges from understanding language to pronunciation in the early
stages of reading, limiting their access to information compared to more
proficient readers (Stanovich, 2017).
The reading process encompasses a range of cognitive and metacognitive strategies, and higher-order reading metacognitive strategies
are crucial for enhancing students’ academic performance. Research has
demonstrated that reading is a multifaceted cognitive process, requiring
individuals to selectively extract data, facts, and opinions from written

Huang et al., 2021). It involves not only comprehending the text’s
meaning but also establishing connections between prior and current
information, ultimately evaluating its usefulness and applicability. This
process is closely related to individuals’ metacognitive ability.
Reading metacognitive strategies encompass a set of strategies
employed by readers during the reading process to enhance comprehension, memory, and learning (Xia et al., 2022). Broadly speaking,
cognitive strategies assist students in processing information through
techniques such as memorization, making connections between new and
existing knowledge, summarizing, and more. Meanwhile, metacognitive
strategies empower students to acknowledge their cognitive abilities
and actively monitor their ongoing thought processes, facilitating the
development of plans and the implementation of self-regulation to adapt
their learning behaviors accordingly (Flavell, 1979; Weinstein & Mayer,
1983). Specifically, reading metacognitive strategies encompass a range
of techniques, including self-monitoring, goal setting, activating prior
knowledge, prediction, refining key information, and note-taking,
among others (Xia et al., 2022). These strategies have been shown to
significantly improve students’ reading abilities (Chen, 2020; Huang
et al., 2021; Mohseni et al., 2020; Muhid et al., 2020; Sutiyatno &
Sukarno, 2019).
However, not all students are able to master these higher-order
reading metacognitive strategies. For instance, research indicates that
Chinese students often rely on memory-based cognitive strategies,
which may restrict their reading efficiency, while in contrast, students in
Singapore who prioritize reading metacognitive strategies tend to
demonstrate superior reading performance (Wu et al., 2021). In addition, students’ demographic characteristics have a significant impact on
their mastery of reading metacognitive strategies, such as gender, family
economic, social, and cultural statuses (ESCS), and school characteristics (Vazquez-Lopez & Huerta-Manzanilla, 2021; Xie, et al., 2023; Zhou
et al., 2020; Vazquez-Lopez & Huerta-Manzanilla, 2021; Zhou et al.,
2020). These studies suggest that students may exhibit significant differences in both their overall mastery level and specific patterns of
reading metacognitive strategies due to various influencing factors.
The differences in the mastery patterns of reading metacognitive
strategies might be a contributing factor to the variations in students’
reading literacy and learning efficiency. However, previous research on
reading metacognitive strategies predominantly employed a variablecentric approach, assuming homogeneity in the relationships between
variables across different samples, thereby disregarding the inherent
heterogeneity within groups (Xia et al., 2022; Xie et al., 2023; Xia et al.,
2022; Xie et al., 2023). To address this limitation, adopting a personcentered approach allows for a more nuanced understanding of the individual differences and heterogeneity within groups, enabling researchers to capture the unique profiles of students’ mastery patterns of
reading metacognitive strategies and their impact on learning efficiency
and performance.

1.3. The present study
Utilizing reading metacognitive strategies is a promising approach
for enhancing students’ mathematical performance. However, previous
studies had failed to consider the heterogeneity in patterns of students’
reading metacognitive strategies. Therefore, it is necessary to employ a
person-centered approach to explore the various patterns of students’
reading metacognitive strategies. This study aims to identify the
different types of reading metacognitive strategies by using latent profile
analysis (LPA). Additionally, the present study will analyze the factors
that influence the classification of different patterns, as well as potential
variations in mathematics learning efficiency and performance among
students exhibiting different patterns of reading metacognitive
strategies.
The present study hypothesis that: (1) Students exhibit different
patterns of mastery in reading metacognitive strategies, and they show
differences in mathematics learning efficiency and performance; (2) The
different patterns of students are influenced by demographic variables,
and female, high ESCS, and city school students are more likely to have
acquired reading metacognitive strategies; (3) Students who fail to acquire reading metacognitive strategies may have poor mathematical
learning efficiency and performance, and they may need to spend more
time to compensate for the deficiencies in their learning strategies.
2. Methods
2.1. Data source
2.1.1. Participants
PISA is an international research program sponsored by the organization for economic co-operation and development (OECD), conducted
every three years to evaluate students’ proficiency in reading, mathematics, and science (OECD, 2021). For the PISA 2018 study, participants
were selected from 361 schools across four Chinese provinces. The
current study completely removed 65 cases with missing data (0.53 %),
due to the lack of data for the key variables. Finally, a total of 11,993
samples were included in the analysis. The sample consisted of 5743
females and 6250 males, all of whom were 15 years old.

1.2. Reading and mathematics performance
Reading plays a crucial role in the study of mathematics, as it aids
students in understanding and solving mathematical concepts and
problems (Hart et al., 2009; Lapasau et al., 2022). Researches indicate
that even students with strong spatial, logical, and mathematical
reasoning skills may struggle to solve practical problems due to deficiencies in their reading abilities (Derya, 2020; Özcan & Doğan, 2018).
There is also evidence supporting the predictive role of reading ability in
the progress of mathematics achievement. A longitudinal study has also
revealed that students with stronger early reading abilities are more
likely to experience significant improvements in their mathematics skills
(Grimm, 2008).

students often encounter difficulties and make errors in various mathematical problems (Leiss et al., 2019; Schumacher & Fuchs, 2012). For

Finally, a multiple linear regression was performed separately for

multiple linear regression analysis, it is possible to determine the size,
direction, and statistical significance of the effects of different predictor
variables on the outcome variable, thereby establishing a predictive
model for further prediction. We also examined VIF (Variance Inflation

measure the presence of collinearity issues, and it was generally believed
that a VIF value below 10 indicated the nonsignificant multicollinearity
problems (Shrestha, 2020). These analysis aimed to investigate differences in mathematics learning efficacy among the various student types.

2.1.2.2. Mathematics performance and learning time. Ten different
possible mathematics performance scores (PV1MATH-PV10MATH)
were calculated for each student. In the current study, the average of
these ten scores was calculated to obtain the categorical variable
(MATHACH) from the students’ mathematics performance scores.
Additionally, the students’ weekly learning time of mathematics
(MMINS) was also used.

3. Research results

2.1.2.3. Demographic variables. The demographic variables such as
gender (ST004D01T), ESCS index (ESCS), and the school location
(SC001Q01TA) were used in this study. A dichotomous variable for female turns 1 if the person is female, and 0 otherwise. No third nonbinary option was provided by the survey. The ESCS was derived from
the students’ report on the availability of 16 household items, other
possessions in the students’ homes, education and work status of the
students’ parents. It was used to assess the level of economic, social and
cultural background and resources of a family. Finally, the current study
used the population of the area where the school was located as a criterion for school’s location, village, hamlet or rural area (fewer than
3000 people), town (3000 to about 100,000 people), and city (with over
100,000 people).

3.1. Common method bias analysis
The result of common method bias analysis showed that the overall
model extracted 64.8 %, while the first factor accounted for 31.0 %, well
below the classic threshold of 50.0 % (Harman, 1960), suggested that
common method biases did not significantly influence current findings.
3.2. Correlation analysis
The results of the correlation analysis were presented in Table 1. The
results indicated a positive correlation between mathematics performance and all three dimensions, as well as the total scores of reading
metacognitive strategies (rs = 0.299–0.464, ps < 0.001). On the other
hand, weekly mathematics learning time exhibited a negligible negative
correlation with the dimensions of “Understanding and remembering,”
“Summarizing,” and the total reading metacognition score (rs = − 0.031
~ − 0.041, ps < 0.01). However, there was marginally and positive
significant correlation between weekly mathematics learning time and
performance (r = 0.017, p = 0.057).

2.2. Data analysis
Firstly, to examine common method bias, we conducted Harman’s
single-factor test by performing an exploratory factor analysis on all
items of the dependent, independent, and control variables (Harman,
1960; Podsakoff et al., 2012). It is generally considered that if a single
factor explains <50 % of the variance, there was no significant presence
of common method bias (Harman, 1960).
Secondly, due to its higher flexibility and interpretability compared
to traditional hard clustering techniques, latent profile analysis (LPA)
was capable of better handling sample overlap and fuzziness, thus
providing more accurate classification results (Wang et al., 2021). The
current study employed Mplus 8.3 software to conduct a LPA in order to
identify groups of students who utilized different combinations of
reading metacognitive strategies (Muthén, 2021). The optimal number
of profiles was determined by initially fitting a single-profile model,
gradually increasing the number of profiles, and then evaluated and
compared model fit indices. AIC (Akaike Information Criterion), BIC
(Bayesian Information Criterion), and aBIC (adjusted Bayesian Information Criterion) were utilized as indicators of model fit in this study. A
higher value of Entropy (no <0.7) and lower values of AIC, BIC, and
aBIC indicate better model fit (Jung & Wickrama, 2008; Meyer & Morin,
2016). Additionally, the K-profile model was considered a better fit than
the K-1 profile model if the p-values from the LMR (Lo-Mendell-Rubin
likelihood ratio test) and BLRT (Bootstrap likelihood ratio test) were
<0.05 (Nylund et al., 2007). Furthermore, for a particular profile,
generalizability was not considered if the population size is <5.0 %
(Marsh et al., 2009; Muthen & Muthen, 2000).

They seldom utilize higher-order reading metacognitive strategies,

resources in their environment result in students lacking necessary
reading support and guidance, which contributes to their transition into
the “Novice - indifferent” or “Novice - arbitrary” types. This, in turn,
diminishes their reading metacognition and mathematic performance.
In math classrooms, teachers need to particularly focus on developing these students’ reading metacognitive skills. When students
encounter various reading materials and tasks, teachers should not only
teach them reading skills and strategies but also guide them to analyze
and compare different strategies. This helps students identify effective
reading metacognitive strategies for specific learning goals and encourages reflection and evaluation of different methods. Such an
approach enhances students’ awareness and cognitive understanding of
strategy effectiveness, fostering a lifelong learning ability.

Overall, these results confirmed most of our hypotheses. Different
students indeed shown variations in their mastery patterns of reading
metacognitive strategies, which were mainly influenced by gender and
ESCS. Furthermore, different types of students also exhibited differences
in their mathematical learning efficiency and performance. In the discussion section, we will further explore and discuss these findings.
4. Discussion
The current study employed LPA to identify patterns of reading
metacognitive strategies among students based on PISA 2018 data from
four regions in China. Six distinct patterns of application emerged,
namely “Novice-indifferent,” “Veteran-average,” “Novice-low evaluating,” “Veteran-skilled,” “Novice-mixed,” and “Novice-arbitrary.” The
study also explored the influence of gender, ESCS, and school location
on these patterns. Additionally, it investigated the relationship between
students’ mathematics learning time and performance within each
pattern, aiming to understand how promoting reading metacognitive
strategies could potentially alleviate students’ academic burden.
4.1. Potential patterns of reading metacognitive strategies used by
students
4.1.1. “Veteran - skilled” students excel in mathematics
The “Veteran - skilled” type exhibited the highest percentage (38.3
%) among all types. These students demonstrated a strong ability to
employ effective reading metacognitive strategies, allowing them to
achieve superior performance in mathematics compared to other types.
The result is consistent with previous research indicating a positive
relationship between academic performance and reading proficiency
(McGeown et al., 2014). According to some researchers, heightened
awareness regarding the efficacy of reading strategies in the context of
diverse reading tasks and objectives was positively associated with a
greater propensity to employ them effectively to attain desired learning
outcomes (Xia et al., 2022). Consequently, this engendered the acquisition of a more extensive body of knowledge and valuable information,
ultimately culminating in enhanced performance in the field of
mathematics.
Additionally, the results reveal a significantly greater proportion of
girls and high ESCS students in the “Veteran - skilled” type compared to
the other types. These findings reflect the impact of physiological
development patterns between males and females, as well as the influence of family environment (Economic, social, and cultural factors) on
students’ acquisition of reading metacognitive strategies. Previous
research had found that girls often outperformed boys of the same age in
reading due to variations in brain functional organization and early
physiological development (Burman et al., 2008; Kutner et al., 2007;
Reilly et al., 2019), and girls also have higher metacognitive awareness
of reading strategies than boys (Demirel et al., 2015). Meanwhile,
families with a high ESCS index can provide students with a more
favorable learning environment. For example, this environment can
affect students’ reading skills directly or indirectly through factors such
as parental education levels, family education style, and access to
educational resources (Chen, 2020). In educational practice, particular
attention needs to be given to boys and those with low ESCS due to their
deficiencies in employing reading metacognitive strategies. Teachers
should tailor distinct approaches and enhancement strategies for cultivating reading metacognitive skills based on the characteristics of
different student populations.

4.2. Assessing credibility strategies has the greatest effect on mathematics
performance
Interestingly, “Novice - mixed” students have better mathematics
performance compared to “Novice - low evaluating” students. However,
both “Novice - low evaluating” and “Novice - mixed” exhibit similarities
in the dimensions of “Understanding and memorizing” and “Summarizing.” The main difference between them lies in “Assessing credibility.”
Furthermore, the results of the correlation analysis also indicate that
“Assessing credibility” has the highest correlation with mathematics
performance (r = 0.435). These results indicate that the ability to assess
information might be a crucial reading metacognitive strategy that influences students’ mathematics performance. According to Xia et al.
(2022), information evaluation strategies have a stronger impact on
students’ mathematics performance compared to understanding and
remembering strategies and information summarizing strategies (Xia
et al., 2022). Mathematical problem-solving, such as arithmetic skills,
relies on procedural knowledge and the accuracy, flexibility, and
fluency in performing arithmetic operations involving natural numbers,
real numbers, and algebra (Xie et al., 2019). In these more complex
cognitive processes, the dimensions of “Understanding and memorizing”
as well as “Summarizing” might not be as important as the “Assessing
credibility” dimension.
4.3. Implementing personalized strategy to reduce the learning burden on
students
Some studies have supported a positive correlation between mathematics learning time and performance (Shen, 2014; Zhao et al., 2020),
while others have not (Tang & Fu, 2007; Wang et al., 2014). This study
has highlighted the existence of different types of students with varying
correlations between mathematics learning time and performance. This
finding helps explain why previous studies have found limited or no
correlation between these two variables. Therefore, it is essential to take
into account the characteristics of students in order to alleviate their
academic burden by reducing their learning time.

4.4. Limitations
Our current study had four limitations. Firstly, the PISA 2018 test
data only covered relatively affluent regions such as Beijing, Shanghai,
Jiangsu, and Zhejiang, where educational attainment was developed. As
a result, there is a lack of significant empirical research in less affluent
areas, potentially leading to an underestimation of the impact of demographic characteristics on students’ use of reading metacognitive
strategies. Future research using a more representative sample is
needed. Secondly, the PISA 2018 data adopted a cross-sectional design,
which limited the ability to establish causal relationships. Therefore, it is
important for future research to consider employing longitudinal or
experimental designs, as these methodologies can provide stronger evidence and further enhance the development of the current research
topic. Thirdly, the PISA test’s reading metacognitive strategies were
limited to three dimensions. However, reading is a complex cognitive
process that involves more than just “Understanding and remembering,”
“Summarizing,” and “Assessing credibility.” Future research could
expand to the development and implementation of other metacognitive
strategies within reading tasks. Fourth, the analysis revealed a substantial number of potential profiles, making it more challenging to
analyze and apply the findings in practical settings. Further discussions
are required to understand the application patterns of reading metacognitive strategies among students.
