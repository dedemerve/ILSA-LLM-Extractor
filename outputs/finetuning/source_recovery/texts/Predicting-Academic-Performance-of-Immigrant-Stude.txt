ABSTRACT
The education sector has been effectively dealing with the prediction of academic performance of
immigrant students, and the research associated with this domain proves beneficial enough for those
countries where the ministry of education has to cater to such immigrants for altering and updating
policies in order to elevate the overall education pedagogy for them. The present research begins with
analyzing varied educational data mining and machine learning techniques that help in assessing the
data fetched form PISA. It’s elucidated that XGBoost stands out to be the ideal machine learning
technique for achieving the desired results. Subsequently, the parameters have been optimized using
the hyper parameter tuning techniques and implemented on the XGBoost Regressor algorithm. There
is low error rate and higher level of predictive ability using the machine learning algorithms, which
assure better predictions using the PISA data. The final results have been discussed along with future
research work.
Keywords
Machine Learning, Prediction Algorithm, XGBoost

RELATED WORK
Over the last 10 years the approach of EDM known as Educational data mining has spurred
magnificently and has proven effective in exploring patterns and trends in the education field through
advanced techniques. Using the two prime algorithms of advanced machine learning and data mining
EDM targets towards the following: (1) exploiting unprocessed data from educational sites (these can
be huge no: of assessments, students records comprising of their academic progress or even log info
from LMS or e-learning systems), (2) identifying various patterns, relations and trends in education,
and (3)employing the explored information towards enhancing the process of decision-making in the
realm of educational sector. By the means of EDM technique, complex issues pertaining to education
can be tackled effectively.
Data mining techniques are intensely adopted by EDM for exploring novel knowledge gained from
educational settings (Baker & Yacef, 2009), supporting decision-making in educational institutions
(Toon & Mykola, 2012). Existence of abundant data in the education field has attracted worldwide
researchers in imbibing EDM for determining potential fixes against educational issues, uplifting the
overall standard of online learning (Ducange et al., 2017), prior anticipation of student dropouts (Aulck
et al,. 2016), predicting the academic performance of students’ and also scrutinizing those students
who might be at the verge of academic failure (Hussain et al., 2019). Researchers dealing with EDM
aims towards exploring predictive, learning, behavioral, and visual analytics (Hanan et al., 2019).
According to the systematic review studies there is an exponential surge in the researches carried out
on EDM alongside its diverse implementations in the educational domain (Baker & Inventado, 2014).
Apparently, data mining methodologies employed in various sectors such as (healthcare, corporate,
agriculture…) are equivalent to the methods employed in Educational data mining.
Existing data mining methods adopted falls under two types based on the existing target/
dependent variable in the data: first is the supervised or the predictive methods and second is the
unsupervised or the descriptive methods. For predicting a particular target variable that pre-exists
in the data, supervised data mining methods are utilized. Usually, the education researchers cater to
those research challenges wherein prediction of an outcome or dependent variable is sought from a
set of predictors (Berland et al., 2014). That’s the prime reason that the EDM applications inculcate
supervised data mining methods in contrast to the unsupervised ones. Unlike traditional methods,
prior researches clearly elucidate that high prediction accuracy has been gained by employing the
supervised data mining methods. EDM research usually caters to anticipating students’ performance,
learning skills and academic scores via data gathered from learning activities, interactions with the
LMS and socio-economic state. There is elaboration of the above discussed work by the means
of machine learning techniques for enhancing prediction accuracy. Upgrading the ‘prediction rate
accuracy’ has been much preferred interest of researchers and so the present study too emphasizes
on elevating the prediction accuracy for PISA data which no doubt can be instilled for other type
of data too. In both the efficiency and literature strands, a significant number of papers have been
produced concentrating on the determination of performance gaps between immigrants education
knowledge. However, to date, no international evaluation of educational effectiveness has taken into
consideration the various obstacles posed by various immigration policy regimes.

PROPOSED WORK
The present research begins with analyzing varied educational data mining and machine learning
techniques that helps in assessing the data fetched form PISA. It’s elucidated that XGBoost stands
out to be the ideal most machine learning technique for achieving the desired results. The following
academic databases like Scopus, Web of Science, Springer Link, Science Direct, ACM Digital Library
and IEEE Explorer were considered for study purpose including publications between 2008 and 2020.
The study reviews also included education offices and universities. Following are the stages carried
out in the study: 1. educational data mining and machine learning algorithms are studied, 2. Using
PISA, performance evaluation of machine learning techniques is carried out and thereby, the ideal
most algorithm is determined for research purpose. Lastly, there is fine tuning followed by elaborating
the overall contributions from various authors in machine learning research and techniques implied
in the educational domain for ‘prediction’ along with their outcome. Objective - the aim is to predict
the academic performance of Immigrant students residing in UAE, Australia, Qatar, Canada, and
Spain by the means of XGBoost Regressor algorithm.
PISA Data
The samples have been collected from the 2018 administration of OECD (Organization for Economic
Co-operation and Development) PISA (Programme for International Student Assessment). PISA which
is known as Programme for International Student Assessment is a study programme for evaluating the
skills and knowledge of 15-year-old students. It measures the student’s skills in reading, mathematics
and science (OECD, 2018). The 2018 administration of PISA comprises of tentatively 540,000 students
of age group 15 years from 72 participating countries and economies. The EDM (educational data
mining) technique has been adopted for the PISA 2015 dataset of the shortlisted countries. There is
assessment of UAE (United Arab Emirates), Qatar, Canada, Spain and Australia PISA 2018 dataset
for examining association among the student’s reading, learning, science and mathematical skills. The
formulated dataset is made available publicly on the PISA site. Countries enlisted in PISA with their
schools and students depicts the entire population and traits of their educational pedagogy. Data set
of near about 30,000 students have been scrutinized that spans all the enlisted countries jurisdictions
along with the student information. PISA data employed is categorized under two sections: first
comprises of Science, reading and Mathematics and second comprises of socio-economic background
that impacts student performance. The student’s answers from the questionnaire are encoded using
a set of variables. By rule, the immigrant students must be 15-year-old students and so each student
is allocated specific weight for syncing the sample with the true population. The flow diagram in
Figure 1 presents the workflow of the model.
Performing secondary analysis with PISA data portrays complexity since most of the attributes
describing the students do not resemble the actual or original attributes. Rather they are preprocessed
using various sophisticated techniques and are accessible as derived variables. For instance, there
are no single performance scores for the cognitive test in PISA datasets. This means that for every
student and its assessment domain - reading, math, and science, ‘plausible values’ (PVs) are generated.
There are no direct or single score provided in PISA reports and learning analysis. Alternatively, the
student’s skills and performances are assigned Plausible Values (PVs). The testing time allocated to
each student is fixed and limited. Also, the testing covers up a wide range of knowledge and skills
and hence only certain students respond to each task allocated to them. For comparing the student’s
results in a reliable manner even if they have not answered precisely to the same set of tasks, PISA
makes use of a generalized approach referred to as the Rasch Model (OECD PISA 2009). The PISA
dataset was created to assess immigrant students’ education literacy, as well as their attitudes and
preferences toward their learning experiences. The PISA dataset findings indicate that students’ levels
of learning competency vary widely, and that this variation can be explained by a variety of factors,
including demographic variables, socioeconomic status, students’ participation in education-related

This process is referred to as boosting and aids in minimizing the bias, on the other hand bagging

proves to be highly effective and fruitful. A number of appropriate algorithms has been deployed as

that the XG Boost Regressor algorithm surpasses rest of the algorithms in terms of performance and
other significant metrics. Though the performance of XGBoost regressor can be enhanced further by
utilizing the hyper parameters. Hence the XGBoost regressor algorithm is fine-tuned by the techniques
of hyper parameter tuning which in turn boosts up the performance. Values for the hyper parameter
variables are specified by the user itself while setting up the Machine Learning model and are stated
prior to the parameter specification. Also, they aid in evaluating the optimal parameters of the model.
Like for instance, max_depth is a hyper parameter in Random Forest Algorithms. It can be deduced
that identifying the optimal hyper parameters aids towards building the best-performing model. The
present work has adopted GridSearchCV that employs the Grid Search technique to determine the
optimal hyper parameters for elevating the performance of the model. This technique integrates
multiple hyperparameters and their values to compute the performance for each combination and
thereby shortlists the ideal most value for the hyperparameters. Resultant, there is high processing
time as per the number of hyperparameters present. GridSearchCV employs cross validation during
model training along with Grid Search. Usually, prior to training the model, the data is split into two
parts namely training data and test data. In cross-validation, the data is split into the training data and
the validation data. Algorithm’s performance boosts up after passing the specified hyper parameters.
