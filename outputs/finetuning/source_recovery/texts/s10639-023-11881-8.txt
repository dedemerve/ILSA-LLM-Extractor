Identifying the contextual factors related to the reading
performance of Moroccan fourth-grade students from a
Machine Learning-based Approach.

Abstract
Based on the 2016 Progress in International Reading Literacy Research (PIRLS)
data, this study determined the contextual characteristics that distinguished Moroccan fourth-grade kids with high- and low-achieving reading abilities. The PIRLS 2016
public dataset included 5446 Moroccan pupils, with 1545 scoring well and 3901 doing
poorly on the reading portion of the exam. The objective of the present research was to
determine the ideal combination of significant contextual elements that jointly affect
fourth-grade children’s reading ability. Machine learning technique such as gradient
boosting was used to find the most critical contextual variables that might distinguish
between the two groups of students. The findings demonstrate that 12 significant contextual variables from GradientBoosting, selected from 486 contextual variables at
the levels of the student, teacher, and school, Identifying high- and low-performing
students collectively have consequences for teaching and learning in the future about
the reading ability of primary school students.
Keyword Contextual factors, Machine learning, Moroccan students, PIRLS 2016,
Reading performance

1 Introduction
Reading ability is a crucial skill that is essential for both people and society Mullis et al.
(2004) is part of the Progress in International Reading Literacy Study (PIRLS), a global
macro study. The PIRLS gathers data on the reading processes that fourth-graders have
gone through. Every five years, it has evaluated their reading abilities since 2001.
Because it is believed that the learning environment has a substantial impact on how
well pupils read Hu (2014), the PIRLS framework Mullis et al. (2017) incorporates
a considerable number of contextual variables in a range of situations relevant to
primary students’ reading at the student, teacher, and school levels. According to the
PIRLS, some research focuses on numerous contextual variables that have an impact
on fourth-graders reading proficiency Law (2009), Mullis et al. (2020), Park (2011),
Cordero et al. (2017), such as the impact of students’ attitudes toward reading on
their reading performance at the student level Gnaldi et al. (2005), and the effect of
a student’s socioeconomic background on their family’s reading performance Mullis
et al. (2004); Regarding the impact of educational policies, school resources, and
student composition on students’ achievements Tramonte and Willms (2010), Machin
et al. (2013), Jing et al. (2015) at the school level.
The value of reading for each person makes it imperative to investigate the variables
contributing to pupils’ outstanding reading abilities. Numerous studies have been conducted on the effects of various variables on pupils’ reading proficiency Carlisle et al.
(2009). PIRLS, one of the most significant global macro studies, evaluates the reading
literacy of 9-year-old pupils all around the globe every five years. To investigate the
potential variables influencing kids’ academic achievement, PIRLS provides essential
data, including information on the students themselves, teachers, and schools Mullis
et al. (2012).
Although earlier studies have looked at the significance of contextual factors from
a specific angle, there has been a little study that examines influential variables from
the standpoint of the joint influence of contextual aspects Tse and Xiao (2014). This
study investigates the collective impact of important contextual factors on fourth-grade
students’ reading performance by looking at a wide range of contextual variables,
mainly through the benchmark of an international survey PIRLS. The theoretical
justification and prior research do this.
Moroccan pupils do poorly on the PIRLS 2016 reading literacy exam compared to
other students. However, few studies examine how context affects pupils’ reading in
Morocco. Therefore, it is crucial to investigate the contextual elements that genuinely
contribute to the exceptional reading performance of Moroccan pupils in PIRLS 2016.
This research tries to pinpoint these elements and explore how they affect students’
reading.
These are the order of the remaining sections: Section 2 presents related works
to this study, Section 3 summarizes the data and explains machine learning (ML)
methodologies, Section 4 presents and evaluates estimation results, and Section 5
concludes the paper.

2 Related Works
Numerous studies have shown that context has a significant impact on student’s academic achievement Creemers and Kyriakides (2010), Chen et al. (2021). Gender has
been revealed to be a considerable influence. In terms of linguistic skills, women often
do better than men Burman et al. (2008), Berninger et al. (2008). Student aptitude,
defined as their capacity to achieve the subsequent learning objective Gustafsson and
Balke (1993), is a significant determinant of their academic achievement. Pupils’ aptitude also includes motivation, which is significantly influenced by attribution belief
Law (2009). Positive student attitudes about their reading skills may inspire students to
participate in learning-related activities and boost their academic success Gnaldi et al.
(2005). Another critical component is attitude. According to research concentrating on
the PIRLS 2016, students with unfavorable attitudes about reading are less able to read
Mullis et al. (2017). Additionally, highly engaged students often do better on reading
tasks than the PIRLS’s worldwide mean score Wei and Hu (2020), Areepattamannil
et al. (2011).
The home environment has a significant role in reading literacy acquisition, and
this beneficial relationship may aid in the growth of reading literacy Epstein (2018).
The influence of the family is thought to have a significant effect on pupils’ academic
success Farver et al. (2006). To develop reading skills, the importance of families has
long been highlighted Ngorosho (2011). Furthermore, studies that examine the impact
of the home environment on kids’ reading literacy in underdeveloped nations come to
similar encouraging conclusions Aram and Levin (2002), Ngorosho (2011).
Numerous studies have shown that the context at the school level impacts how well
pupils read Farver et al. (2006). The following variables may be used to examine the
school setting that affects students’ reading literacy: school type, school size, school
economic status, principal leadership, teacher engagement, teacher qualification, and
ICT resources at school Rutter and Maughan (2002).
The importance of school type in the context of a school has recently been explored
in the educational community. According to specific research, public schools are inputfocused institutions that need more innovation and are resistant to student and teacher
demands Jehangir et al. (2015). Private schools, in contrast, have greater flexibility
and room for creativity since they can do without the red tape and strict rules Lee
and Burkam (2003). Numerous studies have demonstrated the impact of school size
on student accomplishment, and they indicate that, in primary schools, small schools
have a more significant effect on improving students’ academic performance than big
schools Giambona and Porcu (2018). The socioeconomic condition of the school has
a substantial impact on how well pupils do. Students attending schools in more robust
financial standing may do better academically. Additionally, specific instructional
strategies may help to close the achievement gap amongst pupils Rutter and Maughan
(2002).
This study closes the knowledge gap by examining the effect of student, teacher,
and school variables on high and low-performing children using machine learning
techniques since few studies in Morocco concentrate on contextual factors affecting
kids’ reading proficiency. In this investigation, two research issues are addressed:

SVM: is a technique used to build classifiers Hearst et al. (1998), Lyaqini et al.

on the outcome are not secret John and Langley (1995). One of the best inductive

K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN): is a sample-based learning approach for classification that utilizes all training data Jiang et al. (2012). It locates the variables’ nearest

well this method performs Li et al. (2003).
Logistic Regression: The associations between dependent and independent variables may be researched using LR, much like conventional regression models. The

group of internal nodes, and a group of leaves make up a decision tree. Each internal
node of the tree represents a property, while the leaves and arcs connecting the nodes
stand in for the assignment classes and tests conducted on the nodes, respectively. A
decision tree’s internal nodes have one parent node, two or more daughter nodes, and
internal nodes.
GradientBoosting: Gradient boosting is a technique for creating classification and
regression models, which are often referred to as decision or regression trees and are

on the decision nodes, it has nodes and leaves that provide predictions. Although
regression trees are poor models on their own, they significantly increased in accuracy
as an ensemble. As a result, the ensembles are constructed incrementally and progressively such that each ensemble fixes the flaw in the preceding ensemble Aler et al.
(2017).

teachers and schools are now more than ever expected to be responsible for the academic performance of their students, education researchers must provide them with
research-based support. That was the main objective of our empirical investigation.

5 Conclusion
This research sheds light on the crucial factors that influence reading achievement in
Moroccan pupils. The study analyzed a wide range of student, teacher, and school
characteristics, and identified twelve significant predictor variables, including early
literacy tasks, student confidence in reading, parental commitment, school emphasis
on academic success, and safe and orderly school environment. The findings from Gradient Boosting strongly suggest that these variables are key determinants of reading
success in Moroccan pupils. Policymakers, educators, and other stakeholders interested in improving reading outcomes for Moroccan students can benefit from this
