Abstract
Non-cognitive skills are crucial for both educational success and personal fulfilment. Collectively, they constitute an essential skill set that enables students to
excel academically and engage as active citizens. Although studies have explored
the development of these critical skills, few have harnessed the potential of machine
learning techniques. Accordingly, the current study aims to identify the most influential factors that affect non-cognitive skills using a broad range of determinants.
Conventional Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) and supervised machine learning algorithms, including Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO),
Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM), and Random Forest (RF), were employed to
evaluate the predictive performance using data drawn from the Programme for International Student Assessment 2022 (N=75,217). Findings indicate that variables
across different groups contributed significantly to students’ non-cognitive skills,
with cognitive-motivational traits emerging as the most influential predictor group.
In terms of model performance, results demonstrate that advanced machine learning
techniques outperformed conventional statistical analysis in identifying and ranking key predictors. Overall, this study highlights the complex, multifaceted nature
of non-cognitive skills by identifying several variable-level predictors. Moreover,
application of machine learning algorithms in this context represents an effective,
novel analytical approach that can serve as foundation for future studies.

squares · Lasso · Gradient boosting machine · Random forest

1 Introduction
Non-cognitive skills are broadly defined as attributes, traits, and characteristics that
encompass behavioral patterns, mental states, and emotional dispositions (Lee and
Stankov 2018; Liu et al. 2024a). These skills have long been acknowledged as critical
components of students’ holistic development and life outcomes, including academic
achievement, personal growth, physical and mental well-being, and future career performance (Kankaraš and Suárez-Álvarez 2019; Soto et al. 2024). While a plethora
of studies advocate for the indispensable role of non-cognitive skills in determining
learning trajectories, a concurrent body of research highlights the critical need to
investigate the antecedents and mechanisms underlying their development from an
early age (Abrahams et al. 2019). Prior empirical evidence reveals that non-cognitive
skills can be responsive to life experiences and educational interventions, underscoring their malleability across different stages of development and learning contexts
(Liu et al. 2025a; Kautz et al. 2014). Moreover, an increasing corpus of research
demonstrates that non-cognitive attributes interact dynamically with cognitive and
contextual factors, producing cumulative effects that support students’ academic success both in the short and long term (Gong et al. 2021; Primi et al. 2021). Despite
growing interest from researchers, policymakers, and educational practitioners, the
prediction and assessment of non-cognitive skills continue to lag behind that of cognitive competencies. To this end, the identification of determinants influencing students’ non-cognitive skills is vital for advancing these abilities and designing targeted
interventions guided by evidence-based diagnostic insights.
It is noteworthy that the non-cognitive domain remains a highly complex field, as
the evolving and context-dependent nature of these characteristics poses substantial
challenges for their conceptualization, measurement, and prediction (Boon-Falleur
et al. 2020; García 2016). Over the past few years, efforts have been progressively
devoted to identifying factors shaping individuals’ non-cognitive outcomes. Nevertheless, this process has repeatedly encountered conceptual and methodological
constraints when elucidating the complex interactions between non-cognitive skills
and different predictors. According to the OECD’s social and emotional framework,
the structural complexity of non-cognitive abilities entails considerable challenges
for educational systems, policymakers, and researchers, as the variability of these
traits among individuals and contexts complicates their prediction, evaluation, and
development (De Fruyt and Scheirlinckx 2025). In addition to these conceptual drawbacks, methodological challenges may also arise from the over-reliance on traditional
statistical methods (Bennett et al. 2022; Meng et al. 2024; Steyerberg and Harrell
2016). Importantly, the contemporary educational landscape is increasingly defined
by the requirement to deal with large-scale datasets, leading traditional methods to
inadequately address the high-dimensionality of current educational data. Consequently, research is shifting toward more adaptable and robust analytical tools, such
as machine learning, which are better equipped to manage the complexity and scope
of contemporary educational data (Ayanwale et al. 2024; Bzdok et al. 2018).

1.1 Non-Cognitive Skills Framework
Conceptually, non-cognitive skills are delineated as “individual capacities that are
manifested in consistent patterns of thoughts, feelings, and behaviours” (Kuo et al.
2020). Given their relevance across life domains, it is necessary to develop a comprehensive understanding of these competencies and the factors that contribute to their
development (King et al. 2024). To this end, several frameworks have been proposed
to provide an organizational and a theoretical understanding of non-cognitive skills
development over time, as they include a broad range of human feelings, thoughts,
and behaviors (Guy et al. 2011). In this vein, the OECD model has emerged as a
prominent theoretical model, widely lauded by theorists for the systematic classification of non-cognitive skills using a facet-level perspective, providing deeper insights
into the intricate dimensions of these skills (Liu et al. 2025b; Danner et al. 2021).
The framework adopted in this study maps the key domains and their associated
facets, as shown in Table 1. Specifically, the OECD's conceptual model categorizes
non-cognitive skills in five main domains, reflecting one of the most well-established
frameworks in the field of educational psychology: Task performance, Emotional
regulation, Collaboration, Open-mindedness, and Engaging with others. Each of
these five domains is further divided into narrower subdomains that capture more
specific non-cognitive skills (OECD 2021b).

insights into how educational policies, school systems, and learning environments
affect academic and non-academic outcomes across contexts. Although prior research

2 Literature Review
2.1 Non-Cognitive Skills Predictors
Integrating the development of non-cognitive skills into educational practice is critical for promoting quality education (Shuey and Kankaraš 2018). Previous empirical
findings reveal that individuals' non-cognitive skills can be promoted through various
interactions with family members, peers, and instructors, both at home and in the
classroom settings (Chernyshenko et al. 2018; Duckworth and Yeager 2015; Lee and
Shute 2010). In this regard, a broad constellation of personal and contextual variables
have been thoroughly elucidated, including socio-demographic characteristics, environmental influences, and psychological factors. More precisely, individual factors
such as gender, socio-economic status, parental education levels, students’ sense of
belonging, and immigration background have been associated with students’ socioemotional experiences and personality traits (Alink et al. 2023; Gamazo et al. 2017;
Orr 2023; Liu et al. 2025c; Pholphirul 2017). For example, Chen et al. (2024) investigated the role played by gender differences in influencing students’ non-cognitive
skills. Their findings concluded that compared to males, female counterparts experienced statistically significant declines in non-cognitive abilities. In a similar manner,
previous research examining the role of gender peer effects on students’ emotional
and behavioral outcomes suggest that boys exhibited higher enthusiasm and selfconfidence when they are surrounded by female classmates (Gong et al. 2021).
Regarding socio-economic and contextual influences, research has established
consistent positive associations between family socio-economic status and the level

of non-cognitive abilities. Pai et al. (2023) argue that students from wealthier families
exhibit higher levels of non-cognitive outcomes, possibly due to expanded access to
enriching resources, supportive home environments, and opportunities for engaging
in different activities. Extending this perspective, parental education, as an integral
component of socio-economic status, contributes substantially to fostering the development of learners' personality traits and non-cognitive attributes. Recent studies
posit that students whose parents have attained higher levels of formal education
are more likely to develop greater social and emotional capabilities (Hvidtfeldt and
Schultz-Nielsen 2025; Waters et al. 2021). This is probable because such parents
often set higher goals, educational aspirations, and emphasize the cultivation of
habits that promote perseverance, self-regulation, and social competencies. Furthermore, extensive investigations have also highlighted the impact of family immigration background on the development of students’ academic outcomes, particularly
their non-cognitive skills. Existing studies on immigration and biculturalism have
established both positive and negative associations between immigration status and
non-cognitive skills (Alivernini et al. 2020; Motti-Stefanidi and Masten 2020).
In a related context, there has been a growing interest in the extent to which noncognitive skills are influenced by different cognitive-motivational traits, suggesting
that non-cognitive skills cannot be treated in isolation, as they are impacted by both
facilitating and inhibiting cognitive aspects. In this context, an emerging body of
literature indicates that the associations between non-cognitive skills and academicmotivational aspects may differ from one context to another (Ackerman and Heggestad 1997; Apostolov and Geldenhuys 2022; Graham and Lachman 2014). On the
one hand, improvement in cognitive-motivational traits may lead to reinforced noncognitive skills and personality characteristics. Several studies have highlighted the
significant role of academic self-concepts including academic self-efficacy, motivation, and subject preference in shaping learning outcomes that are closely related to
students’ non-cognitive skills such as emotional regulation (Frawley et al. 2014),
interest (Marsh et al. 2005), persistence (Yang et al. 2023), and well-being (Chen
and Gong 2021). On the other hand, students’ social and emotional experiences are
likely to be influenced by low levels of cognitive-motivational abilities or negative
academic experiences in the classroom and school environment, such as academic
anxiety, which may negatively affect individuals’ non-cognitive competencies and
decrease their level of motivation (Z. Wang et al. 2020a, b).
Extant research has concentrated on analyzing the associations between noncognitive skills and students’ academic performance (Cunha et al. 2010; Duckworth
and Yeager 2015; Richardson et al. 2012). Students’ cognitive performance and test
scores are key influences in determining several outcomes, including the level of education achieved, mental health, and physical well-being (Jin 2023). Similarly, studies suggest that the associations between cognitive performance and non-cognitive
skills might vary greatly according to the researched field and the form of assessment
utilized (Brandt et al. 2020; Hübner et al. 2022). For instance, existing evidence
revealed significant associations between open-mindedness domain of non-cognitive
skills and general cognitive performance and standardized test scores, whereas only
certain facets within task performance domain showed a positive relationship with
academic grades as compared to other non-cognitive attributes (Meyer et al. 2019).

higher academic achievement do not necessarily report greater non-cognitive skills
or psychological well-being (Bücker et al. 2018; Steinmayr et al. 2016).
2.2 Machine Learning Techniques
Machine learning encompasses a set of computational techniques that allow computers to learn from data and improve their analytical performance autonomously (Feng
et al. 2022; Kersting 2018; Simmering et al. 2019). With its robust predictive capability, machine learning models leverage algorithms to efficiently analyze complex data
and uncover patterns that may be difficult to detect using traditional methods. Hong et
al. (2020) posit that machine learning is more adept in analyzing the effects of several
predictors simultaneously. Recently, machine learning approaches have gained popularity in educational research by offering innovative methods for exploring students’
performance, learning behaviors, and educational outcomes (Masci et al. 2018; Rebai
et al. 2020). Several machine learning techniques, such as Support Vector Machine
(SVM), Random Forest (RF), K-Nearest Neighbours (KNN), Decision Trees (DT),
and Lasso regression have been employed to predict students’ performance and identify the key factors influencing success or failure. For instance, Pan and Cutumisu
(2024) have used supervised machine learning approaches, namely Random Forest
and K-Nearest Neighbours to identify the predictors of life satisfaction among school
students from Japan and UK. Although studies indicate that the accuracy of machine
learning methods in predicting students’ success may differ, both complex tree-based
and kernel-based machine learning methods such as Random Forest (RF) and Support Vector Machine (SVM) often exhibit higher efficacy in educational applications
(Aksu and Doğan 2018; Pejic et al. 2021).
Despite the extensive potential of machine learning methods in predicting different outcomes, most existing studies have primarily focused on academic achievement and cognitive skills, with scarce attention given to non-cognitive domains
(Alnasyan et al. 2024; Kordbagheri et al. 2025; Parkavi et al. 2024). Concurrently,
extant research on non-cognitive skills development has often overlooked the influence of background characteristics, motivational predictors, and cognitive abilities
on non-cognitive skills (Hegde et al. 2025; Sayed et al. 2025; Ye and Yuan 2022).
To address these gaps, the current study leverages machine learning techniques to
examine the predictive influence of factors across background characteristics, cognitive-motivational traits, and cognitive performance on students’ non-cognitive skills
development using the PISA 2022 dataset. Specifically, ordinary least squares (OLS)
regression was first employed to explore the relationship between a wide range of
factors and students’ non-cognitive skills. Following that, Least Absolute Shrinkage
and Selection Operator (LASSO), Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM), and Random
Forest (RF) were developed to predict students’ non-cognitive outcomes. In light of
these objectives, the current research attempts to answer the following questions: (1)
To what extent do students’ background characteristics, cognitive-motivational trait,
and cognitive performance variables independently predict the levels of non-cognitive skills? (2) What is the relative collective significance of each group of variables

3 Methods
3.1 Participants
The current study utilizes the publicly available PISA (Programme for International
Student Assessment) 2022 student dataset, comprising responses from N = 75,217,
15-year-old students across ten OECD countries and partner economies: Australia,
Spain, Estonia, Hong Kong (China), Ireland, Korea, Macao (China), Singapore, Chinese Taipei, and Turkey. PISA is a large-scale international assessment, conducted
globally and designed to evaluate the knowledge and skills of 15-year-old students
across different countries (OECD 2024a; Liu et al. 2025b). The survey focuses predominantly on the key subjects of reading, mathematics, and science literacy. To
provide valuable insight into students' capabilities beyond the traditional cognitive
assessment, PISA 2022 extended data collection to cover other domains of financial
literacy, health and well-being, and non-cognitive skills. In addition, PISA 2022 collects data from teachers and schools through complementary questionnaires, capturing data on instructional practices, school climate, and institutional resources,
providing a comprehensive perspective on student development beyond traditional
assessments (Liu et al. 2025d).
3.2 Analysis and Research Procedure
Analyzing the contributions of predictors individually raises empirical challenges, as
traditional approaches, such as Ordinary Least Squares (OLS), may introduce bias
due to their rigid assumptions. Furthermore, collinearity across predictors could minimize their distinct effects. Adopting machine learning algorithms, therefore, offers
several benefits over traditional statistical tools. In the context of this study, statistical
analyses were performed using STATA version 18 + STATA version 19 (Stata, Stata
Corp LLC, College Station, TX, USA) software. Specifically, data extraction, processing, and basic regression analyses were performed using Stata packages, while
Python 3.12 was introduced to accomplish data pre-processing and model evaluation. For visualizations, Stata software and H2O machine learning platform in Stata
were used to generate plots and illustrate feature performance figures, while Python
3.12 has been utilized to produce SHAP plots and figures displaying the performance
metrics.

3.3.1 Non-Cognitive Skills
An equally weighted composite index was developed to represent students’ overall non-cognitive skills, encompassing the five key domains grounded in the OECD
framework (OECD 2021a). Although the current study treated non-cognitive skills
as multidimensional concepts, the composite score was utilized primarily to capture
their combined contribution at the predictive level. Suryaprasad et al. (2020) argued
that a summated scale, generated by combining multiple dimensions into a single
composite score, is an effective approach for reducing the measurement error and
producing a more stable and interpretable index. In this vein, studies have proposed
aggregated scales combining several non-cognitive variables into a single composite
measure. For example, Liu et al. (2024) have used the China Family Panel Studies
(CFPS) to generate a composite score of non-cognitive skills by averaging the values
of the five personality dimensions in order to examine the impact on educational
mismatch. In the context of the current study, the equal weighting method was chosen
for its effectiveness in constructing composite indicators in large-scale assessments
(OECD 2008). Additionally, its theoretical simplicity and methodological consistency allow to understand the composite index of non-cognitive skills (Greco et al.
2018). Particularly, the index encompasses several variables that indicate different
actions, behaviors, and traits. The measure for each construct has ten items, each
assessed on a five-point Likert scale ranging from “strongly disagree” to “strongly
agree”. To ensure the reliability of the constructed outcome, Composite Reliability
(CR) was computed to confirm the robustness of internal consistency assessment,
resulting in a value of 0.74, which also falls within the generally accepted range for
reliable measurement (Bacon et al. 1995).
3.3.2 Background Characteristics
A broad collection of environmental and socio-demographic variables was included
in the analysis. Principally, a total of ten variables are applied to reflect the multidimensional influence of background characteristics on students’ non-cognitive skills,
including student age (AGE), gender (GENDER), socio-economic status (ESCS), ICT
resources at home (ICTHOME), Early childhood education (DURECEC), expected
educational level (EXPECEDU), students’ sense of belonging (BELONG)…etc.
3.3.3 Cognitive-Motivational Traits
Cognitive-motivational traits were derived from students' subject-specific beliefs,
attitudes, feelings and behaviors, in addition to exposure to mathematics content
and perceptions of mathematics support (OECD 2024a). The analysis examines the
contributions of cognitive-motivational characteristics utilizing ten factors, includ-

4 Data Preparation
4.1 Analytical Procedures
Prior to embarking on the analysis and model evaluation, the dataset underwent
meticulous data cleaning and preparation to ensure the analytical rigor. Missing
data were handled in accordance with OECD standards, with invalid, skipped, and
not applicable responses recoded as system missing. All observations with missing
data were then dropped. Following data pre-processing, a nested cross-validation
procedure was implemented. As presented in Fig. 1, this process adopts a five-fold
outer loop to obtain performance estimates, while each outer training set (four folds)

13

D. Tahri et al.

Fig. 1 Data analysis procedure of models and performance metrics

underwent hyperparameter tuning through an inner five-fold cross-validation loop.
The remaining fold in each cycle functions as a test set for performance assessment
(Pan and Cutumisu 2024). After identifying the optimal hyperparameters through
the inner validation loop, all models were retrained and evaluated on corresponding
outer test folds. Predictive performance of the models was then assessed using the
aforementioned metrics: RMSE, MSE, MAE, and R², with values averaged across
all outer folds to provide generalizable performance estimates. Finally, to enhance
the model interpretability and provide insights into predictive mechanisms, a final
SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations) analysis for the best performing model was
conducted to provide detailed examination of individual feature contributions (Meng
et al. 2024).
4.2 Inclusion Criteria
By design, large-scale assessments such as PISA often contain missing records due to
the structure of the tests and questionnaires . To ensure the accuracy and reliability of
the analysis and to reduce the potential sources of bias, missing values across all variables were addressed by eliminating the database records that contain missing values.
More specifically, the inclusion criteria for the study subject selection are as follows:
(1) The total number of participants who took part in Module 8 of the PISA 2022; (2)
Students included with less than 10% of data missing; (3) Students excluded for missing values in the dependent variable (non-cognitive skills); (4) Students excluded for
missing values in each factor of the independent groups (background characteristics,

5.2 Machine Learning Feature Importance
The analysis employs complex machine learning techniques to accurately capture
complex interactions and identify the most influential predictors of students’ noncognitive skills. The models applied in this study are particularly effective for analyzing multiple predictors simultaneously and identifying influences that conventional
approaches may overlook. In this regard, the Lasso technique was used to determine
the top five predictors in each predictor group separately as illustrated in Fig. 4. The
model demonstrates similar results, with students’ sense of belonging emerging as
the most important predictor in the background characteristics group, followed by
gender, family support, age, and highest expected level of education. Within the cognitive-motivational traits group, mathematics proactive behavior rates as the most

6 Discussion
The accurate identification of key predictors of educational outcomes is essential
at all levels to inform evidence-based interventions. Accordingly, this study applies
both conventional and advanced machine learning techniques to identify the most
important predictors of students' non-cognitive skills across diverse variable groups
using data from the Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA). This
approach reflects the growing international consensus on the fundamental role of
these skills in developing students’ personal, psychological, and academic outcomes.
Leveraging machine learning analysis on a large-scale dataset allows for better
management of data complexity and prioritization of important variables, ensuring
more accurate, practical, and meaningful insights. Notably, the findings showed that
the employed models produced varying results in terms of feature ranking and predictive accuracy, with Gradient Boosting Machine consistently outperforming all
other methods, as evidenced by the evaluation metrics. Furthermore, Gradient Boosting Machine exhibited more reliable and stable predictions, reflecting the model’s
ability to balance predictive accuracy with stability. A cumulative body of empirical
evidence indicated that Gradient Boosting Machine models provide several advantages for diverse learning tasks, including flexibility in optimizing differentiable loss
functions, managing missing values, and high computational efficiency, which make
them particularly effective across applications (Adler and Painsky 2022). Consistent with the findings of this study, Gradient Boosting Machine models have often
shown superiority over other methods, particularly in predictive accuracy in edu-

13

Machine-Learning Applications in Predicting Students’ Non-Cognitive…

cational data analyses. For example, Huang et al. (2025) used data from six East
Asian education systems in the PISA 2022 to identify and rank factors influencing
middle-school students’ academic outcomes. Their analysis revealed that Gradient
Boosting approaches exhibited a higher prediction accuracy compared to Multiple
Linear Regression, Support Vector Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest.
The primary objective of the present study is to highlight the significant role
of predictors in determining the level of students’ non-cognitive skills. Notably,
the research employs SHAP analysis to interpret the model outputs by measuring
the contribution of each factor. The findings indicate that “cognitive-motivational
traits” were identified as the most influential group, followed by “background

13

D. Tahri et al.

characteristics” and “cognitive performance indicators” predictors. This finding
is in line with previous findings in the field. Particularly, Xiao and Sun (2021)
found that cognitive-motivational factors, including mathematics self-efficacy,
motivation, anxiety, and proactive behaviors, are strongly correlated with both
emotional and behavioral aspects as well as the level of academic achievement.
This perspective is grounded in the expectancy-value theory of achievement
motivation (EVM), which explains the mechanisms through which learners’
beliefs about their abilities in a specific domain influence persistence, motivation,
and task engagement (Batchelor et al. 2019; Xiao and Sun 2021). Conversely, the
findings demonstrated that mathematics anxiety, mathematics preference, and the
perception of mathematics as easier exhibited a negative influence. Mathematics
anxiety, as a critical domain-specific emotional factor, involves unpleasant feelings of stress and under-performance, which may adversely influence students’
non-cognitive skills (Pollack et al. 2021; Yang and Zheng 2024). Furthermore,
the findings also align with previous investigations on the impact of academic
perceptions on non-cognitive outcomes. Prior evidence highlighted the negative
relationship between academic and career preference and extraversion, agreeableness, and neuroticism (Coenen et al. 2021). Consequently, the substantial
influence of cognitive-motivational traits underscores the need for ensuring
positive student attitudes, goal-setting behaviors, and self-regulation strategies
in educational interventions (Zakariya et al. 2022). Meanwhile, the variations
in SHAP feature importance within the group imply that educational interventions that target non-cognitive skills development should adopt a differentiated
approach, prioritizing the most important aspects of cognitive-motivational traits
based on students’ needs (Coenen et al. 2021).
Regarding the contribution of background characteristics, students’ sense of
belonging was identified as a key positive predictor of non-cognitive outcomes.
This finding aligns with prior studies, which posit that when individuals “feel
personally accepted, respected, included, and supported by others” they are more
likely to show favourable social-emotional, behavioral, and motivational outcomes (Goodenow and Grady 1993). The concept of social belongingness, which
is often regarded as subjective experiences of social attachment to home or school
environment, enables individuals to develop their personal growth and emotional
well-being. Recent scholarly investigations argued that individuals’ sense of
belonging is critical for building social capital, developing social and emotional
functioning, and reducing the risks of illness and behavioral problems (Beatson
et al. 2024; Van Herpen et al. 2020; Wang et al. 2023). Additionally, the analysis
reveals that students’ highest expected level of education is a significant predictor of non-cognitive outcomes. Setting positive expectations throughout students’
academic progression is closely related to their ability to engage in collaborative
practices and social interactions with peers and teachers. Earlier studies found
that students with higher educational aspirations tend to exhibit elevated levels of
optimism and trust (Hoy et al. 2006; Lopez-Agudo et al. 2021; Portes et al. 2010).
Moreover, family support has also been found to play a critical role in explaining students’ non-cognitive levels (Rey et al. 2019; Tian et al. 2022; Wilke et al.
2018). In line with prior findings, parental supportive practices, including par-

13

ent-child relationship and parental academic involvement, are widely recognized
as crucial elements of an environment that improves children’s psychological,
emotional, and academic well-being (Dabney et al. 2013; Rey et al. 2019). Additional scholarly efforts across various disciplines have highlighted the influence
of parental supportive environment, particularly through the economic, labour,
and social capital lenses. Grounded in the Family Stress Model, students from a
higher socio-economic status often experience positive learning environment and
greater access to learning resources, which may strengthen their cognitive and
non-cognitive development (Alivernini et al. 2020; Zhang et al. 2018).
The influence of cognitive performance and intelligence on different social
and emotional outcomes has been consistently examined in educational and
developmental research. For instance, previous studies on the interplay between
cognitive and non-cognitive skills found that students’ cognitive achievement is
associated with their personality change in adolescence (Israel et al. 2019). However, the predictive capacity of cognitive achievement alone remained relatively
small, explaining only a limited proportion of the variance in non-cognitive and
socio-emotional attributes (Miyamoto et al. 2024). Particularly, the findings
align with previous evidence, underscoring the role of academic achievement in
enhancing broader skills beyond cognitive domains. Despite the modest association between cognitive domains and non-cognitive skills, the findings from feature importance and SHAP indicate that encouraging excellence in key cognitive
subjects can still contribute positively to the development of personal attributes,
such as socio-emotional development and self-regulation skills (Slot et al. 2020;
Liu et al. 2024c). Therefore, educational interventions focused on non-cognitive
skills should simultaneously emphasize the improvement of cognitive skills,
which can lead to more holistic improvements in both academic achievement and
personal development.
6.1 Contributions and Implications
This study provided important theoretical, methodological, and practical implications. Theoretically, the current study offered a thorough understanding of the
key factors influencing students’ non-cognitive skills by comparing a variety of
determinants across different groups and identified the most influential predictors.
Methodologically, using a combination of traditional and machine learning statistical approaches to investigate students’ non-cognitive skills allowed for more
significant advancements in educational research. To the best of our knowledge,
no studies to date have employed machine learning models to identify the most
influential predictors influencing non-cognitive skills. The study showcased the
utility of using Lasso, Gradient Boosting Machine, and Random Forest regression to allow comparing the predictive accuracy of these models. Practically,
this study provides clear implications for the design of educational interventions.
The identified factors offer actionable insights for educational practitioners in the
field of non-cognitive skills development. Building on these perspectives, educators should emphasize strategies that improve students’ academic self-beliefs,
promote academic motivation, and enhance interest in cognitive domains. Inter-

13

D. Tahri et al.

ventions may include goal-setting tasks, self-regulation training, peer learning
and collaboration, and personalized support for students facing academic or
emotional challenges. Aligning instructional practices with students’ psychological and motivational needs may allow teachers to be more capable of nurturing
both cognitive and non-cognitive abilities, thereby supporting students’ holistic
growth.
6.2 Limitations
Although the findings of this study offer valuable insights, several limitations
warrant consideration. A central constraint lies in the study’s design, which prioriti zes predictive accuracy over theoretical interpretation. Although the analysis
identified the most influential variables across groups, the study does not provide
detailed explanations of the mechanisms that might account for these patterns.
Future research should extend beyond predictive modelling by exploring these
processes using analytical designs that enable stronger interpretation. Another
key limitation is the use of cross-sectional data, which restricts the ability to
draw causal inferences between predictors and the outcome variable. Future
studies should employ longitudinal or experimental designs to better examine
causal mechanisms. Moreover, while this study draws on a large-scale dataset
from multiple OECD countries, analyzing all countries as a single sample may
overlook important cross-national differences. Socio-economic conditions, cultural contexts, and educational systems can significantly shape how these factors
influence students’ non-cognitive skills. Future research should conduct crosscountry comparative analyses to investigate the effects of the cultural and contextual heterogeneity and to assess the relative significance and contribution of
these features across diverse educational and cultural settings. Furthermore, the
present study relies on an equal-weighted composite measure for non-cognitive
skills. Although this method is commonly used in large-scale education research,
it has been often criticized as conceptually and analytically weak, as it treats all
dimensions as equally important regardless of their contributions. Future studies should examine alternative weighting approaches, such as hierarchical CFA,
to derive a more precise representation of the structure of non-cognitive skills.
Finally, while this study uses advanced machine learning analysis that reduces
the risk of analytical bias, the extent to which the variability captured across
contexts is restricted may introduce potential overfitting. Future investigations
should consider accounting for and addressing contextual variability.

7 Conclusions
Evaluating students’ non-cognitive skills through large-scale assessment surveys has become a trend in educational and psychological research, underscoring
their importance in shaping different academic, personal, and professional outcomes. This study implemented conventional and supervised machine learning
approaches to investigate the effect of factors from different groups in predicting

Machine-Learning Applications in Predicting Students’ Non-Cognitive…

students’ non-cognitive attributes across ten countries. The findings showed that
Gradient Boosting Machine outperformed Random Forest and Lasso in terms
of predictive accuracy. Notably, the Gradient Boosting Machine model identified several variables from each group as significant predictors of students’ noncognitive skills with cognitive-motivational traits variables identified as the most
influential group. By using machine learning regression techniques, this study
provides valuable insights for understanding and developing students’ non-cognitive skills.
