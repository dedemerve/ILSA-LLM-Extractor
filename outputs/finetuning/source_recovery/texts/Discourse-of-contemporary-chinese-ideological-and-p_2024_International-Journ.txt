A B S T R A C T  
The aim of this research is to evaluate educational approaches and teaching methods in ideo­
logical and political education in China by assessing students’ perceptions of ideological and 
political concepts and their participation in socio-political life. The study is based on the model of 
the International Civic and Citizenship Education Study (ICCS) and the longitudinal approach to 
civic education research. The results are moderate and demonstrate that the teaching methods do 
not promote youth participation in socio-political activities. Among the types of socio-political 
activities, students are most active in those encouraged by the university (student elections, 
volunteering), with the main motivation for political activity being additional points and rec­
ommendations for job searches. The theoretical contribution lies in the development of theo­
retical approaches to the thematic analysis of ideological and political education. The practical 
contribution is a simple and replicable approach for evaluating the methods and outcomes of 
ideological and political education. The empirical results enable the formation of more successful 
practices and the implementation of effective modern teaching methods in ideological and po­
litical education.   
1. Introduction 
Education, as a means of knowledge transmission, plays a significant role in both individual and social development. It is an 
effective way to promote societal prosperity, a priority of various global programs (Guo et al., 2019). The aim of education is to 
facilitate learning that leads to the acquisition of knowledge and the development of skills necessary for high wages and improved 
quality of life. Therefore, the quality of education and the methods for achieving it are crucial (Darling-Hammond et al., 2020). 
China is the largest country with the highest population among developing nations, and over the past few decades, it has made 
significant progress in educational development. Today, China ranks second in the world for the number of annual higher education 
graduates, with over 8 million graduates, and boasts more than 3013 higher education institutions, around 500 of which host in­
ternational students (Ministry of Education of the People’s Republic of China, 2023; OECD, 2023). Considering that approximately 70 
Chinese public universities are included in the QS World University Rankings (Top Universities, 2023) and that tuition fees at China’s 
top universities are still several times lower than those at prestigious European and American universities, China is poised to become 
an attractive destination for students not only from the Asian region but globally. 
Despite the fact that the modern development of China’s economy, cultural life, and educational system occurs in the context of 
globalization, a distinctive feature is the continuous implementation of ideological and political education throughout the entire

This characteristic makes the study of contemporary Chinese ideological and political education a subject of interest to researchers, 
particularly in terms of the teaching methods employed. For instance, the structure of political education in Chinese universities, 
including curricula and practice, has been analyzed (Svensson, 2023). Attention is given to the issue of institutionalizing patriotism as 
an ideological concept for students (L. Liu et al., 2023). It is emphasized that the theory and practice of teaching ideology develop 
worldviews and civic consciousness (L. Liu & Li, 2023). However, the methods of teaching political theory often result in students’ 
understanding being limited to the level of "rational cognition" (Su & Li, 2022). Researchers also note that contemporary ideological 
and political education for modern students must meet the requirements of effectiveness and relevance (Zhang & Sang, 2016) and keep 
pace with the times by actively incorporating innovations (Li, 2017). 
The increasing relevance of studying the issue of ideological and political education in China within the academic community, 
along with the growing number of publications in journals indexed in the most authoritative databases, has been noted since General 
Secretary Xi Jinping came to power. He has significantly strengthened policies aimed at ideological and political enlightenment. 
However, it is noted that researchers have mainly focused on the normative and descriptive aspects of this issue (Ouyang et al., 2024), 
while the aspects of how students themselves evaluate the perception of ideological and political concepts and how this education 
impacts their involvement in socio-political life are insufficiently covered. 
Thus, this research aims to expand knowledge in the area of the pedagogy of ideological and political education in China, to outline 
possible future directions in pedagogical research, and to develop administrative policies for higher education institutions in this field. 
Since the primary goal of political-ideological education in China is to shape members of society with values, beliefs, and civic 
consciousness established by the party, the main research question is to understand how contemporary students perceive ideological 
and political concepts and how educational approaches in this area affect their participation in socio-political life. The primary 
objective of this study is to evaluate the educational approaches and methods of ideological and political education in China. 
1.1. Literature review 
Discussions around the quality of education and its drawbacks are still open in the global educational community. High-quality 
education, accompanied by modern teaching methods, provides the necessary knowledge and skills and develops values. It also ac­
tivates logical and critical thinking skills, inspires students, and reveals their potential, creativity, and ability to conduct research and 
discover new knowledge (Ruiz-Alfonso et al., 2021). Since the beginning of the 20th century, a rapid transformation has begun in 
China. This process fostered the reconstruction of political, economic, social, and cultural systems. Education has gained great 
importance in various reform programs (Oyeniran & Uwamahoro, 2017). At the same time, China’s educational reforms and pedagogy 
relied on both foreign practices and the country’s traditions. This fact made it possible to develop a modern education system, which is 
one of the largest in the world (Guo et al., 2019). 
Quality education has become the focus of educational reforms in China (Wang & Liu, 2022). Among outdated educational methods 
and approaches, the direction of “exam education” has received especially pronounced criticism. This method tends to draw increased 
attention to mechanical memorization, which is irrelevant to everyday life and prevents the development of creative skills. Indeed, 
there are objective reasons for introducing the practice of education focused on memorization. First, education uses the Gaokao 
entrance exam system, which creates fierce competition for admission to universities and colleges (Gierczyk & Diao, 2021). The issues 
of quality education and pedagogical methods are urgent for political education in China. In general, political education is an 
important component of ideological education at the university. Therefore, many countries of the world implement it explicitly or 
covertly to guarantee the further participation of graduates in their country’s development. Young Chinese live in the system of 
ideology and political education from an early age. They follow a specific educational curriculum and visit museums and thematic 
exhibitions. Moreover, there are specially developed applications for social networking, computer games, and even dramas filmed for 
television and online platforms (Lin et al., 2015). 
It should be noted that all educational institutions in China, both public and private, are centrally controlled by the government. 
Relevant ministries develop and provide the key components that must be included in the curriculum, and educational materials are 
reviewed before being implemented in practice. For Chinese undergraduate students, the study of ideological subjects (Marxist- 
Leninist thought, modern Chinese history, and Chinese socialism) is mandatory regardless of their major (Ouyang et al., 2024). 
A strong impetus to the development of political education and the study of Political Theory in China was in 1989. It should be 
clarified that political theory in the Chinese context involves the study of specific theories and concepts. Mandatory education within 
the framework of Political Theory includes the theory of Marxism, the ideas of Chinese leaders (Mao Zedong, Deng Xiaoping, and Xi 
Jinping), the history of China (including the socialist period, reforms, and modern history), law, and moral education (Chen & Wang, 
2023). Overall, the specificity of studying political theory in China can be represented by the following components: (1) Main content 
(Marxism, Mao Zedong Thought, Deng Xiaoping Theory, The Important Idea of the “Three Representations”, A Scientific View on 
Development, Xi Jinping Thought on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New era, Party Line, Guiding Principles, Policies and 
Resolutions, Education of ideals and beliefs, Education of patriotism, etc.); (2) The central value (Collectivist value) (Wang & Zhong, 
2020). In 2004, the Communist Party of China and the State Council approved the “Opinions on Further Strengthening and Improving 
the Ideological and Political Education for University Students.” It states that the actual task of higher education is to improve the 
ideological and political education of students who are to become the successors of Chinese socialism. In 2015, the government 
adopted a directive aimed at strengthening the political training of teachers and later initiated several more documents. The latter 
called for intensifying ideological work at universities and raising political awareness of students. In 2019, the specialists of the 
Ministry of Education of China published a teacher training plan, which focused on Marxism directions. It is mandatory to include the

Finally, in 2022, the government developed and approved a Plan. It outlined the work and measures to promote ideological and

economic achievements of the country and its success in geopolitics (Svensson, 2023). 
Furthermore, it is noteworthy that in addition to a separate course on political theory, mandatory for all Chinese students, ideo­
logical and political education permeates many social sciences and humanities disciplines (Zhang & Sang, 2016). Given that China 
implements the concept of "ideological and political education within the curriculum," all courses taught at universities must include a 
component aimed at ideological and political education. Thus, specialized knowledge is imparted alongside the promotion of values 
aligned with party directives. It should be noted that after the adoption of this concept at the state level, educational institutions had to 
integrate elements of ideological and political education into specialized courses (including mathematics, languages, social sciences, 
etc.), which posed certain challenges for educators (Li et al., 2024a). 
The methods of teaching Political Theory aim to provide training for generations who support the leadership of the Communist 
Party of China and the socialist system. The primary goal is to further follow the path of building socialism with Chinese specifics, as 
well as strengthen the population’s self-confidence to achieve national revival. Educational practices and approaches also foster the 
spirit of patriotism in students, directing their feelings to support the Communist Party of China and follow its ideas (Xue et al., 2023). 
The methodology of teaching Political Theory rests on such methods as theoretical propaganda, practical tasks, independent and 
additional education, comparative identification of information, and so forth (Yang, 2021). The most traditional methods of teaching 
Political Theory in China include classical lecture classes. These classes imply the in-depth study of official textbooks, party theory, and 
the leading party ideology. In practical classes, students explore their ideological status in practical activities. Practical classes show 
students whether they have ideological consciousness and the ability to perceive and understand the party’s ideas and serve their 
country (Zhou & Fu, 2023). 
To achieve the set goals of Political Theory, compulsory courses at the university go beyond traditional approaches. The auxiliary 
methods encompass the incentives created by the university administration and teachers. These incentives engage students to actively 
participate in party organizations, social practices, or volunteer activities (Zhang & Fagan, 2016). This approach should direct students 
to the practical application of the acquired knowledge about the concepts of Marxism and the party’s ideology. Students should use 
their skills to combat poverty and build a socialist system. Thus, the educational system uses methods that integrate Political Theory 
into all spheres of a student’s life, including social (Cao, 2018). It is also important to note that contemporary Chinese students, more so 
than previous generations, are familiar with the peculiarities of education and the socio-cultural life of youth in other countries 
worldwide due to globalization and the development of the Internet, facilitating content dissemination and communication among 
students from different countries. Therefore, an intriguing task arises to assess how Chinese students currently perceive ideological and 
political concepts, the effectiveness of educational approaches implemented for their study, and to what extent such education pro­
motes their active involvement in the socio-political life of Chinese society in accordance with governmental imperatives. 
2. Methodology 
2.1. Purpose and research procedures 
The primary aim of this research is to evaluate the educational approaches and methods of ideological and political education in 
China. The main research question focuses on understanding how contemporary students perceive ideological and political concepts 
and assessing the effectiveness of educational approaches implemented for their instruction. Specifically, the study examines how 
these approaches contribute to students’ active participation in socio-political life. To achieve the stated objective, the following tasks 
have been formulated: 
– Evaluation of the methods and implementation strategies of the ideological and political education curriculum at the university. 
– Assessment of students’ learning levels in the realm of ideological and political education. 
– Evaluation of the outcomes of ideological and political education among students through measures of participation in the 
country’s political and social life. 
– Assessment of the impact of ideological and political education on students’ engagement in socio-political life. 
2.2. Sample formation 
The study was conducted at a university located in a metropolis with a population exceeding 11 million inhabitants. This university 
is among the most prestigious institutions in China, ranking in the top 20 nationally and within the top 250 according to the QS World 
University Rankings. 
The sample consisted of Chinese students who were required to undergo a course in political and ideological education throughout 
their undergraduate studies. Invitations to participate in the study were sent to the email addresses of third and fourth-year students 
who had studied political theory at the university for several years and had been involved in civic activities aimed at implementing

The study invited students from disciplines not directly related to political science, resulting in the following final distribution:

total of 9 provinces ensured geographical diversity within China), predominantly from urban areas (73.01 % of participants). 
2.3. Research methodology and tools 
The basis of the study was the model of the International Civic and Citizenship Education Study (IEA) (Schulz et al., 2022). The 
model proposed tools that collect data for variables, in particular, regarding the education of citizens who understand and practically 
use their country’s ideals. The survey of the study participants explained the differences in the resulting variables. The original tool has 
regional modules developed for the countries of Asia, Europe, and Latin America. However, since China has specific ideological and 
political education, the questionnaire was adapted to the national context. The study also used the Citizenship Education Longitudinal 
Study (CELS). It is a longitudinal study of civic education conducted among young people. This study allows researchers to track the 
attitude of students toward the curriculum and educational approaches in civic and political education (Donbavand & Hoskins, 2021). 
The rationale for using these tools and approaches was that they allowed for assessing the perception of political training in Chinese 
universities. At the same time, the direct participants’ opinions on the educational context, methods, and program received special 
attention. The study covered 5 aspects related to the variables of learning Political Theory (the result of learning; teaching methods; 
assessment methods; methods preferred by students; and student suggestions aimed at improving the learning process). 
To assess students’ perception of ideological and political education, the study indicators of trust in various socio-political in­
stitutions and the intention to participate in socio-political life. The civic participation of students in the socio-political life of China 
was assessed through indicators of participation in socio-political activities and their civic self-expression (Parji & Feriandi, 2020). To 
investigate the Chinese approach to implementing ideological and political education, the study included a set of criteria: 
– criteria for civic participation in the life of a socialist country (participation in public events, volunteer or socially useful activities, 
financial assistance to those in need, and discussion of social problems in a close circle); 
– criteria for socio-political participation in the country’s life (participation in state elections, contact with officials of state in­
stitutions, activities within the framework of political celebrations and events). 
Data collection for the study involved empirical methods. The study had several stages: 
Stage 1 – The questionnaire was adapted to collect specific demographic characteristics and evaluate opinions about the training 
program in the field of ideological and political education. The survey aimed to investigate the overall perception of ideological and 
political education among students. Electronic questionnaires were distributed to students for completion. 
To assess the degree of agreement with the statements of the questionnaire, the study utilized a popular tool for assessing attitudes 
and opinions – a five-point Likert scale. On the scale, an answer interpreted as “absolutely disagree” received 1 point, while “absolutely 
agree” received 5 points. 
The analysis of the survey data used the statistical package for social sciences SPSS. This tool was enough for research in the field of 
education. To measure students’ trust in political institutions and their civic intention to participate in socio-political life, the study 
employed a single-sample t-criterion. It verified whether the mean value of the aggregate was equal to a certain value. To assess 
respondents’ participation in various manifestations of socio-political life, it was necessary to calculate modes (typicality). The cal­
culations determined one or more values in observations that occurred most frequently. To assess the relationship between teaching 
the Chinese concept of political theory and the socio-political activities of respondents (the assumed relationship between the 
dependent variable and the independent variables), a regression analysis was necessary. It was also necessary to assess whether re­
spondents understand how political and ideological education can contribute to their future civic engagement and socio-political 
activities. In this case, the study calculated an additive index as a parameter formed by adding indicators characterizing various as­
pects of the same concept. Internal consistency was tested with Cronbach’s α (= 0.876, which is a sufficient indicator). 
Stage 2 involved conducting a series of interviews with randomly selected students (98 participants were randomly chosen, with 94 
ultimately participating; 4 declined due to personal reasons) to explore specific student experiences with the study of political theory at 
the university. The interviews provided an opportunity to understand better the motivations that influenced students’ responses to the 
survey questions and facilitated a deeper understanding of students’ attitudes toward political and ideological education. The in­
terviews were conducted in an audio-only format (without video or personal identification of participants), and audio settings allowed 
for slight voice modulation, which participants could utilize. Audio recordings of the interviews were transcribed and analyzed 
subsequently. 
2.4. Ethical issues 
Students participated in the study voluntarily and had the right to withdraw at any time. The survey questionnaires were anon­
ymous. Each recorded interview was assigned a specific code without mentioning the personal data of individual participants. In­
terviews were conducted in an audio-only format, without video and personal identification of participants. Audio settings included an

3. Results 
3.1. Stage 1 – results of the assessment phase of students’ overall perception of methods of ideological and political education and 
approaches to its evaluation 
Table 1 presents an evaluation of various approaches to teaching in ideological and political education. 
The results presented in Table 1 show that ideological and political education somewhat uses all the discussed methods. Never­
theless, their use is unlikely to have any regulations or balance. The most common teaching method is the provision of information 
from normative educational material for students to remember (the answer “frequently” was 44.8 %, and the answer “constantly” was 
26.2 %, which means 80 % of participants in total). Another typical pedagogical method is PowerPoint Lessons (64 % of affirmative 
answers). However, the methods also include training based on theory and real-life practice (49.4 %) and student project activities and 
teamwork (52 %). That is, there is a tendency to use more flexible teaching methods, which provide students with socio-political 
experience. Fig. 1 illustrates the use of various assessment methods in ideological and political education. 
Points for testing or answering questions are the most common pedagogical practices for assessing students’ knowledge: 78 % and 
66 % of students received them, respectively. For slightly more than a third of the respondents (35 %), the main assessment criterion 
was the quality of their scientific papers. Only 30 % of students had points for teamwork and preparation of group projects, and only 9 
% received a collegial assessment from the teacher and other group members. Table 2 shows the results of the survey devoted to the 
formed results of ideological and political education at the university. The main criteria are the measures of understanding a Chinese 
citizen’s participation in the socio-political life of society. 
Thus, the respondents noted the general effectiveness of ideological and political education at university. They noted that the 
course mostly achieved its goals. The exceptions were Patriotism, socialist morality and values (less than half (45.7 %) of participants 
agreed), Active participation in Chinese political life and social life (only 15 % of participants agreed), and The ability to accept responsibility 
as a citizen and a participant in political life (only 47.7 % agreed). Most participants (52.3 %) believed that the problem of the income gap 
was especially important for them. The students also understood the connection between the Chinese approach to teaching political 
theory and practice (54.6 %). Many agreed that ideological and political education formed their civic qualities: the ability to critically 
perceive information (51.4 %), cooperate to achieve public goals (59.7 %) and show respect for other members of society (55.7 %). 
Nevertheless, 14.7 % noted a lack of a sense of patriotism, as well as socialist morality and values. This goal is one of the main ones, 
according to the state educational program of ideological and political education in China. In addition, 76.2 % provided negative 
responses regarding Active participation in China’s political and social life. 
Further, students shared their opinions on how to reform the methods of ideological and political education. Thus, the survey 
revealed the most preferable areas. Table 3 presents the results. 
The study participants generally agreed that each method should teach political and ideological theory and enhance awareness of 
political and social life. Interestingly, students had markedly different opinions on the need to use social networks and the media more. 
Some students demonstrated a significant interest in using media resources in teaching, while others were cautious about them as 
sources of political knowledge and information.

3.2. Stage 2 – the main results of the interviews 
In the previous stage, students participated in a survey regarding their experience of ideological and political education using 
various methods. The interviews were conducted to discuss some of the most prominent survey findings using the questionnaire (which 
were not disclosed to the students) in order to uncover more details and gather the students’ opinions. 
During the interviews, a predominance of memorization of study materials from textbooks and working with presentations was 
identified. This fact sparked some dissatisfaction among the respondents in personal interviews. For instance, in response to questions 
such as, "To what extent do PowerPoint Lessons and memorization-based textbook information contribute to achieving learning 
outcomes and participation in public life?" students noted that such teaching approaches render information abstract and the material 
is not fully understood without real-life context. Among the responses formulated by students, common remarks included: "This 
method helps me perform well in exams because I can prepare for tests and written assignments with clear assessment criteria, but I 
don’t understand how the theory works in practice" or "I excel in exams easily, but it does not motivate me to actively engage in public 
and political life." 
The survey of students regarding their attitudes towards assessment methods revealed that exams in the form of multiple-choice 
tests failed to engage students in real societal and political life. These exam methods primarily require students to demonstrate 
their knowledge of dates, places, party decrees, or the chronology of historical events, and to write statements about these events. The 
results also indicate that students cannot develop an interest in the political life of society if the criterion for evaluating written re­
sponses is their adherence to lecture or textbook content. For example, in response to the question, "Is it valuable to attempt to go 
beyond the established exam materials and express your own assessment of events and their consequences?" the majority of responses 
conveyed the sentiment: "I do not express my ideas in exams to avoid receiving a lower grade." 
During the interviews, students were asked questions such as "Which sources of information are most sought after for learning?" 
Interestingly, only about 30 % of respondents identified textbooks approved by the Ministry of Education as their primary source. More 
than half of the responses from respondents included statements like: "I prefer getting information from the Internet, often watching 
educational videos," "I remember well what the teacher says during lectures," "I learn about current events and their connection to 
historical chronicles from the news," "I have a good foundation from school; I remember information about many events and important 
dates and can still ask my teacher about them." When the respondents had to choose only one source of information, 33.6 % preferred 
the Internet, 30 % - television news and other serious media, 29.4 % - educational literature, and 28 % - lectures and discussions with 
school teachers and university teachers. Thus, various kinds of mass media and media, including Internet resources, stimulate students 
to learn more about political life. 
The study included additional interviews regarding whether students considered themselves active participants in socio-political 
life. These interviews revealed that while students could articulate the concept of citizenship, not all fully understood the term 
"citizenship" in its broader sense. Consequently, some considered citizenship as related to political terminology. In response to the 
question, "Is it possible for students to participate in civic activities?" answers varied, with some stating, "I’m not sure that activities 
students engage in can be considered civic." There were also responses suggesting that civic engagement equates to the Chinese 
concept of collectivism. 
Additional interviews about socio-political activity in the context of participation in individual events and spheres of life showed 
interesting results. The university often encouraged students to help low-income people (more than 50 % noted their participation in 
similar events at their university) and participate in volunteer organizations. Thus, more than 70 % reported their active membership 
in various volunteer movement communities. In response to the question, "What does participation in such initiatives provide?" re­
sponses often included statements such as, "It looks good on a resume when seeking good employment, especially if you can provide a 
letter of recommendation." Students also frequently participated in events organized by the Communist Youth League of China. When 
asked, "Why do students consider it important to participate in such events, and does it provide additional benefits?" formal responses 
often included additions like, "I want to earn extra points for social activities," "I am considering the possibility of joining the party 
soon." This motive was particularly significant for students planning to pursue careers in government institutions. 
Interviews regarding the general attitudes of respondents towards the ideological and political education program revealed stu­
dents’ concerns. Expressed opinions often included statements like, "The program does not always provide current information about 
the socio-political life of the country," "The material presented in textbooks is not sufficiently interesting and outdated." In response to 
the question, "Which sections, theories, or concepts are the most difficult to understand?" the majority reported, "Concepts of Marxist 
theory." Additionally, most students noted that studying China’s international relations and its geopolitical position in the modern 
world was significantly more interesting. 
4. Discussion 
The research aims to evaluate educational approaches and methods in ideological and political education in China, assessing the 
perception of ideological and political concepts and the participation of students in socio-political life. Overall, it is noted that Chinese 
students reflect on the political life of society and its social functioning aspects, actively engage in socio-political events, and express a 
desire to become even more active members of society. This finding aligns with earlier studies asserting that ideological and political 
education is an integral part of education for Chinese students at national universities (Xi, 2019), demonstrating its transformation into 
an institutionalized system that institutionalizes patriotism as a primary ideological perspective for students (X. Liu et al., 2023). In this 
case, it is noteworthy to observe the difference in forms of political activity demonstrated by students in China compared to those in 
European countries. According to our findings, the most frequent type of activity in which Chinese students participate is official

celebrations (e.g., those organized by the Communist Youth League of China). In regional studies, however, it is noted that political 
participation among European students varies widely, ranging from party-affiliated to protest activities (Oana, 2019). 
According to the results, it is noted that not all of the state-set goals of ideological and political education were achieved. Spe­
cifically, 14.7 % of respondents did not perceive a focus on fostering patriotism or socialist morality and values, despite this goal being 
a primary objective according to China’s state educational program for ideological and political education. This finding contrasts 
unexpectedly with earlier research results (Li et al., 2024a), which highlighted mainland China’s significant achievements in patriotic 
education compared to Hong Kong. Thus, there is a need for further research to investigate this discrepancy in results. Factors such as 
the geographic location of universities (e.g., coastal provinces with developed trade and a higher percentage of foreigners), proficiency 
in foreign languages, and increased consumption of global information content may potentially influence these findings. 
The survey results indicated that knowledge gained within the educational program on political theory positively influenced the 
civic engagement of students. The respondents reported their greatest experiences of socio-political activities in public events orga­
nized by the university and the Communist Youth League of China, although they infrequently participate in elections. Direct political 
involvement among students was minimal, aligning with previous findings (Ding et al., 2022) that students actively attend 
university-promoted events for which they receive additional credits. Additionally, it was concluded that the understanding and 
implementation of civic activism coincide with traditional national aspects of China. Nevertheless, students lack public-political 
manifestations of their civic stance (76.2 % did not perceive outcomes such as "Active participation in China’s political and social 
life" from their education). This supports earlier observations that China’s traditional educational system tends to focus on shaping 
students’ political allegiance rather than their personal development as citizens (Li & Li, 2019). Some researchers suggest that key 
stakeholders in Chinese education should reconsider policies regarding the ideological preparation of youth for life in a globalized 
world (Li et al., 2024b). This could be achieved by recognizing the potential use of pedagogical methods such as discussion (suc­
cessfully employed in other disciplines) in ideological and political education to cultivate conscientious citizens. 
Currently, students’ understanding of political theory largely remains at the level of "rational cognition." Respondents rated their 
knowledge and skills acquired through education for engaging in socio-political activities as moderate, indicating that the educational 
program inadequately addressed fundamental questions about societal and political life and structure. It can be inferred that these 
educational areas require enhanced effectiveness. Moreover, innovative teaching methods have been minimally utilized in teaching 
ideological and political concepts. Previous research has highlighted that the use of virtual and augmented reality with realistic 
historical or social situations can significantly enhance educational effectiveness (Li & Li, 2019). In the era of new technologies and 
media, ideological education should harness their potential (Yu, 2022). Researchers have also proposed the introduction of a precision 
teaching method for political courses as an alternative to extensive methods, providing educators with the ability to dynamically adjust 
parts of the curriculum (online learning, classroom instruction, and social practice) according to students’ needs (Huang et al., 2023). 
5. Conclusions 
The research findings indicate that the most widely used methods in ideological and political education include PowerPoint lessons 
and the presentation of information from normative study materials, which students must memorize and comprehend. Examination 
testing and question-and-answer responses are the most common pedagogical tools for assessing students’ knowledge. Such practices 
imply memorization of extensive information without understanding its practical application, highlighting the prevalence of extensive 
teaching methods in these courses. 
Overall, political and ideological education has generally achieved its objectives, with a high emphasis on fostering civic qualities 
among members of socialist society and the assertion that educated citizens actively participate in their country’s socio-political life. 
However, simultaneously, respondents infrequently participate in national elections (although active in student elections and 
volunteer activities), have limited interaction with governmental institutions, and almost never engage in socio-political events. Ex­
ceptions are events directly incentivized by the university that may offer additional credits. Among the motivations for such activities, 
respondents mentioned positive recommendation letters that facilitate future job searches. This presents a paradox where students 
acknowledge what they should gain from their educational course but apply acquired knowledge less frequently in practice, partic­
ularly in terms of personal initiative. 
Interestingly, opinions on the necessity of using social media and mass media in teaching political theory are divided: there is both 
high interest in employing innovations and media resources in education, and skepticism regarding their reliability as sources of 
political knowledge and information. Students demonstrate the greatest trust in political information received from their families and 
educators, possibly influenced by traditional Chinese respect for elders. However, when obtaining political information, they often 
turn not to printed publications and textbooks, but to online video content. Overall, this may reflect a characteristic trait of the new 
generation, which finds it easier to digest video information rather than large volumes of data from books. Students perceive infor­
mation from textbooks abstractly and have noted that this method of teaching focuses solely on examination outcomes rather than 
fostering engagement in real socio-political life. 
The study had certain limitations, notably, the sample only covered one university, albeit a large educational institution with 
students from many provinces across China. In future research, it would be beneficial to explore the influence of additional factors such 
as exposure to political content from global media, the specific academic disciplines in which students specialize, family membership 
in political parties, or socio-economic backgrounds. Additionally, some participants were members of the Communist Youth League of 
China, which could have influenced the assessment of students’ political activities. 
The theoretical contribution of the study lies in the development of theoretical approaches to thematic analysis of ideological and 
political education as a whole, as well as the advancement of the issue of ideological and political education in China and its impact on

The practical contribution consists of a proposed straightforward and replicable approach to assessing methods and outcomes in

teaching methods in ideological and political education. Examples of such practices may include programs based on more flexible 
teaching methods, implicit learning approaches, activities integrating theory and practice, internet resources, educational videos, and 
similar initiatives.
