ABSTRACT
The importance of Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics (STEM) subjects for the present and future society is 
clear. With assessments such as the Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA), the possibility of an analysis on 
student-­related variables predicting results in STEM areas opens. The aim was to identify the PISA 2018 variables that predict the 
achievement in STEM competences in Spanish students through data mining techniques. Using three data mining methods (i.e., 
Random Forest, support vector regression [SVR], and ridge), based on 35,943 Spanish students aged from 15.25 to 16.17 years old, 
100 variables were found to predict performance in mathematical and scientific competences. Subsequent coding and classifica-
tion made it possible to determine that, in mathematical competence, the variables related to access, use, teaching, interest, or 
skill in Information and Communication Technology (ICT) stand out. In scientific competence, these variables only represented 
17%, giving way to others such as metacognition and variables related to mother tongue and reading. The personal and proximate 
student's context is central to performance in STEM areas.
1   |   Introduction
The development of competences in future citizens is one of the 
ultimate goals of education to achieve a sustainable and mean-
ingful social development, which is why its momentum is aimed 
to increase in the upcoming years (Sustainable Development 
Goals, SDGs, 4.4. or 4.6; e.g., Bárcena et al. 2018). This seeks 
to nurture youth talent. Specifically, talent related to Science, 
Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics subjects (hereinaf-
ter STEM), as this is an essential resource of 21st-­century societ-
ies for the economic growth of countries and in the achievement 
of the SDGs, as well as a priority for any education system 
(Herce-­Palomares et al. 2022). In Spain, governmental and ed-
ucational institutions have included STEM education as one of 
their main priorities, seeking to increase vocations in this field, 
especially among young girls (Estévez-­Mauriz and Baelo 2021).

1.1   |   What Influences STEM Competence 
Learning?
Numerous studies have been carried out to date aiming to de-
termine the main predictors of overall academic achievement 
(hereinafter AA) in students at different educational stages. 
One of the most extensive is being conducted by Hattie (2024), 
which is based on the constant review and inclusion of meta-­
analyses on influences on AA, and which has identified more 
than 400 variables up to date, a figure that is likely to increase 
as it is continually being updated. Following the framework 
proposed both in this study and previous research (i.e., Hattie 
and Anderman 2013; Hattie and Yates 2013), the influences 
on AA can generally be found in personal elements of the 
students themselves, their family context, as well as in the 
educational center: the classroom, the teacher, the type of 
teaching-­learning strategies employed or the curriculum it-
self. When only those factors having “considerable potential 
for AA improvement” according to Hattie  (2024) (107 vari-
ables) are explored: 15.89% (17 variables) belong to the stu-
dents themselves and no factors are related to the family or 
home context; these factors mainly belong to the teaching-­
learning strategies (44.86%, 48 variables) and curriculum 
(16.2%, 16 variables). However, although this evidence is rele-
vant, it comes from numerous studies in different contexts and 
with a very notable variability.
1.2   |   PISA
Nowadays, it is possible to find different indicators about AA 
in specific STEM competences that help us to explore differ-
ent populations at a worldwide level and in each competence 
in every country (e.g., Trends in International Mathematics 
and Science Study, TIMMS). One of the most famous is config-
ured by the scores offered by the Programme for International 
Student Assessment tests (hereinafter PISA), developed by the 
Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development 
(OECD 2019). PISA is an “International assessment study, pro-
moted by the OECD and the participating countries, which at-
tempts to respond to a need common to all current education 
systems: ‘to define, describe and explain what 15-­year-­olds, who 
will soon exercise their rights as citizens, know and know how 
to do, applying their knowledge to a variety of environments and 
contexts, at the end of their compulsory education’” (Spanish 
Ministry of Education and Vocational Training 2019, 15). This 
test focuses on three main competences: reading, mathematics, 
and science. The last two are the most closely linked to STEM 
education.
The PISA tests are of particular interest because they provide 
relevant information on school and environmental factors, as 
well as on the results of the learning process, both nationally 
and internationally (Cordero et al. 2013). This fact makes it pos-
sible to contrast the existence of relationships between AA or the 
level of proficiency in each of the areas addressed (i.e., reading, 
mathematics, and science) with a series of indicators related to 
the students themselves, their families, and their school envi-
ronment, with a significant sample of students in each country 
(see OECD 2019). More precisely, among these indicators there 
are questions regarding educational career (e.g., “What do you 
think you will be doing 5 years from now?”), ICT familiarity 
(e.g., “Time spent using digital devices during classroom lessons 
in a typical school week”), well-­being (e.g., “How did you feel the 
last time you did your homework/studied for school?”), and fi-
nancial literacy (e.g., “I can decide independently what to spend 
my money on”).
In comparison with other studies such as Hattie's (2024) meta-­
analyses, PISA tests could shed more light on these questions 
with similarly measured, objective and validated variables 
(OECD  2019). Moreover, it has recently been suggested that 
these categories should be approached in a reorganized way to 
better accommodate the structures proposed by international 
assessments (Kuger and Klieme 2016; in Gamazo and Martínez-­
Abad 2020), as it would be the case of the PISA indicators men-
tioned before. In this regard, a recent scoping review (Odell 
et al. 2020) pointed out that students' ICT use, interest, and au-
tonomy may moderately predict AA in mathematics and science 
in PISA.
1.3   |   PISA Tests in Spain
1.3.1   |   Spanish STEM Competences in Context
Currently there is only one official report in the case of the 
PISA 2018 results in Spain, the one published by the Spanish 
Ministry of Education and Vocational Training in 2019, and 
some secondary research has been published after. This re-
port showed that the Spanish competence in mathematics 
(481 points) was significantly lower than the OECD mean (489 
points) and the European Union’ (EU) average (494 points). 
In the case of science competence, Spain's average score (483 
points) was significantly lower than the OECD's (489 points) 
and the EU's (490 points) averages. Furthermore, while the 
data for the successive years since the 2009 test showed a cer-
tain stability in mathematics performance—a fact that differ-
entiates Spain from the rest of the OECD countries suffering 
a decline—, in the case of science performance the decline is 
significant, following the general trend of the OECD coun-
tries. Considering these data and the importance of these com-
petences, it is of great social interest to know how to facilitate 
the development of these STEM competences in Spanish stu-
dents (Herce-­Palomares et al. 2022).
1.3.2   |   Research on PISA 2018 Results
The 2018 PISA test, with respect to that of 2015 (analyzed in 
previous works such as Gil-­Madrona et  al.  2019), involves a 
greater emphasis on the non-­cognitive elements of the student, 
specifically on well-­being, since that is the first PISA test in 
which that very instrument was incorporated. In the case of 
the Spanish context, reference is made to cognitive well-­being 
(i.e., growth mindset) and psychological well-­being (i.e., bully-
ing, time spent on the Internet, sense of belonging to the school, 
parental involvement in school tasks, life satisfaction, meaning 
in life, self-­efficacy, and fear of failure) from the student's point 
of view.

Regarding the Spanish context obtained in the report, addi-
tional research such as the study of Rodríguez-­García and 
Arias-­Gago (2021) focused on the positive association between 
reading competences in PISA 2018 and teachers' use of an ac-
tive model. Also, Navarro-­Martínez and Peña-­Acuña  (2022) 
found that excessive use of technology and social networks 
is negatively associated with AA in PISA tests, especially in 
boys. There is also another research developed by the Spanish 
data from PISA 2018 (Rodríguez et al. 2020) that has explored 
how being immigrant or native affects the AA in mathemati-
cal and scientific competences from PISA 2018 and well-­being, 
revealing that the natives presented higher levels in all cases, 
except in the case of life satisfaction, for which no differences 
were found. More recently, the research conducted by Molina-­
Muñoz et al. (2022) sought to identify the variables that af-
fected AA in PISA 2018 mathematics competences by gender. 
They found that there are variables that affect both boys 
and girls similarly (e.g., grade repetition and socioeconomic 
and cultural status); while some such as school tenure only 
affected AA in girls and other languages spoken at home or 
student-­teacher ratio only affected AA in boys. Also, Molina-­
Muñoz et al. (2023) found that some factors, such as resilience, 
achievement motivation, competitiveness, perceived cooper-
ation at school, and social connectedness with parents posi-
tively impacted on students' AA in mathematics in PISA 2018. 
These authors also pointed out some possible negative factors 
such as bullying, physical self-­concept, meaning in life, and 
perceived competitiveness at school. Altogether, these results 
show that it is of special interest to explore in detail the possi-
bilities that PISA 2018 offers in the Spanish context; moreover, 
knowing that some of them contrast with previous results in 
other PISA tests (e.g., ICT use for Gamazo et al. 2018), while 
others seem to be maintained over time (e.g., importance of 
cultural capital for Lozano and Trinidad 2019).
1.3.3   |   Current Possibilities in Spanish PISA Research
As it is possible to see, although the previous studies focused 
on Spanish data, the analyses focused on very specific vari-
ables (e.g., teaching strategies, ICT use, immigration, etc.). If 
we compare the previous works with the international results 
of the study developed by Hattie (2024), it is likely to see how 
AA can be affected by multiple variables from the different 
contexts linked to education. Examining PISA variables collec-
tively may provide a comprehensive understanding of student 
performance by acknowledging the impact of various factors 
on learning. This approach helps to identify areas of special 
interest or sensitivity, which can be addressed to enhance ed-
ucational practices and inform better policies. However, to our 
knowledge, there is no analysis that takes into consideration all 
the variables obtained in PISA 2018 that could be influencing 
the AA of STEM competences in Spanish students. While it is 
true that similar works exist (Gamazo and Martínez-­Abad 2020; 
Martínez-­Abad 2019; Martínez-­Abad et al. 2020), these have an 
international character, not focused only on Spain; a context 
which owns some characteristics that made it prone to be of 
interest for being exclusively analyzed (e.g., segregation among 
communities, different economic levels and implementations 
of ICT use, etc.; Fernández-­Gutiérrez et al. 2020; Murillo and 
Martínez-­Garrido 2018).
Following this research line, researchers have a responsibil-
ity to explore the different databases and search for relation-
ships between variables to reach new conclusions that reports 
elaborated by OECD have not yet done. For this purpose, data 
mining techniques are emerging to identify the main factors 
affecting AA, under a new theoretical framework that allows 
researchers to refine existing models (Gamazo and Martínez-­
Abad 2020). The choice of specific data mining models over 
alternatives is based on various factors that can vary depend-
ing on the context and specific project goals. One of them is 
that some models are more robust when dealing with miss-
ing or noisy data compared to others. If the dataset is prone 
to outliers or missing values, using a model that can handle 
these situations mentioned above will allow better results. 
Methods such as Random Forest (RF) can also combine mul-
tiple models to create a stronger and more robust model that 
might enhance the overall predictive performance and miti-
gate problems such as overfitting. Likewise, using data min-
ing models allows researchers to learn patterns from the past 
and use that knowledge to identify similar patterns in the fu-
ture, making predictions based on them (See Section 2 for a 
more extended rationale of the methods).
1.4   |   The Present Study
Despite the above, there is a lack of studies focused on em-
ploying these concrete data mining techniques to exclusively 
explore the PISA 2018 tests in Spain, even though they repre-
sent an advantage for further analysis. This situation leads us 
to formulate two research questions: (a) Among the variables 
included in PISA 2018 in Spain, which of them are the best 
predictors of AA in STEM competences? and (b) Could these 
predictors be classified or organized into major areas of in-
terest for STEM education? Therefore, the aim of the present 
work was to identify the PISA 2018 variables that best predict 
the achievement in STEM competences (i.e., mathematics and 
science) among the Spanish students through data mining 
techniques.

2.2   |   Variables and Instruments
The variables addressed are delimited by the instruments 
used for the PISA 2018 tests (Spanish Ministry of Education 
and Vocational Training  2019). Considering the scope of this 
research, only the tests of mathematical and scientific com-
petences were included as criterion variables. Furthermore, 
the last PISA edition included a list of questionnaires related 
to the students' context such as student questionnaire or well-­
being questionnaire. To this end, the data provided by these 
questionnaires have been included in this research as predictor 
variables. These questionnaires address constructs that refer to 
school management and organization, teaching and learning 
factors, family and student contexts, and noncognitive or meta-
cognitive factors, as in previous works (Gamazo and Martínez-­
Abad 2020). Nevertheless, in the case of the available data in the 
Spanish database, the analyses included the following question-
naires: student questionnaire, educational career, ICT famil-
iarity, financial literacy, and well-­being. Please see Section 2.4 
below to know the procedures followed to deal with the 1118 
variables initially included into the database.
2.3   |   Procedure
Regarding data gathering and according to PISA 2018 reports 
(Spanish Ministry of Education and Vocational Training 2019), 
data were taken at high schools through online questionnaires 
disposed of in laptops between April and May 2018. Considering 
the different variables included in the present study, the partici-
pants took a 2-­h test to assess not only mathematics and science 
competences, but also reading and global competences. After 
that, they completed the context questionnaire, which lasted 
about 1 h.
2.4   |   Data Analysis
The database provided by the INEE contained 1118 variables, 
90% of which were ordinal and nominal. To use the data in the 
regression process, each predictor variable was first examined 
to assess its behavior and check for balanced categories. It was 
found that in most of them there was an imbalance (e.g., if a 
variable contained four categories, many of them had very few 
responses compared to others). Therefore, a recategorization 
was performed considering the meaning of each category for 
that variable. Additionally, other variables with only two catego-
ries where more than 85% of answers were in a single category 
were identified and eliminated, since these variables would not 
contribute information during predictions (i.e., the model lacks 
diverse records for those variables with very few records for that 
information to undergo proper learning). Also, variables with 
≥ 70% empty records were eliminated.
Next, a Cramer's V was conducted, which helped identify vari-
ables that were highly correlated with each other, leading to the 
elimination of such variables. Subsequently, an analysis of vari-
ance (ANOVA) was used to determine the order of the variables 
for further testing. To prevent the model from being impacted 
by potential issues like multicollinearity, experiments were con-
ducted with different models using various sets of variables. It 
was observed how the predicted results varied between each of 
the experiments. For the target variables in the PISA database 
used, it was observed that there were 10 variables for each of the 
subjects (i.e., mathematics and science). When examining the 
explanation of each variable in Statistical Package for the Social 
Sciences (SPSS), they were only numbered without providing a 
more detailed explanation. Consequently, a decision was made 
to calculate the average of all these variables, resulting in a sin-
gle variable to predict for each competence.
After database curation, data mining was applied through 
three methods (i.e., RF, support vector regression [SVR], and 
ridge). The use of techniques such as RF has a number of ad-
vantages, such as the fact that their analyses are based solely on 
the data incorporated, without the need to start from previous 
specific models, and are especially designed to work with large 
databases; therefore, they are especially recommended when 
dealing with databases such as PISA (Gamazo and Martínez-­
Abad 2020). RF is a combination of decision tree predictors, so 
that each RF generated depends on the value of a random vec-
tor that also governs the growth of each tree. This algorithm 
is based on growing or generating a set of decision trees from 
the training data and allowing them to vote for the most pop-
ular class (Breiman 2001). Moreover, the use of RF has several 
advantages over even other classification algorithms, such as 
it avoids overfitting through the Law of Large Numbers, it is 
relatively robust to outliers and noise in the data, and the ac-
curacy achieved is as good as with similar machine learning 
techniques being faster (Breiman 2001). However, RF is not the 
only data mining technique; there are others of special interest 
for the educational field, such as SVR and ridge tests (see Zhang 
and O'Donnell 2020 for further development). Therefore, in this 
study all three methods have been employed.
To do so, hyperparameter tuning (i.e., parameters to which a 
certain value can be assigned to control model learning) was 
performed for each of the sets with the models to be used (RF 
and SVR) to obtain the best model result. From there, the RF 
classification model was first used for each of the sets. As indi-
cated in the introduction, the use of RF has several advantages 
over other prediction algorithms (e.g., it prevents over-­fitting 
by means of the Law of Large Numbers, relative robustness to 
outliers and noise in the data, and accuracy and speed of analy-
sis). Second, the SVR classifier was used to create a problem to 
optimize the learning of a regression function, which assigns 
predictor variables to the resulting observed values. This model 
helps to balance model complexity with prediction error, it can 
handle high-­dimensional data, and one of its advantages over 
other models is that it can project nonlinear regression prob-
lems to a hyperplane where the data can be linearly discrimi-
nated against (Zhang and O'Donnell 2020). It is also important 
to highlight that both mentioned models contain hyperparam-
eters. Therefore, it was necessary to perform an adjustment of 
these. This implied performing several runs of the model test-
ing different values for each of the hyperparameters and thus 
obtaining the best combination of values that helps us to reach

Finally, Ridge regression was employed, which is very popular 
because it helps with the collinearity problem in a multiple lin-
ear regression context. Unlike other models when trying to solve 
the collinearity problem, the model used does not eliminate any 
of the independent variables. This methodology works with a 
class of biased estimators that are indexed by a nonnegative sca-
lar parameter. The objective is to determine which of these esti-
mators to use in the context of the problem entered in the model 
(Zhang and O'Donnell 2020).
Since this was a quantifiable result, the variable selection tech-
nique used in all cases was ANOVA, since all the predictor 
variables were categorical, and the target variable was continu-
ous. With the aim of identifying the specific variables that were 
most influential in the different models for each competence, 
they were initially tested with the introduction of 20 variables, 
progressively increasing from 10 to 50; after that, it was subse-
quently tested on 80 and 100 variables to arrive at the 6 sets men-
tioned above (see Table 1). The most acceptable model from the 
statistical point of view was the 100-­variable model despite the 
loss of concreteness in it. It is possible to observe how that set 
for science with SVR and ridge models exceeded 60%, which is 
a very good result.
Considering the aforementioned, it is possible to affirm that RF 
use is not recommended for predicting mathematical and sci-
entific competences; since, in spite of not reaching more than 
60%, we can observe that the one with the lowest value is the 
RF. This occurs mainly in the sets of 100 and 80 variables, al-
though in the remaining sets we can observe a different behav-
ior. This might occur because the variables used in sets 100 and 
80 have a linear gap, which helps ridge, linear, and SVR models 
that use linear hyperplanes to predict, obtain the best results. 
It should be noted that for each model run, a cross-­validation 
of 10 folds was used to obtain a higher accuracy of the results 
(Figure 1).
Finally, to determine the main areas to which the 100 most 
influential variables in the performance of each of the com-
petences belong a second analysis was conducted with a cod-

derived variables according to the index established by PISA 
2018 (OECD 2020a). That codification was regrouped according 
to the derived variables that were noted in the different PISA 
2018 annexes report (OECD 2020a); in the annexes, it is feasi-
ble to check the rest of the classifications, the included items, 
and the definitions of the general categories used by PISA 2018. 
Regarding the items that were excluded from these classifica-
tions (n = 79 in both areas), a codification regarding the political 
action where the different variables were found was performed

in both cases, it helps to reach an acceptable level. Specifically, 
in scientific competence, the Ridge classifier (based on a linear 
model) reached the highest prediction percentage: 62.27%. The 
same classifier indicated a prediction of 56.10% in the case of 
mathematical competence. Among the three classifiers, the 
Ridge model shows the highest percentage of data prediction.
As previously indicated, the interest in identifying the 100 vari-
ables that best explain the AA for each competence lies in the 
ability to pinpoint the areas or domains where greater empha-
sis can be placed in educational improvement processes. The 
variables are ranked based on the number of variables included 
in each domain after the qualitative coding and classifications 
process. In the case of mathematical competence and according 
to the classification, it is possible to check that 32 out of 100 
variables that predicted AA in PISA tests referred to the access, 
use, teaching, interest, and ICT skills not only at schools, but 
also out of them. It is also remarkable to enhance the percent-
age reached by well-­being (n = 19) and supportive educational 
context (n = 17). The economic and cultural level of the parents 
has also a relevant impact (n = 8). The rest shows little influence 
in comparison to the aforementioned variables. These results 
can be seen in Table 2 (more detailed results can be found in 
Table B1).
In the case of scientific competence, despite the wide percentage 
of ICT, they only assume 17 out of 100 variables. Well-­being (20) 
and everything related to contextual support (18) showed the 
main influence of the selected variables. Metacognition (n = 7) 
has a relevant role in comparison with mathematical compe-
tence (n = 1). Socioeconomic and cultural status show a similar 
influence in science competence (n = 7). Last, it is important to 
highlight the variables related to mother tongue and reading 
(n = 16). These results can be seen in Table 3 (more detailed re-
sults can be found in Table B2).

The present research aimed to answer two questions regard-
ing the PISA 2018 test in Spain: (a) which variables are the best 
predictors of AA in STEM competences (i.e., mathematics and 
science)? and (b) Could these predictors be classified or orga-
nized into major areas of interest for STEM education? To ad-
dress these questions, data mining techniques of RF, SVR, and 
ridge were used to determine the variables, and these were qual-
itatively codified and classified by two researchers. Results in-
dicate that, in both competences, related ICT variables have a 
huge influence, especially in mathematical competence, where

These results should be interpreted in light of the review by Odell

conclusions with positive, negative and nonsignificant results

in the present study, although in the specific case of Spain the 
authors pointed to negative associations between AA in math-
ematics and science and the use of ICT at school. However, in 
Spain there is also previous evidence indicating that the use of 
ICT in the classroom is positively associated with AA in PISA 
2015 (Gamazo et al. 2018). Thus, this influence in PISA 2018 
should require a more precise and individualized study.
Both wellbeing and supportive context account for almost 40% of 
the variables identified, with a somewhat higher presence in sci-

the Spanish context, the data on well-­being do not include those 
referring to school climate, since this country did not participate 
in such parents' questionnaires (Spanish Ministry of Education 
and Vocational Training 2019), this finding is aligned with pre-
vious evidence showing that positive emotions were associated 
with higher scores in mathematics (Elizondo et al. 2018). To this 
end, intervention programs in the educational setting should 
also focus on improving emotional intelligence since this ap-
proach aims to promote greater emotional stability in students 
(Molina-­Muñoz et al. 2023). Regarding supportive educational 
context, recent systematic reviews indicate that educational 
centers (e.g., schools and high schools) are a crucial element 
in positive socialization for establishing and maintaining cul-

there (Cobo et al. 2019; Iglesias-­Díaz and Romero-­Pérez 2021). 
Literature also points out the importance of the sense of belong-
ing to the school center and its positive influence on science AA 
(Gil-­Madrona et al. 2019). However, despite its mediating role 
between the school context and AA, it is not sufficient when 
there are previous difficulties in AA (Dotterer and Lowe 2011).

Several other variables should be considered. For example, these 
results show how the economic and cultural level of parents has 
a very huge impact in both science and mathematics' AA. It is 
also evident in previous studies of an international nature in the 
case of economic level (Gamazo and Martínez-­Abad 2020) and 
in previous years in Spain, as it was the case of cultural capi-
tal in the PISA 2015 tests (Lozano and Trinidad 2019) and in 
2018 in mathematical competence for both genders (Molina-­
Muñoz et al. 2022). In this line, a recent meta-­analysis showed 
how socioeconomic status influences also the supportive ability 
that parents can offer to their children and the impact of such 
support on AA (Tan et al. 2020). It is, therefore, a variable that 
should always be considered when trying to identify influences 
on students' AA and, even more so, in massive studies such 
as PISA.
Regarding scientific competence, it is important to highlight the 
role of metacognitive variables, which appear almost to the same 
extent as in the case of economic and cultural levels. These re-
sults are consistent with the approach of the recent review by 
Willison et al. (2023) and it is useful to consider them according 
to their conclusions: These authors highlight that, if the use of 
knowledge and strategies linked to metacognition were facil-
itated in an expert and effective way, science learning in stu-
dents would improve. Nevertheless, this does require a deep and 
fundamental comprehension of metacognition, which may not 
be present in teachers. Perhaps this finding also implies consis-
tency with the fact that, for scientific competence, variables re-
lated to mother tongue and reading accounted for a considerable 
proportion of the predictors identified in the present study; this 
highlights the importance of reading comprehension and cul-
ture in dealing with scientific challenges nowadays.
4.1   |   Limitations and Strengths
One of the main limitations of the present study lies in its design, 
since it is a secondary analysis of data taken in a cross-­sectional 
study and, therefore, makes it impossible to reliably determine 
cause–effect relationships. However, this limitation is compen-
sated for by the large sample size and the reliability of the instru-
ments developed by the OECD (2020b, 2020a).
Regarding the statistical treatment, as indicated by Gamazo and 
Martínez-­Abad  (2020), although the advantage of using data 
mining lies in the production of exploratory studies to identify 
potentially significant relationships within large amounts of 
data, it certainly requires subsequent confirmatory studies to 
consolidate the findings. In the present study, although it was 
not possible to carry out confirmatory studies, an attempt was 
made at least to contrast the robustness of the analyses carried 
out from data mining by comparing the different techniques 
(i.e., RF, SVR, and ridge).
Data mining, as any other statistical analysis technique, also 
presents some weaknesses that must be considered, such as 
possible misinterpretations, information overload leading to the 
construction of highly complex systems of relationships, or the 
difficulty for education professionals to interpret the results of 
data mining (Gamazo and Martínez-­Abad 2020). In this sense, 
the limitation in the present study is shown in the need to have 
had to select up to 100 variables from those included in the PISA 
study to obtain predictive values appropriate to the statistical 
models in each of the competences. Similar higher prediction 
percentages with a smaller number of variables introduced 
would have made it possible to know even more precisely the 
predictors of AA in both competences. Nevertheless, efforts 
have been performed to clarify the interpretation of the results 
through coding and peer grouping among researchers, who con-
tacted the agency that makes the PISA data publicly available in 
Spain (i.e., the INEE) to confirm the rigor of the interpretation.
4.2   |   Future Outlook and Recommendations
Spain is one of the most segregated countries in Europe (Molina-­
Muñoz et al. 2022). School segregation varies among the com-
munities, with some having low levels and others, such as the 
Community of Madrid, having very high levels only surpassed 
by Hungary within the European Union (Murillo and Martínez-­
Garrido  2018). In this line, Fernández-­Gutiérrez et  al.  (2020) 
previously found that specific characteristics of some Spanish 
communities, such as the use of ICT, are positively related to 
PISA's outcomes in Science. Therefore, as suggested by Molina-­
Muñoz et al. (2022), it would be interesting to make a deeper 
analysis based on data mining in an internal study considering 
the different autonomous communities of the country.
On the other hand, it would be especially advisable to delve 
more deeply into the results regarding the use and competence 
of ICT, both at home and in the classroom, and their impact on 
academic results in STEM areas. In this sense, it has also been 
shown that science performance could benefit from the use of 
metacognitive strategies. Considering these results, proposals 
such as the AMERT (which stands for self-­Aware, self-­Monitor, 
self-­Evaluate, self-­Regulate, and self-­Transfer cognition) frame-
work (Willison et al. 2023) could help facilitate the transfer of 
these strategies, especially in STEM disciplines, by enhancing 
the self-­regulation of the learning process. This should be ad-
dressed at multiple levels, encompassing not only students but 
also teachers. It should be integrated into the curriculum along-
side initiatives that prioritize students' well-­being. For instance, 
intervention programs aimed at enhancing emotional intelli-
gence can be advantageous for both mathematical competence 
(Molina-­Muñoz et al. 2023) and scientific competence.
5   |   Conclusion
More than two decades of evaluations in education have already 
recorded the progress made in educational quality. Analyzing 
the effects of the social conditions of students is essential to un-
derstand the mechanisms that determine their level of learning. 
Moreover, using data mining and machine learning techniques 
enhances this analysis by uncovering deeper insights and 
patterns that traditional methods might miss. Through such 
techniques, combined with large databases such as PISA and 
theoretical knowledge, this research has aimed to properly

results are of major interest to the educational community, espe-
cially with the advent of artificial intelligence, and on the other 
hand, future analyses of the collected information should utilize 
the most cutting-­edge techniques available at the time to explore 
their potential and impact on the fields of public and social life.
