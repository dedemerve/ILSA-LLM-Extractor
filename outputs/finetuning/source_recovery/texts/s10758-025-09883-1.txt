Abstract
This study explores ChatGPT’s ability to solve reading skill questions from the PISA 
exams, an internationally recognized assessment designed to evaluate 15-year-old students’ 
reading, mathematics, and science competencies. 90 questions from the 2000 and 2009 
PISA reading assessments were administered to ChatGPT twice within one week. Ques-
tions were analyzed based on their text type (continuous, non-continuous, or multiple), 
question format (open-ended, multiple-choice, or short answer), and difficulty level defined 
by the PISA framework. The accuracy of ChatGPT’s responses was evaluated using the 
official answer keys provided by the OECD. Results indicate that ChatGPT achieved a 91% 
accuracy rate in both rounds. However, there were discrepancies in the questions answered 
incorrectly between the two rounds. ChatGPT performed better on continuous texts than 
non-continuous texts and achieved its highest accuracy rates on questions of lower diffi-
culty. The analysis also highlights inconsistencies in ChatGPT’s responses, particularly 
in handling non-continuous texts and higher-level questions based on Bloom’s taxonomy. 
While ChatGPT has limitations, such as occasional incorrect or inconsistent answers, it 
remains a valuable tool with the potential for educational integration, particularly in auto-
mating assessment processes. The study underscores the importance of understanding 
ChatGPT’s strengths and limitations to enhance its application in educational settings.

1  Introduction
In the twenty-first century, where technological advancements are progressing at a dizzy-
ing pace, radical innovations are becoming an almost daily occurrence. Among these, a 
revolutionary development has recently captivated the world: ChatGPT, an artificial intel-
ligence-based system. ChatGPT is a natural language processing model developed by Ope-
nAI, designed to generate human-like text responses based on context and prior interac-
tions (OpenAI, 2023). ChatGPT describes itself as follows: “I am ChatGPT, a sophisticated 
artificial intelligence language model developed by OpenAI. I can provide information on 
various topics, answer questions, and engage in conversation on various subjects” (Chat-
GPT, 2023). Since its debut in November 2022, ChatGPT has attracted significant attention 
from both the media and the scientific community (Baek & Kim, 2023; Sætra, 2023). One 
of the most striking indicators of its impact is that it amassed one million users within five 
days of its launch (Dynel, 2023).
Further evidence of its rapid rise comes from Dempere et  al. (2023), who analyzed 
Google search trends and found that ChatGPT is the most researched topic by a consider-
able margin. While many factors contribute to its widespread appeal, the most notable is 
its ability to generate fluent and swift human-like responses (Choi et al., 2023; Lee, 2023). 
ChatGPT can assist teachers, students, and researchers in empowering the educational pro-
cess (Dalalah & Dalalah, 2023). Thanks to its capacity to generate human-like responses 
to text inputs, it can provide tailor-made learning experiences, create educational content, 
grade, and even offer student counseling (Mahir Pradana and Syarifuddin, 2023). ChatGPT 
can promote learner autonomy and enhance learning experiences because it is customized 
to each student’s needs and preferences (Pereira et al., 2023). ChatGPT allows users to 
provide quick access to a variety of information, and in doing so, it is more functional than 
classic search engines because it presents the information needed in seconds instead of 
just a list of sources (Farrokhnia et al., 2023). Writing assignments, articles, stories, and 
marketing and advertising content are valuable tools for preparing research paper abstracts 
(Duong et al., 2023). Within the framework of scientific research, ChatGPT can identify 
research questions, address potential gaps, justify sample size, automatically generate 
visual elements, summarize data and the results of academic papers, and determine data 
analysis techniques (Peters et al., 2023). Writing the correct prompts to do these is the key 
to getting qualified answers from ChatGPT, and ChatGPT also provides the flexibility to do 
this in many languages (Lin, 2023).
However, reactions to ChatGPT have been paradoxical, with a divide between those 
embracing innovation and those responding with apocalyptic concerns reminiscent of 
the Terminator films (García-Peñalvo, 2023). The first is that The Future of Life Institute 
wants work on an AI system more potent than GPT-4 to be stopped immediately for at least 
6 months (Peters et al., 2023). The opposite development took place in Singapore, which 
is also at the top of the PISA exams, and the Singapore Ministry of Education has started 
a guidance activity for teachers in schools and higher education institutions on how to use 
these tools to improve learning, taking into account that artificial intelligence tools such as 
ChatGPT will become widespread over time (Abdullah, 2023). The Royal Melbourne Insti-
tute of Technology University has opened courses for the effective use of ChatGPT, while 
some universities in Hong Kong and France are known to ban the use of ChatGPT and, in 
some cases, impose harsh sanctions (Romero-Rodriguez et al., 2023). Similarly, schools in 
Los Angeles, Baltimore, Seattle, and New York have banned the use of ChatGPT due to 
cheating concerns in the US (Lambert & Stevens, 2023).

Going back a bit, the reactions to ChatGPT (Barrot, 2023) are like those given to 
every scientific innovation. For example, calculators such as ChatGPT have also caused 
societal controversy (Ellis & Slade, 2023). Moreover, what has not changed is that 
universities have a history of banning technologies that threaten students’ academic 
success, including computers, mobile phones, and internet access (Ajevski et  al., 
2023). When we look at today, we see that these technologies have become a part of 
our daily lives. From this point of view, it is possible to say that ChatGPT will go 
through similar phases. However, during this process, educators should know its capa-
bilities and limits. If it is seen as an opportunity and used as an educational tool, it can 
enrich the learning environment; but otherwise, it can lead to ethical problems (Arif 
et al., 2023; Grabb, 2023; Luo et al., 2024; Frick, 2024; Lin, 2023; Lim et al., 2023; 
Farazouli et al., 2023; Bin-Nashwan et al., 2023; Liu & Ma, 2023; Choi et al., 2023; 
Adilov et al., 2023; Perkins, 2023; Anderson, 2023; Kasneci et al., 2023). According 
to García-Peñalvo (2023), we are still at the dawn of a technology that is on its way 
to becoming a disruptive innovation, and denying or banning it will have absolutely 
no effect on stopping this tsunami effect that has already begun. It is important not to 
stand against the field of artificial intelligence, which is evolving daily, but to under-
stand how to work with it (Castonguay et al., 2023). This research aims to explore the 
capabilities and limits of ChatGPT. For this reason, the reading skills questions of the 
PISA test were asked ChatGPT.
2  Literature Review
2.1  PISA Exams
PISA (Programme for International Student Assessment) is an international exam 
launched by the OECD (Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development) in 
1997 and first implemented in 2000, which aims to evaluate education systems around 
the world by testing the skills and knowledge of 15-year-old students in participating 
countries (OECD, 2023). The PISA exam, which is not based on the national curricu-
lum, is held every three years (the exam was held in 2022 and was planned to be held 
only in 2021 during the pandemic period) and examines the reading, math, and science 
performance of 15-year-old students (Pulkkinen & Rautopuro, 2022).
In each PISA cycle, the three main cognitive domains (reading, math, and science) 
are assessed with a subject area tested in detail (Wang et al., 2023). 2000, 2009, and 
2018, reading and mathematics were in 2003, 2012, and 2022; In 2006 and 2015, sci-
ence was the main assessment area (OECD, 2017, 2023).
This research focuses on the questions in the PISA reading skills test. PISA assesses 
reading literacy instead of reading. Reading literacy is defined as “understanding, 
using, evaluating, reflecting on and engaging with texts to achieve one’s goals, develop 
one’s knowledge and potential, and participate in society” (OECD, 2019). PISA does 
not test students’ most basic reading skills; instead, it measures students’ ability to 
access and retrieve information, think about the text’s features and content, and inter-
pret it (OECD, 2017). So, reading skills tests are composed of various text types, and 
the difficulty level of the task ranges.

2.2  PISA Exams and ChatGPT
When the literature is examined, both PISA exams and ChatGPT may continue to be the 
center of attention in society and scientific literature in the coming years. Despite the posi-
tive outcomes and potential benefits of chatbots, there is no framework to guide educa-
tors in incorporating chatbots into their programs (Lin & Chang, 2023). Therefore, it is 
essential for educators to constantly experiment with this technology to integrate it into 
education and better understand how to use it (Fesenmaier & Wöber, 2023), knowing its 
capabilities and limitations. This leads to the necessity of testing ChatGPT’s knowledge 
at the academic level. Most research in this field is at the undergraduate or graduate level.

When the above studies are examined, it is seen that ChatGPT is successful in the exams 
that are the subject of the research, but it has disadvantages, such as providing incorrect 
information and explaining the questions it answers incorrectly in a logical way. In another 
study, ChatGPT was given the prompt to act like a 15-year-old student and asked to sum-
marize the reading comprehension text from the PISA tests, and the result was mixed 
between the summaries of 30 15-year-old students, and 30 teachers evaluated these texts

dent summaries, and ChatGPT significantly exceeds student performance in content and 
stylistic issues. The result is important in revealing the integration of ChatGPT into the 
learning environment from a creative and critical point of view. In this study, unlike the 
research in the literature, ChatGPT was asked questions selected from the field of reading 
skills in the PISA exam, and the researchers evaluated the answers. For this purpose, the 
research problems were created as follows:
1.	 Does ChatGPT’s performance differ according to PISA reading skills questions asked 
in different years?
2.	 Is ChatGPT’s performance affected by the content of PISA questions (continuous or 
non-continuous texts)?
3.	 Does ChatGPT’s performance vary depending on the type of PISA reading skills ques-
tions (open-ended, multiple choice)?
4.	 How is ChatGPT’s performance affected by the difficulty level of PISA reading skills 
questions determined by PISA?
5.	 Does ChatGPT’s performance differ according to the cognitive domain levels of Bloom’s 
taxonomy?
6.	 Is ChatGPT’s performance consistent regarding its answers to the same questions asked 
at different times?
7.	 How does ChatGPT perform in understanding and correctly answering PISA questions 
in reading comprehension?
3  Method
This web-based exploratory research, one of the qualitative research methods, aims to test 
the reliability of ChatGPT’s answers to the questions in the reading skills area of the PISA 
exam (Ali et al., 2024). This research aims to examine ChatGPT’s ability to solve the ques-
tions asked in the field of reading skills for the PISA exam, which is an international exam. 
The questions asked in the PISA exam are text-based and have graphic or visual content.

Since it is impossible to ask such questions to the ChatGPT 3.5 model, which is freely 
accessible to the public, the ChatGPT 4 model, which allows such questions to be solved, 
was preferred in the research. For this purpose, an account has been opened first so that 
ChatGPT can be used. After the account was opened, the questions determined by log-
ging into the account were asked to ChatGPT with copy–paste. No separate prompt has 
been entered for the questions. However, since some questions need to mark a place on 
the image and ChatGPT does not have this skill, a prompt was written in addition to these 
questions as “Can you describe….?”. The answers given by ChatGPT are directly quoted 
and recorded in a Word file with the date the transaction was made.

2000 and 2009. 45 questions were asked and published in 2000, and 45 questions pub-
lished from the application in 2009 were asked to ChatGPT. Some of the published ques-

In PISA 2000, a reading literacy scale based on the general average was developed to 
facilitate the interpretation of the scores given to students, and this average was 500 in 
2000, where the standard deviation was determined as 100 (OECD, 2010). Given that 
nearly two-thirds of pupils across OECD countries score between 400 and 600, a scale has 
been developed in which these three levels are then divided into five skill areas (Level 5 
corresponds to scores greater than 625, Level 4 to scores in the range of 553 to 625, Level 
3 corresponds to scores between 481 and 552, Level 2 corresponds to scores between 408 
and 480, and Level 1 corresponds to scores between 335 and 407) (OECD, 2002). For 
PISA 2009, based on the same classification, the scale was divided into seven skill areas 
and made more precise. Level 1b is the lowest level defined, followed by Level 1a, Level 2, 
Level 4, Level 5, and Level 6. At level 1b, it can complete tasks at that level and is unlikely 
to excel in higher-level skills. Level 6, the most challenging level in the field of reading

4  Research Design
This research examines ChatGPT’s performance in answering the questions asked in 
the PISA exam regarding reading skills from different perspectives. For this, a research 
design was made based on the research problems. First, the questions to be asked to 
ChatGPT were determined from the published questions. The PISA test is applied every 
three years. In each cycle, one primary domain is looked at in depth. These areas con-
sist of reading, mathematics, and science. Major domains were reading in 2000, math-
ematics in 2003, and science in 2006. In 2009 and 2018, the primary domain was read-
ing literacy (OECD, 2018). Applying reading skills as a predominant area has been an 
essential criterion in this selection. That is why the questions asked in 2000 and 2009 
were chosen. The questions asked in 2018 were not picked up because not all released 
questions were asked in the main PISA exam. For this reason, the questions published 
in 2000 and 2009 and asked in the exam were preferred. Secondly, the researchers ana-
lyzed these questions regarding text types, question types, and difficulty level, which 
were in line with the standards published by PISA. In the third stage, ChatGPT was 
asked questions using the copy–paste method, and the results were saved in another file. 
In the fourth stage, ChatGPT was logged in with another account, and the questions 
were asked again one week after the date they were first asked. The consistency of Chat-
GPT was tested. In the last stage, the researchers analyzed and reported the results using 
the answer key published by the OECD. In line with this plan, the flow in Fig. 4 below

4.1  Reproducibility of Results
To test the consistency of ChatGPT’s answers, ChatGPT was asked the same questions a 
second time, a week after the first transaction with a different account.
4.2  Analysis of Data
OECD publishes PISA questions by conducting a comprehensive analysis. It publishes 
a very detailed report that includes the type of text preferences used here, the achieve-
ment measured by the question, and the answer key of multiple-choice answers, as well as 
answers that can be accepted as correct, partially acceptable, and not at all acceptable for 
open-ended questions (OECD, 2002, 2009). This report was used to analyze ChatGPT’s 
responses. The researchers analyzed the responses given by ChatGPT, taking into account 
the sample answers in this report. After analyzing ChatGPT’s answers, descriptive analysis 
was adopted. The percentages showed the rate of correct and incorrect answers.

6  Discussion
This study evaluated ChatGPT’s ability to solve reading skills questions in the PISA exam. 
In this direction, 45 questions asked in the reading skills area of the PISA exam in 2000 
and 2009 were asked to ChatGPT. The results obtained are discussed below.
The overall performance of ChatGPT is over 90%, according to the research results. No 
studies in the literature investigate ChatGPT’s performance in PISA exams, but many stud-
ies examine its performance in medicine and related sciences. Qarajeh et al. (2023) found 
that ChatGPT has over 80% success in correctly classifying potassium content. Öztermeli 
and Öztermeli (2023) asked the questions of the medical specialization exam and found the 
success rate to be around 60%. Horiuchi et al. (2023) examined its performance in neurora-
diology and concluded that ChatGPT’s accurate detection rate is 50%, which is lower than 
other studies. These results show that ChatGPT has excellent potential for use in education.
Questions generated from continuous and non-continuous texts in the Pisa exams were 
asked to ChatGPT twice, one week apart. Interestingly, the number of errors ChatGPT 
made by text types remained the same in both cycles. According to the research results, 
there were more incorrect answers in the non-continuous text type than in the continuous 
text type. Similarly, Guerra et al. (2023) found that ChatGPT’s success in text-based ques-
tions was higher than in visual questions. This means that ChatGPT needs to update and 
improve in solving visual-based questions.
In the research, according to the five different types of questions asked to ChatGPT, 
it was determined that the accuracy rate of ChatGPT’s performance ranged from 91 to 
50% in the first round to 96–66% in the second round. On this subject, Ali et al. (2024) 
compared the performance of ChatGPT for different question types and concluded that it 
ranged between 70 and 100%. These results clearly show that ChatGPT does not show the 
same success in all question types.
The study analyzed the questions according to Pisa’s difficulty level and Bloom’s tax-
onomy. According to Pisa’s difficulty level, it was concluded that the highest accuracy rate 
(100%) in the first round was at levels 1–3–6, and the lowest accuracy rate (78%) was at 
level 5; in the second round, the highest accuracy rate (95%) was at levels 1–2–3, and the 
lowest accuracy rate (0%) was at level 6. According to Bloom taxonomy, it was concluded 
that the highest accuracy rate (100%) in the application step in the first round, the lowest 
accuracy rate (83%) was at the analysis step; in the second round, the highest accuracy rate 
(100%) was at the application step, and the lowest accuracy rate (75%) was again at the 
analysis step. Since ChatGPT’s answers are inconsistent, it is possible to say that although 
the accuracy rate in difficulty levels varies, the lowest accuracy rate is at level 4, according 
to Pisa, and at the analysis step, according to Bloom’s taxonomy.
A significant result of the research is that ChatGPT gives original answers to the same 
question, even if the answer remains the same. Similarly, Elkhatat (2023) found that

ChatGPT produced unique but compatible answers in his research. However, the possibil-
ity of giving inconsistent answers also arises here. Indeed, this situation occurred in the 
research, and it was observed that ChatGPT answered the questions it answered correctly in 
the first round incorrectly and answered the questions it answered incorrectly correctly. In 
support of this finding, it was found that ChatGPT sometimes gave contradictory answers, 
its answers were contradictory even when the same question was asked, and it did not con-
sistently perform the same (Stojanov, 2023). According to Sumbal et al. (2024), ChatGPT 
continues to learn and improve, and the time at which the study is conducted may affect the 
answers given by ChatGPT.
The research determined that ChatGPT shared irrelevant links while sharing links on 
the subject and mentioned some elements not included in the question. Chen et al. (2023) 
concluded in their research in the field of neurology that ChatGPT makes assumptions 
about information that is not included in the neurology exam. Similarly, Masters (2023) 
confirms ChatGPT that, although it seems accurate, it cites references and sources that do 
not exist. Ensuring the accuracy and reliability of the information provided by ChatGPT 
is an important issue, and it is difficult for students with limited knowledge to detect such 
errors (Lee, 2023).
In the research, it was also determined that ChatGPT has many advantages. Some of 
these features are that it produces human-like speeches and uses sincere and motivating 
language. In his research in Stojanov (2023), ChatGPT found that ChatGPT uses respectful 
language, is motivating, and supports his learning. An essential feature of ChatGPT is that 
it can generate effective and efficient responses quickly, and while doing this, it is always 
accessible. In their research on ChatGPT (2023), Klang et al. (2023) concluded that Chat-
GPT is swift and effective in terms of time in producing tests.
ChatGPT has entered the educational landscape, marking the onset of a significant 
transformation (Cooper, 2023). Furthermore, its potential to revolutionize teaching and 
learning processes is considerable (Rawas, 2023; Yang & Stivers, 2023). Recent studies 
have begun empirically exploring its contributions to student learning (Yan, 2023; Yılmaz 
& Yılmaz, 2023), expecting such research to proliferate shortly. The advantages and limi-
tations of this technology are also reflected in research findings and literature (Mohamed, 
2023; Vecchiarini & Somià, 2023). Balancing taking maximum advantage of ChatGPT’s 
positive aspects and potential concerns is crucial (Luo et al., 2024). Given these devel-
opments, it is reasonable to assert that despite its drawbacks, when used wisely (Mizu-
moto, 2023), ChatGPT will soon become an indispensable educational tool. Consequently, 
educators must develop a nuanced understanding of its strengths and weaknesses (Glaser, 
2023). Rather than dismissing, banning, or perceiving ChatGPT as a threat, efforts should 
be directed toward effectively integrating it into the educational process.
7  Conclusion
ChatGPT’s performance in solving questions in the field of PISA’s reading skills was 
examined in this study. For this purpose, 90 questions were asked to ChatGPT from the 
questions asked in 2000 and 2009. The results regarding text types, question types, and dif-
ficulty levels were analyzed. In addition, to test the consistency of ChatGPT, the questions 
were asked again at intervals of one week. The findings obtained in the study were com-
pared with the sources in the literature, and it was seen that the research results coincided 
with the data in the literature. As a result, with its limitations (which can be minimized

with current versions) and superior features, ChatGPT stands there as a valuable tool. The 
real question is: as an educator, will you close your eyes and pretend it does not exist, or 
will you look for ways to integrate it professionally into the education process?
8  Suggestions
This study evaluated ChatGPT’s ability to solve reading skills questions in the PISA exam. 
In light of the research findings, these recommendations have been developed: Given 
ChatGPT’s high level of accuracy, it can be leveraged as an educational teaching tool. 
Experimental studies can be carried out to explore its effectiveness in this context. Quan-
titative, qualitative, or mixed-method research can be conducted to examine ChatGPT’s 
ability to solve questions in mathematics and science for the PISA exam. A comparative 
analysis of ChatGPT’s performance can be made by asking questions about reading, sci-
ence, and mathematics for the PISA exam. A comparative analysis can be made by asking 
the questions asked in the PISA exam to different artificial intelligence tools (Bing, Bard, 
ChatGPT).
9  Limitations
In this study, a total of 90 questions asked and accessed in the reading skills area of the 
PISA exam in 2000 and 2009 were asked to ChatGPT. It is known that there are reading 
skills questions that are not accessible or that are asked in different years. For this reason, 
the limitation of the questions included in the research is one of the limitations.
