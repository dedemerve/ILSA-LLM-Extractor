Abstract—This study explores the possibility to improve 
students' math proficiency prediction with a multi-class neural 
network model by augmenting the training dataset with 
synthetic data. The original training dataset was based on the 
publicly released PISA 2012 computer-based database. Three 
math proficiency classes were established: low, mediocre and 
high. Minority class with the least number of samples was the 
high proficiency class. SMOTE, VAE and CTGAN methods 
were used to augment the minority class with additional data 
samples. G-mean was used as a performance measure for 
observing the enhancement to the prediction of the minority 
class. 
Keywords— multi-class, predictive model, computer-based 
assessment, data augmentation 
I. INTRODUCTION  
Application of machine learning on datasets to predict a 
specific outcome is nowadays a research topic of great 
interest. In the context of education a common subject of 
scientific studies is prediction of student performance or 
achievement. With the tight integration of information 
technologies into the learning process the learning 
environment has transitioned to a digital space, where data is 
generated after each interaction with the digital educational 
tools, whether they are online video courses, exercise tasks or 
tests and knowledge assessments. While there is an 
abundance of collected data, not all data is suitable for 
machine learning. As the machine learning datasets have to 
be carefully constructed and optimized for the models, it is 
actually often a challenge to obtain suitable data in the 
required volume to successfully train and validate a model. 
There are many techniques that can be applied to the models 
to compensate for inadequate training datasets. Lately data 
augmentation methods gained a lot of popularity, as they can 
generate synthetic data based on small sample sets. 
 
Programme for International Student Assessment 
(PISA) is an international large-scale study of 15-year-olds’ 
performance in reading, mathematics and science. It is 
administered triennially since 2000 by the Organisation for 
Economic 
Co-operation 
and 
Development 
(OECD). 
Databases of the conducted PISA assessments are publicly 
released. They consist of answers to student, parent and 
school questionnaires, offering a rich set of background 
variables, as well as the results of the knowledge and skill 
tests. These databases have been used in a number of data 
mining studies [1-7].  
 
The goal of this research was to compare the effect 
of Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique (SMOTE), 
Variational Encoder (VAE) and Generative Adversarial 
Network (GAN) on the math proficiency prediction in PISA 
2012 dataset. These data augmentation techniques are 
implemented and reported for various use cases. In [8] 
accuracy of different data augmentation techniques was 
compared. [9] reports credit card fraud detection by 
classification in which the small number of classes was 
augmented with synthetic data generated by GAN. In [10] 
VAE with deep neural network is used for network intrusion 
detection. Crash prediction models were compared using 
synthetic data in [11]. A joint VAE and GAN model is used 
to generate synthetic data of electrical load and photovoltaic 
production in a smart home dataset in [12]. XGBoost 
classifier was compared with multiple datasets and with 
multiple types of SMOTE techniques in [13]. Hybrid GAN 
has been developed in [14] to improve the predictive power 
of a recommendation system by avoiding imbalanced data. 
Experiments with the combination of GAN and SMOTE 
were conducted in [15]. A dual autoencoder GAN is 
proposed for fraud credit card detection in [16].  
 
While a wide number of research papers were 
published in the field of image data augmentation [17-22], 
there are less research papers reporting about tabular data 
augmentation. In [23] synthetic data generation with VAE 
and SMOTE is explored in a classification problem with 
tabular data of numerical and character types. In the field of 
educational data mining student performance prediction from 
data collected from surveys was conducted and reported in 
[24], where SMOTE was applied for data rebalancing. In an 
educational data mining study where uncovering factors 
influencing student progress was the goal, an initially small 
number of available data samples was synthetically increased 
with SMOTE [25]. 
 
In this paper the possibility to improve the math 
proficiency prediction in PISA with data augmentation is 
explored. A multi-class machine learning model was 
developed. In the assembled dataset three math proficiency 
classes were established. The class with the best performing 
students was the smallest by the number of samples. For the 
purpose of the minority class prediction, data augmentation 
was required. Three data augmentation methods were 
explored as a way to generate synthetic data samples for the 
deficient third class: SMOTE, VAE and GAN. Two variants 
of VAE and four variants of GAN were examined further to 
identify the data augmentation method which provided the 
best performance measure with the trained models.

II. EXPERIMENTAL DATASET 
As the target of the prediction the overall mathematics 
result was extracted from the PISA 2012 computer-based 
assessment database. Background variables were used from 
all three available questionnaires: students, parents and 
schools. The tabular dataset consisting of 17671 samples was 
split up into training, test and validation sets. 20% of the 
dataset was taken for testing, additional 20% was earmarked 
for validation in the predictive model, while the remaining 
samples were used for training. All columns in the tabular 
dataset have been normalized.  
In this research background variables which were 
selected by all of the feature selectors described in [26] were 
considered, as well, as the background variable ST29Q01, 
which was added manually. The background variables in the 
PISA questionnaire are of different types. In this research 
ordinal, interval and ratio types of data were used.  
The following background variables were used in the 
experiments: "Student math attitudes" - ST29Q01, "How 
many books at home" - ST28Q01, "Mathematics Efficacy" -

III. METHODS 
Students’ results of the math assessment were divided 
into three proficiency levels: low, medium and high. The 
third, highest achievement level is the least numerous, so the 
dataset is out of balance. Predictor models have difficulty 
handling unbalanced data. Data augmentation techniques are 
widely used in methods using deep learning when the amount 
of available data is insufficient, when the generalizability of 
the model needs to be improved, or when the dataset is 
imbalanced. Three types of synthetic data generators were 
explored in the research, SMOTE, VAE and GAN. The 
results of the test prediction were observed and the 
performance of models taught with the augmented dataset 
was compared with the performance of the models taught 
with the original dataset.  
SMOTE works with an approach in which the minority 
class is augmented by generating synthetic data rather than 
oversampling [27]. It relies on the feature vectors of the 
minority samples to obtain the new synthetic samples by 
finding the nearest neighbor of the observed sample, 
calculating the difference between the two vectors, 
multiplying it by a random number between 0 and 1, and 
then adding the result to the original sample. 
VAE was proposed to generate synthetic data [28]. Data 
generation with VAE is based on a machine learning 
approach. The algorithm learns the distribution of data and is 
able to create similar data based on feature distribution of the 
original data. New samples are generated in two steps. First, 
the original data passes through a neural network, this is the 
encoder part. The latent data appearing at the output of the 
encoder is then input to another neural network. The second 
neural network is the decoder, at the output of which the 
synthetic samples are obtained. During teaching, the loss 
function is minimized. The loss function consists of two 
parts, the first part being the difference between the samples 
at the output of the decoder and the samples at the input of 
the decoder, and the second part being the Kullback-Leibler 
divergence between the approximate later and true previous 
of the latent variable. VAE with latent space was also tested. 
GAN was proposed in [29]. The GAN consists of a 
combination of two models, in which the generative model 
generates data based on the distribution of the training 
dataset, while the discriminatory model estimates the 
probability whether a data sample is original and not 
synthetic generated by the generative model. During 
learning, the generative model should maximize the 
likelihood that the discriminatory model will fail. The 
generative model improves its ability to create patterns 
similar to the original, while the discriminatory model 
improves its ability to notice fake data. Two neural networks 
playing a competitive game result in better and better 
synthetic data. Based on the universal approximator theorem, 
the GAN must be able to generate dataset of any distribution 
[30]. 
Classification experiments were performed on GAN 
models with four different neuron configurations. GANs 
performance was measured using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov 
distance, as shown in (1). The KS distance between the real 
and synthetic data was measured to evaluate the artificial 
data. This distance is independent of the loss function and 
shows the largest difference between the cumulative 
distribution functions of the two datasets [30].

successfully overcomes difficulties such as non-Gaussian 
distribution and unbalanced input features. It works with 
both categorical and continuous variables. In the generator 
discrete columns are fed through two hidden layers with

Mean weighted precision and weighted recall values and 
precision and recall values for each class were measured in 
all models (Table V.).  
 
A high recall value indicates that the number of false 
negative classification is low, i.e., there are few samples in 
the third class that the model has mistakenly classified as a 
lower math performance class. A high precision value, on the 
other hand, indicates that the false positive value in the 
classification is low, i.e., there are few samples in the low-
performance class that the model incorrectly classified into 
the high-performance class. In the research, the change of 
recall and precision values were observed, when the size of 
the third class was increased for teaching purposes by 
synthetic data generation by different data augmentation 
techniques. 
 
In addition to recall and precision values, F1 score and 
Cohen kappa values for each class were observed (Table 
VII). The Cohen kappa score shows the match between the 
actual and the predicted values. A Cohen kappa value 
between 0.21 and 0.4 is considered as fair agreement. The 
metrics in the scikitlearn library were used for the 
calculations. 
 
The advantage of the G-mean metric is that it also takes 
into account how much the classification of the class from 
which there are few samples has improved, but also how 
much the classification of the majority class has deteriorated. 
G-mean is calculated as the square of the product of 
sensitivity and specificity (4).

V. CONCLUSION  
The objective of the study was to examine whether the 
neural network based model for predicting students’ math 
proficiency on the PISA assessment can be improved by 
training the model with a synthetic dataset. The original 
dataset was expanded with synthetic data generated using 
data augmentation techniques. Three math proficiency 
classes were identified in the dataset, where the high 
proficiency class had the least data samples by far. Thus, the 
goal of the data augmentation was to increase the number of 
data samples for the high proficiency class. 
Synthetic data generators based on the SMOTE, VAE 
and CTGAN methods were explored and used to create 
datasets for conducting experiments. One SMOTE, two VAE 
and four CTGAN variants were compared in order to identify 
the best synthetic data generator for the examined models. 
The results of the experiments showed that the synthetic 
datasets have enhanced the prediction performance of the 
model for the minority class. However, being a multi-class 
model, the prediction performance for the two majority 
classes has suffered deterioration. Still, the benefit of 
improving the minority class prediction can be considered as 
a worthy trade-off. 
In future research further experiments shall be performed 
with different CTGAN architectures, various synthetic data 
sets and neural network models.
