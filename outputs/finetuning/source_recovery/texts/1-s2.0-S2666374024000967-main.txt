A B S T R A C T
The purpose of the study is to investigate the predictive roles of intellectual curiosity and perspective-taking on 
science achievement among Emirati early adolescents. Utilizing data from 24,600 participants of the 2022 
Program for International Student Assessment (PISA), this non-experimental, correlational study employs a 
Structural Equation Modeling Partial Least Squares (SEM-PLS) approach to explore these relationships. The study 
addresses four key research questions: (1) the direct impact of intellectual curiosity on science achievement, (2) 
the direct impact of perspective-taking on science achievement, (3) the combined predictive power of intellectual 
curiosity and perspective-taking, and (4) the mediating role of perspective-taking in the relationship between 
intellectual curiosity and science achievement. Results reveal that intellectual curiosity significantly predicts 
science achievement with a path coefficient of 0.19. Perspective-taking also shows a substantial direct effect with 
a path coefficient of 0.15 and additionally mediates the relationship between intellectual curiosity and science 
achievement with an indirect path coefficient of 0.09. These findings demonstrate the significant influence of 
these cognitive skills on educational outcomes, suggesting that both intellectual curiosity and perspective-taking 
are crucial for enhancing science achievement. This underscores the importance of integrating educational 
strategies that foster these cognitive skills to improve science education outcomes. Recommendations for 
educational policy include enhancing curricula to foster curiosity and perspective-taking and developing as­
sessments that evaluate these competencies. The study highlights the need for further research using longitudinal 
designs and intervention studies to confirm these findings and refine educational practices.
Introduction
Intellectual curiosity and perspective-taking are crucial cognitive 
skills that significantly impact educational outcomes, especially in sci­
ence education. These intertwined processes enhance student engage­
ment, deepen understanding, and foster academic success. Curiosity, 
which involves a profound desire to learn and discover, pairs naturally 
with perspective-taking, the ability to consider and understand different 
viewpoints. Together, they enrich student interactions with educational 
content and contribute to a well-rounded learning experience (Cole 
Millett, Samuel, & Eacott, 2020; Singh & Manjaly, 2022).
In science education, curiosity is essential for driving the inquiry 
process, prompting students to investigate, formulate hypotheses, and 
seek solutions (Gottfried et al., 2016; Whitesides, 2018; Abu Khurma & 
El Zein, 2024). This drive not only encourages learners to engage with 
scientific questions but also supports sustained interest and engagement 
through the rigorous demands of scientific investigation (Litman, 2008; 
Zion & Sadeh, 2007). Research consistently demonstrates that curiosity 
aids in enhancing science learning by promoting an openness to explo­
ration and a critical approach to evidence evaluation (Casey, 2014; 
Spektor-Levy et al., 2013). Perspective-taking is equally critical, helping 
students grasp the complexities of scientific concepts by viewing issues 
through various lenses. This skill is vital in science, where understanding 
and integrating multiple perspectives are necessary to solve complex 
problems effectively (Cole et al., 2020). The synergy between curiosity 
and perspective-taking has been shown to substantially influence aca­
demic performance. Evidence suggests that high levels of these skills 
correlate with improved scientific literacy and greater overall academic 
success (Wulaningsih, Rahid, & Miarsyah, 2024; Jirout, 2020).
Despite abundant research on these individual elements, there is a 
distinct gap in comprehending how they interact to mediate science 
achievement, particularly among Emirati early adolescents. This study 
aims to explore how intellectual curiosity predicts science achievement 
and whether perspective-taking acts as a mediating factor within the

Literature review
Curiosity
The concept of curiosity has significantly evolved since Daniel Ellis 
Berlyne (1954) first categorized it into sensory exploration and 
knowledge-seeking activities. Debates have since emerged about 
whether curiosity is an inherent trait or a fleeting emotion (Weible & 
Zimmerman, 2016). Berlyne’s early research focused on the transient 
state of curiosity, spurred by encounters with unfamiliar or complex 
objects, which compel individuals to seek further information and 
integrate new understanding into existing knowledge frameworks 
(Derry, 1996). Piaget (1969) viewed curiosity as an innate urge to un­
derstand the unexpected (Loewenstein, 1994). Over time, the definition 
of curiosity expanded to include observable behaviors such as height­
ened attention, thoughtful analysis, and persistent engagement (Silvia, 
2005). This evolution led researchers like Kashdan et al. (2004) to 
develop scales measuring curiosity, defining highly curious individuals 
as those especially drawn to novel and challenging experiences.
Intellectual curiosity
Intellectual curiosity can be defined as a profound desire to acquire 
knowledge and understanding, driven by an intrinsic motivation to learn 
and explore new concepts. It plays a vital role in educational settings, 
particularly in enhancing scientific achievement (Weiss, 2024). Weiss 
highlighted the importance of intellectual curiosity in healthcare edu­
cation, suggesting that fostering curiosity is crucial for enabling students 
to learn effectively and adapt to new challenges. By employing strategies 
that promote an inquisitive mindset, educators can enhance students’ 
learning experiences and better prepare them for professional roles that 
require ongoing learning and innovation. Further supporting the impact 
of curiosity on educational outcomes, Wulaningsih, Rahid, and Miarsyah 
(2024) found a strong association between curiosity and higher levels of 
scientific literacy among high school students. This finding suggests that 
focusing on nurturing curiosity can significantly enhance students’ un­
derstanding and retention of scientific knowledge. Additionally, Singh 
and Manjaly (2022) emphasize the role of curiosity in preventing school 
dropout and enhancing academic performance, highlighting how 
curiosity-driven learning strategies can significantly improve educa­
tional outcomes.
Perspective-taking
In the field of educational psychology, perspective-taking is 
increasingly acknowledged as a crucial cognitive skill, particularly 
valuable in science education. This skill enables students to grasp the 
complexity of scientific concepts more fully by considering multiple 
viewpoints before forming their own conclusions. Perspective-taking not 
only deepens students’ understanding of material but also enhances 
their ability to engage with complex scientific data and theories, which 
may be interpreted differently based on individual perspectives. Cole 
et al. (2020)
argue for a robust theoretical framework for 
perspective-taking, emphasizing that it must consider the various 
cognitive elements that affect how students process and synthesize 
diverse viewpoints. Research by Wulaningsih, Rahid, and Miarsyah 
(2024) underscores the importance of perspective-taking, showing a 
strong correlation between it and scientific literacy, mediated by factors 
such as curiosity and reading comprehension. Their results indicate that 
students who effectively adopt multiple perspectives and are motivated 
by curiosity tend to excel in science. Further, Jirout (2020) suggests that 
perspective-taking facilitates deeper scientific understanding by pro­
moting information-seeking behaviors and encouraging students to link 
disparate pieces of information through inquiry-based learning. More­
over, engaging with peers’ viewpoints is crucial for developing a 
comprehensive understanding of scientific problems. This collaborative 
approach not only improves individual comprehension but also fosters a 
learning environment where diverse scientific ideas and disciplines 
converge, leading to richer educational outcomes. Singh and Manjaly 
(2022) highlight that curiosity-driven strategies, which encourage 
viewing topics from various angles, can decrease dropout rates and 
boost academic performance. These strategies advocate for a shift in 
classroom dynamics, where information is presented and discussed in 
ways that encourage multiple perspectives, thus enhancing the learning 
experience without necessitating additional resources.
Curiosity and perspective-taking
Curiosity and perspective-taking are deeply interconnected cognitive 
skills that are pivotal in enhancing the educational experiences of stu­
dents, particularly in science education. Curiosity, which is the innate 
desire to learn and discover, naturally complements perspective-taking, 
the ability to understand and consider diverse viewpoints. These skills 
together not only enrich student engagement but also deepen their un­
derstanding of complex scientific concepts. Cole Millett, Samuel, and 
Eacott (2020) discuss how perspective-taking can enrich educational 
experiences by allowing students to consider various viewpoints and 
underlying assumptions, thus deepening their understanding and stim­
ulating their curiosity. This in turn encourages students to explore 
further the complexities of scientific issues. Singh and Manjaly (2022)
expand on this by explaining how curiosity can be ignited by exposure to 
different ways of thinking.
Curiosity and science achievement
Intellectual curiosity, a profound desire to acquire knowledge driven 
by intrinsic motivation, plays a critical role in enhancing educational 
outcomes, especially in scientific domains. Weiss (2024) underlines its 
significance in healthcare education, stressing that cultivating curiosity 
is crucial for students to adapt effectively to new challenges and for 
continuous professional growth. In science education, curiosity propels 
learners to actively engage with complex problems and explore new 
solutions, significantly enriching the learning process (Litman, 2008).
Liliasari (2011) identifies curiosity as one of the key factors that 
influence the learning of science, together with beliefs, reasoning skills, 
and an understanding of causal relationships. This curiosity drives stu­
dents to forge connections between scientific concepts, aiding effective 
understanding and interpretation of phenomena. Oudeyer et al. (2016)
and Lindholm (2018) further emphasize that nurturing a robust sense of 
curiosity in students can serve as a catalyst for innovation, encouraging 
them to delve deeper into content, ask critical questions, and engage in 
problem-solving (Casey, 2014; Wang, 2014; De Villiers, 2020).
Curiosity not only fosters active learning but also enhances the 
development of crucial scientific inquiry skills. According to Gottfried 
et al. (2016) and Whitesides (2018), the development of questioning 
skills through curiosity enables students to validate assumptions and 
formulate hypotheses, essential for conducting thorough scientific in­
vestigations. Zion & Sadeh (2007) and Casey (2014) highlight how such 
inquiry-based learning activities are instrumental in drawing scientifi­
cally valid conclusions based on empirical evidence, thus strengthening 
the link between curiosity and effective learning strategies.
Beyond educational environments, the benefits of curiosity extend to 
broader academic achievements. Silvia (2005; 2008) and Zuss (2011)
note that curiosity enhances students’ ability to identify patterns and 
retain information, fostering a deeper engagement with learning mate­
rials and an appreciation for more nuanced viewpoints. Moreover, 
studies show that children who exhibit higher levels of curiosity not only

The relationship between curiosity and educational performance has 
been explored through various empirical studies. Powell and Nettelbeck 
(2014) examined the correlation between intellectual curiosity, fluid 
intelligence, and conscientiousness in university students, finding that 
while all these factors were associated with academic performance, 
Typical Intellectual Engagement particularly added predictive value. 
Similarly, Schroeders et al. (2015) assessed the impact of Typical In­
tellectual Engagement on ninth graders’ math and science performance, 
observing its initial enhancement effects, which, however, diminished 
when fluid intelligence and interest were accounted for.
Evans et al. (2023) conducted a study focusing on how instructional 
practices that either promote or suppress curiosity affect preschoolers in 
math and science, noting that curiosity suppression negatively impacts 
children’s engagement, while curiosity promotion showed no significant 
correlation with better outcomes. In contrast, Tang and Salmela-Aro 
(2021) investigated high school students, distinguishing between 
interest-type and deprivation-type curiosity. Their findings suggest that 
interest-type curiosity directly enhances performance in matriculation 
exams, while deprivation-type curiosity has an indirect influence 
mediated by subject motivation. Further exploring curiosity’s role in 
education, Lee et al. (2022) studied the use of concept maps as advance 
organizers within a sixth-grade virtual reality science curriculum. Their 
findings indicate that such tools not only improve students’ test per­
formance but also promote higher emotional engagement, facilitated by 
interest-type curiosity and active learning.
Perspective-taking and science achievement
The relationship between students’ perspective-taking abilities and 
their science achievement is not immediately obvious, yet evidence 
suggests a valuable connection through the promotion of divergent 
thinking. Divergent thinking—a cognitive process that generates crea­
tive ideas by exploring various solutions—is integral to perspective- 
taking, as it involves considering multiple aspects of a problem. Cole 
Millett, Samuel, and Eacott (2020) highlight the complexity of 
perspective-taking, arguing that mastering this skill could substantially 
improve educational outcomes, particularly in science education. They 
emphasize the integration of perspective-taking with other cognitive 
processes, such as mental imagery, which aids students in visualizing 
and tackling scientific problems from diverse angles. Furthermore, 
Singh and Manjaly (2022) connect perspective-taking to enhanced cu­
riosity and intellectual engagement, crucial drivers of academic success 
in science. They demonstrate how curiosity, fueled by the desire to 
address information gaps and comprehend complex phenomena from 
various perspectives, significantly boosts students’ motivation and 
educational achievements. Research by Jirout (2020) also supports the 
idea that curiosity, coupled with perspective-taking, can deepen stu­
dents’ understanding and retention of scientific knowledge. By moti­
vating students to investigate and question from multiple viewpoints, 
educators can create a learning environment conducive to profound and 
meaningful engagement with science. This approach not only encour­
ages a deeper grasp of scientific concepts but also prepares students for 
success by fostering critical thinking and problem-solving skills.
The present study
The significance of the current study on the relationship between 
intellectual curiosity, perspective-taking, and science achievement 
among Emirati early adolescents lies in addressing a notable gap in the 
literature. Previous research has extensively explored the impact of 
curiosity on educational outcomes and how curiosity can foster 
engagement and retention in academic settings. However, there is 
limited research directly connecting students’ perspective-taking capa­
bilities with their levels of curiosity and subsequent science 
achievement. This gap is particularly evident in the context of educa­
tional systems like that of the UAE, where cultural factors might 
uniquely influence cognitive and educational processes.
Research questions
The study aims to elucidate the extent to which intellectual curiosity 
can predict science achievement and how perspective-taking may serve 
as a mediating factor in this relationship. This is explored through the 
following research questions: 
1. How well does Intellectual Curiosity predict Science achievement in 
Emirati early adolescents?
2. How well does Perspective-taking predict Science achievement in 
Emirati early adolescents?
3. To what extent does Perspective-taking mediate the relationship 
between Intellectual Curiosity and Science achievement in Emirati 
early adolescents?
Methodology
The study "Intellectual Curiosity, Perspective-taking, and Science 
Achievement: Examining the Mediating Pathways among UAE Students" 
involves a comprehensive analysis of data from 24,600 UAE students 
who participated in the 2022 Program for International Student 
Assessment (PISA). The focus is on evaluating educational outcomes and 
cognitive behaviors to understand the intricate relationships impacting 
science achievement. To ensure the integrity and quality of the data, the 
initial dataset underwent rigorous screening, including outlier detection 
methods that refined the sample size to 21,047 student responses. This 
meticulous approach guarantees a robust dataset for detailed analysis. 
The data analysis was conducted using SmartPLS 4 software, which 
supports the Structural Equation Modeling Partial Least Squares (SEM- 
PLS) approach. This method allows for precise estimation of both 
measurement and structural models, facilitating a thorough examina­
tion of the constructs involved. The reliability and validity of these 
constructs were rigorously tested. This involved assessments of indicator 
reliability, internal consistency, convergent validity, and discriminant 
validity, ensuring that the measures used were both reliable and valid. 
For the structural model, path analysis was performed using the boot­
strapping technique, which included 5000 resamples. This robust sta­
tistical method was employed to ascertain stable path coefficients and to 
explore the mediation effects among the constructs. Such an approach is 
crucial for capturing the complex interrelations and providing deep 
understanding into how cognitive processes influence the academic 
performance of Emirati students in science (Wang & Wang, 2023).
Research design
The research design for the study is a non-experimental, correla­
tional design utilizing secondary data from the 2022 Program for In­
ternational Student Assessment (PISA). This design is appropriate for 
investigating the proposed relationships between intellectual curiosity, 
perspective-taking, and science achievement without manipulating the 
environment or conditions of the participants. The structural model, as 
depicted in (Fig. 1), shows intellectual curiosity as an independent 
variable, perspective-taking as a mediator, and science achievement as 
the dependent variable.

the observed data and the pattern of missingness to estimate the most 
likely values for the missing data.

involved screening the data for both univariate and multivariate out­
liers. This meticulous scrutiny was essential to ensure the integrity and 
reliability of the dataset for further analysis. Out of a total dataset size of 
N=24,600, the screening identified 2328 univariate outliers and 762 
multivariate outliers. Consequently, this process refined the dataset, 
leaving 21,510 cases that were deemed valid and suitable for compre­
hensive analysis (Tables 1, 2 and 4).

R-square
The R-square values for Perspective-taking and Science Achievement 
constructs are 0.35 and 0.09, respectively. The R-square of 0.35 for 
Perspective-taking suggests that 35 % of the variance in the dependent 
variable can be explained by the independent variables associated with 
this construct. This is a moderate level of explanatory power, indicating 
that the model has a reasonable fit for the data within the Perspective- 
taking construct. For the Science Achievement construct, however, the 
R-square value is just 0.09, implying that only 9 % of the variance in the 
dependent variable is explained by the model.
Reliability and validity
Table 5 presents metrics indicative of both reliability—through 
Cronbach’s alpha and composite reliability (rho_a and rho_c)—and 
validity, via the Average Variance Extracted (AVE). The Perspective- 
taking construct exhibits a high level of internal consistency with a 
Cronbach’s alpha of 0.93 and similarly high composite reliability scores, 
suggesting that the items within this construct are well-correlated and 
reliable. Its AVE of 0.73 also indicates a good level of convergent val­
idity, as it captures a substantial proportion of the variance in the 
observed variables. Science Achievement scores even higher in terms of 
reliability (0.98 across Cronbach’s alpha and both rho metrics) and 
validity, with an AVE of 0.86, showcasing an excellent consistency 
among its items and strong evidence of validity. Intellectual Curiosity 
also demonstrates strong reliability (with scores around 0.94–0.95) and 
a satisfactory level of validity with an AVE of 0.69.

Table 7 outlines the Variance Inflation Factors (VIF) values for 
Perspective-taking, Science Achievement, and Intellectual Curiosity 
within an SEM framework. A VIF of 1 indicates no correlation between 
the independent variable and any other independent variables in the 
model, suggesting that Intellectual Curiosity exhibits no multi­

and Intellectual Curiosity both have a VIF of 1.54 when predicting Sci­

Findings
The analysis of the structural model provides the following results to 
the research questions: 
1. How well does Intellectual Curiosity predict Science achievement 
in Emirati early adolescents?
Intellectual Curiosity plays a substantial role in predicting Science 
Achievement among Emirati early adolescents, as evidenced by the data 
from the structural model analysis. The path coefficient (β) from Intel­
lectual Curiosity directly to Science Achievement is 0.19, indicating a 
significant direct influence. This relationship is further underscored by a 
high T statistic of 22.57 and a P value of 0, demonstrating that the effect 
is not only statistically significant but also robust. Intellectual Curi­
osity’s impact is fundamentally important in the context of academic 
performance, as it reflects a deep, intrinsic motivation to learn and 
understand new concepts, a trait crucial for success in science education. 
Further analysis reveals that Intellectual Curiosity’s total effect on Sci­
ence Achievement, which combines both direct and mediated impacts 
through Perspective-taking, stands at 0.28. This significant increase, 
supported by a T statistic of 42.96, shows that when Intellectual Curi­
osity is aligned with effective perspective-taking strategies, its influence 
on educational outcomes is amplified. 
2. How well does Perspective-taking predict Science Achievement in 
Emirati early adolescents?
Perspective-taking has a moderate but significant predictive power 
on Science Achievement in Emirati early adolescents, as indicated by a 
path coefficient (β) of 0.15. This effect, with a T statistic of 18.98 and a P 
value of 0, confirms that the ability to understand and consider multiple 
viewpoints is crucial in science education. This cognitive skill facilitates 
a deeper comprehension of complex scientific concepts, which often 
require the integration of various theoretical and practical perspectives 
(Ramírez-Montoya et al., 2022; Kruger et al., 2024). The significance of 
perspective-taking in education stems from its role in enhancing critical 
thinking and problem-solving skills, which are essential for navigating 
the challenges of scientific inquiry (Southworth, 2022).

between Intellectual Curiosity and Science Achievement in Emirati 
early adolescents?
Perspective-taking significantly mediates the relationship between 
Intellectual Curiosity and Science Achievement, as demonstrated by the 
indirect path coefficient of 0.09. This mediation is statistically robust, 
supported by a T statistic of 18.69 and a P value of 0. This finding in­
dicates that Intellectual Curiosity contributes to Science Achievement 
not only directly but also by enhancing Perspective-taking abilities, 
which in turn positively affects academic performance in science. The

This exploration delves into how intellectual perspective-taking and

and science learning. Intellectual perspective-taking, the ability to 
comprehend and consider diverse viewpoints, concepts, or theories, is 
crucial for students to critically appreciate multiple scientific theories, 
understand conflicting evidence, and evaluate various explanations. 
This cognitive skill is integral to higher-order thinking skills like critical 
thinking and problem-solving, essential for thriving in science educa­
tion. Researchers suggest that perspective-taking boosts cognitive flex­
ibility, facilitating the ability to shift between different conceptual 
frameworks or theories with ease. Such flexibility is vital in science, 
where new evidence can dramatically alter understanding (Kuhn et al., 
2000). Additionally, students proficient in perspective-taking are more 
adept at engaging in argumentation and scientific reasoning, funda­
mental elements of science education (Mercier & Sperber, 2011). On the 
other hand, intellectual curiosity—the intrinsic desire to seek new 
knowledge and understanding—propels learners to explore novel topics 
and deepen their scientific comprehension. This trait is directly associ­
ated with academic performance, particularly in science, where the 
motivation to engage deeply with complex and counterintuitive content 
is critical (von Stumm et al., 2011). Curiosity fosters increased 
engagement and time investment in learning activities, which enhances 
comprehension and retention of scientific information. This drive for 
exploration is linked to positive outcomes in STEM fields, encouraging 
deeper understanding and fostering innovation (Kashdan & Yuen, 
2007).
Importantly, the influence of intellectual curiosity on science 
achievement may be mediated by perspective-taking. Curiosity en­
courages students to explore and understand various theories and 
viewpoints, while perspective-taking equips them with the cognitive 
framework necessary to process and synthesize these diverse inputs. 
Research indicates that curiosity prompts students to engage in 
perspective-taking more frequently, enhancing their comprehension and 
ability to apply knowledge flexibly across different contexts—a crucial 
component of science achievement (Litman, 2005). Moreover, 
perspective-taking facilitates deeper learning by enabling students to 
connect disparate pieces of information, vital for constructing compre­
hensive scientific knowledge and understanding complex systems 
(Davidesco et al., 2019).
Integration of findings with literature review
The findings from the structural model analysis that Intellectual 
Curiosity has a significant direct effect on Science Achievement among 
Emirati early adolescents align well with existing literature. According 
to Weiss (2024) mentioned in the literature review, Intellectual Curi­
osity plays a vital role in enhancing scientific achievement, driven by an 
intrinsic motivation to learn and explore new concepts (Shaljan Aree­
pattamannil et al., 2023). This notion is further reinforced by the review 
where curiosity is described as a cornerstone in educational environ­
ments, significantly boosting scientific achievement by promoting an 
openness to exploration and critical evaluation of evidence (Casey, 
2014; Spektor-Levy et al., 2013). The synthesis of these sources with the 
empirical data underscores the crucial role of nurturing an inquisitive 
mindset to enhance students’ learning experiences and academic out­
comes in science. The moderate but significant predictive power of 
Perspective-taking on Science Achievement, as found in the structural 
analysis, is consistent with the assertions in the literature review. Cole 
et al. (2020) emphasize the importance of perspective-taking in science 
education for understanding and integrating multiple perspectives to 
solve complex problems effectively. This skill is highlighted as critical 
for helping students grasp the complexities of scientific concepts by 
viewing issues through various lenses, thus enhancing their critical 
thinking and problem-solving skills. The findings suggest that fostering 
perspective-taking in educational practices can significantly enhance 
students’ comprehension and engagement, leading to improved aca­
demic performance in science.
The combined effect of Intellectual Curiosity and Perspective-taking 
on predicting Science Achievement, as demonstrated by the structural 
model, is well-supported by the literature. The synergy between these 
cognitive skills enriches the educational experience, facilitating deeper 
engagement and understanding of scientific concepts (Cole Millett, 
Samuel, & Eacott, 2020; Singh & Manjaly, 2022). This interplay is 
particularly effective in fostering a comprehensive educational envi­
ronment where curiosity and perspective-taking together enhance sci­
entific literacy and overall academic success, as noted by Wulaningsih, 
Rahid, & Miarsyah (2024) and Jirout (2020). The literature supports the 
empirical evidence that these interconnected skills are pivotal in

Conclusion
This research underscores the pivotal role of intellectual perspective- 
taking in mediating the relationship between intellectual curiosity and 
science achievement. Openness to diverse ideas enhances critical 
thinking by enabling students to assess information from various sources 
in light of their initial inquiries. This process not only boosts their 
analytical abilities but also deepens their scientific understanding. Stu­
dents can juxtapose their curiosity-driven questions against established 
scientific knowledge shared by peers or instructors, fostering rigorous 
analysis of data, identification of argumentative weaknesses, and a so­
lidified grasp of scientific concepts. Moreover, embracing multiple 
perspectives helps mitigate cognitive biases that often impede scientific 
inquiry, paving the way for a more balanced and objective interpretation 
of scientific data. By engaging with a range of perspectives, students 
challenge their preconceived notions, which promotes a more impartial 
approach and encourages consideration of alternative explanations. This 
leads to well-supported, evidence-based scientific conclusions. Engage­
ment with diverse ideas refines students’ curiosity, significantly 
bolstering their scientific achievement. This study provides robust evi­
dence that intellectual curiosity and perspective-taking are essential 
cognitive skills that profoundly influence science achievement among 
Emirati early adolescents. The findings reveal that intellectual curiosity 
directly impacts science achievement substantially, while perspective- 
taking influences and mediates this relationship, highlighting its dual 
role in enhancing scientific learning.
Implications
The study highlights several implications for educational policy­
makers and practitioners in the UAE, emphasizing a comprehensive 
approach to enhancing science education. Firstly, there is a significant 
need to integrate modules and activities that foster curiosity and 
perspective-taking within the science curriculum through project-based, 
inquiry-based, and problem-based learning approaches. Such methods 
encourage deep engagement with scientific concepts and can dramati­
cally improve students’ understanding and enthusiasm. Secondly, there 
is a crucial demand for professional development programs that provide 
educators with strategies and tools to nurture curiosity and perspective- 
taking, aiming to create an engaging and inclusive classroom environ­
ment. Furthermore, the development and implementation of innovative 
assessment strategies are recommended to evaluate not just knowledge 
but also the application of perspective-taking and curiosity in problem- 
solving, offering valuable perspective into the effectiveness of educa­
tional interventions. Lastly, increased parental involvement through

Study limitations and future studies
The study’s cross-sectional design presents limitations in establishing 
causality between Intellectual Curiosity, Perspective-taking, and Science 
Achievement, as longitudinal data would provide a clearer picture of 
how these relationships develop over time. Additionally, the focus on 
Emirati early adolescents may limit the generalizability to other cultural 
or educational contexts. Future research should consider longitudinal 
studies to track the developmental trajectories of these skills and 
intervention studies to assess the effectiveness of pedagogical ap­
proaches that enhance curiosity and perspective-taking. Comparative 
cross-cultural studies could also explore the universality of the results 
and identify cultural factors influencing these skills.
