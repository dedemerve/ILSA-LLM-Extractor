High-quality math instruction is essential for fostering students’ critical thinking and problem-solving skills. This
study explores the potential of inquiry-based pedagogy on student math attitude and performance in Vietnam,
leveraging the 2022 Programme for International Student Assessment dataset comprising 5711 students. Using
structural equation modelling, findings show that inquiry-based pedagogy significantly enhances math attitude
(std.β=0.42, p<0.001) and math performance (std.β=0.08, p<0.001). Math attitude positively and partially
mediates the relationship between inquiry-based pedagogy and math performance (std.β=0.07, p<0.001). Disaggregated analysis by region, school type, and geographic location further highlights the significance of
instructional pedagogy in improving instructional outcomes across contexts.

1. Introduction
Finding ways to improve students’ math performance has been a
central priority for educators worldwide, and Vietnam is no exception to
this commitment. The Vietnamese government’s policy emphasizes the
development of a knowledge-based society, positioning education as a
vital driver for socio-economic development and innovation (Tran and
Nguyen, 2021). This strong commitment has catalyzed significant
educational reforms, including training math teachers, STEM integration, and Realistic Mathematics Education (RME), focusing on
improving pedagogical approaches to elevate instructional quality (Tran
et al., 2022). Traditionally, the Vietnamese education system has relied
on more conventional forms of teaching methods, which were regarded
less-compatible with demands for cultivating creativity and critical
thinking skills in the 21st century. In response to these challenges, the
Ministry of Education and Training (MOET) advocated for a national
transition towards more interactive and student-centered teaching
methods. However, despite these reforms, Vietnam’s performance on
international large-scale student assessments, has seen a decline in math
performance ranking from 17th in 2012 to ranking 31st in 2022 (OECD,
2022), raising concerns about the effectiveness of these reforms.
Inquiry-based pedagogy, rooted historically in the principles of Socratic questioning, extends the long-standing educational philosophy of
improving reasoning and dialogue by encouraging student-driven

exploration and providing opportunities for hands-on experiences and
independent problem-solving (Akinde, 2015). As an instructional
model, it emphasizes active engagement, critical thinking, and a deeper
understanding of content through collaborative group work and discovery learning (Oliver et al., 2021). Inquiry-based pedagogy has gained
considerable attention in mathematics education as an effective method
for bolstering students’ cognitive development and problem-solving
skills (Yu and Singh, 2018). Relatedly, inquiry-based pedagogy is
well-suited for secondary students aged 11–15, as their developmental
stage enhances abstract reasoning and problem-solving, making it
practical for fostering critical thinking skills (Liu et al., 2021). Research
has consistently shown that this pedagogy boosts engagement and academic performance and creates an inclusive classroom environment that
supports long-term personal and intellectual growth (Yang et al., 2024).
Additionally, inquiry-based pedagogy is economically advantageous,
potentially reducing the need for remedial instruction and lowering
educational costs by addressing learning gaps early (Abdulrahim and
Orosco, 2020). Grounded in Constructivist Learning Theory, through
these methods students can better grasp knowledge through active
participation and inquiry, aligning inquiry-based learning with the core
tenets of constructivism to foster deeper cognitive engagement and
comprehensive understanding of mathematical concepts (Bada and
Olusegun, 2015). In Vietnamese math education, reforms like using
Realistic Mathematics Education (RME) encourage students to solve

Empirical investigations into the determinants of student performance in mathematics have identified many influential factors, with
student’s attitude towards the subject emerging as a particularly salient
predictor (Hu et al., 2024). For instance, when students persistently try
to comprehend what is taught in mathematics class, they engage in
effortful learning, which would buttress their mathematics capability,
improving their cognitive development (Keller et al., 2024). This phenomenon stems from the resilience contingent on mathematics interest,
allowing students to navigate cognitive challenges with greater perseverance and adaptability. This observed correlation demonstrates that
students who view mathematics as engaging or rewarding are more
inclined to put in necessary effort to understand complex concepts,
reinforcing both their mathematical skills and their self-confidence in
the subject (Feng et al., 2024; Hidayatullah and Csíkos, 2024). Consequently, the focus on reinforcing students’ constructive attitudes toward
math can act as a motivating drive that encourages consistent learning
habits, ultimately contributing to robust overall academic performance
in mathematics (Auld et al., 2019).
Although prior studies have highlighted the benefits of inquiry-based
pedagogy on student performance (Liu et al., 2024), there is limited
research exploring this approach in the Vietnamese educational context.
This study seeks to address this gap by examining effects of
inquiry-based pedagogy on teaching and learning in mathematics,
focusing on its influence on student outcomes. Importantly, it also investigates the mediating role of students’ attitudes toward mathematics,
which has not been thoroughly explored in previous research. This study
aims to determine how these factors contribute to math achievement
among Vietnamese teen learners by analysing various interplays. This
study employs a structural modelling approach to assess the relationships among inquiry-based pedagogy, student attitudes, and mathematical performance, emphasising attitudes’ mediating role in linking
instructional practices to academic success. In doing so, the study seeks
to provide insights into the potential of inquiry-based pedagogy to
enhance educational outcomes in Vietnam.

2.2. Unpacking the key role of math attitude
Research consistently indicates a positive relationship between students’ attitudes towards subjects and academic performance, with
mathematics being no exception (Berger et al., 2020; Kiwanuka et al.,
2022). Understanding factors that influence student achievement is
crucial, particularly as educational strategies in schools increasingly
align with global development goals (Kyriakides et al., 2018). Students
who approach mathematics with a positive mindset often exhibit greater
academic efficacy, as enjoyment and interest in the subject serve as
significant drivers of achievement (Gjicali and Lipnevich, 2021).
Particularly, math attitude is multi-dimensional, involving students’
emotions toward mathematics, beliefs about their capabilities, and
behavioural tendencies in engaging with math-related tasks (Kiwanuka
et al., 2015). Key components include self-confidence in math skills,
interests in math, resilience toward completing math tasks, and enjoyment from math activities (Dowker et al., 2019). These attitudinal dimensions are widely acknowledged in the scholarly literature as pivotal
determinants of math performance, making cultivation of positive attitudes a priority in math education (D Rural et al., 2022; Kihwele and
Mkomwa, 2022). For example, students with positive attitudes towards
mathematics tend to outperform their peers with less favorable views
and negative attitudes about the subject (Li et al., 2022). Another study
identified that high achievers in mathematics often display strong
motivation and a positive disposition towards math (Choi and Han,
2020). Existing evidence suggests that attitude may mediate the relationship between inquiry-based pedagogy and student performance,
potentially acting as a key link that enhances the effectiveness of
instructional approaches.

2. Theoretical framework
2.1. Linking inquiry-based pedagogy and math performance
With a growing emphasis on international standards and assessments, there has been mounting scholarly and policy interest in the
impact of teaching methodologies on student achievement in mathematics (Bland and Gareis, 2018). Inquiry-based pedagogy has emerged
as an instructional strategy that can enhance student performance, by
engaging learners more actively in the learning process (Hatisaru and
Erbas, 2017). Piaget’s cognitive development theory and Vygotsky’s
Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD) collectively underpin constructivist learning theories by highlighting key roles of individual exploration and social interaction in cognitive development. Based on this
framework, inquiry-based pedagogy promotes critical thinking and
problem-solving skills, motivating students to explore, question, and
apply mathematical concepts (Tinh et al., 2021a). However, despite its
demonstrated benefits, implementing new pedagogy in Vietnam faces
steep challenges, as only 20 % of teachers reportedly possess the depth
of subject knowledge and diverse teaching skills necessary to meet the
demands of modern pedagogical approaches (Tinh et al., 2021b). The
traditional method of mathematics instruction in Vietnamese secondary
schools, catering to students aged 11–15, predominantly involves
teacher-centered practices such as lectures, drills, and extensive homework (Tran et al., 2020). These approaches occupy approximately 90 %
of classroom time, leaving limited scope for individual or group
collaborative work, constituting just 8 % and 2 % of lesson time,

3.1. Participants
The present study employs the publicly available dataset from the
Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA) to investigate
the relationship between inquiry-based pedagogy, math attitude, and
math performance among 5711 Vietnamese students who participated
in PISA 2022. Administered by the Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development (OECD), PISA is a global standardized evaluation for 15-year-old students in various educational systems (Liu et al.,
2024). This assessment is nationally representative of 15-year-old
teenagers in Vietnam and encompasses questions regarding their attitude and performance in math and their teachers’ implementation of
inquiry-based pedagogy (Hori and Fujii, 2021). The present study focuses on PISA 2022 because it is focused on math as a major domain. In
2012, Vietnam’s performance in math was high but saw a decline
through 2012-2022, as shown in Fig. 2 below. By concentrating on
students included in PISA 2022, this study provides timely,
domain-specific insights into factors influencing math performance in
Vietnam.
The present study focuses on math due to the growing emphasis on
enhancing student achievement in this critical subject, which is essential
for preparing students for future challenges in science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM) fields (Asadullah et al., 2020;
Molina-Muñoz et al., 2023). Furthermore, math was a key domain in
PISA 2022, where students took cognitive assessments and completed
questionnaires on their attitude toward math and their perception of
how it is taught.

overlapping missing responses. Consequently, 5711 respondents were
included in this study, who have provided complete information. For

3.2. Data analysis

In the present study, statistical analyses were conducted using
STATA version 15.1, developed by Stata Corp LLC, College Station, TX.
In alignment with key research objectives, a structural equation model
(SEM) was crafted to examine the interrelationships among inquirybased pedagogy, student math attitude, and student math performance. This model was applied to a sample of 5711 subjects in Vietnam,
with an additional mediation analysis employed to assess the extent to
which student math attitude mediates the relationship between inquirybased pedagogy and student math performance. From a methodological
perspective, SEM offers several advantages over traditional methods,
such as Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression analysis. A principal
benefit of SEM is its capacity to analyze multiple direct and indirect
effects with precision, thereby enabling researchers to test complex
hypotheses using empirical data (Liu et al., 2024). This feature has
contributed to the increasing adoption of SEM in educational research,
where it often aligns effectively with theoretical research motivations
(Liu et al., 2023). Additionally, SEM reduces the likelihood of type-1
errors by estimating and testing multiple pathways in a joint exercise
(Mascha and Vetter, 2018). This study utilized SEM modelling with the
dual objectives of minimizing error variance and bolstering model’s
explanatory power. To ensure comparability across variables, all constructs were standardized to a global mean of zero and standard deviation of one. This standardization process is designed to ameliorate
potential issues related to structural multicollinearity, thereby
enhancing the robustness of the analysis (Shrestha, 2020). Applying
these methodological strategies ensures a rigorous and comprehensive
examination of the research hypotheses.

4. Instruments
4.1. Inquiry-based pedagogy (IBP)
In the present investigation, inquiry-based pedagogy is the independent variable. This variable is operationalized as a latent construct,
which is assessed via a seven-item scale embedded in the student
questionnaire. The questionnaire items inquire about the frequency with
which students encounter specific inquiry-based pedagogy scenarios
during their mathematics lessons. Examples of such scenarios include:
(1)“The teacher asked us to solve mathematic problems without
computing anything”,(2) The teacher asked us to explain how we solve a
mathematic problem”, (3) The teacher asked us to explain our reasoning
when solving a mathematics problem” etc.
Each of the seven items on this scale offered five response choices.
Students ’ answers were gathered using a five-point Likert scale,
allowing them to choose from (1)“Never or almost never”, (2) “Less than
half of the lessons”, (3)“About half of the lessons”, (4) “More than half of
the lessons”, (5) “Every lesson or almost every lesson”. The construct
validity of the inquiry-based pedagogy scale is substantiated by metrics
and standardized factor loadings information presented in Table 2. The
internal consistency of the scale, as measured by Cronbach’s alpha, is
0.808, indicating a high degree of reliability. Additionally, the Kaiser–Meyer–Olkin (KMO) test for sampling adequacy yields a value of
0.831, further attesting to the construct’s structural validity. The standardized factor loadings for the seven items, detailed in Table 2, range
from 0.68 to 0.90, underscoring the empirical validity of the latent
construct.

4.3. Student math performance
Student math performance is the dependent variable in this study. It
is measured as a latent construct consisting of ten plausible values. These
values are derived from student responses to a comprehensive set of
mathematical questions administered during the assessment. Each value
represents a possible score that a student might have achieved. The
average mathematics score obtained by students is 469.40, with a
standard deviation of 85.03.

of Fig. 4 juxtaposes math performance between public and private
schools; density curves reveal that public school students outperform
private school students on average. Panel C of Fig. 4 illustrates differences between rural and urban areas, with urban students having a
higher mean in math than their rural counterparts. These disaggregated
scores highlight persistent gaps in math performance related to region,
school type, and geographic location, pointing to underlying disparities
that may require targeted educational interventions.

students’ math performance. This means that at the operational level,

be fruitful in promoting this pedagogical approach among math teachers
through various pre-service and in-service teacher training and professional development programs (Callaghan et al., 2018). By so doing,

adopting inquiry-based instruction may help reduce instructional disparities by offering all students, regardless of background, opportunities
to engage in creative problem-solving and critical thinking (Zarifa et al.,
2019). This approach can help bridge the urban-rural educational gap,
creating a more inclusive learning environment across different contexts
while enhancing critical thinking and a positive attitude toward math

by providing all students with opportunities to engage in creative
thinking and contribute to innovation (Stephens et al., 2014). Findings
from the current study offer a baseline framework for educational
practitioners, school administrators, and policymakers to initiate a
paradigm shift in pedagogy.
The significant mediating effect of student attitude linking inquirybased pedagogy and student math performance highlights the essential role of attitude on academic success. When teachers involve students
in active exploration, questioning, and problem-solving, they not only
help build conceptual understanding but also promote a positive
disposition toward math learning. This positive attitude, in turn,
emerges as a powerful mechanism that augments the benefits of inquirybased instruction, linking it more directly to improved math outcomes.
Relatedly, favourable math attitude is indispensable, as it transforms
learning gains from inquiry activities into sustained motivation for
mastering math, ultimately leading to higher achievement. A study by
Choi and Han (2020), identified that high achievers in math often
display strong motivation and a positive attitude towards the subject.
For this reason, there is a need to emphasize pedagogies that address
both cognitive, behavioral, and emotional engagement to optimize
student outcomes in math. From a theoretical perspective, results from
the mediation analysis highlights the importance of incorporating affective dimensions into existing educational frameworks. Findings also
underscore the development and implementation of inquiry-based

pedagogical strategies in the math classroom and the development of
programs that equip teachers with the skills to create supportive
learning environments where positive attitudes can flourish, thereby
enhancing overall student performance.
Disaggregated analysis reveals notable differences across regions,
school types, and geographic locations in Vietnam. While students in
urban areas, northern regions, and public schools demonstrate relatively
higher math performance, their counterparts in rural areas, central and
southern regions, and private schools tend to lag behind. These disparities suggest that the positive influence of inquiry-based pedagogy may
be constrained by contextual factors such as resource availability,
teacher training, and access to supportive learning environments (Liu
and Du, 2022). Policy interventions should therefore, focus on
addressing these disparities by ensuring equitable distribution of
educational resources, expanding professional development opportunities for teachers in underserved areas, and enhancing access to quality
instructional materials in rural and less-advantaged regions. From a
theoretical perspective, this emphasizes the need to contextualize
inquiry-based learning models to account for structural inequalities that
may hold sizeable influence on educational outcomes.
7. Conclusions
The present study explored the relationship between inquiry-based
pedagogy, math attitude, and math performance among 15-year-old
Vietnamese students who participated in PISA 2022. Findings indicate
that inquiry-based pedagogy positively correlates with student math
attitude and performance. Also, student math attitudes positively and
significantly mediates the relationship between inquiry-based pedagogy
and math performance. Findings highlight the transformative potential
of inquiry-based pedagogy in math instruction, particularly in the
Vietnam. There is an urgent need for comprehensive teacher training,
well-designed instructional strategies, ongoing institutional support,
and professional development to ensure inquiry-based methods’ successful implementation and scalability. Additionally, incorporating
collaborative projects, real-world problem-solving exercises, and peerled seminars can enhance students’ cognitive and non-cognitive
capabilities.
8. Limitations
While offering valuable insights, there are several key limitations in
this study. The primary constraint is the reliance on cross-sectional
survey data, which restricts the capacity to infer causality using the
structural equation model. Additionally, the study’s scope is limited to
lower-secondary school students in Vietnam, as per sampling protocols
of PISA 2022, which warrants prudence when extrapolating findings to a
broader age-groups and cultural contexts. Future research may consider
employing longitudinal data to facilitate more robust inferences.
Tracking changes over time could provide further insights into the
sustained effects of inquiry-based pedagogy on student outcomes.
Relatedly, future work could also explore how inquiry-based pedagogy
shapes student attitude and performance on a more qualitative level via
classroom observations. This would provide a more nuanced and
comprehensive understanding of the mechanisms through which
inquiry-based approaches in math education are effective.
