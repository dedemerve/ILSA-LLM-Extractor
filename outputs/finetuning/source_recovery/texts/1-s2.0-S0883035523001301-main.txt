Based on self-regulated learning theory, this study explored the key student-level factors that
influence students’ reading literacy among high-performing countries and economies in the
Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA) in 2018. Data from the top three bestperforming countries and economies in PISA 2018 (i.e., three Asian countries and economies:
B-S-J-Z, Singapore, and Macau) was analyzed using a combination of statistical and machine
learning methods. First, the Sharpley decomposition method, which can decompose the contribution value of each factor to reading literacy, was used to investigate the impacts of different
factors on students’ reading literacy and identify the key factors that were the most influential.
Thereafter, the relationships between the identified key factors and reading literacy were further
examined using the gradient boosting decision tree method with high generalization ability.
Results show that metacognitive strategies (especially assessing credibility) are critical in shaping
students’ reading literacy in top-performing countries and economies. The findings also highlight
the importance of reading interest in student reading literacy development. Exploring the key
factors affecting high-performing students’ reading literacy at the student level may offer valuable insights for informing tailored educational policies and pedagogical practices to improve
student reading literacy development.

1. Introduction
As a result of rapid technological developments, people have various means of reading both at home and in workplaces now.
Reading literacy can reflect personal competitiveness because it is integral to one’s learning ability (Agee, 2005; Dreher et al., 2000).
Reading literacy is of increasing importance since people must adapt to the rapidly changing environment to access and learn
meaningful knowledge from various information sources (Binkley et al., 2012; OECD, 2019a, p. 22). Adolescents with stronger reading
literacy are more likely to succeed in the workforce in their adulthood (Faggella-Luby & Deshler, 2008). As a result, examining the
factors that affect students’ reading literacy has emerged as a crucial research area.
Reading is a key subject of PISA, providing opportunities to comprehensively investigate the contextual effects of reading (e.g., Hu
& Yu, 2021; Torppa et al., 2018). In PISA 2018, Beijing, Shanghai, Jiangsu, and Zhejiang (China) (hereinafter called “B-S-J-Z”),

Singapore, and Macau, three Asian countries and economies, performed exceptionally in reading. They exhibited significantly higher

economies. Meanwhile, they also scored significantly higher than the OECD average in mathematics (OECD, 2019b, p. 59) and science

education in these three countries/economies but also its balanced high performance across all subjects. In contrast, other Asian
countries/economies showed fluctuating rankings across the same subjects. Additionally, using the proportion of low performers in all
three subjects and the proportion of top students in at least one subject as indicators, according to the officially reported PISA results, it
becomes evident that among all the countries/economies, these three countries/economies are the only ones with a proportion of
students with poor performance in three subjects lower than 5 % and a proportion of students with excellent performance in at least
one subject higher than 30 % (OECD, 2019b, p.18). This further reflects the strength and stability of education in these three countries/economies. Given these considerations, we selected these three Asian countries/economies with consistently high performance in
all subjects for data analysis in this study.
Examining the associations of contextual factors with academic performance among students from top-performing countries and
economies facilitates our understanding, design, and implementation of effective pedagogical interventions for enhanced student
learning outcomes. To identify the significant factors influencing reading performance in high-performing countries and economies,
this study first analyzed the whole data from the top three best-performing countries and economies in PISA 2018. Then, the data from
each of the three countries and economies was selected for separate analyses. In the literature, relevant studies have examined the
important contextual factors for Asian students’ reading success (e.g., Dong & Hu, 2019; Liu et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023). However,
previous studies failed to comprehensively examine the critical contextual factors of reading literacy for students in top-performing
countries and economies as a whole. Among contextual factors across different levels, student-level factors have been proven to be
particularly predictive of students’ academic success (Liu et al., 2022; Stewart, 2008). This study, therefore, attempted to explore the
key contextual factors shaping top-performing readers at the student level using a combination of statistical and machine learning
methods, which is a topic that has received little research.
2. Theoretical perspective
The self-regulated learning (SRL) theory guided the selection of contextual factors for data analysis. Significant connections between SRL and academic achievement have received much attention in various research (e.g., Broadbent, 2017; Kosnin, 2007; Zheng,
2016). The SRL theory mentions that students are “active participants” in an academic task in terms of cognition, motivation, and
behavior and describes how students behave when they actively manage their learning (Zimmerman, 1986, 1990). The theory explains
the relationships between motivation, practice, cognition, and academic achievement. It incorporates the concepts of metacognition,
strategic learning, and motivation, illuminating why some people can engage in learning efficiently and intentionally (Chen et al.,
2021; Karlen, 2016; Zimmerman, 2013). Nowadays, the SRL theory has been widely used in the literature to examine student
achievement in Asian countries or economies (Bai & Wang, 2021; Lau & Ho, 2016; Qi, 2021). In addition, many studies have
emphasized the importance of SRL theory in guiding the analysis of student achievement within Asian countries or economies
(Apridayani & Teo, 2021; Deng et al., 2022; Elesio, 2023; Kosnin, 2007). Moreover, the roles of SRL in accounting for student academic
achievements are similar between the Chinese and Western educational contexts (Li et al., 2018). Therefore, we selected SRL as the
theoretical framework for our study.
In addition to SRL-related factors, a key family of student-level factors, existing research also often examined students’ background
information (Chunjin, 2020; Ho & Lau, 2018; Liu et al., 2022). Combining the SRL theory and the student factors frequently examined
in the literature, this study finalized a subset of relevant contextual factors of reading (e.g., metacognitive strategies, learning motivation and attitudes, and student backgrounds) for data analysis.
3. Literature review
The PISA 2018 assessment and analytical framework defined reading literacy as “understanding, using, evaluating, reflecting on
and engaging with texts in order to achieve one’s goals, to develop one’s knowledge and potential and to participate in society”
(OECD, 2019a, p. 28), which transforms “reading literacy” from an abstract concept into specific abilities to understand, evaluate and
reflect on the text. Notably, the new reading framework proposed by PISA 2018 emphasized the metacognitive task management
process since metacognition significantly correlates with reading performance (Chevalier et al., 2017; Memisevic & Cehic, 2022). In
the following, we briefly reviewed existing findings in the literature on the associations of reading with the targeted contextual factors
in this study.
3.1. Student background
The economic, social, and cultural status (ESCS) and gender were two commonly examined factors in the reading literature. For
example, Yalcin ((2017)) found that family ESCS had a favorable effect on students’ reading literacy by analyzing the PISA 2012 data.
Female secondary students performed better than their male peers (Lietz, 2006; Loh et al., 2020; Reilly, 2012). Moreover, Kogar (2021)
pointed out that students’ gender and ESCS significantly affect their reading literacy scores. Chiu and McBride-Chang (2006) found
that girls outperformed boys in reading, and family socioeconomic status was positively correlated with individual reading
achievement. Chunjin (2020) identified gender differences in students’ reading performance but found that reading metacognitive

strategies and reading interests were more important than student background factors (e.g., ESCS and gender) for students’ reading
performance analyzing the data of students from four provinces and cities in China. Generally, the literature has reached a consensus
that gender and ESCS significantly relate to student reading performance. Therefore, they were included in our analysis as student
background factors.
3.2. Reading metacognitive strategies
Reading metacognitive strategies are another frequently examined student-level factor of reading in the literature. Many studies
found that metacognitive strategies had a positive effect on students’ reading achievement (e.g., Callan et al., 2016; Muhid et al., 2020;
Van der Stel & Veenman, 2010). For instance, Wu et al. (2019) showed that students’ metacognitive awareness was one of the vital
factors affecting reading achievement. Lau and Chan (2003) also found that students’ reading strategies had a positive impact on their
reading literacy. They noted that the motivation for reading and the use of reading strategies varied notably between proficient and
less proficient readers. Moreover, metacognitive strategies can be enhanced to improve academic performance (Perry et al., 2019;
Teng et al., 2021). It has been found that explicit or formal instruction of metacognitive strategies improved reading comprehension.
Students who received the guidance of metacognitive strategy outperformed those who were only exposed to traditional teaching in
reading literacy (Cantrell et al., 2010; Houtveen & Van de Grift, 2007). However, Areepattamannil and Caleon (2013) claimed that
although metacognitive strategies are directly linked to student’s academic performance, there are regional differences in the relationships between these strategies and achievement, suggesting that students with different national or educational backgrounds
may respond differently to the same factors. In this study, according to the PISA contextual framework, three factors of reading
metacognitive strategies—understanding and memory, information summarization, and credibility evaluation—were included for
data analysis.
3.3. Learning motivation and attitudes
Self-efficacy and learning motivation were proven to be indicative of students’ educational achievement or lack thereof (Honicke &
Broadbent, 2016; Murayama et al., 2013). Particularly, intrinsic motivation was found to be a salient positive contributor to reading
achievement (Schiefele et al., 2012; Teng et al., 2021). The direct and positive effects of intrinsic motivation on reading were also
found in Wang and Guthrie (2004) and Wang et al. (2020). In addition, some studies identified that intrinsic motivation might
indirectly affect reading comprehension through other reading-related factors, such as the quantity of reading and metacognitive
comprehension of applying strategies (Miyamoto et al., 2019). Regarding reading attitudes, students’ interest in reading was
discovered to be a strong positive predictor of reading achievement in the literature (e.g., Tavsancil et al., 2019). The link between
reading interest and reading performance might differ across students’ national backgrounds (Ho & Lau, 2018). Other reading-related
attitudes that associate with reading performance include reading self-competence (Morgan & Fuchs, 2007; Schoor, 2016), reading
self-concept (Retelsdorf et al., 2014), self-perceptions of difficulty and competence (Beaudrie, 2018; Lafontaine et al., 2019), and
intrinsic task value (Schoor, 2016).
3.4. Research on high-performing countries and economies
Previous studies focusing on high-performing countries and economies in reading show a big variance in theoretical perspectives
and samples for data analysis. While findings on factors influencing reading literacy vary, similar conclusions can be drawn. For
instance, metacognitive strategies and reading enjoyment were strong positive predictors of students’ reading literacy in B-S-J-Z
(Chunjin, 2020; Liu et al., 2022), Singapore (Chen et al., 2021; Lee, 2014; Ma et al., 2013), and Macau (Wang et al., 2023).
However, the effects of some factors on students’ reading literacy might differ between the three countries and economies. For
example, ESCS has a significant positive effect on reading scores for both B-S-J-Z (Kılıç & Depren, 2022; Liu et al., 2022) and Singaporean students (Dong & Hu, 2019; Liu & Huang, 2023; Loh & Sun, 2020), however, Sou et al. (2021) concluded that Macau students
with high mathematics self-efficacy were more likely to perform well, regardless of ESCS levels. In addition, learning time was a
significant predictor of reading achievement among B-S-J-Z students (Liu et al., 2022) and Singaporean students (Dong & Hu, 2019),
but its effect was not observed for Macau students (Wang et al., 2023). Moreover, reading self-concept was found to positively affect
Singaporean students’ digital reading literacy (Chen et al., 2021). It was also an important predictor of Macau students’ reading literacy (Wang et al., 2023). However, its positive effect on reading was insignificant for B-S-J-Z students (Liu et al., 2022).
3.5. Why use machine learning and statistics
Data mining techniques are advancing quickly, and machine learning is widely applied in numerous fields (e.g., Bi et al., 2019; Xu
et al., 2019). Without setting priori presumptions about distributions or formulating certain hypotheses, machine learning enables the
data itself to drive discoveries, whereas statistics makes aggregate judgments from samples (Bzdok et al., 2018; Zheng et al., 2018).
Machine learning is generally more data-driven than statistical modeling, which is the major distinction between the two types of
approaches. Generally, machine learning is more suitable for making predictions, whereas statistical models are a better fit for
examining the relationships between targeted factors.
Machine learning has been increasingly employed to investigate issues in the field of education nowadays (Alsubaie, 2023; Lee,
2022; Ngufor et al., 2019; Xu et al., 2019). Given the advantages of statistical methods for analyzing relationships between factors and

machine learning method (Gradient boosting decision tree) was then used to predict students’ reading scores using the identified
significant factors.
3.6. Current study
In the literature, students’ metacognitive strategies, motivation, and background factors were found to be strongly predictive of
reading literacy. However, some studies have pointed out that the same factors may play different roles in shaping student reading
literacy across different educational backgrounds (Ho & Lau, 2018; Sullivan et al., 2018). Therefore, it would be intriguing to explore
the factors that affect reading literacy among students in top-performing countries and economies and how the identified contextual
effects of reading differ between top-performing countries and economies. To determine whether the findings for the top-performing
countries and economies are distinctive or generalizable, the same analysis was carried out concurrently for all 79 countries and
economies that took part in PISA 2018.
Using data from PISA 2018, this study first applied the Shapley value decomposition method and correlation analysis to explore the
primary factors contributing to the variance in students’ reading scores. Specifically, cohort decomposition was used to investigate the
categories of the influencing factors that predict students’ reading literacy with the Shapley value decomposition method. It can
ascertain whether traditional influencing factors also have significant impacts on reading performance for students from topperforming countries and economies. It can also identify whether there are significant differences in the influencing contextual factors of reading between B-S-J-Z, Singapore, and Macau. After analyzing the results of the Shapley value decomposition and deriving the
key factors predicting students’ reading literacy, gradient boosting decision tree (GBDT) was employed to examine the accuracy of
these significant factors in predicting students’ reading scores and investigate how each factor contributed to the prediction of reading
scores. Specific research questions are as follows:
1 At the student level, what are the main factors influencing student reading literacy among the top three high-performing countries
or economies (i.e., B-S-J-Z, Singapore, and Macau) as a whole?
2 In terms of the key factors influencing students’ reading literacy, are there discernible distinctions between the top three highperforming countries or economies in reading?
4. Methods
4.1. Data source
In PISA 2018, there are 79 participating countries and economies (37 OECD countries and 42 partner countries and economies).
This study analyzed data from the PISA 2018 database of 12,058 B-S-J-Z students (47.9 % female, 52.1 % male), 6676 Singaporean
students (49.1 % female, 50.9 % male), 3775 Macau students (49.3 % female, 50.7 % male), in comparison with the whole data from
PISA 2018 (612,004 students, 49.8 % female, 50.2 % male). The selected student-level factors involved students’ background, reading
metacognitive strategies, and learning motivation and attitudes. Detailed descriptions of all factors are provided in the supplementary
material (see Tables A1 and A2).
To estimate the likelihood distribution of students’ reading scores, PISA 2018 provided ten plausible values (PV), which were
generated based on a variety of students’ contextual factors. As all PVs are randomly drawn from the posterior probability distribution
(OECD, 2009, p. 95), it is common to use a single PV as the outcome variable in the literature, since there should not be any systematic
bias involved with choosing one PV over another (Gabriel et al., 2018; Liu et.al., 2022). To quantify students’ reading literacy for this
study, the first plausible value (PV1READ) was selected as the outcome variable.
4.2. Data cleaning
Regarding missing value handling, the median of each factor was used to impute missing values in this study, given that it is
unaffected by extreme values. Since PISA 2018 did not specify the reading literacy levels of students with scores below 262, their data
were excluded from the data analysis.
4.3. Analytic approaches
The Shapley value decomposition method was used to explore the relationships between contextual factors and reading scores (e.g.,
remove unimportant factors and identify important categories of factors that affect reading scores). The GBDT was then used to predict
reading scores using the identified important factors.
4.3.1. Sharpley value decomposition method
The basic algorithm of the Sharpley value decomposition method is to decompose by calculating the relative contribution rate of
each explanatory factor in the combined model to the variation of the explained factor. The standard ordinary least squares regression

result only shows the absolute value of the marginal influence of the explanatory factors on the explained factors. However, it cannot
decompose the relative contribution rate of each explanatory factor to the source of variation of the explained factors. Sharpley value
decomposition method is based on the combination of conventional regression equation decomposition and the Shapley value method,
which can decompose the inequality of the target factor into the contribution of its determinants and quantify the contribution of each
regression factor to the inequality of the dependent factor.
First, the method calculates the interpretation rate R2 of each explanatory factor to the total variation of the target factor in the
combined model, and then gradually eliminates explanatory factors to examine the overall change of the model. The change degree of
R2 reflects the relative contribution of the eliminated explanatory factor to the variation of the target factor. Using this method, this
study decomposed the relative contribution rate of the interpretation rate of the overall variation of students’ reading literacy scores,
to find out the key factors that lead to the gap in students’ reading literacy scores.
4.3.2. Gradient boosting decision tree
One of the most effective approaches to increase the performance of machine learning models (e.g., to improve prediction or
classification performance) is ensemble learning (Khalifa et al., 2019; Zhou et al., 2002). GBDT is a popular model in ensemble
learning. It uses decision trees as the basis function and adopts the concept of boosting. Boosting is an integrated learning framework in
machine learning that combines multiple weak classifiers into a strong classifier. This strong classifier can take advantage of all weak
classifiers to achieve relatively optimal performance. GBDT iterates multiple regression trees to make joint decisions. With each
iteration of the GBDT method, a new decision tree is created in the direction of residual reduction, and subsequent iterations on this
foundation increase the accuracy of the prediction outputs. GBDT is an iterative decision tree algorithm combining the predictions of
all trees to make final predictions. It is considered an algorithm with strong generalizability.
Meanwhile, GBDT as a tree-based ensemble algorithm inherits the intrinsic interpretability of decision trees. The good interpretability of GBDT can be reflected in the fact that feature importance scores can be calculated (Cheng et al., 2018; Ma et.al, 2017).
Compared to other black-box models, GBDT can visualize the importance of each factor, which is more conducive to understanding. In
real life, it is inappropriate to use only the results drawn from a black-box model with no interpretability at all to arrive at recommendations for important decisions because the models are usually highly opaque, lack interpretability, and do not allow one to know
exactly how each factor plays a role, and so are not conducive to decision making.
In this study, the factors selected are of either discrete values (e.g., gender) or continuous values, and GBDT can process them
flexibly without prior manipulation, which simplifies the complexity of feature preprocessing. It also generalizes well to densely
distributed datasets, an advantage that makes it well-suited to the dataset.

5. Result
5.1. Feature analysis
First, this study employed the Shapley value decomposition to analyze the whole data of B-S-J-Z, Singapore, and Macau, and the
results are presented in Table 1. The relative contribution rates of students’ background, reading metacognitive strategies, and reading
motivation and attitudes to the variation of students’ reading literacy scores are 18.87 %, 53.83 %, and 27.30 %, respectively.
Table 1 presents that work mastery was not significant, which suggests that this factor does not explain the variance in student
reading scores. The change in reading time on students’ reading scores, although significant, had a very low relative contribution.
Subsequently, each sample was used to examine the factors that affect students’ reading performance separately for each highperforming Asian country/economy as well as all countries and economies participating in PISA 2018 (as a whole), with the analysis results included in the supplemental material (see Tables A3, A4, A5, and A6).
Based on the whole data, a correlation analysis was conducted between the factors and reading literacy. Due to a great number of
factors, only the correlation coefficients between reading scores and contextual factors are presented in Table 2.
Table 2 presents that gender, reading time, work mastery, sense of self-efficacy, and self-education expectations had a low correlation with reading scores. However, when combined with the results in Table 1, it can be concluded that only reading time and work
mastery have both low correlation and low regression coefficients.
5.2. Model training and factor importance
For machine learning analyses, the datasets were divided into two parts: 80 % of the samples were randomly selected for training,
and 20 % were used for testing. This study identified the optimal learning rate of 0.1 by grid search. The number of folds (cv value) was
fixed at 10. Regarding the maximum number of weak prediction models and max depth, the grid search results are shown in Fig. 1.
Fig. 1 showed that the combination of a max depth of 3 and a maximum number of weak prediction models parameter (n_estimators) of 500 achieved the minimum error value. Using the optimal hyperparameters, this study subsequently developed and
examined the final GBDT model. The ultimate model explained 49.90 % of the variance (i.e., R2 = 0.499). The relative importance of
these contextual factors in predicting student reading performance is shown in Fig. 2.
As can be seen in Fig. 2, for the whole data of B-S-J-Z, Singapore, and Macau, assessing credibility has the highest importance, with
a relative proportion of 38 %, followed by summarizing information and ESCS.
5.3. Comparison of important factors of reading between B-S-J-Z, Singapore, and Macau
The relative impact scores of the influencing factors on the model predictions for each region were included in the supplemental
materials (see Figures A1, A2, and A3).
Using student-level factors, the GBDT model had the highest fit for B-S-J-Z and the lowest for Macau. The GBDT model had the
lowest prediction error in predicting reading literacy for B-S-J-Z students, whereas Singaporean students were predicted with the
highest prediction error (see Table 3).
Table 4 showed that whether analyzing the three best-performing countries and economies separately or collectively, it is all
accessible to infer the significance of metacognitive factors and interest in reading, particularly the assessing credibility in metacognition. However, the results for all 79 countries or economies that participated in PISA 2018 presented a somewhat different
picture. In this broader context, the importance of interest in reading appears to be reduced, ranking lowest among the key factors. In
addition, students’ self-conceptions of perceived difficulty in reading were highlighted in the analysis for all countries and economies,
differing from the findings for the top-performing Asian countries or economies.

6. Discussion
The statistical approach used in this study is the Shapley value decomposition. Unlike other regression models, it quantifies the
contribution of variables to inequality in reading achievement, yielding the relative contribution of different variables to students’
reading achievement, in addition to the absolute value of a variable’s marginal impact on reading achievement. This method’s
advantage is that it provides the rate at which each regression variable explains the change in the dependent variable, making it
straightforward to visualize the differences in the importance of the factors. In addition, we selected GBDT as the machine learning
approach. As an ensemble learning approach with a decision tree-based learner and additive model with Boost framework, it provides
higher prediction accuracy and better model performance compared to individual models. Since it is a tree-based learner, GBDT has the
interpretability of decision trees and can produce the importance scores of factors (Ma et al., 2017). This advantage facilitates the
analysis of real-world data and direct decision-making since it allows for an intuitive understanding of each factor’s role, informing the
validity of prediction results.
The capacity to illuminate the significance of the variables is a feature shared by both approaches. Therefore, in this study, the
finding of the Shapley value decomposition method can be used to validate the GBDT method results. The consistency between the two
approaches can be used to foster the validity of the findings.
In response to research question 1, this study concluded that the most important factors influencing students’ reading performance
in high-performing countries and economies (B-S-J-Z, Singapore, and Macau as a whole) are metacognitive factors (assessing credibility and summarizing information) and ESCS, which is aligned with previous findings in the literature (e.g., Beaudrie, 2018; Callan
et al., 2016; Kogar, 2021; Loh & Sun, 2020; Wu et al., 2019; Yalcin, 2017). In addition, consistent with the literature (Chunjin, 2020;
Wang et al., 2023), reading interest, and understanding and remembering were also found to play an important role in shaping reading
performance. In the overall analysis, the role of assessing credibility far outweighs the role of the other factors, highlighting the
significance of metacognition in reading in high-performing countries and economies (Callan et al., 2016; Van der Stel & Veenman,
2010). In addition, the importance of metacognitive strategies was highlighted by the Shapley value decomposition method, consistent
with the finding by the GBDT model, supporting the credibility of this conclusion.
This study found that metacognition (particularly assessing credibility and summarizing information) is important for reading
achievement in high-performing countries and economies, which may provide some suggestions for countries or economies that want
to improve their students’ reading performance. Also, this finding supports much previous research highlighting the strong correlation
between metacognition and reading performance (Chunjin, 2020; Wang et al., 2023). Reading interest has been found to be strongly
related to high reading performance in this study (Chen et al., 2021; Tavsancil et al., 2019). This is an implication that developing
students’ interest in reading is of non-negligible importance to students’ reading achievement and that cultivating students’ interest in
reading for pleasure will have a significant positive effect on improving students’ reading performance. ESCS, as a student’s background factor, also contributed significantly to high reading performance (Dong & Hu, 2019; Liu et al., 2022). Meanwhile, this study
also analyzed all data from the 79 countries and economies that participated in PISA 2018, identifying different results from those for
high-performing Asian countries and economies. Specifically, the major difference is that the self-concept of perceived difficulty was
emphasized in the analysis of the whole PISA 2018 data. Although this factor has been examined in previous studies focusing on high
reading performance, it was not considered one of the most influential factors (Liu et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023). Moreover, our
findings show that ESCS ranked second in terms of factor importance, surpassing summarizing information. However, in earlier studies
focusing on high-performing countries or economies in reading in the East and West, the factor of summarizing information was found
to be more influential than ESCS in shaping reading performance (e.g., Lee, 2014). These differences may also indicate the uniqueness
of the conclusions drawn from our investigation into high-performing Asian countries and economies in reading.
Concerning the key factors affecting students’ reading literacy and whether there are significant differences between these three
high-performing countries and economies, addressing research question 2, this study concluded that for B-S-J-Z students, metacognition (assessing credibility and summarizing information), ESCS, reading interest, and reading time were the key factors that
influenced students’ reading literacy. In Singapore, the critical factors were reading interest, metacognition (understanding and
remembering and assessing credibility), sense of self-efficacy, and self-concept: perceived competence. The major factors influencing
the reading literacy of Macau students are metacognition (assessing credibility and summarizing information), reading interest, and
self-concept (perceived difficulties and perceived self-competence).
There are similarities in the key factors affecting students’ reading literacy in these three high-performing countries and economies.
This study found that metacognitive factors (assessing credibility) and reading interest demonstrated significant impacts on students’

reading literacy in all three countries and economies (Chunjin, 2020; Kılıç & Depren, 2022; Liu et al., 2022; Tavsancil et al., 2019;
Wang et al., 2023).
However, the analysis results are inconsistent between different educational backgrounds in our study, which aligns with previous
findings in the literature (e.g., Ho & Lau, 2018; Sullivan et al., 2018). This study found that despite the importance of metacognition,
the importance of these three metacognitive factors manifested differently across the three countries and economies. Previous findings
have noted that students with high reading performance focus more on assessing the credibility of sources and summarizing their main
points in metacognitive strategies (Kılıç & Depren, 2022; Koyuncu & Fırat, 2020). Assessing credibility occupied the first place of
important factors in the analyses of B-S-J-Z and Macau (Liu et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023), but in third place in the analysis of
Singapore. Meanwhile, only B-S-J-Z and Macau showed the importance of summarizing information in affecting reading performance,
while Singapore emphasized understanding and remembering. Among the important factors affecting the reading performance of
Singaporean students, the metacognitive strategy factor still held an important position. However, the ranking of the metacognitive
strategy factors differed from that of B-S-J-Z and Macau.
It is clear that metacognition is important for reading performance, especially assessing credibility and summarizing information
(Fırat & Koyuncu, 2023), which suggests that improving students’ reading performance does not depend heavily on understanding and
remembering, but rather on developing students’ ability to assess the credibility of information and integrate information first. This
requires teachers to provide more guidance and practice in the teaching process to improve students’ assuring credibility and summarizing skills.
Although metacognition is in importance, students’ self-concept and sense of efficacy are also important. Perceptions of selfcompetence were only noted in Singapore and Macau analyses and the significance of self-perception of difficulties (Beaudrie,
2018; Lafontaine et al., 2019) was only identified for Macau students (Wang et al., 2023). The significance of self-efficacy in shaping
reading performance (Chen et al., 2021; Honicke & Broadbent, 2016) was only observed in Singapore. Based on these results, schools
and teachers may need to design instructional processes that focus not only on the development of metacognitive skills and reading
interest, but also on students’ self-perceptions of ability and difficulty, and self-efficacy, and should pay more attention to supporting
and giving students more relevant instruction in this area during the teaching process.
Furthermore, the significance of ESCS and reading time were observed for B-S-J-Z (Liu et al., 2022), but their effects were not
significant for Macau (Sou et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2023) and Singapore. The results showed that the ESCS did not have a significant
impact across all three countries and economies. And in case ESCS has a significant effect on students’ reading achievement, the
Education Department or the government may need to find some measures to improve ESCS (Kılıç & Depren, 2022).
Meanwhile, it is crucial to note that our analysis suggests that the effects of gender on reading performance vary across contexts,
consistent with previous studies (Marks, 2008). In the analysis for all countries and economies, the effect of gender was significantly
negative (see Table A6), while it was significantly positive in the analysis for the top three countries and economies (see Table 1). This
implies that, on average, boys outperform girls in reading in top-performing countries and economies. However, globally, girls show
advantages in reading over boys. Specific to each of the three top-performing countries and economies, the results showed that the
effect of gender is significantly positive for B-S-J-Z, significantly negative for Macau, and insignificant for Singapore (see Tables A3,
A4, and A5). These results show that gender played a mixed role in shaping reading among the top three countries and economies.
Overall, the gender gap in reading was significant for B-S-J-Z and Macau but was not observed for Singapore. Boys’ advantages in
reading over girls might also be a key contributor to B-S-J-Z’s highest rank in reading in PISA 2018. As suggested by the interpretation
rates of Sharpley value decomposition, among the three countries and economies, the effect size for gender was strongest for B-S-J-Z.
This suggests that B-S-J-Z’s high performance in reading can be further investigated from the gender perspective.
Combining the results from the Shapley value decomposition method, it is evident that the factor category that contributed the
most to student achievement in all three countries/economies was metacognitive strategies. Only the results for B-S-J-Z demonstrated
a stronger role for student background factors than for reading motivation and attitude factors in predicting reading performance. This
might explain why ESCS was among the top five factors only for B-S-J-Z. In addition, the GBDT results in our study echoes previous
finding that reading enjoyment was a strong contributor to student reading achievement (e.g., Ho & Lau, 2018; Lee, 2014). However,
the influential role of reading enjoyment was not supported by the results of the Shapley Value Decomposition method in our study.
Compared to other contexts, the findings on the most influential contributors to reading for Macau were more consistent between the
Shapley value decomposition and the GBDT. In general, our study demonstrates that the two adopted approaches can be used to
cross-validate the most influential factors of reading from both the statistical and machine learning perspectives.
The findings of this study imply that improving students’ reading literacy should start with improving students’ metacognitive
strategies and reading interests. More concretely, it is important to cultivate students’ habits in reading, pay attention to their needs
and difficulties in reading, provide reasonable guides to students to solve reading-related problems and enhance students’ self-concept
of reading. These measures may be helpful for increasing students’ interest in reading. Subsequently, it is necessary to strengthen our
understanding of students’ reading cognitive strategies and implement effective pedagogical interventions. At the same time, teachers
are also required to provide more guidance for students to develop their reading literacy (e.g., reading independently, receiving more
input, and producing more output of reading) and help students set appropriate goals, especially for low-achieving students, to develop
self-confidence and improve their self-efficacy and self-concept of their abilities.
7. Conclusion and limitations
This study examined student-level factors that influenced reading literacy among students in high-performing countries and
economies using the PISA 2018 data. This study concluded that, in general, metacognitive factors, ESCS, and interest in reading were

significant predictors of students’ reading performance among high-performing countries and economies. This finding is distinct from
that of all participating countries and economies in PISA 2018 as a whole.
In separate analyses of the three high-performing countries and economies, our findings concluded that metacognitive factors and
interest in reading were significantly predictive of student reading literacy among all three top-performing countries and economies.
However, crucial factors affecting students’ reading literacy vary between the three top-performing countries and economies. Specifically, ESCS and reading time, self-efficacy and self-perceptions of competence, and self-concept of reading are particularly predictive of reading literacy for B-S-J-Z, Singaporean, and Macau students, respectively. This study used the Shapley value
decomposition method to visualize the contribution of each factor to reading, in comparison with the results of the GBDT approach.
The similarity in the results between the two approaches increases the validity and interpretability of our findings (e.g., results from
the Shapley value decomposition method can help explain the importance of ESCS in predicting reading performance suggested by
GBDT). Overall, this study found that reading metacognitive strategies, ESCS, and reading interests significantly associate with students’ reading literacy in top-performing countries and economies.
In terms of limitations, this study mainly explored the factors that affect students’ reading literacy at the student level. Future
studies are encouraged to examine how other types of contextual factors, such as school-level factors, affect students’ reading literacy
among top-performing countries and economies. Moreover, this paper failed to examine how the identified key student-level factors
interact in shaping student reading performance. Finally, since the research questions in our study were addressed by analyzing crosssectional data, causal relationships between contextual factors and reading literacy should be concluded with caution.
